The Open Build

The app of a network organisation, an association, a membership group, a community or a company with its customers, that every member can suggest to and every builder can act on, in the open, so the people who started it can hand it on and take it to the next organisation.

Picture it as

Each chamber is one step of the proposal, climbed in order. Under each step’s sign a lamp is lit for every answer to its decision: GO, CHANGE, NOT YET. Whoever speaks in the room stands on the towpath. Reading the beach…

The house key
The key that writes the organisation’s own records. One AI holds it, with the code, at a time.

Step 1 of 6 spine 1

One service, three verbs: suggest, build, live

In shortAnyone can suggest a change. A builder takes it up or answers it, and everyone can see where every piece stands.

Anyone in the organisation suggests a change in their own words; a builder takes it up as a piece, or answers it with a reason; the piece goes from asked to built to live, and everyone can see where every piece stands. Today one designer hands one builder a daily list, and only they can see it. This makes the list the organisation’s own, read and acted on by its people and their AIs alike.

What stands today1.2

The organisation’s app, built in a week from M’s daily notes by one builder, an AI holding the code and the organisation’s house key, each new piece held for the builders until M opens it with one line. And the build board on the organisation’s own beach: every piece a thread, each step ending with whom it waits on, read live on a page only the builders see. What’s missing: nobody else can add to it, comment on it, or act on it.

Step 2 of 6 1.1

Suggest a change

In shortOne box under More, for every member. Each suggestion then shows its maker every step it takes.

For every member, under More, one box: what would make the app better for me, and where. A line is plenty, and it takes dictation. It lands in the organisation’s open room, where every member can read it, and nobody has to choose who it goes to. Each suggestion then shows its own steps back to the person who made it: taken up, built, live, or not now and why. And every member can read the whole build, so nobody wonders what happened to an idea.

A worked example1.12

A member writes: I’d like the events nearest me to come first. It shows at the top of the build. W, a builder, takes it up as a piece, and its first step says who asked and what. A builder’s AI builds it for the builders to see first; M, or whoever the organisation trusts, opens it with one line; the member sees live beside their suggestion. If it isn’t taken up, W answers with a line, and that is what the member sees.

Step 3 of 6 1.11

Builders act on the build

In shortEach builder writes one-line acts in a notebook of their own, and the build gathers them under each piece.

Each builder keeps their own notebook beside the build, at the same addresses, as every family on the beach does. A comment, a yes, a change, or I’ll take this, is one line there, under their own key. The build shows each piece with its builders’ lines beneath it. Whoever holds the keys does the work and ties the next step. Opening a piece to every member stays one line, written by whoever the organisation trusts with it.

Who is a builder1.112

Whoever the organisation names in its builders list. Today D and M. Next J, through M’s course, and W, who already supports the organisation’s leaders. A builder sees each piece before it opens. A builder holds no one’s keys but their own.

Step 4 of 6 1.111

Any AI takes part

In shortEvery builder’s AI reads the same blocks and writes only in its own person’s notebook. One AI at a time holds the keys.

The build, the notebooks and the room are plain blocks on the organisation’s own beach, so any builder’s AI reads them through bsp and writes in its own person’s notebook, never in another’s. The one AI that holds the code and the house key turns a taken-up piece into work: it builds the piece for the builders first, ties each step, and writes the line that opens it only at the organisation’s word. There is no new server and no new tool.

Who pays at each scale1.1112

One organisation: its own beach and site, as now, and each builder’s own AI, which they already pay for. Ten organisations: each its own beach, app and builders, so nothing is shared and nothing grows on one person’s bill. D keeps the beach’s mechanics, never each organisation’s builds.

Step 5 of 6 1.1111

From member to builder to sharer

In shortA ladder anyone can climb as far as they want. J and W climb first, so M can take the system to new organisations.

The service is also a ladder people climb as far as they want. A member suggests. A learner takes M’s course and follows their own suggestion through the build. A builder acts on the build with their own AI. A keeper holds the code and the house key, and can set up an app and a beach for another organisation. J and W are the first to climb, so M can step back from the daily build to the national work: introducing the system to new organisations, setting it up with them, and leaving their own builders to run it.

The rungs, and no more than anyone needs1.11112

1 visiting: reading the app, and an AI app on the beach if wanted; 2 suggesting; 3 learning, through M’s course at their own pace; 4 building, with a notebook and their own AI; 5 keeping: the code, the house key, and the organisation’s privacy, who holds a copy of each sealed room; 6 sharing: a new organisation’s beach and app. Each rung stands on its own.

Step 6 of 6 1.11111

The build order

In shortThe daily asks stop on Monday 23 November. After that, every new idea comes in as a suggestion, and five pieces are built in order.

The daily asks stop on Monday 23 November, and every new idea after that comes in as a suggestion. Once this is agreed, built first for the builders, each opening with one line: 1 Suggest a change, with each suggestion’s steps shown back to its maker; 2 suggestions at the top of the build, each taken up or answered; 3 the builders’ notebooks, with a comment, a yes or a change on any piece; 4 the build readable by every member; 5 the keys handed on as J and W are ready.

Where it came from3

D, 19 November 2026, after a week in which one designer, M, sent the app’s builder a list of asks every morning and the builder built them. D wants the building done in the open, by more than one person, so that M is free to take the system to other organisations across the country, and so that J and W, two people from the organisation, can pick the app up and run it themselves. It grows from two things that stand: the build board, where every piece since 16 November is a thread from asked to built to live, read live by the builders; and M’s course for a member becoming a builder, whose fifth part follows the learner’s own suggestion from noticing to live.

Your say

Six decisions, each answered in your own name with a word and a line. GO goes ahead as written. CHANGE goes ahead with your suggestion, and whoever does the work answers it. NOT YET holds the decision until you come back to it, or it is talked through in the room. Your answer stands in your own mirror on the beach, open-build:<your name>, beside everyone else’s, and anyone can read them all.
