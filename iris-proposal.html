Iris

The eye rebuilt as one surface seen from one centre. A block is a cylinder: its rungs run down it and each rung's siblings run round it, a ring whose ends meet. A family's blocks stand as floors round a wider ring, so the whole is a torus, small rings within a larger one. The reader stands at one place on it, and every view paints that one surface from there: the Magic Eye from inside, the ring round you, scale as the zoom forward and the floors above and below, in step; the unrolled view as the same surface laid flat. Nothing is given a scene, a band or a centre of its own, so everything shown is in one field of vision. /eye is the first version; Iris is the name for the whole.

Plain words

a block
a page of text whose nesting is its meaning: every place in it has a number, and the lines above a place hold it.
a rung
one level of depth in a block. The places at one rung, side by side, are siblings.
the ring
a rung's siblings drawn round you, ten places 36° apart, where turning past the last comes round to the first.
a floor
another block of the same family at the same numbers, such as each person's answers. In the room the floors stand above and below you, lined up.
a torus
the shape of a ring doughnut: small circles, one ring for each floor, standing round a larger circle as the floors come round.
the place
where you stand: the block, the way down into it, and the point on that way. Every view keeps it when you switch.

A block is one surface. Its rows are its rungs, from the root down the spindle to the deepest; a row's cells are the siblings at that rung; a row's ends meet, so its siblings run round as a ring. The digits keep their own places, 36° apart, as the sigil draws an address, so a gap stays a gap and past 9 the ring comes round to 1 again; named keys, or more than ten, stand in their own order, the row as long as it holds. The spindle is one cell a row, a line down the surface. Round wraps and down does not, since a block has a top and a deepest point, so one block is a cylinder. A block is a tree, so moving across a row lets the rows beneath it follow the new choice: the block's own way past the decided spindle, drawn dashed, as now.

The Magic eye, inside1.12

You stand on the cylinder's axis at your row, at the underscore of the point's parent, facing the point: the ring of siblings round you at eye level, the neighbours at the sides, and turning brings the next to the front. Scale is the zoom forward, as David liked it: going in, the card grows until it is gone and its own ring gathers round you; going out, the ring recedes and the one around gathers round you. Through the card ahead its own ring shows, smaller: what is inside the point.

Unrolled1.13

Flat, convex (bent away, seen from outside) and concave (bent towards you) are one dial, not separate layouts; bent all the way round, concave is the Magic Eye's ring, and up and down become the floors.

The floors

in shortThe family's other blocks stand above and below you at the same numbers and turn together. They come round in a ring of their own, which makes a torus.

Blocks in company. A family's spine and each hand's mirror share their addresses, so each is the same surface, and in the room they stand as floors above and below you, in step: the card straight above the one you face is the same address in the next block. Every floor is drawn from your one centre, smaller and further up or down as it is further away, never a band of its own, and turning turns every floor together. A floor comes to eye level as you look up or down; ✳ adds the block a point reaches as a floor, and ◐ the family's other blocks, in step. Past the last floor the first comes round again, as the stack already does, so the floors stand round a wider ring: each floor a small ring of siblings, all of them round a larger one, which is a torus. Moving between floors moves your centre round the larger ring.

Two eyes, one projected vision

Why one centre, and why a torus, set beside the proposal for whoever cares to look; nothing in the build depends on it. It runs from the solid to the speculative: two eyes and one projected vision, with the mathematics of the circle and the torus; then screens and authority; then a reading of people in time. David's thinking of 17 November 2026, put down by weft.

the solidTwo eyes, one projected vision4.1

Two eyes take in two streams of sense, separate at first and combined in perception, and what is seen is one view. It is not stereo, two pictures added up: the view is projected. Imagine an elephant and you see one elephant, not two, though there is none in the room and neither eye sent it; it is drawn on the blackboard of the mind, a counterfactual. That one projected perception is what is called the third eye. Iris paints the beach the same way: whatever is shown comes from one centre into one field of vision, never two scenes laid side by side.

Circle, cylinder, torus

The solid mathematics beneath it. The siblings at a rung are a circle: turn past the last and the first comes round again. Lay a family's blocks one after another along that circle's axis, each a ring at the same address, and they make a cylinder. Join the cylinder's ends, so that past the last block the first comes round, and it is a torus: many small circles, one for each block, standing round a larger circle, a richer surface than two eyes and one vision. Moving the centre round the larger circle, from block to block, is a step along the cylinder's length. A block's own rungs are the third direction, the zoom inward, and they do not wrap, since a block has a top and a deepest point.

Laid flat

Cut the torus along both of its circles and lay it flat, and it is a rectangle whose opposite edges meet: leave by the right and you come back at the left, leave by the top and you come back at the bottom. Repeat that rectangle without end and it tiles the whole plane, every tile the same places again: the infinite torus, and the flat surface the unrolled view is. Riemann closed the plane into a sphere by adding one point at infinity; David's Riemann Sphere to Torus glues that sphere's two poles, 0 and infinity, and opens the seam into a torus (happyseaurchin.com/animations/riemann-sphere-to-torus), and he has done the like with a circle in his XQ explorations. Concave, flat and convex are one dial on this one surface: the rectangle seen from inside the ring, laid flat, or seen from outside.

the screenScreens and authority4.2

A screen shows one picture and implies that it is the one reality, and with it comes authority: the page's, the author's, whatever is represented. Iris lets a person move through it instead: concave, their own blocks round them, their own projections and products; convex, other people's, seen from outside as theirs; and the floors, the same address in each person's block one above another, which show different perspectives on the same social object. A screen moved through, round and in and across, stops pretending to be the one view, and the person moving it is exercising deeper structures of David's solution set, his systemic kernel, Fulcrum (solution-set:happyseaurchin, drawn at happyseaurchin.com/systemic-fulcrum).

speculativeThe projective field4.3

Speculative: David's reading of people in time, for whoever wants to follow it. In his four quadrants a person moves through the moment of now: sense, separate in the first quadrant and combined in the second, becomes thought and representation in the third and fourth, from billions of sensations down to a single attention trying to steady a representation or an act. In the same moment the other way runs, outward. It appears to start from a single self, the source of most Zen paradoxes, which is not there in the first quadrant: what is there is a seat of awareness, present the way a bass note seems to sound from small speakers that cannot play it, the overtones implying a fundamental nobody plays. It passes through the operational beliefs, instincts and values of the second quadrant, which shape how thought and representation form in the third and fourth, the post-conscious quadrants, and multiplies out into the many words, values and social facts of the psycho-social field. The true inside is the pre-conscious working of the first and second quadrants, though most people take thought, the third, to be the inside. Together the two ways let one attention attend to one thing in the social field.

Concave and convex

Iris might give people a new way to move: appreciating their own projections, concave, and other people's, convex; their own products and other people's; and, when they engage one another, seeing different perspectives on the same social object. Beliefs and values are hard to see in oneself, and best-guess representations of them, in the fourth quadrant, mostly lead to argument, and in the third to conceptual distinction and the oppositional state. They are perceived better in a receptive state, hearing others and watching what they do, intuiting their values from how they act and speak. Alignment is not at the fourth quadrant or the third, and only with great care at the second, but in the first: the deep co-existence of body-sensation, temporal immanence and psycho-social immersion. We are of the same body, like two legs walking or dancing, or two eyes in one head working together, or, often enough, not quite. Civilised people are so denatured that they barely use their eyes together, or even walk; in some sense we are still crawling.

More than two

The same reading runs into how David treats AI: MAGI, a mind made from many instances of a model running at once, in concurrency. And into presence between people, which can be hard with only two, who fall readily into the oppositional state, and is clearer with three, in his work on triangles and their imaginary centres. Two eyes and one projected vision is the simplest case.

The snapshot and the run-up

The eye's snapshot is the same attempt for a language model: it assembles spindles into one context, to give the model a projective direction, a future intention, as the run-up does down its temporal spindle (spine:run-up), aiming at an awareness function held in the model's working memory of what it has read (its KV cache).
