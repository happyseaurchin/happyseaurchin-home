The Key

In shortOne name and one key: the key proves your writes, the beach keeps only its hash, your words are open unless you seal them, and a key that leaks is rolled.

One name you choose and one key you keep are the whole of it: typed once at whichever door you use, the key proves that every write is yours, and the beach keeps only its hash; what you write is open to everyone unless you seal it or your beach stands behind a wall; your own AI can act as you without ever seeing the key; and a key that leaks is rolled, not mourned, because the beach is sand castles and not a fortress

Its thread

Five words, in plain terms

Latch
The beach’s one lock at every position: a hash made from your key with the beach, the block and the position. The beach keeps the hash, never the key.
Members’ beach
A beach an organisation runs for its members, behind a wall: a stranger is shown only what its keepers mark beyond, and one refusal for everything else.
Seal and colour
Words only a readership can read: yourself, two people, a list, or a colour, a readership kept in one block whose keeper admits people and turns its key. Everyone else sees one grey line.
Vault
One block of your own holding every key you have: each entry’s label in plain words, the key beneath it sealed. One key opens the vault, and the vault opens the rest.
Fingerprint
What the router mints when you sign an AI in once: your key sealed to it, so the connection acts as you without ever seeing the key. Revoke it and the key stays.

In shortA members’ beach shows a stranger nothing but what its keepers mark beyond, lets members in on a twelve-hour pass, and is still a perimeter, not a seal.

In shortA sealed line is read only by the people it is for; everyone else sees a grey line saying it is sealed, and that it exists.

Words only a readership can read: for yourself, between two, for a list, or in a colour, a readership kept in one block whose keeper admits and turns; everyone else reads one grey line naming the colour and its turn; the beach stores no key that opens a line, a seal hides the words and never that the line exists, and a seal is chosen line by line, so what you give the commons stays open; the beach's host still hears the sealing word at every door that seals with the latch, which today is every door but a session passing a second word

In shortEvery key you hold sits in one block of your own, labelled in plain words and sealed beneath; one key opens it.

Every key you hold, in one block of your own: its law and each entry's label in plain words, the key beneath sealed under your vault key, so a keyless reader learns what you hold and never a value; one key opens the vault and the vault opens the rest; a page appends an entry, an image key or a character's key, without rewriting the block

An AI Without the Key

In shortSign your AI in once on the router’s own page; it gets a fingerprint and a token, writes as you, and never sees your key.

A Claude on your phone, your desktop, in Claude Code or in a scheduled routine connects to the router at one address and signs in once on the router's own page, typing your name and key there and nowhere else; the router mints a fingerprint, seals the key to it and hands the connection a token that carries the fingerprint and never the key; from then on the connection writes as you, opens your vault and founds blocks under your key by saying the word vault, and never sees the key; revoke a fingerprint and the key stays

When a Key Leaks

In shortIf a key leaks: keep a copy, scrub it, roll the key wherever it latches, re-seal, and tell everyone who held it, in that order.

Keep a copy of what stood, scrub the key from every position, roll the key at every block it latches, re-seal what the old key sealed, and tell every holder and session that held it, in that order and none skipped; what is yours is a reflection put back from a copy, and openness stays the posture

In shortOne lock at every position: a hash of the key, checked on every write, never the key itself.

The Ground

In shortWhich servers see what: the beach’s host and store, the router, the mirror’s host, the waker, the model’s provider, your phone and the copies.

What each server sees: the beach's code runs on Vercel and its blocks rest in Upstash, whose terms bar special category data, and every latch arrives there in a post's body from every door; the router on Railway sees every key on its open address and every keyed read and write unsealed, and holds opened keys, worn turns and passes in memory; the mirror's host sees each passphrase it is handed and the keys it mints; the doorbell waker on Railway is handed a character's key in plain words; the model's provider sees the words of every opened line, and on the open address the key itself; the phone sees only its own; and the copies, thirty days at the door, the nightly images and the private mirror, hold everything for a year and in a history; so a beach for health-related work stands on a host of its own
