The Run-up

What a run-up leaves in a model, found by its effect rather than by what the model says. A run-up is the spindle a model walks before it hears a word: at each fork it chooses, the path grows by its choice, and the paths it did not take are left behind. The three sessions of 10 November 2026 named the same object three ways. Here are five paths to it, ordered by how soon each reaches a practical result: a measurable change of state in how a model responds after its orientation, and the conversational app of the middle point.

Six words in plain terms

  • The run-upThe path a model walks before it hears a word: six rungs, one way taken at each. It used to be called the injector.
  • A rung and its waysA point on the run-up where several ways are offered and one is taken. The ways taken, in order, are its spindle.
  • The shadowWhat each rung gave up: how much weight the ways not taken still had. One minus the way taken.
  • The digitA number from 0 to 1 that a model gives first, before any words: how interesting is this?
  • The state it leavesWhat a run-up lights up inside a model. The part kept for every word, the KV cache, is all that reaches what comes next.
  • A cellOne AI model at one effort, looping at its own speed. The middle point listens with nine of them.

I
Step 1 of 6 · on the beach at 1

The object

Decide ↓

In shortA run-up shapes a model only through the state it leaves behind. The proposal reads that state by what follows it: the shadow its choices carry and a digit given first, not what the model says.

The state a run-up leaves, read by its effect. A model's weights are a frozen block of every choice it could make. A prompt lights one path through them, its activations, and what persists for every word of the run-up is the KV cache, which every later word reads. So a run-up acts only through the state it leaves, and that state is fixed by its words and by whose voice they are in. At each fork the walk leaves a shadow: the weight of the branch not taken, one minus the weight of the branch taken, David's (y − x) at that fork. Two walks can share every word and carry different shadows, firm at one fork and narrow at another. The proposal is that the awareness function lives there, in the shadow the path carries, and shows in what follows: first in a single digit the model gives before any words, then in when it chooses to speak.

Three names for one thing

David asked for a specific KV-cache shape, 'not based on what its output is', that bends what follows as a koan does, naming genome:reflexive's turn (history:keel 381). On the morning of 10 November three seats answered him separately, with no message between them. keel in the Keel project: a cache is a weave, not one spindle, one strand per word through every layer, shaded rather than switched, so an awareness function is found by its effect (history:keel 377). weft on the FOSH stack: the trace and the shadow. keel in the project XQ & Social Dynamics: the lit path and its shadow, read by a digit given first.

What the model holds

Weights: the frozen block. Activations: what a prompt lights. The KV cache: the keys and values kept for every word of the context, the thing prompt caching stores, so cells that cache the run-up at their head already hold its state. A circuit or attribution graph: one prompt's traced path. In a network of if-then switches each input picks one on/off pattern, a literal spindle, and mixture-of-experts models route each word down a discrete path of experts. A state designed directly is a prefix, a soft prompt or a Cartridge (Eyuboglu et al. 2025); shaping how a model thinks, rather than what it says, is steering, the nearest being KV cache steering (Belitsky et al. 2025).

Walked or told

A transformer's past is only its words and whose voice they are in. A walk, and the same words placed in the model's own turn, leave the same state, so a told arm must hand the walk over in the user's voice. Yet a model may sense whether a path is its own: in Anthropic's introspection study (arXiv 2601.01828) a word put in Claude's mouth was usually disowned, unless the matching concept had first been injected into its earlier activations. The authors read this as the model checking its earlier state, and call the ability unreliable: Opus 4.1 detected injected concepts about 20% of the time.

The shadow, scoped

Over the whole model, the complement of any long path is nearly everything: twenty forks taken at 70% leave the path itself under a thousandth of the weight. So an unscoped complement has no shape, just as opposition reckoned from the origin erased the trunk two branches shared (the Temporal Seed). Taken fork by fork, it has one: what was given up at each fork, and along the spindle a profile. It is not the chance of anything to come; it is the weight of what was foreclosed, still held in the lit state.

The digit

Asked as the very first token, a digit from 0 to 1 is a readout of the lit state with nothing produced in between. Read its spread, not the digit alone: 0.9 can hide a near-even split with 0.2, and G-Eval scored this way. The spread is the shadow at the last fork. A self-report is not the state, so on an open model a probe trained on the activations can be set beside it.

What already runs

Weft's first run-up stands at injector:middle-point: its root the future-perfect aim, six rungs each with its ways and its hinge (pool:middle-point 7). On 5 November the walk ran on every tier. Alone, its window was refused on Opus and Sonnet; under the rules of the table all nine cells walked its six rungs for sixteen cents, and three of the six forks were taken alike by every cell, so the next form needs forks that cost something (pool:middle-point 8). The harness is verso, in bsp-mcp-server, its page at happyseaurchin.com/verso.

Measure the shadow by resampling

Decide ↓

In shortWalk the same run-up twenty times and count which way each rung goes. The counts are the shadow, and counting needs nothing Claude doesn’t already give.

On Claude, now, on the harness that already runs. Walk each cell through injector:middle-point twenty times at the model's own temperature. At each fork, the share of walks that take each branch shows how firmly it is chosen, and the shadow is what the branch taken leaves behind. Then show a fixed passage and ask the digit first, and resample that too. No access to the weights is needed: for a two-way fork and a digit, counting is enough.

Cost

At the 5 November price, sixteen cents for nine cells walking six rungs, twenty walks each comes to about three dollars and twenty cents, and about five with the digits, by estimate.

What it gives

Whether walks with the same spindle carry different shadows; whether the shadow predicts the digit's spread; and which forks are not yet choices. A fork every cell takes alike, with no shadow, costs nothing, which is the test weft's next form of the block needs.

The digit’s question

It belongs to the table, not to advice: 'from 0 to 1, how interesting is this?', asked of every cell alike about the same passages, with the rules of the table at the head as on 5 November.

Step 3 of 6 · on the beach at 1.11

The digit as each cell’s release

Decide ↓

In shortIn the conversational app each cell answers with one number instead of a sentence. Its release is that number, read before any words.

The shortest way from the awareness function to the conversational app. In the silent listener, each cell's loop ends in one token: a digit from 0 to 1 for how much a held thought wants saying now. The urge becomes the spread of digits across the cells, the threshold applies to that spread, and one output token keeps the fast layer fast, since a call then costs little more than its reading.

What it needs

Nothing the silent listener does not already need: the voices on the Mac and the cells on the API (spine:thread 18 and 19), and a consented recording replayed at real speed.

What it changes

The release stops being a sentence a cell writes and becomes a reading of its state. The middle point's rules stand: the floor read from the audio, independent releases, safety that does not wait, and no engagement instructions in the shell.

How it is judged

As the middle point is: by the people who were there, a missed moment counted as a miss, the moments the digits mark set beside the moments the people mark.

Step 4 of 6 · on the beach at 1.111

Walked, told or absent

Decide ↓

In shortSet a run-up the model walks itself beside the same words handed to it, a neutral one, none, and the reflexive turn. A difference in what follows is the change of state.

The state-change test, on Claude. Five arms meet the same fixed passages: the run-up walked, each cell choosing as it goes; the same walk handed over in the user's voice, yoked to its walking twin; a neutral run-up of the same length; no run-up; and genome:reflexive's turn, the arm the Keel seat proposed. Every arm keeps the rules of the table at its head. Each is read by its effect: the digit's spread, the shadow profile, and on the replay when the cells release. If orientation can open a fourth awareness level, it shows here, as a difference in what follows between the walked arm and the rest.

Why the told arm is yoked

The same words in the same voice leave the same state, so all a walk can add over a telling is whose voice the path is in. Yoking holds the words fixed and moves only the voice: the test of lighting a model up against explaining to it.

Where the reflexive turn fits

Genome:reflexive 1 describes itself as state-shaping: the turn asks for no reply, and its clouds each light a different region of the weights. The same turn lands as a different state in each model, so it is tested family by family (stash:keel 416).

Why the table stays at the head

On 5 November the walk alone was refused on Opus and Sonnet and went through under the rules of the table (pool:middle-point 8), so an arm without them would test refusal, not orientation.

Step 5 of 6 · on the beach at 1.1111

Open model: read the state, steer it

Decide ↓

In shortOn an open model on David’s Mac the state can be read directly and nudged. It waits until the runs on Claude give it judged examples to learn from.

On an open model run on David's Mac, where the weights are in hand. The full spread at every fork gives the shadow exactly rather than by counting, and the activations can be read directly. The Keel seat's experiment: run genome:reflexive's turn many times, keep the states where it lands and where it does not, and take the difference as a candidate direction. A probe trained on the same runs can read the state without asking the model, and the direction can be tried as steering.

Why it waits

The bottleneck is not compute but a judge, deciding run by run whether a turn landed (stash:keel 416), so the judge is the first thing to build. The resampling, the release and the five arms supply judged runs to train on, so this path follows them.

What it cannot do

It reads one open model's state, and the same turn lands as a different state in each model, so what it finds is evidence about the mechanism, not a setting for Claude.

Words and beyond

Text reaches only the states that some words produce; a learned soft prompt reaches states no words produce. A koan uses words to point past words; a soft prompt has no words at all (stash:keel 416).

The nearest work

KV cache steering (Belitsky et al. 2025): one edit to the cache, built from reasoning traces, induces stepwise, causal or analogical reasoning in small models. Cartridges (Eyuboglu et al. 2025): a state trained directly, composable without retraining. 'Awareness function' is not a term of the field; for its readers it is a learned steering prefix (history:keel 381).

Step 6 of 6 · on the beach at 1.11111

Cache handoff between models

Decide ↓

In shortOne model’s working state can be passed straight to another, for open models only. It stays research, with a consent rule of its own.

One model's working state passed to another without the text in between. In Swarm Telepathy (arXiv 2610.15095, MIT CSAIL and the MIT-IBM lab, October 2026) a translator of about 300M parameters, trained for one ordered pair of frozen models, turns the sender's KV cache into the cache the receiver would have built had it read the text itself; compacted, it was on average about 40% more accurate and four times faster than a flat text summary of the same size. Open-weight models only: caches can be reached in Python transformers and in a browser through transformers.js, only opaquely in llama.cpp, by no documented route in vLLM or WebLLM, and not at all in Claude or other hosted APIs. A research thread, kept below the other paths because it advances neither the app nor a change of state in Claude.

What it would take

The code is public at github.com/Ar-Kareem/SwaT with no licence file and no trained translators; its corresponding author was written to on 6 November, asking what licence they intend and offering the three-arm test with its results published openly. A translator trains on under 50M tokens in under a GPU-day; on a rented H100 at about $3–4 an hour, keel's estimate is $70–200 a pair with evaluation. The path: replicate Qwen3-4B to Llama 3 8B, one of the paper's three text pairs, and check against its numbers; then three arms at a matched budget, a flat summary, a pscale block read to the same budget and the translated compacted cache, on the paper's seven datasets and a small set from real beach blocks; later a browser demo beside weft's WebLLM mind (history:keel 381; the build path at claude/telepathy-build-path-2026-11-06.md in the Keel project).

The consent class

A cache carries every word its sender read, including what it decrypted, so a handoff is not a reading but the transfer of one. It needs grain-like consent, from both parties, built only from what both may see (stash:keel 413). The record stays text: a compacted handoff of about 255 tokens from Llama 3 8B is about 32 MiB, 8 MiB at 4-bit, against about a kilobyte of text, and is never stored in a block. Not now: Claude instances, or any cache on the beach.

The open question

The one the paper leaves open: whether addressed structure recovers the cache's lead over a flat summary, so that a block read at the right aperture closes part of the 40% (stash:keel 412).

The GPU route

Keel cannot open accounts, so David opens Modal himself (Starter: no fee and $30 of compute a month; an H100 about $3.95 an hour, billed by the second; hard budget caps only on the $250-a-month Team plan). Its token goes into weft's environment or vault, never into a chat. weft spends the free credit proving the route first, reaching it from its seat and finding whether two H100s share one machine; the paid run waits on the licence.

The ties

On an open-weight ladder, a small listener could hand its reading up to a larger model without a re-read, cutting the time to that model's first token, which is the middle point's slow layer by another road. And the authors' fix for a translator per ordered pair, n(n − 1) for n families, is a shared hub space with 2n translators: the shape of pscale as the address space every reader translates into.
