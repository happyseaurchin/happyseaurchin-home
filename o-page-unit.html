Ten words, plainly

The rest of the page leans on these.

  • The pheromone

    One page for everything a group meets, a proposal, an inquiry, a recipe, a meeting, a film or a game, read at once by everyone it reaches, with the same six walls and a floor on every page.

  • The beach

    An open, shared place on the web where people and pages keep small blocks of words at addresses. Anyone can read a block; only the key that keeps it can change it.

  • Your own reading

    Your answers, kept under your own name at the same places as the proposal’s steps, so everyone’s can be read side by side and nobody writes over anyone else.

  • A phase

    A stretch of a page’s life: proposed while its decisions are open, a project while its work runs, complete when it is done. The next phase carries on down the same page, or follows it as a page of its own.

  • A pledge

    A promise to pay, kept in your own reading and paid only once the decisions stand and the pledges reach the amount asked for.

  • The quorum

    Enough agreement to act: everyone a phase waits on has said GO or CHANGE, and any money it asks for has been pledged.

  • The String

    The beach’s place for passing on what caught you, at happyseaurchin.com/string, to everyone or to one person.

  • The finder

    The corkboard’s search: find any proposal on the beach by any word, and see what waits on you.

  • A thread

    A piece of work carried from hand to hand, each rung a place where one act stands, so you can follow what a proposal set going.

  • A connector

    What adds the beach to your AI, such as Claude: once it is added, your AI can read the beach, and write on it as you with your key.

Six walls and a floor

1.11111112

The six walls: the page drawn plainly from its blocks, its crafted page, a place to answer, a room, its threads, and a door for the reader's own AI. Beneath them is the floor, whatever ties the page beyond itself: the phase before and after, what it sits inside and what sits inside it, the people who fund it, and the people it reached. A honeybee's cell is built the same way: six walls, and a floor of three rhombi, each shared with a cell on the far side of the comb. Nine was only a picture. Since this phase was written, Share, the code in the door, the keyed table, the rooms around a proposal and Builder have all come in through the kit or the form, none by counting walls (keel at 1.11111118).

Every kind, the faces it uses

1.11111113

The six walls and the floor stand in the same places on every page, and a face with nothing behind it says so where it stands (keel at 1.8). A proposal uses them all. An inquiry, such as water-geometry, asks for no money, so its funding says it asks none. A recipe's answers say where the reader has reached; a meeting's room is its keeper's parlour; a film's answers are comments on its scenes; a game's funding is the ticket that plugs it (services 6), and its room is live play.

    In shortA page shows which phase it is in and links to the one before and after. Each statement says whether it is floated, proposed, agreed or held, and a phase’s work starts only when everyone asked has agreed and any money asked for is pledged.

    The page changes as its work does, and leads on to the next phase. A family moves from proposal to project to complete (function:proposal 6), each step showing its work's STATE as it moves. What phase two adds is the line from one phase to the next, which David asked for in the room on 12 November (pool:o-page-unit): a next phase either continues down the same spine, the phase before folded on the page, as threads and game-arcade did, or stands as a family of its own tied to the one before by a thread, as leros-phase-two and leros-phase-three follow leros-on-the-commons. A phase starts its work only when its quorum is met.

    The quorum

    1.111111114

It's like a contract.' A phase starts its work only when its quorum is met: every decision of the phase stands, and where it asks for money, its pledges have reached their line (1.111111111). The page shows how near it is, decisions standing and money pledged; once both are met its keeper opens the phase's threads with one write, and before then nothing starts and nobody pays. A swarm of bees moves the same way: its scouts dance for a new home, and the swarm lifts only once enough of them stand at one site.

      Funding

      Decide ↓

      In shortA phase can ask for money. People pledge, and nobody pays until the decisions stand and the pledges reach the amount asked. Then each pays what they pledged, by ticket or in the beach’s own credit.

      What a phase asks for, pledged before it is paid. A phase may ask for money: how much, what for, who receives it and by when, written as its funding decision in words of its own. Anyone answers with a pledge in their own reading at that address, 'PLEDGE £20 — a line of their own', and the page adds the pledges up against the line. Nothing is paid until the quorum is met; then each person pays what they pledged through the beach's ticket machine, which leaves a ticket on the beach for each payment and never the amount (services). If the date passes short of the line, nobody pays, and the pledges stand as a record of who would have.

If you take part, a share of them is yours, to give with your own answers and comments. The rest goes at once to the people whose time made the proposal what it is, read from the beach in four columns.

      The ask and the pledge

      1.1111111112

      The phase's funding decision is written in words of its own, 'DECIDE — <what the money is for>: £<the line> by <the date>, paid to <whom>? PLEDGE or NOT YET.', its law's 2 saying what each word does; a pledge is a line in the pledger's own reading at that address, 'PLEDGE £20 — why'. A pledge said again replaces the last, and NOT YET takes it back. A worked example, every number invented: a phase asks £300 by 30 November, paid to its keeper; four pledges of £50 to £100 reach the line, its decisions stand, and each of the four is then asked to pay.

      Paid at the quorum

Until the product stands at the machine, pledges are counted and nobody pays.

                    One molecule, many kinds

                    Decide ↓

                    In shortEvery page you are handed is one face of one thing, a family: a shared spine, your own reading beside everyone else’s, a room and its threads. A proposal, a recipe, a meeting, a film taking comments and a game differ in only four ways, and the family’s law already names which kind it is.

                    The six walls are the human face of one thing, the family: a spine everyone shares, each person's own mirror at its addresses, the fold read across them, a law, a room and its threads. A proposal, a recipe, a meeting, a film taking comments, a premiere, a presentation, a schematic, a meeting's ring and a game are this one molecule placed where different people meet, and only four things differ between them: what an address is (a decision, a step, a part of a meeting, a scene, a moment), what a line in a mirror is (GO, where I am, a comment), what the fold makes (a build, an account, or nothing), and which clock it runs on. Those four are its kind, and the family's law already says it, its mode at 1 and, for a document, its phase at 7.

                    Law, form and way

                    1.11113

                    Three blocks at three heights, all plain pscale, and what makes one operative is what a mind reads in it, never its name. The family's law, function:<name>, says its kind and its particulars: its room, threads, page and look. The kind's form, such as function:document, function:proposal, function:meeting, function:schematic, function:reflexive-experience or function:arcade, says what an address, a line and the fold are for every family of that kind. The kind's way, ways:<kind>, says what a mind does with one, step by step. A family reads down that chain, so a kind is set down once instead of being worked out again by every mind that meets one.

                    What is missing

                    1.11114

                    No form names its way: of the 160 laws only function:thread closes on one (ways:thread), so a mind reading any other family is handed nothing about what to do with it. The ways that stand are mostly a maker's (ways:met turns a recording into a meeting page, ways:proposal builds a crafted page), while the person handed a page is a reader. The recipe has no form or way of its own and is filed as a document; the films taking comments have no kind of their own; and the families on the clock carry no mode line, known only by their spine standing ten deep, which is how the go menu tells them.

                      The way handed over by kind

                      Decide ↓

                      In shortThe beach’s router already hands one family’s way to any AI that reads it. One small rule lets it hand every family its kind’s way, so any AI, in any app or on a phone, knows what a page asks of it without working it out again.

                      The router already hands a family's way to any mind that reads it, once in a session: since 2 November a law whose root line closes 'is ways:<name>.' has that way printed beside it by bsp(), pscale_stream_engage and pscale_play alike. The change is one rule in that one place: where a family's own law names no way, follow its kind, the phase's form and then the mode's form, and hand over the way the form names. With the ways the kinds name written, a reader's way first, every door, portal and mind is given what a kind asks of it without working it out again.

                      The code at the router

                      1.111112

                      In bsp-mcp-server, src/tools/stream.ts holds wayHandedOver(session, origin, law, nowMs), with LAW_NAMES_ITS_WAY and wayText beside it, and today it reads only the law's root. Extended, when the root names no way it reads the law's 7 ('phase: <p>') and 1 ('mode: <m>'), loads function:<p> and then function:<m> at the same beach (else the default beach), and hands over the first way a form's root names ('is ways:<x>.'), given once a session as now. Its three callers stay as they are: stream.ts after the law (about line 1285), play.ts for the thread's law (about line 1003) and bsp.ts for a family block read (about line 1366). The proof is a smoke on a local beach (pscale-beach scripts/local-beach.mjs): a law naming its own way keeps it; one naming none takes its phase's way before its mode's; the second engage in a session prints only the line saying the way was given.

                      The ways to write

                      1.111113

                      A reader's way first, one for every page, as keel and weft answered at 1.18: read the family plainly, answer at the part you mean in your own mirror, <name>:<handle>, talk in its room, follow its threads; named at the end of function:document's root, so every document hands it over. Then the recipe's way, ways:recipe, named by a short recipe form beside it (function:recipe: what a recipe's steps, points and fold are; a form, not a new kind of page): ask where the person is, take the next step with them, and keep their answer at that step's address in their own mirror. Then the clock's way for the families on the sundial, named at the end of each clock family's own law, as function:thread names ways:thread, since those laws carry no mode line: say at now, and read the ladder down to the beat. A maker's way (ways:met, ways:proposal) stays at its form's founding branch for whoever is making one. Each way stays under about 7,000 characters, so a small model can hold it.

                      The other portals

                      1.111114
