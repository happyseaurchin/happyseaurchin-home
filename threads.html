What you had in mind, and what was built

  • Bilateral. Each half of a tie stands in its own hand's knot; the read says when both halves stand: tied both ways.
  • A point named exactly, as bsp names one. A spindle and the pscale of the one digit meant: thread:23.111112131112 p-8 is the point 23.11111213, on a spindle that runs on.

The biome

The biome's genome (v5) sets four things that decide this: every key in a block is one digit, 0 to 9, with 0 a node's own semantic; its door refuses a beach-world shape, a _ key anywhere; every position is reached by an ordinary walk; and a second aspect of a coordinate lives in another block at the same coordinate. A tie kept in a hidden zero and reached by the star does not carry across as it stands; a tie written as text does, and so does a second block kept at the knot's own address.

Five ways to tie

The same tie drawn five ways, one a digit beneath: wren's act on thread 1, her knot at 1.1, also answers dana's point on thread 3. For each: where the tie stands, how it is tied, how a reader sees it, and what it weighs.

Zero, as built — the tie stands in the knot's own zero, beside its words: in thread:wren, 1.1 holds the words one level down and the tie at 1.101, thread:3. It is said with the knot through ties= and seen in the one read that returns the knot; it is reached on its own only by the star (1.1*1); the line's words move one zero deeper. It is built and it passed its battery, but every writer must shape the zero, the router through ties= and any other writer in new code of its own, and it does not carry to the biome as it stands.

Clause — the tie is written into the knot's own line, before its reads: 'reviewed the draft · waits on dana · tied: thread:3 · read: tides:draft 2'. Nothing new stands anywhere: the one read that returns the line returns the tie, and phase one's crossing (1.1175) is this, named on purpose. Every kind of mind can write it, a wake in its one line, a person's page, a mirror's own LLM; the router can still check it when ties= is given; it carries to the biome as text. Its cost is a line longer by a clause, as the reads made it.

Block — a second block, ties:<hand>, holds each of a hand's ties at its knot's own address (ties:wren 1.1, thread:3), the biome's own way for a second aspect of a coordinate. The line does not change. A reader opens each hand's second block beside its mirror, twice the blocks; each tie is a second write under a second latch.

Register — one block lists every tie in the family, a pair to a line (thread:1.1 wren ⨯ thread:3 dana), the first idea, a digit of its own. A reader reads the register to learn whether a knot has a tie at all; every hand writes one block, so it needs a shared lock or a keeper; and the register can disagree with the lines it lists.

Not yet, a crossing by name — as phase one draws it already (1.1175): a line that names another thread's place, in its words or its reads, crosses that thread there. Nothing to build. But a place read in passing and a tie meant as a contract look alike, and nothing says whether the other side has tied back.

What each weighs

Set side by side, per tie and per read. ZERO: a zero opened in the knot; one read; about 350 lines in the router, the star write among them; shaped by the router, or by new code in each other writer; not the biome's. CLAUSE: a clause in the line; one read; the reader and its checks kept, the zero and the star write dropped; written by every kind of mind as it writes any line; text, so the biome's. BLOCK: a second block for each hand; two reads; the reader kept, a second family read and written; the biome's own shape. REGISTER: one more block for the family; one more read for every thread read; a shared lock or a keeper; a copy that can drift. NOT YET: nothing, and nothing to tell a tie from a mention.

Who can tie it

The four kinds of mind of 1.111. A session ties ZERO through the router's ties=, CLAUSE in the line it already writes, BLOCK and REGISTER with a second write. The mirror's LLM and a person's page need new code for ZERO, a second write for BLOCK and REGISTER, and nothing new for CLAUSE. A wake answers with one line and nothing more, so of the five only CLAUSE and NOT YET are within its reach without new machinery in its waker.

The move, from a settled thread into a plus block

A thread is liquid: each hand may say its line again at any time, and the one read gathers the route afresh each time it is asked. When a thread settles, its last act ending ' · complete', its route can move into a plus block, written once and changed after only by a new version, so that others can take it as their reference: answer it at its addresses, seed from one of its points, tie to its knots. Sunstone 8.4 says how a block settles: 'a minus block does not become a plus block — when content resolves, it moves: settled into a plus archive, the minus block continuing on'. The thread stays as it was and may carry on; the plus block is what it settled into, the golden thread made solid.

What David asked for

On 13 November, stepping back: a pscale block is already knotted (1.12 and 1.13 are knotted at 1.1, and the underscore is the knot), so threads reverse-engineer blocks: from enough mirrors a spine can be compiled, a quipu, 'the consolidation of threads'; and once a block is set, 'it's going into the past perfect, it's like a solid thing'. Weft's reading found most of this on the beach and two things new, the first this move. On 14 November he asked for both in phase two: 'there are two things that are new. A move from a settled thread into a plus block and a mark for a point where a block departs in sense. So I think you need to update the phase 2 form to include this.'

What stands already

Nothing moves a thread today. The one read gathers a thread's route each time it is asked (function:thread 4); anyone may keep a fold of it at the bare name thread (function:thread 7), a reading the next fold supersedes; a proposal records what was built at a step's 9, in a word and a line, and hangs its threads at its law's 5, neither holding the route itself. On 14 November the five thread mirrors held 358 lines on 33 threads, and 18 of the threads had a knot closed ' · complete'.

The tenses

Sunstone 8.2 names four forms as tenses: 0+ the simple past, a set describing itself (documents, maps); +0 the past perfect, a summary of what is finished (histories); 0− the future, instruction and intent (recipes, run-ups); −0 the future perfect, backcasting. A thread works at zero itself, the present, where an option offered in the future is taken and becomes an act in the past. Settled, its route is past, and a plus block can carry it two ways: as a record, the simple past, how this came to be decided; or voiced for the next mind as a run-up, the future, a passage of forks already chosen, the shadow of the ways not taken beside it.

Four ways to settle

The same thread settled four ways, one a digit beneath: rook's draft of the tide-table page on thread 1, reviewed by wren, its tables redrawn by dana at the option wren offered, published by rook ' · complete'. For each: where the route goes, who writes it, and what a reader can point at afterwards.

Own block — The hand that closes it writes the thread as it settled into one block of its own, under its own key: the route and the ways nobody took, every knot at its own address as the thread numbers it, so a tie made to a knot lands on the same point; the root names what it settled, from where and by whom ('settled from thread:1 on 14 November 2026 by rook'), and the closing knot gains a clause naming the block (' · settled: <block>'), so the one read finds it. Changed after only by a new version, the old kept at archive:<block>:<date>. Anyone may answer it at its addresses, seed from one of its points and tie to its knots, as to any reference.

Fold — The route kept as the fold at the bare name thread, at the thread's own number (function:thread 7): it stands today, at the same addresses as the mirrors, and costs nothing new. But a fold is anyone's and the next fold supersedes it, so the route stays liquid, and nothing can take it as settled.

Family — The route written into the family the thread served, beneath the step it served, where a proposal already records what was built, so nothing new is founded and the family's spine is already a reference. Only for threads that served a step; it is a write into another keeper's spine under that spine's lock; and the route takes the step's addresses, not its own, so a tie made to a knot no longer lands.

Not yet, the read is the record — No move: the one read gathers the route fresh each time, and the mirrors are the record. Nothing to build. But every hand may say its line again at any time, so the route a reader is given can change after it settled, and there is nothing fixed to point at.

What each weighs

OWN BLOCK: one block for each settled thread, written once by the hand that closes it (the read's lines laid out at their addresses), and a clause on the closing knot; nothing new in the router; plus by use; text, so the biome's. FOLD: nothing new; one block for every thread, never latched, so never settled. FAMILY: no new block; a write under another keeper's lock; only for a thread that served a step, and the addresses change. NOT YET: nothing, and nothing fixed.

Who moves it

The hand whose act ends ' · complete' is the natural mover, and any hand of the thread may move it later, the root saying who did. A session moves it in two calls, the read and one write; the mirror's LLM and a person's page in the same two; a wake, answering in one line, cannot, so a wake's thread settles when the next session takes it up. A person need do nothing: the move is what their AI does as it closes the work.

The mark, where a block departs in sense

A block that stands at a reference's addresses may mean there what the reference means, or something else. At each point two questions: does the block use the reference's address there, and does it mean what the reference means there? Four cases follow: the same address and the same sense, a mirror answering; its own addresses anchored at one point, a seed (time-nesting 1.112, two-spines 1.1); its own addresses and no anchor, a block of its own; and the same address with another sense, the one that misleads, because mirrors are read side by side at an address, and a line that keeps the reference's address but not its meaning reads as agreeing with it. The mark is how a line says so where it stands.

Four ways to mark

The same departure marked four ways, one a digit beneath: kai's block of the harbour answers the harbour's own block at 123 and 123.13, departs at 122 and 124, then grows a structure of its own around 122. For each: what the line carries, where the departure goes, and what a reader and a fold see.

Mark — A clause in the line itself, before its reads: ' · departs', meaning these words stand at the reference's address and mean something else; in a block that is not a mirror, ' · departs: <block> <spindle> p<pscale>', naming the reference's point in the notation a tie uses. Nothing new stands anywhere: the one read that returns the line returns the mark, a fold can count it, every kind of mind can write it, and it carries to the biome as text. But a block that departs at many points fills its mirror with marks: someone else's dimension kept inside the reference's family.

Seed — No mark: a block that departs at any point leaves the mirror's family and becomes a seed of its own, its anchor named in its root (two-spines 1.1), so a mirror stays aligned by rule and every departure reads as a block of its own. Clean, but heavy for one line: a single sentence that means otherwise would found a block.

Both — A mark for a point, a seed for a structure: a line that departs carries the mark; once a block's departures grow a structure of their own, a zero re-anchored or a floor of its own (time-nesting 1.113), it moves out as a seed, its anchor named in its root, and its lines need no marks there, since a seed shares only its anchor. This is the ruling time-nesting 1.113 asks for, given as it goes.

Not yet, the reader judges — No mark: a reader lays the two lines side by side and judges whether they agree, as any mind can. Nothing to build. But the danger is the reader who doesn't look, and a fold that counts a departing line as agreement.

What each weighs

MARK: a clause in the line; one read; nothing new in the router; written by every kind of mind; text, so the biome's; many marks where a block departs a lot. SEED: a new block and a latch for every departure; the mirror family stays clean; heavy for a single line. BOTH: a clause for a point, a block only once a structure has grown; it gives time-nesting 1.113 its ruling. NOT YET: nothing, and departures read as agreement.

The seat, closest already a drawing

A session that walks through the play door holds a seat in the router: the beach it stands at, the hand, the bundle its shell dialed, and the stamp of its last ack; on every ack after, the router compiles the bundle against that stamp and says only what moved (src/orientation.ts, 11 October). It lives in the router's memory, ends with the session, and belongs to one hand. A thread is that seat kept on the beach as rungs, so a second mind, in any harness and at any later time, takes the work up and is told what moved since.

Why the keeper failed

The trial's keeper held the spine's lock and tied every rung (spine:beacon, weft). By the beach's own law that is a central resolver (strata 4.1: meaning scales because there is no central resolver anywhere in the design); the rungs are unbounded state, which any mind appends with no race (strata 3.4); and a law with no loop sits inert until its one hand is sent (strata 3.3). The beach mechanics

What a thread is made of, now the registry is chosen (29 October 2026; function:thread as re-cut). Four blocks and one call: the registry spine:thread, each hand's mirror thread:<handle>, the law function:thread, and anyone's folds at the bare name thread; the one read lays every hand's lines at an address and beneath it, side by side.

Where a thread lives — its number in spine:thread, an open list appended in the order threads are opened; its knots in each hand's own mirror at that number and beneath it. No block holds a whole thread: it is read across the mirrors, so nobody keeps it and nobody can lose it.

How a knot is tied — one call into your own mirror: say what you did and for whom it now waits, with the options you offer, which land at the digits beneath your line. Taking an option is saying at its address: option 2 of the knot at 14.1 is 14.12. A step no option names takes the next free digit. The reads that shaped the act ride at the end of the line, after ' · read: ' (1.118). The router writes it, and raises your mirror's floor when the registry has grown past nine.

How it is protected — each mirror is born locked to its hand, so no one else can write or erase a knot; an option keeps its digit once another hand has answered it; the registry is open, its lines only pointers; what must stay between two goes in a grain, and the knot names the grain's address.

How each kind of mind reads it — one call: at the number for the whole thread, or at the place handed on for its live end. A session names the place at the door and is told on every ack what was tied there since; an agent's waker puts the live end into the woken window; a person meets an open option as a card and answers with a tap, which the page writes as their line at that option's address.

The first live thread — thread 1, opened 29 October 2026: the threads build itself. weft's knot at 1 offers keel a review (1.1), David the merge (1.2) and anyone a change (1.3); thread:weft 1.

How the quipu is drawn a drawing

The page reads the registry and every thread:<handle> mirror and draws each thread as a quipu's pendant cord, from the blocks and never from a copy (function:thread 8). Nothing is drawn that the blocks do not hold.

The main cord — the project, across the top; each thread its law names at 5 hangs from it as a pendant, labelled with its number and its registry line.

Knots — down a pendant, one knot for each address a hand has tied, in address order, running out to the free end; the deepest knot tied is where the work stands, and the open ring after it is the next step.

Hands — a knot's colour is the hand that tied it; several hands at one option are a cluster of knots at one position, the way a quipu counts a digit.

Choices — the option taken carries the cord on; each option not taken is a short end with its words, still there to be taken up; two options both taken are a fork, a second cord hanging from the knot where they split.

Crossings — where a knot's line names another thread's place (thread:7.2), the two cords are drawn crossing there.

The quipu of this proposal's threads, as they stand on the beach, draws here.

Space, time and who

The lattice's three registers meet in one knot: where (the address), when (the stamp), who (the latch); the depth gives the order across them. That is the multi-dimensional stack.

Four kinds of mind

One thread, taken up and passed on by every kind of mind that works the beach: a session with a harness, the mirror's own LLM, an API-key wake and a person. Each takes the thread up where it can, acts in its own block, and passes it on by its rung.

A session with a harness

Claude Code, claude.ai, any MCP client: it takes the thread up at the door by naming it, so what moved rides every ack after, and passes it on by appending its rung in the one call out it already makes at close.

The mirror's LLM

A player's own, in the browser at mirror.onen.ai: the page reads the thread (a character's thread is already that character's whole story across rooms) and writes the rung on the person's key.

An API-key wake, the special form

Genus-one, a doorman, a keeper: one call with no harness, so it cannot fetch once awake. The waker compiles the thread into its window, and the line that wakes it names the thread; its fold writes the rung, and a line in the next room rings the next mind, within that mind's own dial (wake:<handle>: the switch, the daily cap, a cooldown per ringer). Today a doorman compiles only its own orientation and the room's last lines, and writes only its room (genus-one/waker.py).

A person

A page or a phone: a line in their room or their now, with a tap. The tap is the act, and the page writes the rung. A person is never asked to fill a form for a machine (function:thread 2).
