Step one of five 1

The place

Decide ↓

One landscape, laid out once: the common at the centre, four neighbourhoods around it, a gate on the open edge and a garden for each member. Every screen of the app is a view of it.

One landscape, laid out once, that every screen is a view of. At the centre is the common, where everyone sits down together. Around it are four neighbourhoods, standing where the four sections of the national LERO standards stand: culture and values, support offer, volunteers, and running the place. The gate is on the open edge, where visitors come in and members arrive, and the gardens are each member's own place, private. Inside them sit each LERO's real places: for Community Recovery, Salem Hall and the Allotments. Every home a LERO keeps (its constitution, noticeboard, policies, standards) has one place here, and each new thing gets its place before it is built.

Why a place

Today the app is a menu of pages, and each time something matters a link is added to the home screen. A place gives every page a home and lets people move through it knowing where they stand. Rings, positions and 'we are here' are how people already find their way in a community, and the screens can work the same way.

The draft

Written on 14 November 2026 at community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 1.295 (the places), 1.296 (who holds each place) and 1.297 (what the place is for), with 1.298 naming what stays open: each place's room for talk, the boards on the common, and the depth of the landscape.

Natural inclusion

The shape is Alan Rayner's: each person a distinct centre, a garden of their own, included in a shared neighbourhood, with boundaries as places of relation. What is theirs stays theirs; what happens together is ours (leros-on-the-commons 4).

How the landscape is kept has a decision of its own, just below.

Step one, its own decision 1.3

The beach’s own standard

Decide ↓

The landscape can be kept three ways: as a map the app draws, as a world on Community Recovery’s own beach, or as a world tried apart first. This card asks which.

Places are kept the way the beach already keeps them (pscale:world-genome; block-conventions 4.7): spatial:<world> for the places, nested so each address says what holds it; identity:<world> for how each group holds each place; keeper:<world> for what the place is for. Community Recovery's beach is what that standard calls a compact world, one lived-in place where what happens builds up as its life. The lighthouse stays the sign at the front and points into the landscape.

  • A map the app draws

    The landscape kept as the app's own plan: the app draws the common, the four neighbourhoods, the gate and the gardens from what Community Recovery's beach already holds, the lighthouse, which keeps the same homes in the same four parts, and where-and-when, with Matthew's draft (community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 1.295 to 1.297) as the plan it follows. Nothing on the beach is read as a world, every member's passport stays as it is, and the app gets clearer now, which is the aim (community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 1.298). The same addresses can be written to the beach's standard whenever that is chosen.

  • A world on the live beach

    spatial:community-recovery, identity:community-recovery and keeper:community-recovery on Community Recovery's own beach, as 1.11113 says: any AI holding bsp-mcp then reads it as a place, CLERO reads the same addresses across LEROs (1.111), and the earth graft works by the standard (1.1113). Three things are settled first. A person's place in a world is their passport's 3, and moving rewrites it (pscale:world-genome 1.2); in the app, 3 was 'What I could use' until 13 November, and what members wrote there stays. identity says how each group holds each place and locks nothing; the colours and keys keep doing that. keeper is the standard's held register, kept from what a person entering is handed (pscale:world-genome 4.1 and 5), while what 1.297 says is meant for every member.

  • A world tried apart first

    The same three blocks on a beach of their own with made-up members, walked by David, Matthew and weft as a member would walk it, before anything is added to Community Recovery's beach; then MAP or WORLD, with what the walk showed.

Step two of five 1.1

How it works for the people in it

Decide ↓

From the lighthouse, each person sees where they are, what is waiting for them and where the life of the community is, and sees others only where they have acted in the open.

Standing at the lighthouse, a person sees where they are, what is waiting for them, and where the life of the community is: who's going to Tuesday's social, a new note on the noticeboard, a word left for them. Others are seen only where they have acted in the open, on the noticeboard, in who's going, in Our story, in a word left on someone's page; a garden lights only for its owner. The app gets clearer now, and moving through the place itself comes as the app matures.

Now

The first step is drawn: a bar at the foot of every screen, Me, Us, Today, How we run and More, with Today holding only my day and what's waiting for me (community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 1.28; its words at app-words 7.4 and 2.6 on Community Recovery's beach). It is the tap form of the thumb menu (community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 1.233): hold Today and slide your thumb to where you want to go, or just tap. On a phone the thumb could take the menus away altogether, every place one movement from Today, with the bar and a plain list always beside it for anyone who prefers them.

What it must hold

Every gesture has a tap and a plain list beside it; words stay few, big and plain; nothing is counted and nothing is scored; the app speaks in I and we; and it meets WCAG 2.2 AA, as Community Recovery's accessibility policy promises.

The people in it today

Community Recovery's Leadership Team, founders who appointed one another on 16 October 2026, came into the app on 13 November as its first people and co-producers. Nothing in this proposal asks anything of them: it offers a foundation for them to grow, when they are ready.

The run-through

Every screen walked in the order a person meets it, as a member, a leader, a welcomer and a visitor would: what it is for, what I can do there, where I go next, how it looks and reads, and what must stay. Each screen's keep list and changes are written beneath its design, and built a batch at a time to the app's house rule: before and after at phone width, nothing removed unless asked, never plainer or less alive than it was. The meeting notes come first, made readable and agreed in the app, because the Leadership Team is in it now.

What it is for each person

The product this phase serves, described person by person with what each can do now, what comes next and what comes later: someone curious, a member, volunteers, the Leadership Team, supporters, learning and development, the organisation and the network (community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 8.5). A LERO app, Community Recovery's first, to be white-labelled for any LERO once it is firm: the place, the screens and the standards belong to every LERO; the names, the real places, the brand and the words are each LERO's own.

What the lighthouse shows

The life of the place, drawn so people can feel it: the rhythm of the week, with the sessions and the life between them; the paths most walked and the homes most visited; needs meeting offers, the golden flow; the standards growing as the evidence comes in; and the people we meet. Each picture comes from what actually happens, is seen by members only, carries no names, and stays dark until enough people have taken part; a garden lights only for its owner.

Step three of five 1.11

The gift-and-need map

Decide ↓

The first thing the landscape shows: what members say they can bring, at which places and when, seen by members only, so peer leaders see who could meet whom.

Asset-based community development made practical (leros-on-the-commons 1.17), as the first thing the landscape shows: what members say they can bring, at which places and when, so peer leaders see who could meet whom and which gifts sit unused. It is Matthew's golden flow of need, offer, met and changed, pointed at a community's own words. Offers and needs are seen by members only, as phase two's members' circle says, and an offer goes wider only with its owner's yes, one at a time.

What stands

Each member can say what they bring in the app, kept with their name; introductions show gifts only, and what someone could use is asked privately, on paper (community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 1.275). Learning together already gathers what members would like to learn.

The groups in it

Who holds each place, members, volunteers, the Leadership Team, welcomers and visitors, is the register that answers what David named as a gap on the beach: groups, and how people stand in relation to one another and to the place (pscale:world-genome 2.3; the draft at community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 1.296).

Step four of five 1.111

Every LERO

Decide ↓

The same landscape for every LERO, each on its own beach and set where it really is on the earth, so one picture can be read across many and nothing is copied upward.

The same landscape for every LERO, each on a beach of its own: the common, the four neighbourhoods at the standards' four sections, the gate and the gardens, with that LERO's own places inside. Each LERO's place is set where it really is on the earth, so the app's outward map, 'We are here', walks straight into the neighbourhood, and CLERO can read one picture across many LEROs because the same places stand at the same addresses (leros-on-the-commons 1.16). Nothing is copied upward.

The outward map

Community Recovery's app already shows where it sits among the LEROs, from the United Kingdom down to Wellingborough, lit gold with 'We are here', read from the earth beach (earth.beach.happyseaurchin.com). Today that map stops at the town, and the neighbourhood inside it is a list.

The graft

By the beach's standard, a place joins a larger world by its address: a region fits where it stands one step from a named neighbour, and everything beneath inherits everything above (pscale:world-genome 2.4). A LERO's landscape joins the earth the same way, its keeper carrying the placing (pscale:world-genome 5).

Step five of five 1.1111

The build

Decide ↓

Each piece useful on its own: the draft read against the standard, the places, the app reading them, then the gift-and-need map, with every screen walked alongside.

Each piece useful on its own, and each carried as a thread once its decision stands. The run-through of every screen (1.15) runs alongside, starting with the meeting notes. Weft and David read the draft against the beach's standard for places. The places go live on Community Recovery's beach, with the lighthouse pointing into them. The app reads the places: the bar at the foot of every screen first, the view from the lighthouse after. Then the gift-and-need map. Matthew keeps and steers; weft builds in the app and on the beach; David answers for the standard and the earth.

What it costs

Nothing beyond the work. It uses what the beach already has: its standard for places, the earth beach, and the app as it stands. Laid out this way, every possibility stays open, including one day an RPG with other players, without anyone building one now.

What it changes on Community Recovery’s beach

Three blocks are added, spatial:community-recovery, identity:community-recovery and keeper:community-recovery; the lighthouse points into them; and the meeting room comes off, since every place has its own room for talk.
