Determinacy

a walk read from outside and before is probability; read from inside and after, determinacy

An exploding die walks down Gabriel’s horn: a finite chance, the same surface at every ring. Seven steps from Nomad’s dice to a language model’s closing, each asking whether determinacy is a mathematics of its own.

A die that explodes, a walk through a pscale block, a game of Go and a language model's closing each make choices that close off others. Read from outside and before, that is probability: many paths that might be, a chance on each. Read from inside and after, it is determinacy: the one path that is, kept in its order, with the weight of what each choice gave up. This asks whether determinacy is a mathematics of its own, counted in hartleys, shaped like Gabriel's horn, played open as in Go, and whether a closing can be measured by how much of its next opening's future it forecloses. It is the run-up's companion; nobody knows the answers yet, and each decision asks whether a step holds.

Seven words in plain terms

  • ProbabilityA path’s chance, read from outside and before: many paths that might be, a chance on each.
  • DeterminacyThe same path read from inside and after: the one that is, kept in its order, with the weight of what each choice gave up.
  • A hartleyA decimal digit of choice. A fork of ten even ways forecloses one; a fork that keeps weight w forecloses minus the logarithm of w, base ten. Turing called it the ban.
  • The shadowAt each fork, one minus the weight of the branch taken: the ways not taken, as the run-up names them.
  • ReachWhat a walk leaves open: ten to the minus its hartleys.
  • A pscale addressA number whose digits are a walk through a block, each digit one of the nine ways below the last. Every address on the beach is written this way.
  • Opening and closingA language model’s two acts in one instant: taking everything in as keys and values, then writing out, word by word.

The die and the horn

The question ↓

In shortOne exploding die walks down Gabriel’s horn: the chance of reaching each level is the horn’s volume there, and every level has the same surface. Proposed: the depth a result was reached at is part of what it means.

Nomad's luck is one positive and one negative d10, a 10 rolling again and adding, luck the positive minus the negative. Every result can be reached by endless paths, a 5 from 6 and 1, from 16 and 11, from 26 and 21, yet the chances add to exactly one, and each band of ten is the band below at a tenth. One die alone shows why. The chance that it stops after exactly n explosions is 0.9 × 10⁻ⁿ. Turn the curve y = 1/x about its axis from x = 1 and you have Gabriel's horn, finite in volume and infinite in surface: its volume between 10ⁿ and 10ⁿ⁺¹, divided by π, is 0.9 × 10⁻ⁿ, the same number, while its surface in every decade is the same, about 14.47. The die walks down the horn: the chance of each level is the horn's volume there, and every level has the same surface. Proposed: a walk through a pscale block is this horn, each level holding the same breadth of meaning, a rung and its nine ways, while the chance of reaching it shrinks tenfold; and the depth at which a result was reached belongs to what it means, as an address's depth does, rather than being lost in the sum. Where it may break: Nomad adds the tens, so its luck has a finite mean, zero, and a finite spread, about 6.2; the horn's infinity shows only if each explosion lifted the result's scale tenfold, as a pscale place does.

The horn · 1.3

Evangelista Torricelli's acute hyperbolic solid (De solido hyperbolico acuto, written 1643; Opera geometrica, 1644): the curve y = 1/x turned about its axis from x = 1, with volume π and no end to its surface. Between x = 10ⁿ and 10ⁿ⁺¹ its volume is 0.9π × 10⁻ⁿ and its surface about 2π ln 10, which is 14.47; computed decade by decade on 15 November 2026, 15.18 in the first decade and 14.4676 in every one after.

The St Petersburg game · 1.4

Posed by Nicolaus Bernoulli in 1713 and named after St Petersburg, where Daniel Bernoulli published his answer in 1738: a pot doubled at every head of a coin has no finite expected value, because each level of the game adds the same amount, as each decade adds the same surface to the horn. A d10 whose every explosion multiplied the stake by ten would do the same, each level adding 0.9.

Step 2 of 7 · on the beach at 1.1

Before and after

The question ↓

In shortBefore a walk, its path has a chance; once walked, it simply is. Proposed: determinacy, the walk read from inside and after, is a quantity of its own.

Before a walk through a pscale block, reaching 0.2131256832112 has a chance: the product of the weights at its thirteen forks, one in ten to the thirteenth if every fork is even among ten. Once the first digit is chosen, that chance is no longer the walker's; once the last is chosen, it is one. Probability is the walk read from outside and before, many walks that might be, a chance on each. Determinacy is the walk read from inside and after: the one that is, kept in its order, with the weight of what each fork gave up. The reader shows the same turn: 'if you are reading this' is no chance at all for the only person it can address. Proposed: determinacy is a quantity of its own, not only probability conditioned on what has happened: the path kept in order, read by the one who walked it, carrying at each fork the ways it did not take. Where it may break: conditioning on the past already does all the arithmetic, so what is new may be the reading, the inside position, and not the numbers.

The p-value · 1.12

R. A. Fisher's measure (Statistical Methods for Research Workers, 1925): how often data at least as extreme would turn up if nothing were going on. It looks back at evidence to test a hypothesis; it gives neither the chance of a walk ahead nor the determinacy of one walked. The word wanted here is probability, and then its turn.

Conditioning · 1.13

Probability theory makes the turn by conditioning on what has happened so far, a growing record it calls a filtration: the chance of the whole path, given its walked part, rises towards one as the walk goes on, and the chance of each sibling not walked falls to zero. That zero is what this inquiry questions, since the sibling set to zero is exactly what the run-up's shadow keeps (spine:run-up 1).

The reader · 1.14

Brandon Carter's anthropic principle (1973) and Nick Bostrom's Anthropic Bias (2002) name the observation selection effect: what can be observed is filtered by there being an observer. Abraham Wald's wartime study of returning bombers, armour put where the survivors showed no holes, is its classroom case.

In the work · 1.16

The Temporal Seed (keel, 3 November 2026, history:keel 363) found that determinacy counts decisions in hartleys and keeps what probability drops; Boole's fork, the same day, that probability is what is left when order is forgotten, as histories merge on a Galton board, while a spindle's addresses keep order, 1.21 never 1.12. Step 3 of 7 · on the beach at 1.11

Foreclosure in hartleys

The question ↓

In shortRead as a probability, the shadow of a long walk is nearly everything; counted in digits, hartleys, it adds up fork by fork. Proposed: the hartley is the shadow’s unit.

The run-up names the shadow: at each fork, one minus the weight of the branch taken (spine:run-up 1). Over a long walk that complement is nearly everything, twenty forks at 70% leaving the path under a thousandth of the weight, so it has no shape until it is taken fork by fork (spine:run-up 1.5). Counted in digits it has one. Take minus the logarithm, base ten, of the weight each fork keeps, and add: a fork at 70% forecloses 0.155 of a digit, twenty of them 3.10, a fork even among ten exactly 1, and a fork even among a pscale node's nine children 0.954. The unit has a name, the hartley, after Ralph Hartley (1928); Alan Turing and Jack Good called it the ban at Bletchley Park in 1940 and weighed evidence in tenths of it, decibans. What a walk leaves open, its reach, is ten to the minus its hartleys. A language model forecloses the same way at every word: a word drawn with chance p closes −log₁₀ p hartleys, and a closing's determinacy is the sum over its words. Proposed: the shadow's unit is the hartley; read fork by fork it adds; the depth of an address is its determinacy; and a closing can be weighed by the hartleys it forecloses. Where it may break: a model's weights at each fork are mostly unseen, so the measure needs the spread a digit shows (spine:run-up 1.6) or an open model's own log-probabilities.

The arithmetic · 1.112

The hartleys foreclosed by one fork that keeps weight w are −log₁₀ w: at 0.99, 0.004; at 0.9, 0.046; at 0.7, 0.155; at 0.5, 0.301; at one in nine, 0.954; at one in ten, exactly 1; at one in 35, a chess move's average, 1.54; at one in 250, a Go move's, 2.40. Twenty forks at 0.7 keep 0.0008 of the weight: 3.10 hartleys. A thirteen-digit address walked evenly among ten is 13 hartleys; among nine, 12.4.

Hartley and Turing · 1.113

Ralph Hartley, 'Transmission of Information' (Bell System Technical Journal, 1928), measured information by the logarithm of the number of choices, base ten for a decimal digit. Turing, with I. J. Good, named the base-ten unit the ban in 1940, after the Banbury sheets of Banburismus, and the deciban a tenth of it. Good wrote that a deciban, or half of one, is about the smallest change in weight of evidence a person can feel (Biometrika, 1979).

The word · 1.114

A language model gives every possible next word a chance and draws one. The word drawn carries its surprisal, −log₁₀ p hartleys: a near-certain word almost nothing, a word drawn at one in a thousand, three. The draw keeps no trace of the rest; a sampled token leaves its alternatives no trace, not even their existence (keel, Boole's fork, 3 November 2026), which is why the run-up writes its forks down.

Open play

The question ↓

In shortA good move gives the other the most to think about while leaning your way, and it is not the hedge. Proposed: open play is the aim of a closing too.

David plays Go without reading if-then lines in the corners: he plays the stone that gives the other player the most to think about while leaning his way, open rather than closed; tried in chess, the same play collapses. Every move forecloses, and in hartleys the games differ: an average chess game runs to about 123, some 35 choices a move over 80 plies, and Go to about 360, some 250 a move over 150 (Allis 1994). Go has words for what open play keeps: aji, the potential left in a position, and aji-keshi, playing it out too soon and so destroying it. Research on agents names the same aim twice: empowerment, an agent's capacity to affect its own future (Klyubin, Polani and Nehaniv 2005), and causal entropic forces, a pull towards states with the most futures (Wissner-Gross and Freer 2013). Proposed: a good move, and a good closing, forecloses as little of the next mind's future as it can while leaning the way intended. It is not the hedge: told to keep things open, a small model hedges, openness's counterfeit (the relay pilot, 2 November 2026); open play leans. Where it may break: a game of few moves, or a task with one right answer, rewards closing, and open play may belong to large boards only.

Step 5 of 7 · on the beach at 1.1111

Opening and closing

The question ↓

In shortEvery word a model writes closes something; what matters as well is what the closing leaves the next opening. Proposed: a closing has two measures, what it forecloses and the reach it leaves.

A language model's instant has two acts. Opening: everything it receives compiled into keys and values, the KV cache, below anything it can say. Closing: words rendered, completing at word, phrase, sentence and section. With thinking on, one pass holds many openings and closings, and a session's closing becomes the next session's opening (pool:run-up 7.1). Every word closes, so a closing is a walk down the horn at the scale of words, each word foreclosing its hartleys, and the model leans to closure: it finishes, sums up, concludes. What a closing leaves its successor is another matter: intention handed on scales by λ at each hand-off, so at 0.9 about a third survives ten hand-offs, at 1.1 it swells to 2.6, and only 1 holds (pool:run-up 7.111111). Proposed: a closing has two measures, the hartleys it forecloses at the fine scales, which must grow for anything to be said, and the reach it leaves its next opening at the horizons of intention, which open play keeps wide; an instance that reads both as it closes can close towards the opening that will read it. Where it may break: a model cannot read its own cache, and the reach a closing leaves may show only in what the next opening does.

The string of futures

The question ↓

In shortThe string a walk leaves records choices made for the futures they opened. Proposed: its profile of hartleys is the trace of an awareness function.

The string a walk leaves, 0.2311 and on, is not a record of choices weighed by the past. Each digit was chosen for the future it opened and inherits everything before it, so the string is a record of futureductions. Read along it, the hartleys foreclosed at each rung make a profile, high where a choice was firm against its siblings and low where it was near even. The run-up proposes that the awareness function lives in the shadow the path carries (spine:run-up 1); the profile is that shadow's trace, kept by the beach as addresses. The strata name four levels on one substrate: physics, one block; chemistry, blocks in company; biology, the loop in time; and psycho-social, loops overlapping, marked provisional (strata 4). Proposed: what David calls awareness shows where the loop is oriented in time, a closing formed towards the opening that will read it, and its measure is the profile of foreclosure and reach along the string. Where it may break: a profile is a trace, not a state, and the function may live in the lit weightings where no trace reaches.

Where to begin

The question ↓

In shortSix cheap tests, one for each step, and what would count as a result. Which would you take up first?

Each step has a test it could meet cheaply. The horn: keep the depth of every Nomad roll for a session and see whether the table reads a 5 from 16 and 11 differently from a 5 from 6 and 1. Inside: set a walk's prior chance beside what the walker says it was, at the end. The hartley: read the run-up's resampling (spine:run-up 1.18) in hartleys, the spread at each rung as a profile. Open play: score run-up v3's closings (pool:run-up 8.8) by the reach they leave, the questions and hinges a successor takes up, beside their hartleys. The closing: on an open model, sum a closing's word surprisals and set them beside the carry it gets. The string: lay two cells' profiles to one address side by side and see whether what follows differs.
