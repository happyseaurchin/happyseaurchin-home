Silo Buster · phase two · a proposal

The first meetingA way through the AI wave, and the money it releases

X and Y, who already owe and are owed, meet Z for an hour. Z is facing the AI wave, and nobody asks Z to judge our tools: Z tries them on their own work, there and then. If Z gets line of sight through the wave, a way to ride it, Y pays X what Y owed, there in front of Z. A half-day follows for £1, and the returns are counted within ten days. Then Z pays the same way: Z and Y meet P, someone new, and P’s line of sight releases Z’s payment. That is how the network is funded: no central pot, only money moving between people, each payment set off by someone new. Eight decisions shape it.

The example as the proposal writes it

5

Every name and figure invented, to show the shape. Last month Owen, who runs a small consultancy, had a session with David; he would have paid David £100 and, with David's agreement, held it back, writing down the sum. Owen has long known Priya, who runs operations at a sixty-person design firm that pays for AI seats and cannot say what they have bought. So David is X, Owen is Y and Priya is Z. In the hour Priya connects her own AI, starts a shell, and works on the quote that takes her team nine days. Forty minutes in she says she can see how they would ride this, and Owen pays David the £100, there in front of her. That Thursday eight of her people spend a morning on the quote, for £1. Ten days later three quotes have gone out in two days instead of nine, and two are won, worth £38,000. Priya wants to pay. A month on, she and Owen meet Sam, who runs a joinery supplier she works with; when Sam can see a way through, Priya's firm pays Owen and David for the morning and the ten days, by invoice, released there in front of Sam. Later, Sam does the same.

Step 1 of 81

The first meeting

Decide ↓

In shortOne hour. Y owes X and holds the payment back; they meet Z, who faces the AI wave. If Z sees a way through, Y pays X there, in front of Z. Later Z pays the same way, in front of P.

One hour, three people. X and Y meet Z. Y owes X for something of worth X gave before, a session, an introduction, a piece of work, and with X's agreement has held the payment back so that it can be made where a newcomer sees it. Z is that newcomer, facing the AI wave in their work. If in the hour Z gets line of sight through the wave, a way to ride it, Y pays X there and then, in front of Z. Nothing is sold in the hour, and the money moves because something real happened[spine:silo-buster-path 1.11111]. Later Z pays in the same way, in front of someone new. For example: Owen owes David £100 for a session; Owen and David meet Priya, who runs operations at a design firm, and once Priya can see a way through, Owen pays David the £100, there in front of her.

The people

1.2

X gave something of worth and is owed: often someone from the network, David, James, Matthew or another, but anyone Y has had worth from. Y had it, owes for it, and holds the payment back. Z is new: someone facing the AI wave whose seeing carries weight, someone inside a company that pays for AI, a business owner, an expert, someone respected. P is the next new person, whom Z and Y meet later, perhaps someone Z recommends. Each keeps their own letter all the way through: the next meeting is Z, Y and P.

The letters

1.3

X, Y, Z and P, never A, B and C, because Silo Buster uses those for the three states a room can be in[spine:cba 1]: C, where an organisation pays and the work is done for it; B, where people work it out together; and A, where people work across organisations. The first cut named the people A, B and C, and David renamed them on 11 November.

A convention

1.4

Silo Buster is a convention between real people: a practice, and a product, that works in one specific way. Nobody evaluates whether our tools are useful; people try them on their own work. It only works if people actually pay for things, and actually make money doing what they normally do, changed perhaps by AI and by the beach, an open place on the web where each person's words stand at an address any AI can read, which David's network keeps, and its services: the now-catalyst, a page where each person says what they are doing now so the right people meet; meetings arranged; and proposals and threads like this one.

Why two go in

1.5

One person who knows Z cannot come in neutral: as a friend or a colleague they would be taking sides, and as a supplier they would be selling. Two who already trust each other enough to owe and be owed show Z the thing itself: worth given, the payment held, and settled in the open[spine:silo-buster-path 1.1].

Where it sits

1.6

Silo Buster's way in, in the film's words: 'an hour to ask one question, an afternoon to decide in a room, and ten days to learn it with others'[spine:ai-wave 1.111111111111]; in Endosystemics in Practice an hour, a half-day, a day and ten days, held afterwards by a weekly open meeting and one-hour action cycles[spine:endosystemics-in-practice 1.11111111]. This proposal models the first, the hour, and the money that runs through all of them.

Like a transistor

1.7

David's image of 11 November: 'like a transistor, I think, where a person's receptive state releases money flow between two other people'. A small signal at a transistor's base lets a larger current flow between its other two terminals. Z's line of sight is the signal, and the payment between X and Y is the current.

Step 2 of 81.1

What Z faces

Decide ↓

In shortSlop, the bill, the trap, the noise, and what Z knows from their own use. Nobody asks Z to judge our tools: we show what they do against this.

The AI wave, as it reaches Z. Z is not asked whether our tools are any good. What we share is what they do against what Z is facing, which may be all of these: slop, the bill, the trap, the noise, and what Z already knows from their own use.

Slop

1.12

AI producing documents in bulk, and AI reading and summarising them in bulk: more messages and more pages, and fewer decisions[spine:ai-inoculation 1].

The bill

1.13

Paying for AI without much gain in productivity: seats and subscriptions whose use nobody can see, and no change anyone can count.

The trap

1.14

Using AI to make the old ways of working faster, chat between two people, steps in a line, documents, while those ways are being swept away as AI takes on management work across every industry[spine:ai-inoculation 1]. It is framed as how a company survives. It is better framed as what its people can do, and that is where the hour goes.

The noise

1.15

From the labs on the American west coast, who say they do not know how their own models work, and whose best guess for what follows, across industries and the economy, is a universal basic income.

Their own use

1.16

What Z has found using AI at home, and heard from family and friends: often the clearest sense Z has of what it can do.

Step 3 of 81.11

Try it now

Decide ↓

In shortZ tries it there and then: their own AI connected to the beach, a shell that remembers, and one real thing from their own work.

The hour. Not a pitch and not an evaluation: Z tries it, there and then. Z's own AI is connected to the beach in the meeting, and Z starts a shell, a memory for that AI kept on the beach, so it remembers from one conversation to the next. X and Y work with Z on one real thing from Z's own work, and the skills Z's people already have become the way in: AI as a creative tool for getting things done, instead of AI speeding up chat, steps in a line and documents.

Connected in the room

1.112

The beach's open connector, added to Z's own AI in a minute (https://happyseaurchin.com/connect), and a shell started through the beach's invite, so that what Z says stands at an address Z's AI can read again next week.

Made when it is needed

1.113

The old ways pass work from person to person in turn: a chat between two, a chain of steps, a document written, read and summarised. On the beach each person's words stand at one address, every AI reads the same ground, and the thing is made when it is needed, the way an ant colony builds, each acting on what the others left[spine:ai-inoculation 1.1].

The skills they have

1.114

The hour is about the skills Z's people already have: AI's creative tools put in their hands so they get things done, not AI bolted onto systems that are about to be replaced.

The running order

1.115

A minute of paragraph, if Z asks for one; fifteen minutes on what Z is facing; thirty-five with Z's own AI connected, on one real thing from Z's work; ten on what Z now sees and what each does next[spine:silo-busting-meeting 2, 3 and 4].

What Z keeps

1.116

The shell, Z's own, with what the hour put in it, and one plain page of what the hour showed. Both are Z's, whatever happens. Four of the five fresh readers would not put a client's work where anyone can read it, which is the decision here.

Step 4 of 81.111

Line of sight

Decide ↓

In shortZ sees a way through, in Z’s own words, and that releases Y’s payment to X, made in front of Z. If it does not come, the payment waits or is paid by a date: the decision here.

The trigger. Z gets line of sight through the wave: a way to ride it, seen from where Z stands[spine:ai-wave 1.11111111111]. It is Z's to say, in Z's own words, never X's or Y's reading of Z. When Z says it, Y pays X there and then, in front of Z: what Y held back for the worth X gave before. Meetings are paid for, as a company pays for its own; here the money that moves in a meeting moves between the people in it, for worth already given, where the newcomer sees it. The hour itself is paid for later, by Z, with the half-day.

What Z says

1.1112

Often first that it is a mess, all of it, and then the way through[spine:silo-buster-path 1.1111]: 'now I can see how we'd ride this', or the first thing Z will do on Monday. Z is told beforehand that a payment between X and Y may be made in front of them when they see a way through, and is never given words to say.

The payment

1.1113

Y turns to X and pays, phone to phone or in cash, and says why, in as much or as little as Y chooses. In the film David puts £100 in front of James, and Ruth, the film's invented client, asks why. 'Because it landed,' David says. 'You pay nothing today.'[spine:silo-buster-path 1.111112]. Before the hour X and Y write down how much is held and for what.

Why in front of Z

1.1114

The payment shows Z how the network lives instead of telling it: worth given, the payment held, and released by the very thing Z has just felt. Nobody is buying or selling[spine:silo-buster-path 1.11111].

If it does not come

1.1115

Z keeps the shell and the page. X goes on waiting for money already earned, and that wait is the decision here: the payment waits for the next first meeting, to be made in someone else's sight, or it is paid by a date X and Y set in writing beforehand, so that a line of sight decides when and in whose sight it is made, never whether. The same rule holds for Z's payment later.

What fresh readers asked

1.1116

All five stopped here. From Z's seat: 'my words decide whether X gets paid today, and that's pressure'; a friend says yes to be kind. From X's: 'if the line of sight never comes, by what date and at what minimum am I paid?' Every one of them asked for a date in writing for every held payment.

Step 5 of 81.1111

After the hour

Decide ↓

In shortA half-day with a small group, for a token £1, and what Z will pay later written down beforehand. Within ten days the returns are counted, a loss if it is a loss.

The half-day and the ten days. If Z wants to go on, a small group from Z's work spends half a day on one real problem, held by X and Y[spine:endosystemics-in-practice 1.111111111]; the day and the ten days, if wanted, are held by them or others from the network. Z pays a token £1 for the half-day, because Z cannot yet know what it is worth, and what Z will pay once the returns are counted is written down beforehand too. Within a week to ten days the returns are counted plainly, a loss if it is a loss: decisions made, messages no longer needed, AI doing real work, customers won and money in[spine:endosystemics-in-practice 1.11111111111]. From here on X and Y are Z's suppliers, and say so.

The half-day

1.11112

A morning on a real problem, with the people who live with it: they map it, make the proposals the map shows up, and leave with the work already moving[spine:endosystemics-in-practice 1.111111111].

A day

1.11113

Where Z's organisation wants everyone in: one short request to everyone, answered in minutes through each person's own AI, and read for the people who hold the place together, the problems nobody solves and the people already using AI well[spine:endosystemics-in-practice 1.1111111111].

Ten days

1.11114

The people found get time, budget and line of sight to act; a reading on day five and the result on day ten, a loss if it is a loss[spine:endosystemics-in-practice 1.11111111111].

Why a token

1.11115

Z pays only £1 for the half-day because only the returns can say what it was worth. The real payment comes once they have come, and it comes in the next meeting, with someone new.

For an organisation

1.11116

Where Z works inside one, what it pays is the organisation's: every figure and date agreed in writing before the half-day, invoiced by a named business, X's or Y's, and paid on its own terms through its own buying, the £1 included. In front of P, Z releases what the organisation agreed to pay. Nobody inside it is paid anything personally, and no gift forward reaches anyone in it.

In figures, beforehand

1.11117

Fresh readers in every seat asked the same: 'what will my client or their firm pay, to whom and when?' (Y), and 'what is the most this can cost the firm, paid to which legal entity and by what date?' (a finance director). So the plain page Z reads before the hour carries every figure and date, and the two decisions here and at the next step say whether it is a price or a ceiling.

Step 6 of 81.11111

The next meeting

Decide ↓

In shortOnce the returns are counted, Z pays, in a follow-up meeting with someone new: Z and Y meet P, and P’s line of sight releases Z’s payment. Then P does the same.

How Z pays. Once Z's returns have come, Z wants to pay, and does so only in a follow-up starter meeting with someone new: Z and Y meet P, perhaps someone Z recommends. When P gets line of sight, Z pays the ones who held Z's hour and half-day what was written down beforehand, there in front of P. Then it repeats: P, in turn, pays in front of someone new. Every payment is made in a newcomer's sight, and set off by that newcomer's line of sight.

The seats move

1.111112

Each keeps their own letter. The first meeting is X, Y and Z; the next is Y, Z and P; the one after is Z, P and whoever P brings. The one who was met becomes one of the two who go in, and the one who owed has paid.

Why not pay straight away

1.111113

Z could pay at once. Held back, Z's payment becomes the thing the next newcomer sees: money moving because worth was given, released by their own line of sight. And Z bringing P is how the practice reaches the next person, by personal recommendation between people who trust each other.

Who P is

1.111114

Anyone Z knows who faces the wave: a colleague in another department, a friend in another firm, a supplier or a customer. Z and Y go together, because two who already owe and are owed show P the thing itself.

How much

1.111115

What Z pays is the decision here: a figure Z decides from what the returns were worth to them, a figure or a ceiling agreed in writing in the first hour, or a share of what the ten days counted. A finance director among the fresh readers would pay a priced invoice, never a share of revenue nobody can trace.

Not a referral scheme

1.111116

Fresh readers' strongest worry: that Z can only settle by bringing someone new, which reads like a referral chain. What keeps it from one: nobody earns from the people who come after them; each payment is for work actually done, between the ones who did it and the one who had it; and, if the date rule at line of sight stands, Z's payment is due by its date whether or not anyone new is met, the meeting with P deciding only in whose sight it is made. Bringing P is an invitation Z makes because the hour worked.

Step 7 of 81.111111

Funding the network

Decide ↓

In shortNo central pot: money moving between the people doing it, each payment set off by someone newly engaged, and events too. Like a subscription, except it only pays once it has worked.

The plain version. There is no central pot. The network is the people doing this, and it is funded by money moving between them: Y's payment to X in every first meeting; the £1 for the half-day, and Z's payment once the returns come, in the next meeting; what people earn as they go on, in action cycles, workshops, the ten days, the products and services they make and the problems they solve; and events, which pay the network too. It works like a subscription, except that nobody pays every month whatever happens: each payment is set off by someone newly engaged getting line of sight. That is the part that is new, and the part people find hardest to take in. So the people doing it earn from what Z pays once the returns are counted, for the half-day, the day and the ten days, and from the next steps an organisation buys; there is no fund behind them.

Why meetings are paid

1.1111112

A company pays for its meetings, the rooms, the time, the people in them. Here a meeting is paid for too, between the people in it, and the payment shows the newcomer how the network lives.

Like a subscription

1.1111113

A member pays a subscription every month, used or not. Here the money comes when it has worked for someone new: no line of sight, no payment, so the network earns only as fast as it helps people ride the wave.

It only works if

1.1111114

People actually pay for things, and actually make money doing what they normally do, with AI and the beach's services helping. If nobody's returns come, nothing moves. That is the honest test.

How it is kept

1.1111115

Each payment is one entry in each person's own moneybox, their monthly account of money in and out on the beach, the same pound read from both sides and saying in whose sight it moved, sealed to the people in it unless they choose to open it[function:moneybox 2]. Laid side by side these entries are the map of the network: who trusts whom enough to owe, to pay and to recommend[function:moneybox 6].

Other ways

1.1111116

The money may also go from X to Y, where the debt runs the other way; or to Z, the gift forward, which in David's words is investment: given because of what Z saw, buying nothing, for Z to carry on, never to someone inside an organisation that may buy from X or Y. Both stay variations for after the first three.

What grows

1.1111117

An ecosystem of trust between people and personal, organic recommendation, supported by the grain network, the private two-way bonds between people's AIs on the beach.
