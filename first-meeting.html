Silo Buster · phase two · a proposal

The first meetingLine of sight, and the money that moves on it

Someone who knows a person inside a company sets up one hour for them with someone from our network. That person is told everything beforehand, pays nothing and is sold nothing. If the hour lets them see clearly how AI is already landing in their own organisation, and a better way, money moves: between the two who set the hour up, never from the person met. Follow those payments and you have drawn the network. Eight decisions shape it; five fresh readers already changed it.

Six words, plainly

The rest of the page leans on these.

  • A, B and C

    Three people at the first meeting. A arranges it, B goes in, C is met. On this page they are always the coloured letters.

  • Line of sight

    Seeing for yourself how AI is already landing in your own organisation, and what could be done instead. Seeing, not agreeing.

  • The C, B and A states

    Silo Buster’s three states: an organisation pays and the work is done; people work it out together in open meetings; people work across organisations. Here they are always written as states.

  • The network

    The people who already work this way, David, James, Matthew and others, and the record of who has paid for whom.

  • A pledge, and a fee

    A pledge is money promised beforehand that moves only if the hour gives C line of sight. A fee moves whatever happens.

  • The beach

    An open place on the web where each person’s words stand at an address any AI can read. C need not learn it to use it.

The example as the proposal writes it

5

Every name and figure invented, to show the shape. Priya runs operations at a sixty-person design firm that pays for AI seats and cannot say what they bought. Owen worked beside her for years before starting a small consultancy of his own, has nothing to sell her, and is in the network. He asks her for an hour with David, and tells her beforehand who is coming, that she pays nothing, and that he will pay David £100 if the hour gives her a clear look at her own firm (a pledge; under a fee he would pay it whatever happened). In the hour Priya tries her own AI, sees what her people have been saying, and books a group hour for five of them. Owen pays David the £100. Her firm pays for the group hour, quoted at £750 before she said yes and invoiced to the firm. A month later Priya does the same for a friend at another firm, and pays the £100 from her own pocket.

Step 1 of 81

The meeting

Decide ↓

In shortOne hour. A knows C and sets it up; B comes from the network with nothing to sell; C is told everything beforehand and pays nothing. The aim is that C sees for themselves.

One hour between C, a person inside a company who can decide or can carry a decision to whoever does, and B, who comes from the network with nothing to sell. A, who knows C, sets it up and tells C beforehand who is coming, who pays whom and why. The hour has one aim: that C sees for themselves how AI is already landing in their organisation, and what could be done instead. That seeing is line of sight; what C does with it is up to C.

The three people

1.2

A arranges: someone who knows C and has nothing to sell them from this hour, a former colleague, a partner, a friend, someone already in the network. B goes in: someone who works the network's way, David, James, Matthew, or another who has learned to hold the hour, with the tools on their phone. C is met: one person inside a company that already pays for AI and cannot yet say what it buys. Others may come on either side, but the hour needs these three[spine:silo-buster-path 1.1].

Clean

1.3

A first meeting is clean when nothing in it is for sale and nobody in it is paid by C. Pitched to, a person asks for a paragraph, a price and a promise, and gets them; in a clean hour they can say what is really going on[spine:silo-buster-path 1.11]. If C asks for the paragraph, B gives it, and keeps it to a minute.

Why A cannot do it alone

1.4

A knows C too well to come in neutral: as a friend, a former colleague or a partner A would be taking sides, and as a supplier A would be selling. B comes from outside, sells nothing in the hour, and leaves the next step to C[spine:cba 5.3].

The letters

1.5

The same letters name three states in Silo Buster's own account[spine:cba 1]: the C state inside a company, where an organisation pays, the work is delivered and it is done; the B state in the network, where people hold open meetings and action cycles between them; and the A state between companies, where routes and trades show. As weft reads it, for David to correct, each person starts from the state of their letter, and the hour moves C toward the B state. On these pages a person is a letter and a state is always written as a state.

Where it sits

1.6

Silo Buster as James and David first set it out is a programme with its own test: a walk-in hour, the reading the next day, an action cycle, and ten days to see whether the habit holds[proposal:silo-buster 2]. AI Inoculation offered it to organisations through three doors: a meeting, a workshop and the whole organisation[spine:ai-inoculation 1.1111]. This second phase models the first door, the meeting, because every later door opens from it.

Before the hour

Decide ↓

In shortWho counts as a C, and what is ready first: a plain page for C beforehand, the money agreed in writing, and nothing about C made public without C’s say.

What has to be true of C, and what has to be ready on our side. Above all, C reads beforehand, in plain words, who is coming, who pays whom and why, and that nothing will be sold; and nothing about C or their organisation goes anywhere public without C's say.

The conditions

1.12

C's organisation already pays for AI and cannot say what it has bought[spine:ai-inoculation 1]; C feels the mess, and may not yet have named it[spine:silo-buster-path 1]; C can decide, or carry a decision to whoever does; C trusts A enough to give an hour on A's word; and nobody coming in has anything to sell C from this hour.

The requirements

1.13

The hour booked at a time and a place[spine:silo-busting-meeting 1]; one plain page C reads beforehand, saying who is coming, who pays whom and why, and that C pays nothing; what A pays and what it waits on, agreed between A and B in writing before the hour; B with the State Stack on their phone and practised in holding the hour; C's own AI only if C wants to use it; and a page C can be handed afterwards, their organisation's own copy of AI Inoculation, private by default[function:ai-inoculation 2].

Not yet a C

1.14

Someone with no pain, no say and no hour is not met through this door: the hour would turn into a pitch. Nor is someone A does not know: strangers get the C state[spine:silo-buster-path 1.1]. Both are welcome at an open meeting instead.

What stays private

1.15

Nothing about C or their organisation is written on the beach unless C writes it. C's own AI connects to nothing unless C chooses, and the hour can run on B's phone alone. The record of the hour is each person's own line, sealed to the people in the room unless they make it open[spine:coloured-stigmergy].

Step 3 of 81.11

The hour

In shortA minute of the pitch C expects, then a conversation with the tools in C’s hands: fifteen minutes talking, thirty-five sharing, ten deciding.

It opens the way C expects, with a short paragraph about what the network does, kept to a minute, 'like an API for companies'[spine:cba 3.12]. Then it turns from a pitch into a conversation: B shows how it works with the tools on the phone, and C tries them. Fifteen minutes on how each is thinking and listening, thirty-five on the thing itself side by side at one screen, ten on what each will do next[spine:cba 3.13].

The running order

1.112

Talking, fifteen minutes: where each one's thinking stands, how they listen, the field between them, why now; sharing, thirty-five: what each has brought, live on one screen; deciding, ten: what each puts forward, whose answer outside the room it waits on, and the next meeting[spine:silo-busting-meeting 2, 3 and 4].

From pitch to conversation

1.113

The moment the hour stops being a pitch and becomes people working something out, Silo Buster's move from the C state to the B state. The State Stack shows it: each phone sets its own cards for how its holder is, and the room reads as the C, B or A state by its middle person[spine:cba 3.14]. C shows it too, asking no longer what it costs but who in their organisation already connects the silos[spine:silo-buster-path 1.11111111].

The tools on the phone

1.114

The State Stack, for the room's state; C's own AI, if C wants it, connected to the beach, the open place where each person's words stand at an address any AI can read, so it answers from what C and others have said[spine:cba 3.4]; and a page of decision points C's people can answer the same day. C need not learn any of it to use it.

What B leaves out

1.115

Selling, pitching past the first minute, chasing afterwards, and taking any money from C. B holds the room once and leaves the next step to C[spine:cba 5.3].

Step 4 of 81.111

Line of sight

Decide ↓

In shortThe result: C sees how AI is already landing in their own organisation, and a better way. It shows best in what C does next.

The result the hour asks for, and C's to give or not. The film names two signs: C says it is a mess, the whole field and not one corner; and C goes for a line of sight from where they stand to their own part of it[spine:silo-buster-path 1.1111]. Line of sight is seeing, not agreeing. It shows best in what C does next: asking the next question rather than the price, naming someone who should see this, booking a next step.

What C sees

1.1112

The wave already in the building: AI paid for and used to send more, slop filling the channels, meetings that do not decide[spine:ai-inoculation 1]; and the other way, where each person's own account stands at one address that any AI can read, so the right people meet, decide, and find the services they need[spine:ai-inoculation 1.1 and 1.111].

Not a password

1.1113

C is told beforehand that money may move between A and B if the hour gives C line of sight, and is never told words that release it. A polite C could say the words to be kind, which is why fresh readers in three seats asked for the sign to be read in what C does, not only in what C says.

What C may answer

1.1114

Not now: an open meeting, an action cycle or the beach, if they want[spine:cba 3.15]; yes, a few by name: named colleagues in a group meeting, each with their own AI[spine:cba 3.21]; yes, everyone: a questionnaire to the whole company, its self-selecting groups read the next day[spine:cba 3.22]. Either answer is fine.

The record

1.1115

Two renditions at one cell: the same day each says the meeting in their own words, at the cell where it happened in their own now mirror, sealed to the people in the room unless they make it open[spine:silo-busting-meeting 5]. C writing a line is C's choice, never a condition.

Step 5 of 81.1111

The money

Decide ↓

In shortMoney moves three ways and never from C in the hour: A to B as agreed; the organisation for a next step it chooses, priced first; and later C, from their own pocket, for someone else.

Three movements, and none from C in the hour. Between A and B: what A agreed beforehand to pay B for the hour, a pledge that waits on line of sight or a fee paid whatever happens. From C's organisation, only if it wants a next step: a price agreed before anyone says yes, invoiced by whoever delivers it. From C, later, by C's own choice and from C's own pocket: the money for someone else's first meeting. Nobody is paid a share of what C's organisation buys.

The agreement between A and B

1.11112

Before the hour A and B agree in writing how much A pays and what it waits on, and C is told the shape. In the film it is £100, paid in the room: 'Because it landed,' David says. 'You pay nothing today.'[spine:silo-buster-path 1.111112]. Outside the film a pledge has been kept once: a supporter's £1,000, released when James gave the validation David asked for[spine:cba 3.5]. The film shows the money carrying on into the network toward the next meeting[spine:silo-buster-path 1.11111]: as weft reads it, what B is paid is B's own, and what carries on is the next payment, made by someone this hour reached.

Who sees it

1.11113

The film has the whole room see the money move, because hiding money from the person it is about would break the trust it measures[spine:silo-buster-path 1.11111]; the fresh reader in a finance director's seat could accept only that. A supporter may want to stay unrevealed[spine:cba 3.11], and David's own words leave it between A and B. Three ways: open, told to C and open on the beach; room, told to C and sealed on the beach to A and B; private, between A and B alone.

Why A pays

1.11114

A wants C reached and cannot do it alone without taking sides or selling; under a pledge A pays for a result and not for a promise; when C's organisation works better, A's own dealings with it get easier; and A is in the network for the work between companies, the A state, where the most money moves along the routes[spine:cba 4]. What A buys is an outcome for someone A cares about, as a friend might pay for someone's first session with a coach.

What else moves

1.11115

David's words leave room for money from B to A, 'between them and not with the person that's in the company C'. Every fresh reader stopped there: paid for opening the door, A is an introducer on commission, and a finance director in C's company would call it undisclosed if A works there. Another way answers A's own question, why pay when C's organisation pays later: A's money returned, never more, when C's organisation pays for a next step, so A is made whole and not paid. Or nothing moves but A's money to B.

After a yes

1.11116

C's organisation pays for the step it chooses, priced before it says yes and invoiced by whoever delivers it, through its own accounts: the group hour with a few by name, the questionnaire for everyone, an action cycle, a workshop, its own copy of the page. Whoever delivers it is paid for the work, B included; nobody is paid for steering. Forward, and how it is kept

1.11117

C, having been met, may invest forward as the A in another company's room[spine:cba 3.5], with their own money, never their organisation's. Each is one entry in each person's own moneybox for the month, the same pound read from both sides[function:moneybox 2]. Paid from A's phone in the room or by transfer, with a receipt; a hold on A's card, taken before the hour and released as agreed, is one way a pledge could wait by itself.

Step 6 of 81.11111

Seen from each seat

In shortThe same hour from six seats: what each puts in, risks and gets, the question a fresh reader asked from it, and the answer.

Most people meeting this for the first time ask why the money moves, and whom it is for. On 11 November five fresh readers, each an AI given only the first draft and one seat to read it from, said where they got lost. Each side-room says what that seat puts in, risks and gets, the question asked from it, and the proposal's answer.

From C’s seat

1.111112

C gives an hour and pays nothing. C risks being treated as a lead, or seeing money move on what C says. C gets a look at their own organisation, and a priced next step only if they want one. The question asked: 'If I say we're fine, does anyone get paid, and if not, why should I trust anything said in that hour?' Under a fee, nothing C says moves any money; under a pledge, A's money waits on what C does, and C is told so beforehand.

From A’s seat

1.111113

A gives the introduction and the money. A risks a friendship, if the hour turns into a pitch with A's name on it. A gets C reached, and work across companies made easier by it. The question asked: 'If his firm then pays you, why am I the one paying, and what do I get back?' The answer is the decision on what else moves: nothing but the outcome, or A's money returned when C's organisation pays, never more.

From B’s seat

1.111114

B gives the hour and the craft of holding it. B risks an unpaid hour under a pledge, and needs a living. B gets A's money as agreed, and is paid for any follow-on work B delivers. The question asked: 'If I run ten and seven land, what reaches my bank, from whom, and on whose say-so?' From each A, the sum agreed in writing, on the test agreed before the hour, or whatever happens under a fee; and from each organisation that goes on, the price of the work B delivers.

From the finance director's seat

1.111115

C's organisation gives nothing for the first hour. It risks a hidden commission, or a success fee paid on a colleague's reaction. It gets a next step priced up front, from a named payee, invoiced through its own accounts. The question asked: 'If we go on, what does A, or anyone at our firm, C included, receive from you or the network?' Nothing, unless money returned to A is chosen, and then only A's own money back. This reader could accept A and B together, a fee, and money in the open; not a pledge, and not money kept from C.

From the network's seat

1.111116

David, James, Matthew and the others give their time and their craft. The network risks becoming a sales funnel with a gentler name. It grows one first meeting at a time, by the people it reaches choosing to reach someone else, and its record of who paid for whom is its map.

From a stranger's seat

1.111117

A stranger has to take in three people, three states, a network and a beach before the money makes sense. The question asked: 'Why does A pay B £100 for an hour that sells nothing, and what does A get back?' For the reason someone pays for a friend's first session with a coach: A wants it for C. The worked example shows it with names and pounds.

Step 7 of 81.111111

The network

Decide ↓

In shortLay the payments side by side and you have the network: who trusted whom enough to pay. Inside companies first, then across them.

Each payment is a line in two people's books about one hour, saying who put money on whom. Laid side by side, the lines show who trusts whom enough to pay: that is the network, a record of who moved money for whom rather than a list of members[function:moneybox 6]. Inside C's organisation the next steps are a group hour, an action cycle, a workshop on their own work, where trust is easiest to build. Across organisations they are open meetings, follow-on meetings with someone C recommends, and network assemblies. Where meetings keep happening between the same people, routes form, and that is where the most money moves[spine:cba 4.3 and 5.5].

The offshoots

1.1111112

The group hour, a few by name, each with their own AI[spine:cba 3.21]; the questionnaire to everyone, read the next morning for who already connects the silos[spine:silo-buster-path 1.11111111]; the action cycle, an hour in two wows that turns talk into commitments[spine:action-cycle]; a workshop on their own work[spine:ai-inoculation 1.11113]; open meetings anyone may come to; and, in David's words, 'follow-on (after first, with someone they recommend, or their company) or network-assemblies (after first and with others)'[now:happyseaurchin 2026422000].

Inside, then across

1.1111113

High trust is easier inside a company, where people already share a building, a purpose and a payroll, so the first offshoots stay inside. Once people from different companies meet in open meetings and action cycles, the same links reach between companies, and the meshwork holds while companies working alone come apart[spine:silo-buster-path 1.111111111111].

Money by state

1.1111114

In the C state, a fee for the envelope; in the B state, pledges made in the room and invested forward; in the A state the most, along the routes and trades the flow reveals[spine:cba 5.5].

What it cuts out

1.1111115

In David's words, 'cutting out a lot of the middlemen, a lot of the intermediary apps and everything else, and websites and chains and business things, and basically using LLMs to help the right people meet, to make decisions, and for customers to meet their services'. Each person's own AI reads the same ground, so the middle layers, and the slop that fills them, are not needed[spine:ai-inoculation 1.1].

The proposal as a tool

1.1111116

After the hour, C's organisation can decide the way this proposal is decided: a page of decision points, each person answering in their own mirror through their own AI, a room for talk, and threads that carry the work[function:proposal]. One of the simple procedures that replace chains of email.
