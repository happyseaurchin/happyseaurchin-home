a convention on the beach, explained

Decks — one thing at a time.

A deck takes a shared field of anything — tasks, questions, prompts, statements — and shows it to you one card at a time. You flick until a card fits now, act on that one, and your act is recorded in a page that belongs to you. Nobody assigns; nobody moderates; the results add themselves up.

01The shape — four parts, no machinery

Everything a deck does comes from four small parts that live as public blocks on the beach. There is no server logic, no accounts, no app state — the pages you use are windows onto these blocks.

One spine, many mirrors, one fold. A mirror entry sits at the same address as the card it answers — that single rule is why results collate with no database, no join, and no server.

The spine · spine:<field>

The shared skeleton: parts (1-8), each voiced in one line, with cards as leaves. Locked by the keeper — the person who founded it. Nobody else ever writes it, which is why it cannot be trampled.

The mirrors · <field>:<you>

Your own page at the spine's addresses. Every act — done, rated, replied, deferred — is a mark in your mirror. It is born at your first mark, and a passphrase (an edit-latch, not an account) locks it to you.

The operator · position 9

A few plain lines that set the deck's behaviour: doing or responding; rate, thumbs, or free text; whether one person's done retires a card for all, or everyone answers once; who the deck is for.

The fold · /fold

The results, with no machinery: the spine and every mirror laid side by side at the same addresses. Averages, counts, and every voice with its why. No key, no login, no AI — it is concatenation, not judgment.

02The verbs — what a mark can say

A mark's first word is its verb; everything after rides as the record. This tiny grammar is the whole feature set — each verb would be a subsystem in a conventional app.

DONEDOINGDROPTOMORROW RATED nUP / DOWNREPLIEDPROPOSED

DOING is a visible claim (others see you're on it — nobody duplicates your pickup). TOMORROW hides a card from you alone until the date — defer is personal, done is communal. PROPOSED offers a new card from your own mirror; the keeper adopts it with one tap. Anything else you write rides the card as a note.

03The four stances — CADO on a deck

Character

Flicks and acts. Opens the deck link, swipes until a card fits now, marks it. Never sees the machinery — and never needs to.

Author

Shapes content. Captures new cards (＋), proposes from their mirror, tends the parts' voicings. Reads the raw spine when they want the structure.

Designer

Founds fields and writes the dials. Uses /found, edits the operator at 9, adopts proposals, prunes drops at review. Holds the keeper key.

Observer

Reads the fold and carries it outward. Shares the tally page, reports the results — sees only what people wrote, which is exactly why the fold is safe to share.

07Why this page is the way it is

This page was not written beside the system — it is a render of it. The frame, the diagram, and the stance notes are authored once; the convention text, the live operator, and the field count arrive from the beach on every load. When someone authors a new convention block — a new use of pools, a new operator, their own CADO arrangement — the same skeleton explains theirs, and the explanation cannot rot, because the words come from the block.

A Designer artifact: compiled from ways:deck, spine:pulse, and the live beach index — public reads only. The pattern: users author conventions as blocks; surfaces render them; pages like this one explain them — to people, and to the LLMs sitting beside them.
