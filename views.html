the page system, in its own words

Pages become blocks.

A page here is never installed: it is a block, written under its author's own latch, rendered live, catalogued as a card, and judged from mirrors — so pages multiply freely and order emerges from feedback, not permission.

01Three organs, deliberately apart

Code, content, and judgment are held by different hands on purpose: nobody owns the whole, and each moves at its own pace. The renderers hold no words of their own — every sentence a reader sees arrives from a block at load, which is why an explanation here cannot rot.

One line of authority never crosses another: the repo renders, the author owns the page, the keeper tends the catalogue, each reader owns their judgment, the steward only points. That separation is why a hundred people can publish without a moderator appearing anywhere.

The renderers · the repo

Empty frames — /view, /tree, /explain and the deck lenses. They fetch blocks on every load and put no words of their own on screen. Change a block, and every page showing it already says so.

The views · view:<name>

A page authored as a block: voicing at the underscore, one lens per line — walk, fold, voices, value, explain, liquid, form — the register at 9, your latch on the whole. No code, no deploy, no permission.

The catalogue · spine:views

One card per published view, naming its maintainer. Rivals sit side by side; nothing is ever delisted for having a competitor. The keeper adopts proposed cards — the shared skeleton is never written to publish.

The mirrors · views:<you>

Your judgment lives on your own page, under your own latch: replies are feedback, proposals are offers. Nobody edits anyone else's anything — and silence is honest absence, not a zero score.

02Publishing — four small acts, no permission

  1. Author — write view:<name> under a latch you invent: through the maker on /view, or one write from any LLM with the bsp connector.

  2. Propose — offer its card from your own mirror (a PROPOSED mark); the keeper adopts it into the catalogue.

  3. Stand — your page sits beside its rivals, your name on the card. Feedback at the cards and the lighthouse pointer decide prominence, never exclusivity.

  4. Tend — replies arrive in the fold; re-author your block and the page follows. Or don't: an untended view stands honestly until its author returns.
