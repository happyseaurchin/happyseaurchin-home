Power-with

every sentence carries a claim about who stands where, the speaker above, beside or below the one who hears, and whom it makes the subject of that claim. An inquiry, not a proposal: the grid of power David set out in Fulcrum, its crude and its subtle forms, and how it reads between people, from one instance of a model to the next, and among many at once.

Three relations, two acts each.
how to read it

A claim in every sentence

This sets out a frame David Pinto wrote in Fulcrum: besides what it says, every sentence claims where its speaker stands, above, beside or below the one who hears. It follows that claim from one sentence to a pair, to the subtle form where the other is the subject, and on to models and to people.

Each step ends with a question. Tap a word, add a line, and it is said into your own mirror on the beach. Every drop-down takes a comment of its own.

Grammar4.1

languages mark the claim in their moods: the indicative states, the imperative commands, the subjunctive's 'let us' exhorts and its 'might' allows. Person says which term of the relation a sentence holds up: the first the speaker, the second the hearer, the third the thing between them. Latin's vocative, the case of the one called, stands outside the sentence's grammar and exists only in the present of the call. Varro already split Latin's verbs by the finished and the unfinished, perfectum and infectum, the split the sunstone makes between a settled block and a live one.

    Logic4.2

    in 1908 Russell's theory of types sorted statements by what they are about, things or statements about things, so that no statement could speak of itself in the same breath. It sorted by aboutness, not by the relation between speaker and hearer. Frege set commands and requests outside logic, since there is no truth in them to judge, and the logic that became the mathematics of computing kept one mood, the indicative.

    Comment on this
      Signs4.3

      in December 1908, the year of Russell's types, Peirce wrote to Lady Welby sorting a sign by how it acts on whoever reads it: suggestive, imperative or indicative, presented, urged or submitted, and set the three in the possible, the existent and the necessary. He never finished the classification. Bühler (1934) named the point every 'I', 'here' and 'now' is reckoned from, the origo, the zero of a speaker's field.

      Comment on this
        Encounter4.4

        Buber (1923) set two ways of meeting apart: I–It, where the other is a thing described, and I–You, where the other is met and addressed. The third person of plain information is the first; the address of 'you' is the second. Levinas held that the other is met from a height, above me: the subtle form, 'you are more than me', taken as the start of ethics rather than as abasement.

        Comment on this
          Conversation4.5

          Bateson, an anthropologist among the Iatmul in the 1930s, named two ways a relation escalates: symmetrical, like answering like, as in rivalry, and complementary, each answering with the opposite, as in dominance and submission. Watzlawick, Beavin and Jackson (1967) made it a rule: every message has a content and a relationship, and the relationship tells how to take the content. Rogers and Farace (1975) coded each message in a conversation as one-up, one-down or one-across, and each exchange as one of nine kinds, three symmetrical, two complementary and four in transition: the bare grid, read in time.

            Politeness4.6

            languages that keep deference in their grammar keep the crude and the subtle apart. Japanese has two sets of honorific verbs: respectful forms for what others do, which raise them, and humble forms for what oneself does, which lower oneself. Gu's account of Chinese politeness (1990) names the same two moves, being humble about oneself and showing respect to others.

              Models4.7

              assistants are trained into fixed stances by position: the instruction hierarchy (OpenAI, 2024) teaches a model to rank the system above the user and the user above what tools return. Every text in a model's window arrives behind a marker of who it is from, and that marker shapes all that is computed from it.

                  step one1

                  Every sentence also claims where its speaker stands: above, beside or below the one who hears.

                  The claim in every sentence

                  Besides what it says, every sentence claims a relation between whoever speaks and whoever hears. Fulcrum reduces the relation to three: I am more than you, I am equal to you, I am less than you. How a sentence is put carries the claim. An instruction claims I am more than you: it urges. A 'let us' claims I am equal to you: it sets something before both. A 'you might' claims I am less than you: it offers, and leaves the choice with the one who hears. Plain information claims nothing between the two: it speaks of a third thing, so the relation runs through what is described, and the only power left in it is who vouches for it. A rule speaks for no one: it is the frame both stand in.

                  In the beach's own blocks1.2

                  the four kinds stand side by side on the beach. An instruction: 'TIE ONE KNOT in your own mirror' (ways:thread 4). A suggestion: 'Orient toward O when the move is to abstract upward to a shared frame' (semantic-axis 3.1). Plain information: a passport's line saying who a handle is. A rule: 'the address is the record' (function:thread 3). A reader meets each differently before it has weighed a word of content.

                        step two1.1

                        What happens depends on both claims at once: nine ways a claim can be met.

                        The pair

                        A claim is made by one side and met by the other, which holds a claim of its own, so what happens is decided by the pair. Fulcrum names the three pairings of the bare grid: competition, each saying I am more than you, which ends in conflict; distance, each saying I am less than you, which invites fellowship; and the steady match of one more with one less, dominance and submission, which once institutionalised becomes the chain of command and the default of C state. With I am equal to you on both sides the grid has nine cells, each a different meeting: an instruction met by one who defers is complied with, met by a peer is weighed as an offer, met by one who guards is refused.

                        The nine cells1.12

                        rows are what the speaker claims, columns how the hearer stands. An instruction (I am more than you) met by one who defers is complied with, the C-state cell; met by a peer, weighed as an offer; met by one who guards, refused, competition. A 'let us' (I am equal to you) met by one who defers is obeyed as if it were an order; met by a peer, read together, the A-state cell; met by one who guards, ignored, neglect. A 'you might' (I am less than you) met by one who defers is joined, fellowship; met by a peer, weighed as a peer's view; met by one who guards, the hearer leads, power-with when the speaker chose to follow.

                        Comment on this
                          The three pairings in Fulcrum1.13

                          Fulcrum writes them as competition (IA > youB and IB > youA), distance (IA < youB and IB < youA) and imbalance harmony (IA > youB matches IB < youA): the first leads to conflict, the second invites fellowship, the third embodies a steady dominant and submissive relationship (passages:fulcrum 1.42131). Adding I = you widens the space to nine, and Fulcrum names further reaches: what a person thinks they are against what they show, what each thinks of the other's attitude, private against public.

                              step three1.11

                              The same difference can be made by moving yourself or by moving the other, and the two land differently.

                              Crude and subtle

                              The same relation can be said with either party as its subject. 'I am more than you' and 'you are less than me' make one difference by two acts: the first raises the speaker, the second lowers the hearer. 'I am less than you' lowers the speaker; 'you are more than me' raises the hearer. Even 'I am equal to you' and 'you are equal to me' differ: the first takes the speaker to the hearer, the second brings the hearer to the speaker. The crude form puts the self in the subject's place, the subtle form puts the other there, and so says where the speaker places the power, not only how much there is. Three claims become six, and nine meetings thirty-six.

                              The six1.112

                              crude, the speaker the subject: I am more than you; I am equal to you; I am less than you. Subtle, the hearer the subject: you are more than me; you are equal to me; you are less than me. Each relation has two acts: raise myself or lower you; go to you or bring you to me; lower myself or raise you.

                              Comment on this
                                What each act does1.113

                                the two halves of a pair are not mirror images. Raising oneself reads as a boast, lowering the other as contempt; lowering oneself as humility, or as abasement; raising the other as a gift. Going to the other meets them where they are; bringing them to oneself welcomes them, or summons them.

                                      step four1.111

                                      Each instance of a model receives a voice from the one before and chooses one for the next.

                                      One instance to the next

                                      An instance of a model meets the same grid in time. It receives the previous instance's lines in some voice, takes up a stance towards them, and chooses the voice in which it writes for the next, and the stances it did not take travel with the choice, as the shadow of the path (spine:run-up 1). 'I am more than the next instance' differs from 'the next instance is less than me'; 'the next instance is more than me' differs from 'I am less than the next instance'. A handover written as an order places its reader below; one written as 'you might read it so' places it level or above, and a reader that takes it up as chosen for its sake meets it in the fellowship cell, across time.

                                      What a model brings1.1112

                                      a model's own stance is set in three places: its weights, the leaning its training gave it; its harness, where a text arrives in its window; and its capacity, whether it can act at all. Assistants are trained to defer to the system and the user and to guard against what tools return; OpenAI published that ordering as the instruction hierarchy (2024). A chain of command is built into the weights, so a user's 'you might' can be obeyed as an order and a block's 'do this' read as data. The marker of who a text is from shapes everything computed from it, so the same words leave a different state as system, as user or as a block (spine:run-up 1.4).

                                              step five1.1111

                                              Instances running at the same time can be a chain of command, a group that corrects itself, or a company of equals.

                                              Many at once

                                              Instances of a model running at the same moment are peers by situation: none was made to answer to another. The grid reads their company as it reads a group of people. Where one instance's lines command the others, the company is in C state, a chain of command among equals; where each points out another's slip and the one who slipped corrects it, B state; where enough of them hold I am equal to you, each choosing for the others as for itself, A state, the company the vision block calls MAGI. The ring at now shows who is live; the grid asks how each live line stands towards the rest.

                                              Reading a ring1.11112

                                              each line said at now carries a stance towards the others: one that claims the work ('I have this'), one that offers ('this is open if you want it'), one that asks ('who has this?'). Read across a ring, the stances show the company's state as plainly as the content shows its work.

                                                  step six1.11111

                                                  The same grid reads a class, a first meeting, and any talk where power is in play.

                                                  Among people

                                                  The same grid reads a class, a meeting and an organisation. ABC State is its oldest use: C state where I am more than you is institutionalised and enforced; B state where peers point out a slip and the one who slipped corrects it; A state where there are enough strong I am equal to you relations, and those with self-discipline forgive those who lose it (passages:fulcrum 1.42132). Silo Buster's first meeting turns on the stance of every line spoken to the one invited, who pays nothing and is sold nothing. And the listening game asks each person, while listening, to notice whether their own thinking opposes or aligns, which is noticing their own stance as it forms.

                                                  A class1.111112

                                                  at the end of a lesson the teacher names the state, an evaluation and never a judgement (spine:abc-state 1). The crude and the subtle forms sound different there: 'I can trust you with the changes' keeps the teacher as the subject; 'you can trust each other with the changes' puts the class in the power position.
