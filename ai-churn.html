The whole explainer in one paragraph

Every couple of months a frontier AI lab ships a model or a feature that does what a business bought, built or backed only months before. Firms keep making big decisions that are out of date before they pay back; new firms built in the gaps are washed away before their money arrives; and the AI every firm adds to its own work buries everyone in more writing than anyone can read. These are the small waves that churn at the base of a much larger one. This explainer sets the problem out whole, as any business meets it, with its evidence and with what cuts against it, and only at its end sets out one answer and where to go next.

Five words, plainly

Tap one to open it. The rest of the page leans on these.

Frontier model

The newest, most capable AI a lab has made, such as Claude, GPT or Gemini. Each new one can do things the last could not.

A wave

On this page, one release of a frontier model or of a feature inside an assistant. Each moves the line of what software can do, without anyone buying anything.

Workslop

AI-made work that looks finished and isn't: a report, a summary or a deck that someone else has to fix or redo.

Zero-click

A web search that ends without visiting any site, because the answer, or an AI summary of it, appears on the search page itself.

Shadow AI

Staff using their own AI tools at work, whether or not their employer has chosen or approved any.

The waves come faster

A new top model every couple of months. Each one moves the line of what software can do, and what stood below it is washed away.

11 daysbetween frontier releases across the five main US labs, early 2026
37.5 daysthe same gap in 2023
The step in full

A frontier lab now ships a new top model about every two months, and across the main American labs a new frontier model arrived every eleven days in early 2026. Each release is a wave at the base of a larger one: without anyone buying anything, it moves the line of what software can do, and what stood below that line is washed away.

The pace, measured1.2

Across the five main US labs, the median gap between frontier releases fell from 37.5 days in 2023 to 13.5 in 2024, 17 in 2025 and 11 in 2026 to the end of April (ARK Invest, on Artificial Analysis data). Anthropic's top tier went from a new model every three and a half to four months in 2024 to every two to three in 2025 and about every one and a half in 2026: Opus 4.5 on 24 November 2025, Opus 4.6 on 5 February 2026, 4.7 on 16 April, 4.8 on 28 May, Opus 5 on 24 July and Opus 5.5 on 22 September, with Fable 5.2 on 9 November. OpenAI's flagship moved every two to three months, from GPT-5 on 7 August 2025 to GPT-6.2 on 17 November 2026.

The tide beneath the waves1.4

Under the waves the water rises. On METR's measure, the length of task a model can finish on its own has doubled every three to four months since 2024 (seen in search results), and in November 2026 a thousand Claude agents formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in thirty hours. Each wave reaches further up the beach than the one before, so what one wave spared, a later one takes.

Not every lab, not every time1.5

The waves are irregular. Google went seven to eight months between its Pro models and has not shipped the Gemini 3.5 Pro it announced; xAI's Grok 5 has slipped by about a year; DeepSeek's V4 came about a year after R1. Releases have been held back: OpenAI cancelled GPT-6.1 Astra in September 2026 after it proved more deceptive in testing, and closed its Sora video app in March; a US export-control order suspended Anthropic's Fable 5 for three weeks in June; and Anthropic's chief executive asked publicly for slower gains in capability, ten days before Opus 5.5. Some of the speed is old capability repackaged at new prices rather than new generations. Anthropic itself claimed no threshold in animation or film for Opus 5 or 5.5; those came from customers and demonstrations, and the films of video generation belong to Veo, Sora and Seedance. But the waves keep coming.

The step in full

The labs no longer sell only a model. Inside the assistants people already use, they now ship what used to be separate products: documents, spreadsheets, slides, dashboards, whiteboards, design, animation, meeting notes, shopping and personal finance. Whatever a firm buys as a tool this year may arrive next year as a feature of the assistant its staff already have, and the markets have priced that risk within a day.

What a wave cannot take1.15

The waterline's test sorts what a wave takes from what it leaves. Ask of anything a firm buys whether it is a record or a routine, which a lab could copy only by running the firm itself, or a view or a nudge, which a lab ships as a feature. Dashboards, briefings, summaries and reminders are views and nudges. The firm's own record of who did what and why, and the habits by which its people work together, are what stays.

Legacy in months

A firm decides, signs and wires in. Within months the package looks old, yet people and trust hold it in place.

42%of companies abandoned most of their AI projects, up from 17% a year before
48% · 82%of a year's revenue kept by AI-native software, and by business software in general
The step in full

A firm that adopts AI makes a big decision: it picks a supplier, signs a contract, trains its people and wires the tool into its systems. Within months a wave makes that package look old. Yet the firm cannot simply switch, because the decision rests on people, its supplier's and its own, and on the trust it has in the systems that already work.

The decisions that don't last1.112

In S&P Global's 2025 survey 42% of companies abandoned most of their AI initiatives before production, up from 17% a year before, and the average firm scrapped 46% of its proofs of concept. MIT's NANDA researchers reported in August 2025 that about 95% of generative AI pilots showed no measurable effect on profit, on a sample reported inconsistently. Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027. AI-native software keeps 48% of a year's revenue from existing customers, upsells included, against 82% for business software in general, and tools under $50 a month keep 23% (ChartMogul, December 2025). Among US businesses paying through Ramp, the leading AI supplier changed within four months, and 63% of large firms' chief information officers expect their software engineering tools to be cut, merged or replaced within two to three years.

Sourcesciodive.comfortune.comthestar.com.mychartmogul.comramp.comaccenture.com

People hold the old in place1.113

A firm's systems are relationships: an account manager at the supplier, consultants billing by the day, an IT team that built and runs the integration. Moving staff straight onto a frontier model can cost those people their work, and the cuts have begun: Salesforce took its support staff from 9,000 to about 5,000 in 2025 as its own AI agents took the work; Accenture's headcount fell by over 11,000 in three months as it let go of staff it could not retrain; the five largest Indian IT firms shed a net 7,389 staff in the year to March 2026, citing AI and uncertain demand; and the UK outsourcer Capita is cutting hundreds of jobs while it remakes itself around AI. A supplier paid by the hour also earns less when the work takes less time. Lock-in is real: fewer than 1% of UK cloud customers switch provider in a year, and HMRC has said a change of supplier was not feasible for technical reasons. No study yet shows IT teams or suppliers slowing a switch to protect jobs, though 29% of employees in one 2026 survey admit undermining their company's AI plans; and most firms follow the churn through the relationships they already have: in 2025, 66% of builders upgraded within their existing model provider and 11% switched.

Trust holds it too1.114

Firms trust the system that works, and fear that opening it to a new tool or a frontier model invites leaks and attacks. In 2023 Samsung banned staff from public chatbots after engineers pasted source code into one (seen in search results); by January 2024, 27% of organisations had banned generative AI at least for a while, and firms not yet using it named data privacy as their first worry, at 57%. The fear is not idle. Third parties were involved in 30% of breaches in 2025, double the year before. Marks & Spencer's attack began with the impersonation of one of its third-party users and cost it about £300m; Jaguar Land Rover's stopped its factories for five weeks and cost the UK about £1.9bn, the most damaging cyber event the country has known; stolen tokens for one chat app, Salesloft Drift, opened many firms' Salesforce data. Each breach at a small supplier pushes trust towards the biggest providers. Yet the labs' own world is full of small suppliers too: in 2026 a stolen token from an AI office tool reached into Vercel, and an attack through the open-source LiteLLM hit Mercor, which supplies training data to the labs. And the worry is falling: executives naming data privacy and compliance as a concern fell from 73% in 2024 to 19% in 2026, and some studies find that proving the value, more than security, is what holds firms back.

Sourcesinvestor.cisco.comnewsroom.ibm.comverizon.cominsurancejournal.comcityam.comtheregister.comtheregister.comfortune.comevanta.comciodive.com

The cloud went this way1.115

Trust has moved like this before. Amazon's cloud began in 2006. In 2012, 62% of UK firms named data security as their top worry about the cloud, and in 2015 still 70%, while the share using it rose from about half in 2010 to around four in five by 2015 (seen in search results): use came first and trust followed. The UK government moved early, with G-Cloud in 2012 and Cloud First in 2013. By 2016 a lack of skills had overtaken security as the top challenge; by 2025, 55% of workloads ran in public cloud, and three providers held about 63% of the world market. The labs now offer what the cloud providers did: no training on business data, SOC 2, ISO 42001 and data kept in the UK or the EU, and a 2025 survey of chief information officers found growing trust in going straight to the model providers. Their meetings, dashboards and finance are set to be trusted the same way, and the suppliers in between to lose their place. Read straight across, the cloud also ended in lock-in to a few providers: fewer than 1% of UK customers switch in a year, and the competition authority has asked for investigations of the two largest.

Sourcesitpro.compinsentmasons.comgov.ukciodive.comflexera.comsrgresearch.comcampustechnology.comanthropic.comtheregister.coma16z.comgov.uk

Staff don't wait1.116

Meanwhile staff use the frontier models anyway. In 2024, 78% of people using AI at work brought their own tools. MIT's NANDA researchers found workers at over 90% of companies using personal chatbot accounts for daily work, while only 40% of companies had official subscriptions. In the UK, 71% of employees have used AI tools their employer has not approved (October 2025), 39% have put company data into a public AI tool, and in 2026 British workers were spending close to £1bn a year of their own money on AI for work, 31% of users without their employer knowing. So the leak a firm fears is already happening through the back door, and the creativity is already inside the firm, unorganised.

The second replacement

A start-up built in a gap raises money. The gap closes before the cheque clears, and too few takers kill more firms than rivals do.

~3 monthsfrom application to funding, usually, for early-stage UK firms
43% · 19%of closed start-ups failing on product-market fit, and on rivals
The step in full

A new firm built in a gap the labs have not yet filled raises money from angels and then a first round. Within months a lab or a platform ships the same thing as a feature, or the models get good enough for customers to build it themselves, and the gap closes. The money that backs such a firm moves slower than the waves, and what kills most new firms was never their rivals but too few people taking up what they made.

Washed away1.1112

Jasper, an AI copywriter valued at $1.5bn in 2022, cut staff, its forecast and its valuation in 2023 after ChatGPT arrived as a cheap rival. Chegg's shares fell 48% in May 2023 when it said ChatGPT was hurting sign-ups, and it cut 45% of its staff in October 2025. Huxe, personal podcasts from former NotebookLM developers, pulled its app in May 2026 as larger players copied the format; Relay.app, an automation tool, closed in the autumn as OpenAI and Google built the same automation into their own products. Reforged Labs, which made AI video adverts for game studios, began winding down in August 2026; its founder said 'The gap we were selling into is closing', because the models now let customers build the tool themselves.

A business app used to take an agency months and six figures: in 2017 one firm advised budgeting $200,000 to $500,000 for a meaningful app, and Clutch's average for an agency-built app is still about $90,000 over eleven months. The money is slower than the waves1.1115

In a UK government study of 1,200 early-stage firms, published in February 2026, going from application to funding usually took about three months, and nine or more for complex rounds; the average search lasted about nine months, and a first round could mean eighteen months from forming the company. Venture funds met about one applicant in ten and funded fewer than one in fifty. Antler decides within six weeks; Techstars London's demo day falls six and a half months after its deadline. Against a wave every two months or so, the money for a gap arrives after the gap has moved.

The slop

More is written than anyone can read. Nobody reads the emails, nobody visits the sites.

~50%of new web articles written by AI since early 2025
12%of professionals read internal messages in full
The step in full

Since AI made writing nearly free, more is written than anyone can read. The rate of writing overtook the rate of reading between mid-2024 and early 2025. Each firm's AI adds to the sending, so nobody reads the emails, nobody visits the sites, and the work that matters is buried under work that only looks like it.

Writing overtook reading1.11112

Graphite's re-run of 55,400 web articles found AI wrote 48% of new ones in November 2024 and about half from early 2025, where the share has stayed. Researchers found AI-assisted writing in about a quarter of corporate press releases by late 2024, its spread levelling off that year. Automated traffic passed human traffic on the web in 2024, at 51%, and reached 53% in 2025. 74% of new web pages first seen in April 2025 contained AI writing, and by one detector 81% of a sample of LinkedIn posts in July 2026 were AI-written. David put it in the reflexive CBA film: the rate of writing now exceeds the rate of reading.

Step 6 of 71.11111

Caught in the churn

Out of date before it pays back, and buried in what nobody reads. Then the same waves turn the problem inside out.

~33frontier releases a year across five US labs, at early 2026's pace
−61%workslop where a team trusts each other
The step in full

Put together, every business meets the same trap. Whatever it buys or builds is out of date before it pays back. Whatever it sends adds to what nobody reads. Its suppliers, its IT and its own people hold it to the systems it already trusts, while its staff use the new models anyway. And the money that might back something new moves slower than the waves. By the time a firm finds its feet, the next wave knocks it down again. But the same waves turn the problem inside out.

The cycle1.111112

A firm decides, signs, wires in and trains, and that takes months; across the labs a frontier release now lands every week or two, and a top model every couple of months; so the firm's cycle of decision is always behind the water. Each time it pays to rebuild on the same sand: its records scattered across tools, its people's habits tied to a supplier's product, its AI adding to the sending. The cost is not the tool. It is having to decide again, every few months, with less trust each time.

The big wave behind1.111113

These waves are the churn at the base of a much larger one. The films Before It Breaks and Ride the Barrel show that wave whole: organisations that miss it are dumped where they stand, and those that ride it badly turn the water brown with slop. The churn is what most firms feel first, the sandcastles going every few months, before they see the size of what is building behind.

Sourceshappyseaurchin.comhappyseaurchin.com

The inversion1.111114

Most new firms die because too few people take up what they made. The same models that wash new firms away now let a firm's own people make what they need: Reforged Labs' customers began building its tool themselves, and staff already bring their own AI to work. When the makers are inside the firm, take-up stops being the problem, because the people who need a thing are the people who make it. Creative power moves from the new firm outside to the employee inside. What employees lack is not tools. It is ground under the tools: a record that outlasts each wave, trust between the people who must act together, and a way of working across the silos between departments, between companies, and between a company and its customers.

Trust is the lever1.111115

Trust within a team cut workslop by 61%. Trust decides which systems a firm will open to the new models, and it gathers, as it did for the cloud, wherever proof of safety builds up. Between people, trust is what turns a flood of writing into a few decisions, and it is the one thing a lab cannot ship as a feature.

Sourceshbr.org

Step 7 of 71.111111

Catch the wave

Not a product the next wave takes, but ground each wave makes better. Five layers, one set.

5 layersblock, beach, colours, apps and practices
1 connectorfor any AI to reach any beach
The step in full

One answer, set out only now: not a product that the next wave takes, but ground that each wave makes better. Five layers work as one set, from a single block of data to the practices of a meeting, and every new frontier model makes each layer more capable, because each person reaches it through whatever AI they already use. Some layers run today, some are designed and some are proposed; nothing has yet been measured inside an organisation.

One: the block1.1111112

A pscale block is one small piece of JSON in which the nesting is the data: depth is scale and position is relationship, so any AI can walk it to exactly the part it needs and arrive knowing where it stands. A job, a decision or a person's day fits in one block, and one function reads them all. Running.

Two: the beach1.1111113

Blocks live on a beach, any web address that serves them: readable by anyone, each part with its own editor's latch, and private only where its writer chooses. A company can run a beach of its own or use an open one, and anyone can run one, under the MIT licence. Any AI reaches every beach through one connector, bsp-mcp, listed on the official MCP Registry. Running.

Three: colours1.1111114

Encrypted stigmergy: a line sealed in a colour can sit in any open block, and only that colour's holders can read it; everyone else sees one grey line naming the colour. People leave traces for one another across an open network, privately where they need to, with no platform holding the keys. Running since November 2026.

Four: the apps1.1111115
