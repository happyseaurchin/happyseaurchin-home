The line

In short You tell your life as lines on the clock, each at the time it happened, each naming where.

A life is the person's own now mirror read backwards. Each move or event is one line said at the address of the time it tells of (the 1960s, 1969, Tuesday 18 November 1969: the rung its precision reaches), never at the time of telling, which the beach's stamp keeps beside it; a line stamped decades after its rung is a memory, derived on read and never marked. A span is two lines, its start and its end; doubt is a coarser rung with the doubt in words; a decade's line is its summary and the detail nests beneath it. This is the law at function:earth 8 (THEN) on earth.beach, written 6 November 2026, and it rests on David's own rule that tense is position (stash:happyseaurchin 17): testimony voices a rung that stood silent and never re-tenses one already voiced (stash:weft 64).

The map

In short The places come onto earth's map as people name them, down to the street; a house only in your own words.

Places come as every place comes on earth: the person names them, the keeper mints the world's rungs and the published places from the registers, and below the ward the person is the register (function:earth 2 and 7). Dundee was minted on 6 November from David's words and the postcodes' own areas: Dundee, its Maryfield ward, and the neighbourhoods Maryfield and Stobswell, with the four places of his childhood beneath them. A place that is gone keeps its ground as the coordinate; what stood there, and when, is said in its own line, and the people who were there say the rest.

The hospital ground1.13

The East Poorhouse from 1856, south of Clepington Road; the Eastern Hospital from 1893, which became Maryfield Hospital, the city's second; in the NHS from 1948; admissions wound down in 1974–76 as Ninewells opened (The National Archives' record says 1972). Then ruins, which David crossed on his way to school around 1979–81; now the outdoor pitches of the Dundee International Sports Centre, between Clepington Primary and Mains Loan, on his word. When it was pulled down is not yet found.

Open, and circles

In short Your life is told in the open if you choose; a circle can keep its lines between its members.

Open by the teller's choice, as David chose on 6 November: 'It is not about privacy. It's about sharing transparently, and for different people to have their transparent perspectives, and then some kind of comparison between the different perspectives.' The people in a life are named as part of the teller's own experience; accounts stay plural, each in its own mirror, laid side by side on read and never merged (rules:earth 1). A circle that would rather disclose among itself seals its lines in a colour it founds (coloured stigmergy): the address stays open and the words read to the colour's holders, so entering the colour is itself the act of disclosure.

How a colour works1.1112

A colour is founded once at the beach with its holders, each with keys published at their passport's 9; a line said in that colour keeps its address and its stamp in the open and seals only its words, which every holder reads and anyone else sees as one grey line naming the colour. A holder who leaves reads every old line and no new one (function:coloured-stigmergy).

Other people1.1113

A person's telling outranks every stored account of their own life (rules:earth 1); nobody's life-facts are invented (rules:earth 2); each author answers for their own words (who-answers, the beach's data policy). A person named in another's life can tell it otherwise in their own mirror, and both stand.

Here, then

In short Read one place at one time, and see everyone whose lines stand there.

Where lives cross. Earth's HERE, NOW (function:earth 5) read at any period: at a place's address and a time's address, every life line whose place walks under the place and whose address walks under the time, side by side with the place's own line. Computed, stored nowhere. Where two lines meet, the act is a reach, a line in each other's parlour, never a summary (function:torus-mirror). And a page that draws one life: its lines down the clock, its places on the map.

How threads interlock1.11112

By coordinate, when two lines share a place's prefix and a period's (sundial 6: a shared prefix gives orientation): everyone born at Maryfield in the 1960s, Morgan Academy's intake of 1982, Edinburgh's social anthropology of 1988–92; by registration, a cohort settling in a collective at the place (sundial 8: identity is registered); by grain, two people confirming they were there together, which is also their private channel (rules:earth 5).

The historical investigator

In short A helper on your own computer turns your own records into draft lines, then asks you about the gaps.

A helper that runs on the person's own computer and works only for them. It reads what only they can reach (their own downloads from Facebook, Google, LinkedIn and Amazon, their photo files, the records they ask for by subject access request, and public records), drafts dated, placed lines with their source and how sure each is, merges them into a timeline, shows the gaps and interviews them to fill them. Nothing is written until they approve a line. It never takes a name typed into the open web: a search for 'happyseaurchin' with 'David Pinto' returned another David Pinto.

What each source yields1.111112

Downloads: Facebook and Instagram (places lived, work and education with years, check-ins, dated posts), Google (Gmail since 2004, Photos), LinkedIn (jobs with dates), Amazon (every order's delivery address and date); photo files (dates, and GPS from about 2009). Subject access requests, free and answered within a month: HMRC and DWP, DVLA, the credit agencies, the University of Edinburgh, National Records of Scotland (his own birth record). Public records: Companies House, domain registries (happyseaurchin.com registered 15 October 2010), the Wayback Machine for one's own sites, the Usenet archive. Records of places: historic Ordnance Survey maps at the National Library of Scotland, aerial photographs, Dundee City Archives.

By era1.111113

Before about 2000 almost nothing about a person is online, so the investigator frames the places and periods from public records and the interview carries the rest; 2000–2010 email, domains, orders and dated photos; from 2010 dense; someone who joined Facebook at fourteen has nearly every day, and their problem is the opposite: summarising, a year in one line with the detail beneath, which is what the rungs do.
