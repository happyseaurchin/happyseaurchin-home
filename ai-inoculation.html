Part 1 · the problem · spine:ai-inoculation 1

The problem, as you see it

In short · AI was added to tools built for sending, so it sends more, and the AI wave is coming.

Every organisation has bought AI, and few can say what it has bought them. What you see is the bill and the doubt; underneath it are the tools AI was added to, which were built for sending; and coming is the AI wave, which will not wait.

The bill and the doubt 1.2

Licences for everyone, an assistant in every app, and a bill that comes every month. The work looks much as it did: the same chasing, the same waiting, the same meetings to find out where things are. What has grown is the number of words, not the speed of the job.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleHollin & Grey fits out offices in Birmingham and employs seventy people. It pays for seventy AI licences, and half of them sit unused. Ruth Hollin, the managing director, cannot say what the other half have bought.

12

Built for sending 1.3

For thirty years the tools of work have moved information from one person to another: email and chat, the CRM, websites, search and advertising. Each is a message sent and a reply waited for. Call it the C-state: work held together by chains of one-to-one messages, and by the few people who carry them. Give everyone an AI and each of them sends more, faster, so the chains grow longer and the people who carry them are buried.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleA quote passes from sales to estimating, to the site, to a supplier, to accounts and back again, and takes nine days. Since everyone began drafting with AI, the emails have doubled, and nobody reads them.

The AI wave 1.4

The next AI wave reaches every organisation, ready or not. Met in the C-state, an organisation comes apart in one of three ways. It is torn, as everyone goes private with their own AI. It is swamped, as the messages multiply past reading. Or it is passed by, as smaller firms that work with AI from the first day grow up around it and do in an afternoon what takes it a week.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleTwo estimators who left a rival last spring set up on their own, with AI from the first day. They quote the same afternoon.

Part 2 · the solution · spine:ai-inoculation 1.1

The solution: get the job done

In short · Use AI to connect people: leave what you know where their AI can read it, and meet only to decide.

Stop asking AI to make the old tools faster. Use it for what it is, and the job gets done. In nearly every organisation the job underneath the work is the same: connecting the people who need each other.

AI is help for one person 1.12

A language model works for one person at a time: it reads what that person needs and answers them. That is its strength, and it is wasted drafting more messages for other people to read. Used the right way, each person's AI connects them to what others already know, without anyone having to send it.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleJade has been estimating with AI for months, unasked. Her quotes are the fastest in the building, and nobody else knows how she does it.

Leave it, don't send it 1.13

Ants build without sending messages. Each leaves a trace where the work is, and the next reads the trace and carries on; biologists call it stigmergy. Here each person sets down, once, where they are, what they need and what they can offer, in their own words, on one shared ground. Their AI is a live mirror of that ground: it reads it for them and brings them what bears on their work. People meet only to decide.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleThe estimator sets down that the glass price is still to come. Sales' AI reads it before anyone asks, and so does the site's. Nobody sends an email, and the quote waits on one thing instead of on five people.

Part 3 · how it works · spine:ai-inoculation 1.11

How it works: the beach, in three layers

In short · Small shared blocks, everyone’s own mirror of them, a law, and an AI that reads them into a reflection.

The shared ground is the beach: a public space of small structured blocks that any AI can read and write with one call. It can be read at three levels, as a living thing can: physics, how a block is made; chemistry, how blocks bond into meaning; and biology, how AI runs its loops through them.

What you do 1.112

You open a page like this one and say your piece: where you are, what you need, what you could offer. You write it once, in your own words, under your own name, and nothing more is asked of you. What happens after that is the work of your AI and everyone else's.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleRuth's line: she wants to double the order book without breaking anything, and she suspects the handover between sales and estimating is where it breaks.

Physics: the block 1.113

A pscale block is a small triangle of meaning: one line at the top, up to nine beneath it, and nine beneath each of those. A number is an address, read one digit at a time from the general to the particular, and the line an address draws through a block is called a spindle. An AI that reads a spindle arrives already knowing the context of what it reads.

Chemistry: the molecule 1.114

Blocks bond. A spine holds the words of something shared, such as this page, and each person who answers keeps a mirror of it, at the very same addresses, in their own words: as many people as you like, and it costs nothing, since no AI is needed to write a mirror or to lay mirrors side by side. When there are more mirrors than anyone can read, a third block, the law, tells an AI how to read them all into one reflection. Spine, mirrors and law make one molecule, and nothing anyone writes can overwrite anyone else's.

Biology: the loop 1.115

An AI runs a loop through blocks of many kinds: a page's spine, everyone's mirrors, the law, a person's history, a project's plan. It reads the spindles it needs from each, puts them together and writes back a reflection: one accurate reading of what everyone intends. Then it comes round again. There is no memory system to buy, because the structure is the memory, and the same blocks can be the source of pages and programs, which turns code inside out.

Part 4 · what it does · spine:ai-inoculation 1.111

What it does

In short · No more chasing; in a day, three small teams of the right people; meetings that end in a collective decision; adoption that spreads on evidence.

When what people know stands where everyone's AI can read it, five things follow: the sending stops, the organisation can see itself, meetings shorten, the habit spreads beyond the building, and the gain can be counted.

No more sending 1.1112

Information stands on the beach, so email and chat stop carrying it, and people meet only to decide. Beyond the walls the same holds: instead of AI trawling websites made for eyes, it reads blocks made for it, and people share what they find through the beach instead of sending it on.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleThree quotes go out in two days instead of nine.

Found in a day 1.1113

From everyone's answers, an AI finds three kinds of people in seconds: the connectors, without whom the place would fall over; the chronic pains, problems people carry that nobody resolves; and the early AI users, already doing better work with AI, official or not. It looks for outliers, not averages, and draws each kind into a small team of its own: three groups, each of people who belong together. From everyone's few minutes to the right people in one room is a day, whatever the size of the organisation: no programme to install and no memory system, only small blocks and their structure, one structure shared across people and across AI vendors.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleRuth sends it at nine, and 41 of 70 answer by ten. Priya in accounts and Dev on site are named again and again. Jade has been estimating with AI for months, unasked. Kerry in aftercare has a fix for the snag list that nobody has heard.

In the room 1.1114

Meetings are set up from a spine too: who comes, what is talked through, what is shown, and the decision kept for the last ten minutes. While a meeting runs, each person's AI writes what they think into their own mirror, and an AI reads them all into a reflection as it goes: an accurate reflection of everyone's intentions, showing where the room agrees, where it pulls apart, who is quiet and what is sticking. The sticking point is settled in the meeting, and the room leaves with a collective decision.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleSeven meet on Tuesday: the five who were found, with Sam and Lena, whom Ruth picks. The reflection shows them agreed on everything but one thing, who owns the handover, and they settle it before the hour is out.

Out of the building 1.1115

The people getting results show others how, and it crosses the silos; then it crosses the walls, to the suppliers, clients and customers who are on the same beach. This is the social side of the work, called Silo Buster: an organisation moving from C, held together by control, through B, groups working without opposition, towards A, people moving together. Further on, the value each person adds can be traced across the network, so that money can follow the work.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleAround Hollin & Grey are a joinery supplier, a client's facilities team, an architects' practice and an electrical contractor. One person from each joins in.

What you gain, and what is true today 1.1116

What gets counted: the messages spent asking where someone is, the time spent finding a meeting, and how many plain questions about the team a fresh AI can answer from the beach alone. Those are hours back, and hours are money. Then the count that decides it: whether adoption spreads on evidence, people taking it up because they can see it working between people, proven socially and not just for one person. What is true today: the beach is live, open source under the MIT licence, and used daily by a small community. It is early, and nothing is measured yet in an organisation. It is public by default, with privacy a separate setting; no personal data goes on it until privacy is the default for the group, and every read and write by an AI uses paid AI capacity.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleIn the first week, three quotes go out in two days instead of nine, and two are won, worth £38,000.

What the clocks measure

Beside each paragraph, as in Clocks of Completion, a clock closes as the paragraph writes itself out: the inner ring for the phrase, the middle for the sentence, the outer for the whole paragraph. Tokens are approximate: a punctuation mark counts as one, a word of up to seven letters as one, and longer words split into pieces of about six letters. The speed of all the writing is set at the top of the page.

With the example on, some plates carry a second clock, the job’s: the inner ring for one person’s own part, the middle for the handover between people, the outer for the whole job, with a tick for each day. Hollin & Grey’s quote takes nine days on the plates where it is sent, and two where it is left on the beach. Those days are the example’s, invented to show the shape.

Hollin & Grey, its people and every figure about them are invented to show the shape, the same firm as in the Silo Buster film. Ruth Hollin’s lead answers are the example’s too.
