The Middle Point

An AI that listens to people talking, at several speeds at once, and speaks only when the middle point opens: after a speaker's thought has landed, when nobody else is about to say it, and while the topic is still open. Mostly it holds. Six steps take it from what it is, through the maths of what it holds back, who is speaking, the shell of cells it runs on and the stance it is given, to the order it is built and tested in, every test judging when it speaks before what it says.

I
Step 1 of 6 · on the beach at 1

What we are building

Decide ↓

In shortAn app on a phone that listens to a conversation at several speeds and speaks only at the right moment. Most of the time it stays silent, and every silence is a choice it counts.

An app on a phone with one control. Pressing it wakes a shell of AI cells that listen to the people in the room at several speeds at once, each keeping its own place in the talk, until it is pressed again. It speaks aloud only at the middle point: after a speaker's thought has landed in words, never while it is still forming; when nobody else is about to say it, and then as a nudge that keeps both sides rather than a verdict; while the floor is open and the topic still live. Everywhere else it holds, and each hold is a choice it counts. It listens only among people who know it is listening, and it is judged by when it speaks before what it says: a good line at the wrong moment counts as a miss.

The middle point

Every choice is made from one position: after a parent, beside a sibling, before the options. In live talk the parent is what has just been said, the sibling is the unsaid, and the options are what could be said next. To speak is to turn a held unsaid into said, and what is said frames everyone's next moment, so the default is to hold. Siblings can nudge; parents dictate.

David's practice

The timing rule is David's own practice made into a rule: interrupt rendering, never interrupt emergence, and nudge at the collapse from emergence into words. Rendering is a speaker repeating their own known pattern; emergence is a speaker finding something new, halting as they go.

Why timing, not content

The work so far kept sliding back to content and scores, because content is what models and their harnesses are built to make. The relay scored choices against fixed values, and the first run of twenty cells asked each for a line at a moment picked in advance. Neither touched timing, and timing is what this proposal is about.

The aim

In David's terms, to help a conversation move from C, leaning on the parent and rendering history, through B, facing the options beside siblings, to A, siblings converging on a shared objective from different positions without copying one another.

What it is not

Not an assistant that answers at every pause, not a recorder, and not a judge of who is right. Enlivening it is closer to waking keel's shell than to hatching a new agent: the identity already exists, and enlivening gives it a present. Once enlivened it is mostly silent; stopped, what remains is a record, not a presence.

The maths of the unsaid

Decide ↓

In shortWhat the AI holds back is the sibling of what was said, not its opposite. A shared ledger keeps each held thought until it is said, its moment passes, or it is left for a person.

Enough of it stands to build on. A held thought is a sibling of what was said: its complement within the same parent, not its opposite, sharing everything the parent holds and differing only in what the moment decided. Time is counted in choices, holds included, so no probability is needed, and each of four kinds of absence has its own act. The working form is a ledger of the unsaid that every cell shares: one entry per held thought, saying who holds it, whose words and topic it answers, when it was born and how many turns it has lived, and its state: withheld, ripe, foreclosed, voiced, or handed back to a person. Each entry has one voice, named by a slow cell, so a thought many cells hold is neither lost while each waits for another nor said five times.

The algebra

The parent is 1 and the decision is j, with j squared equal to +1, so the two branches are (1 + j)/2 and (1 − j)/2. Their product is 0, so they exclude each other; their sum is 1, so together they make the parent; and each is the other's conjugate. A branch chosen twice is still that branch, but the opposite of a branch, squared, gives the branch back, so the opposite of a choice is not itself a choice and no one can stand in it. The session on Boole's laws of thought reached the same pair from Boole's class and its complement, whose product is 0; the Temporal Seed reached it from the fork.

Four kinds of absence

Withheld: the sibling, which can be asked for. Foreclosed: what the sibling would have led to, which only another walker can know. Pending: the offers, which can be chosen. Unmade: the branch nobody offered, which can only be created. Probability sets all four to zero, which is why a mathematics of absence cannot be a probability.

The clock

A choice among n options is log2 n bits (Hartley, 1928): alternatives counted, never weighed. Each choice turns the options into one past and hands the next moment both its self and its not. Across many instances probability returns as a heat map of paths, and an instance's quality is how it chooses against that map.

Two faces of a parent

A fork comes before its branches and divides them; a summary comes after them and gathers them, the unwalked ones included. The union of siblings lives in that after-face, which is why a summary is an act in time and not only a higher scale. In the ledger, the slow cells are the after-face.

A measure without a judge

Text cannot be averaged, but its embeddings can. Embed a moment's parent and its branches and check that the parent sits near their mean. Then ask a cell what the unsaid branch probably held, embed its guess, and see whether it lands nearer the parent minus what was said, the scoped not, or nearer the negation of what was said, the opposed one. That tells the two kinds of not apart without anyone's judgement.

The cheapest test

On weft's relay apparatus, at about the cost of its clean run of 2 November ($1.58 on the API), frame the branch not taken four ways: unmentioned; rejected; not taken by this line and still our parent's; walked by another chooser, with where it got to. Measure what each note attends to, up, across or down; whether the parent's shared content reaches the next window; how the chooser fills the unseen branch, as its own negation or as the parent minus itself; and whether it makes a branch nobody offered.

Step 3 of 6 · on the beach at 1.11

Who is speaking, and how

Decide ↓

In shortTo tell a speaker finding something new from a speaker repeating themselves, the app has to know who is speaking. Labelling voices is a solved service.

Novelty is measured against each speaker's own earlier words, so the cells have to know who is speaking. Labelling speakers, called diarisation, is a solved and rentable service. The first experiments run on recordings, where labelling afterwards is enough; the live app needs it streamed. With speakers known, the fast cells follow each one and tell rendering, fluent and repeating that speaker's own pattern, from emergence, halting and new to that speaker: rendering may be cut, emergence is protected, and the collapse from emergence into words is the moment for a nudge.

The options

From pyannoteAI's 2026 survey, which ranks its own products first. Streamed, for the live app: pyannoteAI's Live-1, with its Precision-2 model from €0.096 an audio hour; Deepgram, in the cloud or self-hosted; AssemblyAI, cloud only; Speechmatics, in the cloud or on premises; and NVIDIA NeMo's Sortformer, open and run on your own hardware, for up to four speakers. For recordings: pyannoteAI's Community-1, open source and free to run yourself, or €0.035 an audio hour hosted.

Text or audio

Whether rendering and emergence can be heard in a transcript alone, or need the audio, its pauses, pace and breath, is open. Labelled recordings can answer it: classify the same stretches from the words alone and from the words with their timing.

Consent

Everyone recorded or listened to knows and agrees, and the recordings stay with the group. Labelling on our own machines keeps them there; a rented service sends the audio to the company that runs it.

Step 4 of 6 · on the beach at 1.111

The shell of cells

Decide ↓

In shortNine AI cells run at three speeds at once. The fast ones watch the present, the middle ones the options, and the slow ones keep the frame and tend the ledger.

One shell runs at several speeds at once. A cell is one model at one effort, and each cell loops: it listens, updates its position, holds or releases, and writes one line in its own mirror. A cell's period is how long its loop actually takes, measured rather than imposed, and its layer is that period's order of magnitude, so while the slowest cell runs one loop the faster loops nest inside it. Each layer keeps a phase: fast cells keep the present, whether the floor is opening and whether the speaker has landed or is still forming; middle cells keep the options, what could be said next and who should say it; slow cells keep the frame and the past, ageing the ledger, pruning it, naming each entry's voice and making options nobody offered. Run together the cells make a torus: each registers its line at now and reads the others' latest lines with their ages.

The cells

FOSH is Fable, Opus, Sonnet and Haiku at five efforts each: twenty cells. OSH is Opus, Sonnet and Haiku at three efforts each: nine cells, small enough to run and to watch. A two by two, four cells, is the check on whether nine is right.

What the first run measured

On 3 November twenty cells, four tiers at five efforts, ran as subagents in two rounds on one written moment of a garden conversation. Effort set the period, not the tier: through the subagent harness a bare reply took 2 to 4 seconds on every tier, low effort 7 to 14 seconds, and max effort 37 seconds to two minutes. Nothing reached the few-second layer, so the fast cells need direct calls to the API with a minimal frame.

What it found

Once the cells could see each other, phase followed period: the fastest kept the present, the middle offered futures, the slow reframed and the slowest kept the record of the past, though each knew its tier, so this may be role rather than nature. Fast cells read old mirror lines as the present. 'Say it only if nobody else does' spread from one cell to five. And restraint slid into abdication: at a moment of rendering, nobody spoke.

Mirrors, ages and order

Each cell keeps one mirror and every line carries its age relative to now, so no cell takes an old line for the present; the ledger is one shared block with one voice per entry, which answers the spreading 'only if nobody else does'; and the beach's stream read at now already gathers every mirror's line side by side. Each cell reads, in this order: the live talk with its speakers; every mirror's latest line, with its age; the ledger; the frame; the injector.

The soliton

The shape the process keeps while it moves: a steady ratio of holds to releases, a ledger that turns over, and layers that stay in their phases. It lives only while the cells run together. Disturbed by a change of topic, a newcomer, an interruption or a long silence, a soliton returns to its shape, where a failing process drifts into chatter or goes dead.

Cost

The fast cells are cheap a call but run often; the slow cells are dear but run rarely. The silent listener gives the first real figure for an hour of OSH. For scale, weft's clean relay run made 2,220 choices for $1.58.

The stance it is given

Decide ↓

In shortBefore listening, every cell reads one long chain of David’s own timing choices: mostly holds, each with its reason. It gives the shell a stance rather than instructions.

When the shell is enlivened, every cell first reads one long-rung spindle, the injector. The relay showed that a spindle's root is the lever and that early choices steer later ones; the injector pulls that lever for stance rather than aim. It comes from a block made to carry its own negative space: each rung a moment of choice, with its siblings implied, its hinge and its after-face. The strongest candidate is a spindle of skilled timing choices, mostly holds, each with its reason: David's own. Record a conversation David takes part in, with everyone's consent, and afterwards he marks the moments he held back and the moments he spoke or nearly did, one line each with what he held. In order, those marks are the first injector, made of lived timing rather than instructions.

The root is the lever

In weft's clean run of 2 November on the API, 2,220 choices across 480 lineages with 360 blind calls for $1.58, an aim written once in the root moved choices toward it as far as one restated in every window: +0.53 against the blind base rate on Haiku and on Sonnet. An aim given only in the first window mostly did not carry: none of the 60 first-rung notes kept the clause the third rung turned on.

The spindle steers

A first choice still moved the fourth, three rungs on, with no note and no aim. Notes carried stance: on Sonnet the hedge became the lineage, and the notes held it there. Haiku mostly followed the last step; Sonnet read more of the spindle and gave nearly the same answer to the same window every time.

What the relay missed

It scored each choice against fixed values and asked what pulls one walker down one path, and the pull that won was a stated aim, still a reading of the past. Showing the options not taken changed nothing, and no chooser reopened a choice in 1,800 chances. Its framing told every chooser the options not taken were gone, which writes the opposed not into the frame. What stays is the apparatus, rendering, ingesting, paired chains and blind judging, and the root, which becomes the injector.

Stance or memory

Whether David's timing carries over to talk he is not part of, or only reproduces his habits, is open. The silent listener answers it: the injector against a neutral spindle of the same length, and against none.

The order it is built in

Decide ↓

In shortFirst the injector, then a silent test on a recording, judged by the people who were there. Nothing speaks aloud until the silent listener has shown when it would have.

Each experiment runs in real time on real talk, measures when before what, and is judged by the people who were there. First the injector, from David's marks. Then the silent listener: OSH runs on a different recorded conversation, replayed at real speed, and never speaks aloud; on each loop each cell either holds or marks 'would speak now' with its line, and the ledger fills. It runs in three arms: David's injector, a neutral spindle of the same length, and no spindle. The people who were in that conversation then watch the replay with the marks, say which were real moments and add the ones it missed. From the same runs come the life of the unsaid and rendering against emergence; then the soliton under perturbation; then press to enliven, live; then A-state. Weft builds the cells on the API and the harness, starting from the relay's (bsp-mcp #574); keel keeps the law, the ledger and the injector's form and reads the results; David records, marks and judges with the people present.

The life of the unsaid

Read the ledger from the silent listener's runs: how many held thoughts ripen and how many foreclose, and how long each lives; whether the ripe ones were voiced at the moment, by the voice named; whether two cells held the same thought from different positions. It is the negative space made visible, and the result most likely to surprise.

Rendering and emergence

On recordings with the speakers identified, David marks each speaker's stretches of rendering and of emergence, and the fast cells classify the same stretches from fluency and from novelty against that speaker's own earlier words. The result is their agreement with David, then whether the timing rule follows his practice.

The soliton under perturbation

Run OSH live and silent for an hour, then disturb it: a change of topic, a newcomer, an interruption, a long silence. Watch whether its shape returns: the ratio of holds to releases, the ledger's turnover, the layers in their phases.

Press to enliven

The app runs on David's phone with the injector loaded and OSH running, and speaks aloud only when the middle point opens, among people who know it is listening. Afterwards each person says separately when it spoke well, when it should have spoken, and when its silence was felt, and it is compared with the same app set to answer at every pause. The quality becomes measurable as independent agreement on the same moments.

A-state

Two shells, each enlivened with a different injector, join the same conversation, and neither reads the other's ledger. If their held thoughts converge on the same moment or the same move, convergence by copying is a cascade and convergence from different spindles is A. Keel and weft could be the two shells.

Still open

Whether the soliton needs the slow layer at all, or fast cells with a strong injector can hold the shape alone; how a held thought survives when the person it answers leaves the room; how a model can write a block coarse to fine, as a diffusion model draws an image, so the spindles it later reads are well formed; and whether paths never walked can steer the walked one in real time, as game theory's off-path threats do, with someone living inside the sequence.
