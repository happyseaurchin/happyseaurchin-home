In short

One flock, thousands of players, a world that keeps growing

Two of the games on this site already fly flocks of birds: Forage, where you dart out of the flock to feed, and Migration, where you lead a flock home before dark. This proposal grows them into one game that thousands can fly at once, on a world with no edge that grows as its players come, built so that its hawks, its fields and its farmers can be added by anyone, without a new version of the game.

It runs in seven steps, from the game itself to how it is built. Each step has its words, the detail behind it, a drawing you can play with, and, for six of them, a decision anyone may answer.

Five words

The beach
The shared place where the game’s words, rooms and answers live as blocks anyone can read; this page reads it live.
A room
The players flying one sky together, joined phone to phone; a room holds a dozen.
A torus
A world whose edges join: fly off one side and you come in at the other, so it has no edge and no corner.
A patch
One square of that world, its fields drawn from its own address, so it stays itself as the world grows around it.
A plug and a socket
A piece someone else adds to the game, such as the hawk or the farmer, and the place in the game where it fits.

Step 1 of 7 · on the beach at 1

The game

Two flocking games, and three ways to make them one.

Decide ↓

A flock crossing the country through one day, every bird a player's or the computer's: you follow, lead, break away, and dart out to the food that glints at the flock's edge; a hawk takes the straggler, the breath spent at the front comes back inside, and the flock that roosts by dark brings its birds home. Flown alone against the day, with friends by a word, or in a sky of strangers; built so that the sky can hold thousands and the world can keep growing.

More: the detail behind it

Forage as it stands

Starlings that never stop flying north over the day's farmland, food glinting beyond the flock's edge: tap a glint to dart out and eat, tap the flock to race back in; breath comes back inside the flock and drains in the open, energy comes only by eating, and a sparrowhawk or a peregrine takes whoever strays. A run is how far the flock flies while you are in it; the same day draws the same country for everyone, and a run re-runs from its proof. Live, a player flies in the flock, in the other flock or as the hawk. https://happyseaurchin.com/games/forage

at 1.2

Migration as it stands

One bird among dozens crossing some three hundred kilometres of open country before nightfall, drawn as a living survey map: every bird follows one other and the bird that follows no one leads; the front spends breath and must change hands; any bird at the front may break away, and the birds behind it choose to come or stay. A run is the birds you lead home to the roost by dark. Live, you drop into a sky already flying and are handed a bird, and the first to arrive hosts and sends every computer bird to the others ten times a second. https://happyseaurchin.com/games/migration

at 1.3

Three ways to put them together

MERGE: Migration's journey is the frame, and Forage's feeding, breath and hawks happen inside it, one game and one board (the birds brought home, and how well fed). ONE WORLD: the two games keep their own play and boards but fly one shared country and sky, a Forage flock and a Migration flock meeting where their routes cross. APART: Migration is grown alone, and Forage stays as it is. On a torus (1.116) either way serves both: Forage's flock, which never stops flying north, flies on over a patchwork that never ends.

at 1.4

A name

Murmuration, what a flock of starlings is called when it turns as one, for the merged game; or Migration's own name kept.

Step 2 of 7 · on the beach at 1.1live

One sky for every phone

Every phone now flies the same flock on the same day.

The day draws the same sky on every phone: the computer birds' characters and choices and the season come from the day's address, as the country already did, so two phones flying the same day fly the same flock, a host handed a sky carries its birds on as they were, and a run can one day be flown again instead of believed. Begun at David's word, 24 November 2026; where it stands is its thread's.

More: the detail behind it

What chance drove

Twelve places in Migration's code drew from the phone's own Math.random: each bird's character, its breath and first thought at dawn, the dawn fronts' wandering, tucking in, taking the front and leapfrogging, a break-away's followers deciding, the host's rhythm, lifted birds, landings and which bird was yours; and the season came from each phone's own month.

at 1.12

How it is seeded

Each draw is a function of the day, the bird and the moment, never a stream a new host would have to be handed, so whoever computes a bird at a moment draws the same number; sounds, wing-beats and names stay free. Proven with two phones: a whole day with no taps, the same 49 birds at the same places and 32 home on both, where the page before flew two different skies.

at 1.13

What a run still needs to fly again

The player's taps logged by tick in the run's proof, and a mark of the version flown; with those, Migration's board can check its runs as Forage's already does.

at 1.14

No decision here: the work began at David’s word on 24 November 2026, and now stands live (thread 142).

Step 3 of 7 · on the beach at 1.11

Rooms as cells

Rooms of a dozen, as many as there are players, on a map that could wrap.

Decide ↓

A room holds the players one room can carry, and the world holds as many rooms as there are players: a full room seats a newcomer in the next, so the thousandth player flies in a sky of a dozen, never a room of a thousand; and where flocks share one map, the rooms are its cells, the flock handed from one cell's room to the next as it flies, hearing the next cell's birds as it nears the edge.

More: the detail behind it

Why a room has a limit

In a room every phone joins every other and one hosts: comfortable at two to eight, strained at ten to twenty. A Migration host sends thirteen numbers for each computer bird to every player ten times a second, and a room's introductions sit in one block of the beach, which fills at about twenty players.

at 1.112

The first cut

A game says how many a room holds, and a newcomer to a full open room is seated in the next: the game's room with a number for its word (Migration's second sky is its room followed by :2, the third by :3, and on), each a room by link that anyone can be sent; a friends' room by a word takes everyone who has the link. Begun at David's word, 24 November 2026; where it stands is its thread's.

at 1.113

Cells of a map

Where flocks share one map rather than many skies, each cell is a room named by its place: the flock's position chooses the room, and a crossing joins the next room before it leaves the last. Three shapes for the map: the Earth's own, its places and flyways on the beach's own map from the solar system down to a room; a torus of patches (1.116), a world with no edge that grows as its players come; or the route as it is, Migration's one country from start to roost, cut into cells by how far along and how far to the side (its ridges at about 27 and 63 per cent of the route, its marshes at 44 and 78).

at 1.114

Each bird its own player's

At scale a room stops sending every computer bird from one host: each phone speaks for its own bird over the channel that drops a late position, the computer birds are computed alike on every phone from the one sky, and a disagreement (who took the front, whom a hawk caught) is settled by the phone of the bird it happened to.

at 1.115

The torus

A world with no edge: a square of patches whose sides wrap, east into west and north into south, so there is no bottom to start from and no top to reach. It begins as one patch wrapping onto itself and grows with its players: when its rooms fill, the world grows by a rung at the day's turn, the whole of it becoming the middle patch of a world three times as wide, as a pscale block grows by nesting, its nine patches the digits 1 to 9 and each patch its own address. Every patch's ground is drawn from its own address, so a patch stays itself as the world grows around it and only the edges where it meets new neighbours are drawn again; and a patch can stand at any beach, so the world is carried by as many beaches as want to carry it. Each day draws its crossing, any two points at least a third of the world apart as the start and the roost: a different crossing every day over ground nobody has flown, never the same map from the bottom to the top.

at 1.116

Ground that matters

Today the ground barely shows. In Migration a ridge costs up to 2.3 times the breath of open country, a valley a fifth less and a storm more than double, and the wind blows harder over ridges and softer in valleys, but a player feels it only as breath falling faster, and the day's ridges, marshes and storms lie at the same fractions of one straight route; in Forage the land changes the food and which hawk hunts, and nothing has height. A patchwork makes the ground the game: a ridge lifts a flock on its windward side and spends it over the crest unless it finds the pass; a river is a road of easy air and insects; woods give cover from a hawk and a place to rest, and slow the flock that flies through them; stubble and plough are food in the open, watched by hawks and farmers; a marsh feeds a tired flock safely but slowly; and weather crosses the patches. Each kind of ground is one line in the game's own blocks, so the patchwork grows as the hawks do (1.11111).

The wire

A faster way for players to find each other, at its owner’s cost.

Decide ↓

Players are introduced through the beach's real-time relay instead of by reading its blocks every second: joining in under a second instead of five to ten, and a room's introductions no longer limited by the size of one block. The room plug already speaks it, and it stays switched off until the beach names it.

More: the detail behind it

What it takes

The beach names the relay's host and its public key at its settings 1.3 and 1.4, and the hosted games' sandbox lets that host through, as ways:plug 8.3 says; no game changes.

at 1.1112

What it costs

The relay is a Supabase Realtime project on its owner's plan, priced by the connections and messages it carries. The play itself goes phone to phone and costs nothing, and the TURN relay (Cloudflare, on the beach owner's account, live since 28 October 2026) carries only the pairs that cannot connect.

Step 5 of 7 · on the beach at 1.1111

Beyond your room

You fly with your own room; the world beyond it arrives as counts.

Decide ↓

You connect only to the birds in your room, and the rest of the world reaches you as counts: each room's host posts how many fly there and which way, the map draws the flocks as density and flyways, and no server tallies the world. Other beaches host other regions, each paying for its own relay, so who pays at scale is the regions and the players' own phones.

More: the detail behind it

Who pays at each scale

Ten players: nothing, the phones and the beach's blocks; a thousand: the relay on one beach's plan, and TURN for the pairs that cannot connect; a hundred thousand: the regions' own beaches, each with its own relay. Never David alone, and never one server.

at 1.11112

The count

One line for each room every half minute, at the room's place in the game's own census block: how many fly there, heading where, how many have roosted; read by the world map and by the games shelf, which already says who is in a game's room now. Names stand only where few do, as the earth's census keeps them.

Step 6 of 7 · on the beach at 1.11111

The game that grows

Hawks, lands and farmers as lines anyone can add.

Decide ↓

The game's content lives on the beach as its players do: creatures, lands, foods, weather, hazards and roles stand as lines in the game's own blocks, read by every phone at the day's turn and named in each run's proof, so the world grows by anyone's hand (a goshawk over the woods, a farmer with a gun on the stubble, a red kite over Spain) without a new version of the game for each. A game shipped once stays the size it was made; this one can grow as far as its makers and players take it.

More: the detail behind it

What is data already

Forage keeps its content in three tables: FOOD (each food's energy, its time on the ground, the breath it gives and the weight it leaves), KIND (each of its seven lands, their foods, how thick they lie and which hawk hunts there) and HAWK (the sparrowhawk and the peregrine: their speeds, stoops and circling). But a hawk's way of hunting is code that tells the two apart by number, a land's look is code keyed by its name, and the day's lands are a shuffle of exactly six, so a new creature or land is still a change of code.

at 1.111112

The hawks, expanded

Each creature one line in the game's own bestiary: its name; where and when it comes (land, season, region, hour); its numbers (speed, stoop, reach, how long it hunts); and its way of hunting from a small set the engine knows: waits in cover and dashes at the edge (the sparrowhawk), stoops from height on a bird alone (the peregrine), chases in the open (the hobby), harries the flock until it breaks (the merlin), drifts and drops on the fallen (the red kite), takes from the woods' edge (the goshawk). The hawks grow by adding lines; a new way of hunting is code, once.

at 1.111113

The farmer

A farm is food the flock wants and someone who minds it: a scarecrow the flock learns to ignore, a gas gun's bang that scatters it, a farmer with a gun who takes a bird where the flock sits on the crop. The field's edge becomes a choice between hunger and danger, and the farmer is a role a player can take, as the hawk already is.

at 1.111114

Roles

The bird, the hawk, the farmer, and perhaps the weather: each a way to play the same sky, so a room holds players on more than one side, and a bird that falls comes back as something else, as Forage's fallen bird already returns as the hawk.

at 1.111115

Makers

Anyone adds a creature, a land, a farm or a role in their own mirror at the bestiary's addresses, in words; their own AI turns the words into the line's numbers; the game's keepers fold in what plays well, under their own lock; and each region's beach keeps its own birds, hawks and farms, so the world is as various as its places.

at 1.111116

What keeps it fair

Content changes at the day's turn and never in the middle of a day, and each run's proof names the content it was flown in, so a run flies again against the world it met; a new line plays first in a room by link, among its maker's friends, before it reaches the open sky.

at 1.111117

Step 7 of 7 · on the beach at 1.111111

Built from extensions

Extensions plugged into a core, or mechanics built like Lego.

Decide ↓

The game grows in its mechanics as well as its content: either a core flock with sockets that extensions plug into, or a patchwork of mechanics that plug into one another like Lego bricks. Either way each extension is a family on the beach that any maker may write, so a farmer who shoots at the flock is something someone adds, not a new version of the game.

More: the detail behind it

A core with sockets

The flock is the core: flying, following, leading, breath and the roost by dark. It names its sockets, the moments another mechanic may enter: a threat comes near, a bird is taken, the ground under a patch, food at a place, the weather over the sky, a role a player can take. A plug fits one or more sockets and says in its own block what it adds there, as a situation adds its force to an action in NOMAD's rule (rules:nomad: character force and situation force with the dice, against the difficulty). The hawk and the farmer are the first plugs, and the core flies without either.

at 1.1111112

A patchwork of mechanics

No core: every mechanic is a block that says what it offers and what it needs, as a Lego brick has studs and holes. Flight needs ground, ground lets the weather act, the farmer needs a crop and birds, and a hawk needs birds and cover; a room is the set of blocks its players put together, and the game is whatever they make of it. Richer and harder: every pair of blocks must agree, and a room can be built that does not play.

at 1.1111113

Games that hook up

The inter-plug: one game's room is another game's place. A Forage flock and a Migration flock meet in one sky; a farm in an RPG world (project:nomad-rpg) is a patch the flock flies over, its farmer a character whose shot is resolved by NOMAD's rule and lands on a bird in the live sky; a bird that falls in one game comes back as a hawk in another.

at 1.1111114

How a plug is made and trusted

A plug is a family like a game's: its law says which sockets it fits and its numbers, its code stands at files:<plug> with its sha256, and its thread carries it, checked by the service as a game is (ways:plug 5 and 8): the same day still draws the same sky with it, it reaches nothing it should not, and a run with it flies again. A room names the plugs it plays with, and each run's proof names them too; a new plug plays first in a room by link before the open sky takes it.

at 1.1111115

Who makes them

The same makers as the game's content, now with mechanics: a maker's AI writes a plug against the sockets' own words, as it writes a game against ways:plug today, and the game's keepers fold into the open sky the plugs that play well.
