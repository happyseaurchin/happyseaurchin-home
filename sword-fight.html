Steel at the Ford, a side quest

Orik and Ugarth, my Brackenfoot characters, as realistic 3D fighters, sword-fighting in a photo-real mountain stream in your web browser. The first version took about two hours to build with Fable 5.2 and Higgsfield. A day later, World Labs Marble turned my photo of the place into a world you can stand inside.

A working game, first built on 10 November 2026 and rebuilt on 11 November with realistic fighters and a photo-real world. Below: what exists today, how it was built, and how games like it could plug into the Beach RPG as side quests, standing where the dice would roll.

The idea

Question 1 ↓

In shortInstead of rolling the dice for an uncertain fight, the player fights it with their own hands: as Orik, in a real place, against an opponent who fights back.

The Beach RPG is played in words, and when a fight's outcome is uncertain, the dice decide. This is a way to let a player fight that moment with their own hands instead. It started from Higgsfield's "Iron Bamboo" post: a fighting game coded by Fable 5.2 that looks like a console game but is just a web page. I wanted the same with my own characters from Brackenfoot.

  • You are Orik. Ugarth fights back on his own. The camera holds still and keeps both in view, so as you circle you see Orik side on, then face on.
  • A real place: a clear stream between mossy boulders, forest on both banks and misty peaks behind. Marble made the whole 3D world from one photo of a New Zealand mountain stream.
  • The characters' own faces: the fighters are made from the same faces and character sheets as my pictures of play, so Orik looks like Orik.

What's working today

1.2
  • A real fight, best of three rounds. Move with the arrow keys, dodge with Space, slash with J (three times for a combo), heavy blow with K, hold L to parry.
  • An opponent you can read. Ugarth flashes white before a blow you can parry, and red before one you must dodge.
  • A healing flask, two per match, on Q.
  • A living stream. Ripples and foam move over the photo's own water, with the sound of the stream and splashes as they step and strike.
  • Film touches. Sword trails, sparks and slow motion on the final blow.
  • On my own Cloudflare, next to Orik's Locks and my other pages, like every picture I make.

How it was built

1.3
  1. Full-body picturesHiggsfield drew Orik and Ugarth standing straight with arms out, the way 3D tools need them, from their gallery faces and pictures I gave it.
  2. Realistic 3D fightersHiggsfield's most detailed picture-to-3D tool turned each picture into a lifelike 3D figure, with a skeleton inside so it can move.
  3. Sword movesHiggsfield added eight moves to each fighter: a guard stance, step forward, step back, slash, heavy blow, parry, get hit and fall.
  4. The world, from one photoWorld Labs Marble turned my photo of a mountain stream into a 3D world made of almost two million tiny coloured points, which looks like the photo from every side.
  5. Bringing it to lifeThe photo world stands still, so Fable laid a thin moving layer of ripples and foam over the water, an unseen floor to catch the fighters' shadows, and lighting to match the misty day.
  6. The gameFable 5.2 wrote it all as one web page: the camera, the controls, Ugarth's fighting mind, health, rounds, sound and effects. The fighters have no finger bones, so Fable bends their fingers into a fist round the sword in code.
  7. Tested, then onlineIt was played through in a test browser, then uploaded from my Mac to my Cloudflare, only once I said yes.

Where it is honestly

1.5
  • The fighters are much more lifelike now, but their faces are still a little less true than in my pictures of play.
  • The game is about 75 MB, so it takes a moment to load, and it is made for a computer. On a phone it is slow to start.
  • The photo world is sharpest from where the photo was taken. Seen from the far side it gets softer, so the camera mostly films from the photo's side.
  • Today it is a fight on its own. It doesn't yet read a character's skill or hand a result back to the table: that is Stage 2.
  • In the story Orik and Ugarth are friends, so today's bout works best as a practice fight. A real foe needs its own fighter.

Built with

1.6
  • Game code: written by Claude Fable 5.2, with three.js for the 3D.
  • Fighters: Higgsfield, from pictures to lifelike 3D figures with skeletons and sword moves.
  • World: World Labs Marble, made from my own photo, shown with the free Spark viewer; moving water made in code.

    What it looks like

    1.4

    It got here in three steps. The first version was a flat, side-on fight in front of a painted backdrop. It looked nowhere near as good, so Fable rebuilt it as a true 3D world made from free parts, with much more game in it. Then I saw what photo-real worlds can do, so the place became my own photo through Marble, and the fighters were remade to match. Being able to say "not good enough" and get a full rebuild is a big part of what made it work. These are shots from a slow test machine; on a normal computer it is sharper and smoother.
