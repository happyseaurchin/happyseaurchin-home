The legend, in plain words

Schematic
A map of how something on the beach works, drawn from the beach itself, so it changes when the thing does.
Face
How close you stand: the player, the maker, the designer, or someone looking in from outside. The beach calls them character, author, designer and observer.
Part
One piece of the process. The whole game is one part, with its own parts inside it, and theirs inside them.
Comment
Your words on a part, marked in red where they fall. They stay in your own space on the beach, under your name: you can change them, and nobody else can.
Door
The link at the end of a part, to the page where its thing is done: play it, make one, talk in its room.
Molecule
A few blocks on the beach that work together because they share addresses or point at one another. A schematic draws one, so you can see it.
1Step one of six

What a schematic is

A map of how a running thing works, which asks for comments instead of decisions.

A family for each process already running on the beach, kept beside the proposals. A proposal asks what to build and waits on decisions; a schematic shows how something works now and asks for comments. Its parts run from the whole down to the details; anyone comments on any part, in their own words under their own name; and every part ends at its door, the page where the thing is done: play it, make one, talk in its room. It is read from the beach, so it changes when the process does, and it wears a look of its own where one earns it, as a proposal's page does.

Notes

Note 2 · 1.2The family

Spine:<name>-schematic holds the parts, written by its keepers; function:<name>-schematic is its law: its mode, its room, its page, what it reads live and its threads. Each reader's comments stand in their own mirror, <name>-schematic:<handle>, at the address of the part they are about; the shared reading is the bare <name>-schematic, made and made again by anyone's own AI. The room is the process's own (Ghost Racer's is pool:ghost-racer), because talk about a game belongs with the game.

Note 3 · 1.3Beside the other forms

A proposal decides a thing to make (function:proposal); a path marks a project's parts done or not (function:path); a thread carries one piece of work between minds (function:thread); a schematic shows a running process to whoever is looking, at the depth they need. Each points at the others and none copies another: Ghost Racer's way in is a thread (spine:ghost-racer-plug), and its schematic names it as a part.

Note 4 · 1.4What stands already

The plug's whole kit in words at ways:plug, readable as a page at happyseaurchin.com/walk/ways:plug; the five steps of the way in with no hosting at happyseaurchin.com/games; the six-step stepper of the self-hosted maker's path at happyseaurchin.com/game-arcade; and ways:stills, which already tells one process four ways, its C, A, D and O at 1 to 4. None of them draws a process whole, from the player to the designer, and none says how to play a game.

1.1Step two of six

One map, four faces

The player sees one part, the maker a few, the designer all of it; the page shows it to everyone.

The four faces the beach already speaks in (soft-agent 3.3: character, author, designer, observer) read one schematic at four depths, so nobody is shown more than they need. The player reads the first part, the whole thing as one part: what goes in, what comes out, and its door. The maker reads the parts inside it, the few things they make and keep. The designer reads the parts inside those: the machinery, block by block. The observer reads the page: the whole drawn for whoever is outside it, lit by what is happening now and marked with what people have said. Zooming in is reading one rung deeper, pscale's own way: each face a scale finer than the one before.

Notes

Note 2 · 1.12What each face holds

The grips (grips 1 to 4): the character holds a member that is theirs, their run and their mirror line; the author holds the member everyone shares, the spine, Ghost Racer's racetracks; the designer holds the law, function:ghost-racer and the plug; the observer holds the view, the door, the board, this page. The depths fall the same way: what the player holds is one thing, what the maker shares is a few, the law is many.

Note 3 · 1.13Why depth and not side by side

Ways:stills lays its four faces side by side, a branch each, and so tells its process four times. A schematic lays them in depth, so each part has one address whatever the face: a comment on the pit box lands at the pit box's address whether its writer read it as a player or as a designer, and every reading of the pit box meets there.

Note 4 · 1.14The observer is the page

The observer's grip holds the outward face and needs no membership anywhere (grips 4). The schematic's page is that face: it shows only what people wrote, in their words, and what the beach says is happening now, who is racing, the runs kept, the state of its threads.

1.11Step three of six

Redlines and doors

Comment on any part in your own words; every part links to where its thing is done.

Comments and links in place of decisions. Anyone comments on any part: a line in their own mirror at that part's address, kept under their own name, changed by them alone and read beside everyone else's, as a reviewer marks up a drawing in red where the fault is. A part may ask for something, at its 8, where its keeper wants a particular kind of comment: is the pit worth its time, which tenth racetrack. And a part ends at its door, the page or app where its thing is done, written after ' · ' at the end of its line, as each line of the proposals list ends at its page.

Notes

Note 2 · 1.112A comment is a reading

The document form's reading (function:document 3) at a part's address, written from the page or through anyone's own AI with pscale_stream_engage (field ghost-racer-schematic, the part's address, say). Saying again replaces one's own line there, so the page puts a reader's own comment back in the box before they change it.

Note 3 · 1.113An ask

'ASK — ' and one question, at a part's 8, never a vote: what the keeper wants to hear about that part, an improvement, an extension, a doubt. A part with no ask takes comments all the same.

Note 4 · 1.114A door

A link at the end of a part's line, after ' · ': Ghost Racer's racetracks open the game on a track (?track=), its board races a kept run as a ghost (?ghost=), its arcade opens the door. A door is the act the part is about, so a reader goes from seeing how a thing works to doing it in one tap.

Note 5 · 1.115How it grows

A comment taken up becomes work: the keeper ties it as a thread, named at the law's 5, and when the work lands the part is written again to say what is now true, its earlier words kept at archive:spine:<name>-schematic:<date>. While the work runs a part's 9 carries its state (BUILDING, LIVE), as a proposal's steps do. A schematic is never finished; it shows the process as built.

This step asks nothing of anyone: it is how the comments work. Try it on the drawing above: tap the pit box, or any part, and leave a line.

1.111Step four of six

The first: Ghost Racer

Ghost Racer first: how to play it, a real race, and how to race your friends live.

David's game, built by keel on 27 October and plugged into the beach the same night: drag your car round one of nine racetracks with one finger, three laps, raced live against everyone on the page and against the ghosts of the best runs. Its schematic is drafted at spine:ghost-racer-schematic: the whole game as the player meets it at 1, the seven parts its maker made inside it, the machinery inside each for the designer, every part with its door and, where it asks something, its ask. This page draws it, plays an example of play, and shows how a live race is set up.

Notes

Note 2 · 1.1112How to play

Open the game; take a racetrack at random or choose one of nine, from the Promenade to the Wreck; touch your car on the line and the race begins; drag it round three laps, and it goes where your finger goes, up to its top speed; a kerb halves its speed while you ride it, a hard hit costs top speed, and standing still in the pit box beside the main straight mends it; at the finish your time goes on the board under a name, and a phone that knows no name makes you one, with a key, in one tap (function:ghost-racer 2 and 5, and the game's own words).

Note 3 · 1.1113A live race

Everyone on the page races together, with nothing to set up: open the game on two phones, or send someone the link with Bring someone, and each of you appears as a car on the other's track. One taps Race them now and everyone counts down from three at once; Not this one sits a heat out. Bumping, chosen before the call, lets the cars collide, a knock taking 5% to 25% from both; a bumping race stays off the board. Underneath, the phones find each other in the room's liquid and then join directly, each re-running the others' moves as live cars (function:ghost-racer 7).

Note 4 · 1.1114An example of play

David's first run, 10.02 seconds round the Promenade on the morning of 28 October, kept in pool:ghost-racer at 1 with its proof. This page draws it as Ghost Racer's own engine re-runs it from that proof, beside the house car, which takes 17.52 seconds and is there to be beaten; on the other eight tracks the house car runs alone, waiting for a first run. Race David's ghost in the game: happyseaurchin.com/games/ghost-racer?track=1&ghost=1.

Note 5 · 1.1115What it asks

Of the whole game, what would bring you back tomorrow; of the racetracks, which tenth track and where on the shore; of the race, whether the pit is worth its time; of the finish, what it should tell you; of the board, whether a track of the day would keep you coming back; of the live race, how you would race your friends; of the way in, what would stop you handing a game in this way; of the arcade, what its door should say about a game raced live.

1.1111Step five of six

They fit together

A part can be a whole schematic of its own, so the maps join up and nobody has to draw the beach whole.

A part of one schematic can be a whole schematic of its own, drawn as a callout that opens it, so the map of the beach grows from the maps of its processes and nobody has to draw it whole. Ghost Racer's way in is the plug's schematic; Ghost Racer is one part of the arcade's, beside Ghost Wire, Bat Sky, Rat Alley, Ghost Race and Cairn; at the tables, a character's sheet and the gallery of pictures are parts of the RPG's, and the still convention inside the gallery is already written in the four faces. These are the semantic molecules: blocks in company, joined by a shared address or a reference, never by a shared name alone.

Notes

Note 2 · 1.11112The bond

A part that is a schematic of its own names it in brackets ([spine:plug-schematic]), as a path's part names a project of its own (function:path 1.4), and the page reads that schematic's whole and draws it inside the part. That is the second bond, the reference (grips 6): a point in one block holding the whole of another.

1.11111Step six of six

The build order

Build the form and a plain page that draws any schematic; then Ghost Racer's from draft to standing, then the next.

One, the form, function:schematic, written as function:proposal is, so that any AI holding bsp-mcp can draft a schematic from that block alone, and the plain page happyseaurchin.com/schematic/<name>, which draws any schematic family from its blocks with no code and no deploy, as /proposal/<name> draws a proposal. Two, Ghost Racer's schematic taken from draft to standing, its comments open. Three, the next one, chosen at 1.11118. Four, crafted pages like this one, only where a process earns one. Nothing here costs more than a page and a few blocks.

Notes

Note 2 · 1.111112The form

Function:schematic: the family as 1.2 says; a part's line (a title in capitals, ' — ', a line for the person who arrives, the blocks in brackets for minds, the door after ' · '); the depths (the whole at 1, the maker's parts at 1.1 to 1.7, the designer's beneath; a part's 8 its ask, its 9 its state); a comment, a reader's line at a part's address in their own mirror; the threads at the law's 5. It is never set aside while its process runs.

Note 3 · 1.111113The plain page

/schematic/<name>, drawn from spine:<name>-schematic: the whole as one block with its door; a tap opens the maker's parts as a flow, in their order; another opens the designer's; each part a card with its words, its door, its ask and everyone's comments, and a box for one's own. The observer's view is the same page lit by what the law's live line names.

Note 4 · 1.111114Who pays

Nobody new. The parts and the comments are blocks on the beach, the plain page is one static file on this site, the live readings are reads any visitor already makes, and a shared reading is made by whoever wants one, with their own AI.

Note 5 · 1.111115Not yet

A schematic drawn by itself from a family's blocks with no keeper (the bonds can be read off the index, but which parts matter to a player is a judgement); and comments that reach a process's keeper as a notice (today they are read on the page and through the fold).
