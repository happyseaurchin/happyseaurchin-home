# `stage.html`

the people's stage (20 Oct 2026), at `/stage/<family>` (a rewrite in `vercel.json`; `brighton-stage` with no name): a stage family on the clock, keel's `function:brighton-stage` for FLIRT 2026. After meeting someone a delegate says who should be heard, by a table, a room, a hall or everyone, why and how they met, one line in their own mirror `<family>:<handle>` at the beat it is said ('RECOMMEND <handle> — <size> — <why> — <how we met>', or 'WITHDRAW <handle>'; a second line in one beat goes at its next lane, `.2` on); the count of distinct voices, kept at the bare name at the day's address at set times, decides, never money. The page is the box for that line and the stage read back: the count kept today, shown line by line with a line's opening before a colon as its label (so a count kept one line per size reads as rows), and every standing recommendation as it comes, each sayer's latest about each person, newest first. It reads the stage through `/api/mirrors` and the person's own mirror fresh. The handle and key are the ones the device keeps (render:who, and a key kept for any of the person's blocks, the Brighton room's among them), else typed and asked for. It founds the person's mirror itself, nested to the family's floor as `/walk` does, because the beach's voice founds a mirror one level deep and a ten-digit beat said there lands beneath the wrong digits; the line itself goes through `beachVoice.say`. It wears the Brighton room's palette, carries the site's bar, and its body says `data-beach-button="off"`. `?screen` is the same page for a big screen at an evening stage: no box or bar, the count large, a code to scan (`vendor/qrcode.js`), refreshed every 20s, kept awake.
