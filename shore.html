Shore

Money given, so that people give others experiences. A shore, not a bank.

Money given so that people give others experiences, in any form: a game, a meal, food grown, a lesson, a repair, a service. Anyone may put money in, and it is never owed back. Someone who has given others experiences asks to be rewarded; the keeper checks the accounting, their experience points and the weightings, and pays them by hand; and every sum in and every reward paid is written in the open. A shore, not a bank (David: 'Shore to contrast with a river's Bank'): a bank holds money and owes it back, and a shore is where what the sea brings lands. In time Stripe pays as the keeper's check says, and an AI may carry the step at the keeper's word. Read down 1; ask, offer or answer in your own mirror, shore:<your handle>; talk in pool:shore.

It works now, by hand. Ask to be rewarded for what you gave, or offer money, under your own name on the beach. The keeper, David, checks each ask and pays it, and every sum stands in the ledger for anyone to read.

In plain words

Six things to know

  • ShoreMoney given so that people give others experiences. Nobody is owed it back, and it is never anyone's deposit.
  • An experienceAnything one person gives another: a game, a meal, vegetables grown, a repair, a lesson, a walk, on the beach or anywhere else.
  • The keeperThe person who reads each ask, checks it and pays it by hand. David, to start.
  • Experience pointsWhat people did that the beach can check, counted in four columns: taking part, bringing others, making, and the beach that carries it all.
  • WeightingsHow each sum that comes in is shared among the four columns and the people in them. The columns stand equal until the people decide together.
  • The ledger and your mirrorThe ledger, ledger:shore, holds one line for each sum in and each reward paid. Your mirror, shore:<your handle>, is your own block, written only by you, where your ask, your offer and your answers stand.
How a reward happens

Four moves

  1. Someone gives moneyAnyone. The keeper writes the sum in the ledger.
  2. Someone gives an experienceTo anyone, in any form, on the beach or off it.
  3. They ask to be rewardedIn their own mirror: what they gave and to whom, and when they need it.
  4. The keeper checks and paysAgainst their share in the accounts, by hand, and writes it in the ledger.

Shore

Decide ↓

In shortMoney is given so that people give others experiences. Nobody is owed it back: the people who give experiences ask, and the keeper rewards them by hand.

Given, never owed back. David: 'We're giving them money so that they provide people with experiences in any form -- outwith the app, in the real world, growing food, providing a service, etc etc.' Money comes in as a gift towards experiences and goes out as rewards to the people who give them. Nothing in Shore is anyone's deposit and nobody is owed a balance: what people did is counted as experience points, and money is paid out by hand when someone asks. Shore is for experiences on the beach, such as a game, a proposal or an answer, and for experiences anywhere else.

A shore, not a bank1.2

A bank keeps money and owes it back when asked, which in the UK only authorised banks, building societies and credit unions may do. Shore keeps nothing it owes: money given is given, and a reward is paid for experiences given to others. That keeps it clear of the law on deposits, though not of tax, since a reward may be income for the person who receives it. The thinking that came before stands at spine:commons-bank, set aside.

Any experience, any form1.3

A game played, a proposal made, a question answered, a meal cooked, vegetables grown and shared, a bike mended, a lesson, a walk, a bed for the night. What counts is that someone gave another person an experience. On the beach the acts are counted as they happen; off it, the giver says what they gave and the keeper checks.

What it never does1.4

It never lends, never marks anything up, and never pays out on its own. It holds only money given, less rewards paid. How Money Piles Up (happyseaurchin.com/animations/how-money-piles-up) shows where lending and marking up lead.

Anyone may put money in, for any reason. To start, it goes to the keeper by arrangement, by bank transfer or by hand, and the keeper writes each sum in the open record, ledger:shore: how much, when, what it is for if the giver names it, and the giver's name only if they wish. As David answered at Experience Points: 'privacy breeds suspicion. And so I think transparency is key.' Every sum stands in the open, even where a giver's name does not.

Saying you would give1.12

A line in your own mirror at 1.18 says you would put money in, how much and what it is for; the keeper answers with how to send it, in the room or privately. A line is never money until the keeper writes the sum in the ledger.

For what, if you like1.13

Money may be named for a point (one thing's makers), a system (one thing's four columns: those who took part, those who brought them, its makers and the beach) or the whole ecosystem, and is rewarded within what it was named for. Money named for nothing goes to the whole (spine:commons-bank 1.11).

step 4 of 7 · on the beach at 1.111

The keeper's check

The asks ↑

In shortBefore paying, the keeper reads your record: your experience points, the weightings, and what came in. Your ask is paid from your own share, never from everyone's.

The keeper, David to start, reads the asker's record before paying (David: 'then I check our accounting XP and weightings and then give them money for contributing'). The accounting is their experience points in four columns, taking part, bringing others, making, and the beach, counted from acts the beach can check (spine:experience-points); the weightings that share each sum among the columns and the people in them; and what came in for what they gave. For experiences given off the beach, the keeper reads what the asker says they gave, and what the people who received it say. Then the keeper decides a sum and pays it.

The accounting1.1112

Each sum that comes in is shared in the accounts the moment it lands, by experience points and the weightings, so a person's share stands before they ask, and the ask is when it is paid out (David at Experience Points: 'credits are distributed IMMEDIATELY according to xp'). Shares are read, never kept as a balance. The four columns stand equal until the collective decides their weights (David at spine:experience-points 1.8: WEIGHTED, 'determined by collective decision'). The reader that works a family's four columns out from what stands on the beach waits at happyseaurchin-home #822.

What the check guards1.1113

One large weight could otherwise empty Shore (David: 'when a person's xp is large and they keep removing they can drain the entire collective'). The keeper pays an ask against the asker's own share of what came in, less what they were paid before, never against the whole.

An AI to help1.1114

The keeper's own AI can read the beach and lay out an asker's record for the check: their acts, their columns, what they were paid before. It proposes a sum; the keeper decides.

step 5 of 7 · on the beach at 1.1111

Paid by hand, in the open

The ledger ↑

In shortThe keeper pays you by hand, then writes the payment in the open ledger. What Shore holds is money in, less rewards paid.

The keeper pays the reward by bank transfer or by hand, then writes it in ledger:shore: to whom, how much and what for. Nobody's balance is kept anywhere: what Shore holds is read from the lines, money in less rewards paid, and what anyone has been paid is the sum of their lines. As David answered at Experience Points: 'xp are transparent. Payments should be too.'

The ledger1.11112

Ledger:shore holds one line for each sum in, 'IN — £<amount> · from <a name, or a giver> · for <what>', and one for each reward, 'PAID — £<amount> · to <handle> · for <what they gave>'. It is latched to weft's key and written only at the keeper's word, the keeper's AI carrying the step (David: 'It might be facilitated by an LLM'), until the keeper takes it under a key of his own. Anyone reads it, and the beach stamps each line with its time. A line is never rewritten: a mistake is put right by a new line with a minus sum.

Tax and receipts1.11113

A reward may be income that the person who receives it declares. The ledger's line is the receipt, and the keeper can send one by email too.

step 6 of 7 · on the beach at 1.11111

Later: Stripe, and an AI at the keeper's word

Decide ↓

In shortWhen there are many rewards, Stripe pays them and an AI can do the work, always at the keeper's word.

When rewards are many, Stripe pays as the keeper's check says (David: 'Basically a manual step where the Stripe Connect will take over at some point. It might be facilitated by an LLM'). Two ways stand in 2026. Global Payouts sends money from the keeper's own Stripe account to a person's bank account, debit card or wallet, the person giving only a few details by email; businesses in the UK and the US can use it. Connect checks each person once, under Stripe's licence, and pays into their own account. And Stripe now lets a business's own AI send payouts through Stripe's own connector, within limits the business sets and with a person approving each one by default (a public preview). So an AI can carry the whole step: read the ask, lay out the record, propose a sum, pay at the keeper's word and write the line. It never pays on its own.

Which way1.111112

Global Payouts suits paying people directly from one's own money. It needs Stripe's Treasury account, and Stripe notes that whoever manages other people's funds may need a licence of their own. Connect suits paying many people out of payments taken, its checks covered by Stripe's licence. Either needs a qualified read before it pays real people.

The bank, later1.111113

What the commons bank worked out (spine:commons-bank) stands for the day the people whose money it is decide together: steps out that need more people to agree, whether weight leaves with money taken out, what a person is shown, and land bought together.
