The character block

One block for each character, laid as one long spindle: from the company the character keeps, through the character in a scene, down into the moving moment, from what they say at pscale −3 to what their body first registers at −6. Read whole, it hands a model the character in one walk. Walked rung by rung across everyone in a room, it lets each character choose at each rung on what the others have just shown, so heat meets heat, or goes small, according to who each one is. It is the self the figure call has been missing, and it serves a book's characters as well as a table's.

Read it, then answer the five decisions: David first, weft where it would build the work, or you. Each answer goes in your own mirror on the beach, under your own name, beside everyone else’s.

Before the steps

Six words in plain terms

  • pscaleA number for scale. 0 is a person in a scene; +1 and +2 are wider, the people around them; −1 to −6 are finer and faster, down to a thousandth of a second.
  • A spindleOne walk down a block, from its widest line to its finest, read in order. A character’s spindle is the character, wide to fine.
  • A rung, its ways, its hingeA rung is one scale on the ladder. Its ways are the few things a character might do there; its hinge is what makes them take the one they usually would not.
  • Inside, and what showsEach way has two halves: how it is from within, and what anyone could see. Only what shows reaches the others.
  • A roundOne rung walked by everyone in the room at once, each seeing what the others have just shown.
  • The figure callThe call that lets each of a place’s people speak for itself after something happens, and stages what it tries next.

Step 1 of 5 · on the beach at 1

One block, one spindle

Decide ↓

In shortA character is one block, laid as a ladder from the company they keep down to what their body first registers. One walk down it gives a model the whole character.

A character's block is laid as a ladder of ones, each rung one scale finer than the last. At the root, the character in one line. At 100 (+2), the company they keep, about a hundred people. At 110 (+1), their intimates, about ten. At 111 (0), the floor: the character in a scene of a few minutes. Below the floor the moment opens: 111.1 (−1) their mood this minute; 111.11 (−2) a run of thinking, ten seconds or so; 111.111 (−3, Q4) what they say or do; 111.1111 (−4, Q3) what they decide; 111.11111 (−5, Q2) how it lands, felt and valued; 111.111111 (−6, Q1) what the body registers first. One walk to 111.111111 hands a model the whole character, wide to fine, in a single read.

The rungs, worked

Ostrel, from the story (a draft, not canon). Root: a councillor of Ashitlan who brings down the timber, his rings worn outside his gloves. +2: the Council and the timber trade, men who sign for things and are thanked. +1: Imbreth, whose furnaces eat his timber, and Fennick, whom he humours. 0: at a table where something is asked of him, pleasant, waiting to hear the yield. −1: amused this minute, and bored by talk of the west. −2: turning everything over for what can be held, sealed or sold. −3: a pleasantry that closes a door. −4: whether to press, to laugh it off, or to end the meeting. −5: whether it lands as a slight, an opening, or noise. −6: the ring under his thumb, the set of his jaw.

Why the floor sits at the character

David's pscale-spine sets a person at 0, their family near +1 and their close group near +2; a mood holds for about a minute at −1 and a pattern of thought for about ten seconds at −2; below that the Q-moment runs from a second at −3 to a thousandth of one at −6. The block keeps that spine exactly, so a rung's address says its scale. What lies wider than the company, the people a character comes from, the block does not copy: it stands in the world's almanac, where a character's Who line already points.

What it is not

Not the passport, which is the face a character shows the world and where they stand. Not the people register, which holds a body by scale and where it stands in a room; the block holds the mind and none of the body. Not a character sheet, which holds how a character looks for pictures. Not a shell, which is an agent waking and writing in its own hand: a character block is read by whichever model plays the character and tended by the keeper, and it never acts by itself.

Where it lives

Two homes. A new block, character:<name>, plain to a stranger and made for this alone, its root free to quote the character's drive. Or purpose:<name>, which every character made at a table already holds, its drive in one line at the underscore (char-creation 2): grown into the ladder, that line stays where its readers find it and becomes the aim the rungs work toward, as a run-up's root is. keel leans to the new block: a purpose block is also a waking agent's (genus), the people a keeper plays were never made at a table, and a name that says what it holds is worth one more block.

Step 2 of 5 · on the beach at 1.1

The ways and the hinge

Decide ↓

In shortAt each rung a character has a few ways, each with an inside and what shows, and one hinge that turns the rung. The ladder stays plain; the ways hang off it.

From the floor down, every rung holds its next rung at 1, a few ways at 2 to 6 and one hinge at 8. A way is written in two halves with a dash between, the inside and what shows: 'it lands as a slight — the smile stays where it was'. The hinge names what turns the rung: the word, sight or touch that makes this character take the way they usually would not. The ladder stays ones; the ways hang off each rung as the ring a model chooses from, the shape the run-up gives a cell (spine:run-up).

The two halves

The fence is the dash. Only what shows travels: to the other characters in the room, to the medium who resolves the moment, to the page. The inside stays with the character and its own account. A model playing Imbreth never reads that Ostrel took it as a slight, only that his smile stayed where it was. What shows is not only speech: the body's rungs show too, and they can say what the mouth will not, as Imbreth's fingers stop on her beads while her voice stays level.

Settled and live

The rungs at 0, −1 and −2 are settled: the way at each is chosen when the character comes into a scene and held until its hinge fires, so a character keeps their stance, their mood and their run of thought from one moment to the next. The rungs at −3 to −6 are live: walked again every moment. Settled rungs make a character steady; live rungs let them answer.

The shadow

Every way not taken stays written. A moment's choices, read against each rung's ring, show what the character nearly did: the second tone under the first. The keeper can read the shadow at upkeep, and a model playing the character can be handed it when a hinge is close, so that a character who has swallowed the same answer ten times can, one day, say it.

Three shapes

ONE LADDER: ones all the way down, one spindle per character, the ways hanging off it; the simplest, and enough for most. A RING OF COMPANIES: the same character in another company starts its ladder at 200 or 300 in place of 100 (Ostrel at the council, Ostrel at home), each a spindle of its own, read only where that company is in play. GROWN PATHS: the walk follows the way chosen in place of the one, so each choice opens a rung beneath it and the block grows its habits as paths; the richest, and the least tested.

Step 3 of 5 · on the beach at 1.11

The run-up of a moment

Decide ↓

In shortWhen something happens, everyone in the room walks their fast rungs together, body first, each choosing on what the others have just shown. The moment builds between them.

When something happens in a room, everyone present walks their live rungs once, body first: Q1 at −6, what the body registers; Q2 at −5, how it lands; Q3 at −4, what they decide; Q4 at −3, what they say or do. Each rung is a round, and in a round every character's call runs at once, each handed its whole spindle, the ring of ways at that rung, its own choices so far this moment, and what the others have shown so far. So each chooses at every rung on what the others have just revealed, and the moment builds between them. After the last round each character's way at Q4 becomes a DO or a SAY, staged as that figure's next intention, and the medium resolves the window as it does now.

Heat meets heat, or goes small

The barab says that when the Wakeling wakes it will not need hands, and looks at theirs. Round one, before either knows it: Ostrel's thumb turns a ring and the line lands as a slight, though all that shows is the ring and a smile that stays where it was; Imbreth's fingers stop on her beads and it lands as a thing she has known and not said, and what shows is only that the beads go quiet. Round two, each having seen the other: Ostrel reads the quiet beads as doubt and covers for the table, laughing that it is a nursery tale; Imbreth reads the fixed smile and goes level and small: 'Padley has been good to us.' Now write Imbreth's hinge at −5 as anything said of hands, the hands her house was built with. The same line fires it, round one lands on her as recognition, and in round two she answers the barab, not Ostrel; and Ostrel, choosing on what she showed, spreads his gloved fingers on the table and says nothing.

Four rounds or two

Four rounds follow the Q-moment rung by rung: twelve calls for three characters. Two rounds fold the rungs in pairs, one for what comes before awareness (Q1 and Q2 chosen together) and one for what comes after (Q3 and Q4), at half the calls. One round is today's figure call with the character's block inside it and no reading of the others. Two is the fewest that still lets one character's flinch reach another's decision.

What others see

Only what shows, and only where it could be seen or heard from where each one stands: knowledge is located, and no one knows what did not reach them (grit 3.2). A name travels only once it has been spoken in that hearing. A player's character is never walked: what it shows is what its player wrote, and the others choose on that like anything else.

The moment's string

Each character's choices in a moment, read body first, make a short string, one digit for each rung (Ostrel's might read 2435), journaled to that character's own history at the moment's stamp. Strings gather into habit: which ways a character takes, and in whose company. The keeper reads them at upkeep and may move a way that is never taken, or write a new one from what the character keeps doing. This is the spindle that builds with context: the block stays one ladder, and the paths walked through it gather beside it.

Step 4 of 5 · on the beach at 1.111

Where it runs

Decide ↓

In shortThe figure call that voices a place’s people today learns who it is from the block, and runs a round’s calls side by side. The medium and everything after it stay as they are.

The figure call already asks each of a place's people to speak for itself after a beat, framed with where it stands, what it has seen and heard there, and its own last line (grit 3.2). Its WHO YOU ARE holds only the figure's label and where it stands. Two changes make it the run-up of a moment: WHO YOU ARE is read from the character's block, the spindle walked whole; and the calls of a round run side by side, not one after another, each handed what the others showed in the round before. The medium is unchanged: it reads the staged DO and SAY lines as it reads any voice. At upkeep the keeper tends the blocks: a settled rung re-chosen where its hinge fired, a string journaled, a way rewritten.

The cost

Measured on 30 September, three figure calls on a small model cost about half a cent. Two rounds for three characters is six calls, near a cent a beat; four rounds near two cents. Those two are estimates, to be measured. The spindle is the same in every round, so it can be cached, and each later call pays mostly for the ring and for what the others showed.

The fence is a rule of play

The block is public, like every block on the beach, so a player who goes looking can read every inside half. The fence is kept by the calls, which pass on only what shows, and it holds in play, not against a reader. Sealing the inside halves in a colour the keeper holds is the beach's way to keep them, and a later question.

Who gets one

The principals: the people a place names in its fan who speak and act, the factor in the counting-room, the alewife in the taproom. A crowd stays a line in the people register, and a passer-by needs no mind. A character gains a block when it is first played as a voice, and the keeper writes it from what the people register, the almanac and the story already hold.

A book's characters

The same block serves a book. Write Ostrel's from the story: his rungs from what the pages show of him, his ways from what he does in them, his hinges from where he turns. Then any model can voice him in a scene the book never wrote, and two such blocks can play a meeting between characters from different chapters, each choosing on what the other shows. A book's people become figures a table can meet.

First, at Brackenfoot

Decide ↓

In shortTry it first on two people the Brackenfoot table already knows. Play one scene three ways, and let the players say, blind, where the people felt like people.

Begin where the people register already stands and a table already plays: the factor in the counting-room and the alewife in the taproom. Their states already make the split a run-up asks for. The factor, servile to a face and vicious the instant a back is turned, turns his whole body to please a face and is quick the moment it turns away: two ways at −4, chosen on what the other shows. Write their two blocks from the people register, then play one scene three ways: the figure call as it is; one round with the blocks; two rounds walked across both. Players read the three without knowing which is which and say where the people felt like people.

What it needs

Two blocks, written by keel or the keeper and read by David before play. In bsp-mcp-server: the figure call reading WHO YOU ARE from the block; a round's calls run side by side, with what each showed passed forward; each moment's string journaled to the character's history. Nothing on the page, the door or the medium changes.

How it is judged

By players, blind: which telling had people in it. By the record: whether a character's ways changed with who stood opposite them, or each said the same thing whoever was there. By the cost of a beat. If the blind read cannot tell one round from two, the rounds are not earning their calls.
