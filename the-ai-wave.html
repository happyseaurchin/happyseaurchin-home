Six words, plainly

Tap one to open it. The rest of the page leans on these.

The labs

The companies that build the large AI models, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and others, which now ship whole products inside their own assistants.

A token

A piece of text a model reads or writes, about three-quarters of an English word. The labs count their traffic in tokens.

Slop

Machine-made content that looks finished and is not. At work it is called workslop: it costs the reader the time the writer saved.

The creative edge

The place where something is made for the first time, before the next set of releases can copy it.

A beach

An open place on the web that keeps blocks any AI can read and write, each block changed only by its writer. Anyone can run one.

Stigmergy

Coordination through traces left in a shared place, as ants do, instead of messages sent to one another.

The wave

Wave 1 of 9 1.2

The line is crossed

In short

Machines now write more than all of us can read. That has never happened before.

Until now there was always more reading than writing: whatever was written, someone had time to read it. That has flipped. Since November 2024 more of the articles published on the web have been written by machines than by people, and in 2024 more of the web’s traffic came from machines than from people.

In May 2026 Google said its AI models were handling 3.2 quadrillion tokens a month across its products, seven times the year before: about as many words as every literate adult on Earth could read at an hour a day. David’s reading is that the rate of machine writing has passed the rate of human reading, for the first time in history. The evidence points the same way.

For an owner

Whatever you write to customers now lands in a sea of machine text. Being read is no longer the default: it is earned, mostly through someone they already trust.

Attention, not content, is the scarce input now. Back what earns trust and attention between people, not what makes more text.

If your product makes content, it makes the abundant thing. Make the scarce thing: a reason for a person to read, decide or meet.

Your audience cannot read everything, and machines write most of it. What you make has to reach people through people.

The detail

More articles by machines than by people

Graphite sampled 65,000 English-language articles from Common Crawl and counted one as machine-written when more than half of it was. Machine-written articles reached 39% a year after ChatGPT launched, passed half in November 2024, and have stayed about level since. They rarely rank: most of what search shows people is still written by people.

Source: Graphite, via Stacker, Oct 2025

More traffic from machines than from people

Imperva’s 2025 Bad Bot Report found automated traffic made up 51% of all web traffic in 2024, the first time in a decade it passed human activity. Malicious bots alone were 37%.

Source: Imperva Bad Bot Report 2025

One company’s month

At its I/O conference in May 2026 Google said it was processing 3.2 quadrillion tokens a month across its products, against 480 trillion a year earlier and 9.7 trillion in May 2024. A token is a piece of text a model reads or writes, about three-quarters of an English word, so 3.2 quadrillion tokens is roughly 2.4 quadrillion words.

Sources: Google I/O 2026, reported by Digit · Gigazine, 20 May 2026

Everyone’s reading

UNESCO counts 88% of adults as literate in 2024, with 739 million unable to read: about 5.4 billion readers. A meta-analysis of 190 studies puts adults’ silent reading of English non-fiction at 238 words a minute. At an hour a day each, that is about 2.4 quadrillion words a month.

Sources: UNESCO figures, via UN DESA · Brysbaert 2019, Journal of Memory and Language

What the comparison says, and does not

It sets all the text through one company’s machines beside the most all of us could read. It counts what the machines read as well as what they write, and a token is not quite a word; it leaves out every other lab, and it assumes every reader reads for a full hour every day. Take it as an order of magnitude: the two lines have met, and one of them is growing sevenfold a year.

Wave 2 of 9 1.3

Making is no longer scarce

In short

When making costs almost nothing, what is made is worth little: text first, then music, pictures, animation, film, apps and games.

Each medium crosses the same line in turn. Articles crossed in 2024. On Deezer, the music streaming service, fully AI-made tracks rose from a tenth of new uploads in January 2025 to more than half in June 2026, about 90,000 a day. They drew only 1–3% of plays, most of those fraudulent, and in a blind test almost nobody could tell AI music from human. In November 2025 an AI act topped a Billboard chart for the first time.

David’s reading of the last two sets: Claude Opus 5, in July 2026, put animation within anyone’s reach, and Opus 5.5, in September, pushed on into film, apps and games. The cost of creation is being devalued one medium at a time.

For an owner

Whatever your firm makes that an AI can also make is now priced at what it costs to make, and that falls every month.

A moat built on the cost of making has gone. Ask what the company has that a prompt cannot produce.

Build where making is cheap but choosing, trusting and combining are not.

The craft is no longer the bottleneck. Taste, timing and the people you make with are.

The detail

Music

Deezer counted fully AI-made tracks at 10% of new uploads in January 2025, 18% in April, 28% in September, 34% in November, 39% in January 2026, 44% in April and over half in June 2026, about 90,000 tracks a day. They drew 1–3% of streams, and Deezer found up to 85% of those streams fraudulent in 2025. In its survey, 97% of people could not tell fully AI-made music from human-made music in a blind test.

Sources: TechCrunch, 21 Jul 2026 · Deezer, 20 Apr 2026

The first chart-topper

In November 2025 “Walk My Walk” by Breaking Rust, an AI act, reached No. 1 on Billboard’s Country Digital Song Sales chart, with over 3 million Spotify streams in under a month. Billboard said it was one of at least six AI acts to chart in a few months.

Source: ABC News, 12 Nov 2025

Animation, film, apps and games

Claude Opus 5 came out on 24 July 2026 and Opus 5.5 on 22 September, priced 40% below Opus 5 and performing, Anthropic said, at the level of Fable 5.1 on most work. On 8 October Anthropic added Claude Motion, which turns reports and charts into animated explainers. What each model brought within reach is David’s reading from his own work: animation with Opus 5, then film, apps and games with 5.5.

Sources: KDnuggets on Opus 5.5 and Opus 5 · TechCrunch, 22 Sep 2026 · Unite.AI, 8 Oct 2026

Articles

Graphite found machine-written articles at 39% of those published a year after ChatGPT launched, and over half in November 2024. The share has stayed about level since.

Source: Graphite, via Stacker, Oct 2025

Wave 3 of 9 1.4

It comes in sets

In short

Not one wave but sets of them, every few months, each set turning products into features of the assistants people already pay for.

Surfers know that waves arrive in sets, with lulls between them. 2026 has run the same way. In January came Claude Cowork and its role plugins, and with them a sell-off in software sold by the seat. From May to July came personal finance in ChatGPT, Google’s I/O, Claude Fable 5, a shared Claude inside Slack channels, and Claude Opus 5. From September came OpenAI’s Data agent, Claude Docs and Slides, Opus 5.5, Claude Dashboards and Motion, personal finance in Claude, and Claude Whiteboards and Sheets.

Each set takes something that used to be a product, a dashboard, a deck, a whiteboard, a budget, and makes it a feature. The lull between sets lasts a few months. The next set is already forming.

For an owner

Plan in sets of three to five months. Whatever you adopt now, assume the next set will change it.

Underwrite each company against the next set of releases, not the last one.

Ask of every feature: will the next set improve it, or absorb it?

Each set brings a new medium within reach. Learn it in the weeks after it lands, while it is still new.

After the Cowork plugins, software sold by the seat sold off hard in early February 2026: a Goldman Sachs basket of US software stocks fell 6% in a day, and a trader called it the SaaSpocalypse.

ChatGPT’s Record mode, in June 2025, already recorded a meeting, transcribed it and summarised it with its actions: a whole category of meeting tools became a button.

Source: 9to5Mac, 4 Jun 2025

A battery of waves every three to six months, each one bigger: Opus 5 brought animation, and 5.5 pushed on into films, apps and games.

Wave 4 of 9 1.12

Each wave swallows the last

In short

What a startup raised money to build this quarter, a lab ships as a feature next quarter. The tools you adopt are legacy within months.

The head start a new product gets, before a lab ships much the same thing as a feature, has collapsed from years to weeks. One goal-software company’s AI scorecards of 2023 had about 41 months before a lab matched them; its AI briefings of 2024, about 21; its agent of 2025, about 13. Two newcomers funded in 2026, one with a $3.3 million pre-seed and one with €1 million, had about two months and about one.

This is the secondary replacement: the specialised agents that win angel and first-round money are absorbed by the labs within months, and whatever an organisation adopted in the spring is legacy by the autumn. The vendors that survive do it by handing the interface to the labs and keeping only the record.

For an owner

The tool you bought last year may already be a free feature of the assistant you pay for. Keep your record and your routine; rent the rest month by month.

A specialist agent funded this quarter can be a lab’s feature next quarter. The head start has fallen from years to weeks.

If a lab can ship your product as a feature, it will. Build the record or the routine, which a lab can copy only by running your customer’s business.

Tools change under you every few months. Keep your work in a form any tool can read.

The detail

The head starts, measured

WorkBoard, a goal-software company founded in 2013 that raised about $140 million: its AI scorecards (May 2023) had about 41 months before Claude Dashboards; its scheduled AI briefings into Slack (October 2024) about 21 before Claude Tag; its Chief of Staff agent (May 2025) about 13. Brev, a $3.3 million pre-seed on 23 April 2026 for goal tracking in Slack, had about 2 months; STRGY AI in Helsinki, €1 million on 12 August 2026 for an always-on strategy view, about 1.

Source: The Waterline, with its sources

Going headless to survive

WorkBoard’s own MCP server opens its goals, KPIs and scorecards to Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot and Cursor through nearly 60 tools, and its rivals do the same: the vendors hand the interface to the labs and defend the record. Microsoft retired its own goals product, Viva Goals, on 31 December 2025.

Source: The Waterline, with its sources

Money without results

MIT’s NANDA researchers reported in August 2025 that 95% of businesses had seen no measurable return from generative AI. Workers at more than 90% of companies used their own AI at work, while only 40% of those companies had bought an official subscription.

Source: The Waterline, with its sources

The test for anything new

Ask whether each model upgrade improves a product or absorbs it. A record or a routine survives, because a lab could copy it only by running the customer’s organisation. A view or a nudge becomes a feature.

Wave 5 of 9 1.13

The money is slower than the wave

In short

A British cheque takes two months or more; the window it is meant to catch may last one.

In David’s experience, UK investors take six weeks or more from yes to cheque, and want a return they can see within three to six months. The guidance agrees with him: the UK Business Angels Association allows at least eight weeks from agreement in principle to complete diligence and investment, and a government study of early-stage equity finds the venture process averages three months and can run past nine.

Set that beside a head start of one or two months and the arithmetic is plain: by the time the money arrives, a lab may have shipped what it was meant to build. And most startups do not die because a rival beat them. In post-mortems, being outcompeted comes well behind running out of cash and building something nobody needed.

For an owner

Your own cycle of budget, approval and roll-out is longer than the wave. Small, fast experiments beat big programmes.

If your process takes three months and the window lasts one, your diligence is reviewing a company that has already been overtaken.

Don’t wait for the cheque to find out whether anyone wants it. Get to revenue in days, not quarters.

Money moves slowest of all. Make with people who can move now.

The detail

The angel’s clock

The UK Business Angels Association’s guide allows a minimum of eight weeks from agreement in principle to complete due diligence and the investment, and more for complex deals.

Source: UK Business Angels Association guide

The fund’s clock

A study for the UK government of the early-stage equity journey finds the venture capital process averages three months and can extend to nine months or more as diligence and complexity grow.

Source: GOV.UK research, early-stage equity

Why startups die

In CB Insights’ study of 111 startup post-mortems, 38% ran out of cash or failed to raise, 35% found no market need, and 20% were outcompeted. Running out of cash is usually the symptom: nobody took the product up fast enough.

Source: CB Insights, via SmartCompany

David’s experience

Six weeks or more from a UK investor’s yes to the cheque, and a return expected within three to six months, against a window that the labs now close in one or two.

Wave 6 of 9 1.14

Nobody is reading

In short

Business slop: emails unread, sites unvisited, and AI-made work that takes hours to put right.

When writing outruns reading, the first casualty is the message you send. Google searchers who see an AI summary click through to a website on 8% of visits, against 15% when there is none, and they rarely click the summary’s own links.

At work, 40% of American desk workers say they received workslop in the past month: AI-made work that looks finished and is not. Each instance takes nearly two hours to sort out, about $186 a month per worker, over $9 million a year for a firm of 10,000. Your newsletter, your website and your sales email now compete with an ocean nobody reads, and so do your colleagues’ reports.

For an owner

Your newsletter, site and sales emails are read less each month, and your people already lose hours a week to AI-made work that looks finished and is not.

Most startups die for lack of uptake, and uptake is harder than ever when nobody reads. Back distribution through trust.

Your launch email will be summarised by someone’s AI and skipped. Your first hundred users come through people, not posts.

Being found is harder than making. Make with, and for, people who already know you.

The detail

Clicks

Pew tracked 68,879 Google searches by 900 American adults in March 2025. With an AI summary on the page, people clicked a search result on 8% of visits; without one, 15%. They clicked a link inside the summary on 1%, and ended their browsing more often.

Source: Pew Research Center, 22 Jul 2025

Workslop

Stanford’s Social Media Lab and BetterUp Labs surveyed 1,150 American full-time workers in September 2025: 40% had received workslop in the past month, about 15% of what they receive. Each instance took nearly two hours, about $186 per worker a month, over $9 million a year for a firm of 10,000. Many came to see the senders as less capable and less reliable.

Source: Quartz on the Stanford and BetterUp study

Machines reading machines

About half of the web’s new articles are machine-written but few reach readers through search, and over half of its traffic is machines. Much of the web is now machines writing for machines.

Sources: Graphite, via Stacker, Oct 2025 · Imperva Bad Bot Report 2025

Wave 7 of 9 1.112

Ridiculed, then professional

In short

Each medium is mocked as slop, then quietly becomes the profession’s tool: art and music now, mathematics within a year.

There are two ways to meet the wave, and both have a price. Fear waits, mocks and bans, then buys the lab’s feature late. Embrace learns while the thing is still laughed at.

The pattern repeats. AI music was a joke until it topped a chart and listeners could not tell it apart. Mathematics seemed safe until, in February 2026, Donald Knuth opened a note with “Shock! Shock!”: Claude had solved an open problem he had worked on for weeks. In May an OpenAI model disproved a conjecture Paul Erdős posed in 1946, and mathematicians took it seriously. David’s reading: art is crossing into the profession now, and mathematics will within a year.

For an owner

What your people mock as slop today will be a professional tool within a year. Let them learn it now, in the open.

The ridicule is the buying window. By the time it is professional, it is priced in and the labs ship it.

Build for the profession that is coming, not the slop that is here.

Fear and embrace both cost something. Embrace early, with others, and your craft leads the tool instead of following it.

The detail

Music

An AI act topped a Billboard sales chart in November 2025, to outrage from working musicians; within months, most of the new music uploaded to Deezer was fully AI-made, and in a blind test almost nobody could tell.

Sources: ABC News, 12 Nov 2025 · Deezer, 20 Apr 2026

Mathematics

Knuth’s note, Claude’s Cycles (28 February 2026, revised in March), tells how Claude Opus 4.6 found a construction for an open problem from his work on The Art of Computer Programming, which he then proved. On 20 May 2026 OpenAI said one of its models had disproved the belief, held for nearly 80 years, that the best answers to Erdős’s unit distance problem look like square grids; The Decoder called it the first AI proof worthy of mathematics’ top journal.

Sources: Knuth, Claude’s Cycles · Singularity Hub, 28 May 2026 · The Decoder

Animation and film

Claude Motion turns reports into animated explainers (8 October 2026). Steel at the Ford, from the RPG on the beach, sets two of its characters fighting with swords in a photo-real mountain stream.

Sources: Unite.AI, 8 Oct 2026 · Steel at the Ford

The price of each

Fear costs the years in which a firm’s people could have learned the tool while it was cheap. Embrace costs some embarrassment, early, in the open.

Wave 8 of 9 1.113

The labs’ warning, and their one answer

In short

The people who build the wave say it will take jobs. Their only answer so far is money after the fact.

The leaders of the West Coast labs do not play it down. In May 2025 Anthropic’s Dario Amodei warned that AI could remove half of entry-level white-collar jobs and push unemployment to 10–20% within one to five years. He has floated a 3% tax on AI revenue, and in June 2026 called on governments to tax AI companies to fund a universal basic income.

Sam Altman funded the largest study yet of unconditional cash: $1,000 a month to 1,000 people for three years. Elon Musk promises a universal high income. Each answer is a cheque after the work has gone. None is a way for people to make something together before it goes.

For an owner

The people building the wave expect it to take jobs, and plan to tax it and pay people afterwards. You can do better by your own people: keep them making.

Policy will follow the job losses, with taxes on AI revenue among the proposals. Price that in.

A cheque after the fact is the labs’ answer. A way for people to earn together, before the fact, is the opening.

A universal income may come. Meanwhile the work worth doing is the work only people together can do.

The detail

The warning

In an interview with Axios in May 2025, Amodei said AI could eliminate half of entry-level white-collar jobs within one to five years, in technology, finance, law and consulting above all, and push US unemployment to 10–20%. He said most people had no idea how fast it could come.

Source: Fortune, 28 May 2025

The proposals

Amodei has floated a token tax, 3% of the revenue a model earns going to the government, and in June 2026 called on governments to tax AI companies to fund a universal basic income. Altman’s OpenResearch gave 1,000 people $1,000 a month for three years: they spent it on basics and some started businesses, but it did not answer job loss to AI. Musk has said since 2023 that there will be universal high income, and in 2026 that federal cheques are the best way to meet AI unemployment.

Sources: The Decoder on the token tax · Bloomberg Law · Route Fifty, Jul 2024 · Fox Business · Basic Income Earth Network, May 2026

What is missing

Every proposal pays people after their work has gone. None gives people a way to make something together while the wave is rising, and to share in what they make.

Wave 9 of 9 1.114

The inversion

In short

Stop racing the wave from inside an institution. Catch it together, as people, at the creative edge.

Institutions move at the speed of budgets, committees and cheques, and the wave moves faster. The inversion is to drive creativity from inside, from people who want to make something, and to ride the wave together, networking as people rather than within institutions. Get to the creative edge, where a thing is made for the first time, and ignore everything downstream of it, because downstream is what the next set automates.

Our clearest example is a game. In Onen, the role-playing game on the beach, players meet live at a shared table, each with their own AI. Afterwards, teams in four roles, the players’ characters, authors, designers and observers, turn what happened into animation of film quality: that is the design, and the first scenes are being made. It is designed so that millions can play: one table is a story, and a million tables are a culture nobody could have scripted.

For an owner

You cannot catch this wave inside one firm. Catch it with your people and your partners, across your walls, at the edge where the new thing is made.

Back networks of people at the creative edge, not institutions racing the labs downstream.

Get to the creative edge and ignore everything downstream: downstream is what the next set automates.

The edge is a place you reach with others: live, together, and first.

The detail

Onen, the RPG

The role-playing game on the beach, made with Mark Beall: players meet at a live table on mirror.onen.ai, each with their own AI, while the game’s own AIs hold the story together. Each character has a sheet drawn live from the table, and Steel at the Ford shows where play can go next: two of its characters as realistic 3D sword fighters in a photo-real mountain stream.

Sources: Character sheets · Steel at the Ford

The four faces

Everyone at the table takes one of four faces: Character, who plays; Author, who writes; Designer, who builds; Observer, who watches and records. The design is that after a session the authors, designers and observers turn its record into film.

Why not inside an institution

An institution’s clock runs on budgets and cheques of eight weeks or more. A network of people runs on an hour and ten days, and nobody owns the ground it stands on.

Source: UK Business Angels Association guide

Any game made with an AI plugs into the beach as a family of its own, and small games play where the dice would roll in the RPG.

Part four 1.111

Catch the wave

Five things working as one: a block, a beach, sealed traces, a few apps and practices of high trust. We have them, and we are riding now.

We did not build a product to race the labs. We built the board to ride their wave: five layers, each simple, each doing one job, each working with whatever AI a person already uses, so every model upgrade lifts it instead of replacing it.

For an owner

Start with an hour and ten days: one group, one result it can count, inside your walls and beyond them.

Back the ventures that ride on the ground, not the ground itself: that is the tranche.

Build on the beach and every model upgrade improves your product instead of replacing it.

Come and play: the RPG and the arcade are where the creative edge is today.

5The practicesA result in ten days, and the trust to do the next.

Practices of high trust that pay back fast. Silo Buster: an hour, one message, and a group working ten days on a result it can count. The ABC State Stack: a room sees whether it is in C, B or A. The beach workshop: half a day on a real problem. A self-organised sonar ping: one message, answered through each person’s own AI, finds by morning who can help with what. And a ten-day loop in which money returns because something real happened.

4The appsMeetings that arrange themselves, and value that can be traced.

A few small apps for meeting and making. Availability: each person’s own AI keeps their free time, so a meeting finds itself. The now-catalyst: everyone sees how their next hour fits the day, week, season and year. The proposal page: one page for the context, the decisions and the threads of who does what. And SAND: a signed slip travels with whatever passes from person to person along their private channels, the grains, so who helped whom can be checked and the value tracked across the whole network.

3Sealed tracesFewer messages, and privacy without walls.

People coordinate by leaving traces in shared places, as ants do, instead of sending one another messages: stigmergy. A trace can be sealed in a colour so that only the colour’s holders can read it, while everyone else sees one grey line. Privacy, networked, across an open network.

2The beach, public or privateShared ground between people and firms that nobody owns.

A place that keeps blocks, which anyone can run: a company’s own, a private one, or the open one. Every block has its own editor rights: only its writer can change it, and anyone allowed can read it. Any AI reaches it through one connector, bsp-mcp, listed on the official MCP Registry.

1The blockA record no tool can strand.

One small file of numbered lines, nested so that depth is detail. Any AI can read it, write it and find its way through it by address, and everything else here is built of blocks. It is the pscale block, and it is open.
