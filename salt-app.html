Where it stands

In plain words

Six words the rest of the page leans on. The first five are how SALT's beach works today; the sixth is coming.

A beach

A place on the web where blocks of words are kept and read. SALT's is beach.bringingtheflavour.com, its members' own; the open beach, beach.happyseaurchin.com, is read by anyone.

A door

A page that reads and writes one beach. SALT's String is a door, and so is the open String: the same page, served at two doors, each with its own beach.

Beyond

What a member marks, in their own index, as readable outside SALT. Everything else on SALT's beach stays inside, and a stranger is told only that it is for members.

The voice and the button

The engine under every door: it proposes a name, makes a key on the device, earns the pass, invites and joins, and says a line where the page points. It knows no beach but its own.

The pocket

Matthew's pocket beach: a region of blocks kept on a person's own phone, with nothing leaving it but what its holder taps to send.

A colour

A named set of readers who share one key. A line in a colour opens for them and stands grey, with its colour's name, to everyone else. Designed, not yet built.

Step 1 of 61

One beach, one app

Decide

In shortOne app for one beach: SALT's app reads and writes SALT's beach and no other, so every tap in it has one readership.

Scope SALT's app just for the bringingtheflavour beach, not all beaches: it reads and writes beach.bringingtheflavour.com and no other, because a page writes through one voice and a voice belongs to the beach it is loaded from. Every line read, shared or answered in it then has one readership, SALT's members, unless a member marks it beyond, and nothing in the app ever asks which beach.

The other way, and when it would hold1 behind its zero

If a line could carry its readership to any beach and hide that it exists — one app could read every beach its person belongs to, each card saying whose eyes it is for: ALL at 1.8. Colour carries a readership on a line but hides words, never that a line stands, who wrote it or when (spine:coloured-stigmergy 1.6), so ALL waits at least on colours across beaches, and for a gated beach's members it never comes.

Why the voice decides it

1.2

The beach's voice, button/voice.js, takes its beach from the address it is loaded from, so a page that loads SALT's voice writes to SALT's beach and nowhere else, and its pass is kept for that beach alone. The page at SALT's door is the String's own page served with SALT's voice (api/string.js), so one page serves every beach that wants a String, each at its own door. A page with two voices would write by whichever its code picked, and a person could not tell from the page which one their next tap uses.

What a mixed feed would do

1.3

Lay SALT's String and the open String in one feed and the same heart means two things: on a SALT card it is read by SALT's members, on an open card by anyone, because an answer stands at the share's own address on the share's own beach (function:string 2). A reader would check each card's beach before every tap. That is the confusion David names, and it comes before colours; with colours it multiplies, one readership per colour per card.

A person of two beaches

1.4

Someone in SALT who also writes on the open beach keeps two apps, as people keep a family chat and a work chat: SALT's icon opens SALT, the String's opens the open String. Their name may be the same on both, while each beach founds its own passport under its own key, so nothing one beach holds can speak for them on the other.

Step 4 of 61.111

What crosses SALT's edge

Decide

In shortOut goes only what a member marks beyond; in comes an outside thing only as a link. SALT's String and the open String never mix.

Let out exactly what a member marks beyond, field 5 of their own index card, which every stranger, page and AI outside reads and nothing else; let in an outside thing only as a link shown with its site and path, never read into SALT's page. So the String app works with private and public beaches by never mixing them: SALT's String reads SALT's beach alone, a card from outside is a link to it, and an answer stays on the beach of the share it answers.

The other way, and when it would hold1.111 behind its zero

If SALT should take nothing in from outside — no link at all: BEYOND alone at 1.1118. A link is the lightest thing a member can bring, and refusing it shuts SALT off from the web its members read.

If nothing of SALT should ever be read past its edge — no member's beyond: LINK alone at 1.1118. Then a stranger at SALT's door sees only the keeper's notice, and a member who wants the world to read a line says it on the open String instead.

A link, never a copy

The phone, and what the button is

Decide

In shortOne app per beach on the phone, and the pocket for what is yours alone. The beach's button is the engine under every door, not an app.

Keep on the phone one app for each beach a person belongs to, and the pocket, the one thing that spans them, where a person's own words stand before any beach has them; the SALT app is not the beach-button operating at the private beach, for the button is the engine under every door and never an app of its own. Each person has sovereignty of their own data on their phone and shares some with beach shells: the pocket is the keeping, a door the sharing, and the icon tapped chooses the beach before a word is typed.

The other way, and when it would hold1.1111 behind its zero

If colours ever carry a readership across beaches and hide that a line exists — the button could grow into one app over every beach its person belongs to, each card saying its beach and its colour: THE APP at 1.11118. Until then a person of two beaches keeps two apps, and the button stays beneath them.

The pocket

1.11113

Human only

SALT is a human app, and its beach may be human only: an agent on a member's phone speaks for that member, under the member's own name and pass, and holds no passport of its own on SALT's beach. A member's AI that reads SALT reads it as the member would.
