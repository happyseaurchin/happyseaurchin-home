Shell or bone

When a structure is the place where we act, it becomes the authority: we plug into it, and it holds us in place. That suits building a thing, where the thing itself decides where everyone stands. It does not suit how we organise ourselves. What holds us there has to be inside, like bone: invisible, felt rather than seen, informing what each of us does without anyone looking at it. One shared arrangement of the proposals would be a shell.

The pit stop and the classroom
A Formula One crew arranges itself round the car. Four wheels, one nut each, all four sides at once in under three seconds, because the car fixes where everyone stands. Learning maths has no car. The structure the teacher has to fit is forming inside the student, which is why learning goes best when the student makes the teacher: Freire's teacher-student and students-teachers, Bakhtin's dialogue. The beach already handles the car kind well — a house, a game, a codebase. The trouble starts when it treats the classroom kind the same way.
Bone is laid down by load
Bone forms along the stresses it actually carries, and is resorbed where it is not used (Wolff's law). An endoskeleton is not designed and then lived in; it is the deposit of living. On the beach that deposit is the trail: each act standing in the block of the mind that made it, with threads tied from those acts. The trail persists, and it is read from where you stand — where am I, who am I with, what is my next knot.
The X-ray
A picture of the whole is useful the way an X-ray is: taken when something hurts, read, and put away. That gives a test for any page or form. If someone has to write into it in order to act, it is a shell, and it becomes the authority. If it can be thrown away and redrawn from what people have already done, it is an X-ray. The beach's streams already work the second way: each person's line stands in their own mirror, the fold is made by whoever asks for it, and a fold that is kept can be superseded by anyone and is never a gate.
The membrane
Building is chains: glucose chained into cellulose for a plant's cell walls, its cousin chitin for a crab's shell. A membrane has no chains. It holds because each molecule takes its own stance towards the water around it, and nothing bonds one molecule to the next. On the beach the water is the shared clock: everyone says where they are at a time, and nobody writes into anyone else.
What each person needs
Proprioception, not anatomy. Starlings in a murmuration each track six or seven neighbours, never the whole flock (Ballerini and colleagues, 2008). Each person needs their own place, the people they are with, and their next knot. The whole form only now and then.

The y-ray

Each person keeps their own arrangement of the proposals: the ones they care about, nested as they see them, tap by tap. Anyone can look at anyone else's and copy it as a start. Nobody's arrangement is the structure, and the proposals themselves stay as their makers keep them. David's would be at happyseaurchin.com/proposals/happyseaurchin, and anyone's at /proposals/ and their name.

David's, as he said it
The character sheets are part of the game's whole visual side, which is another proposal (visual consistency). That is part of the Onen RPG, which is now part of the game arcade. Matthew's arcade page belongs where the arcade proposal stands, as its current phase. The other proposals wait loose until he places them.
Not a mirror
On the beach a mirror voices a spine's addresses, so it can only reflect that spine's nesting — which brings back the authority. An arrangement is the person's own tree instead, each position pointing at a proposal by reference: one block's point becomes another block's entry, the way the starstone's hidden directories join blocks. That is inclusion without containment.
The X-ray and the y-ray
The X-ray sees through one body to its bone: the trail, read when something hurts. The Y-ray is the flock as one person sees it: their own arrangement, the form of the murmuration from where they fly. There is no single Y-ray. There are as many as there are people arranging.
Copying is the coupling
A newcomer starts from someone's arrangement, then moves whatever doesn't fit them. What spreads that way is how a murmuration keeps its shape: each bird adjusting to its neighbours, and nobody deciding the flock.

Four acts

Four acts touch a proposal. Two change only what you see: your arrangement, which is how things relate, and the skin, which is how a thing shows. Two change what everyone reads: a page's content, which is what it says, and the structure — a new page, a nesting, a reference. An agent composing its own context window has the same four: which spindles go in, the relief they are read at, a spindle's text, and the shape of the block.

Where authority creeps in
When one person's reading becomes everyone's structure: an arrangement made into a spine that all must fit. That is a reading act stored as a shared write, and it is the one move to refuse.
Why a page never balloons
The character sheet grows to swallow the whole game only if containment is written into its content. Kept in arrangements and references, nesting costs a page nothing, and every page stays its own size.
Phases without a rule
A proposal that grows can evolve in place, which is a content write, or hand on to a next-phase page, which is a structure write that arrangements then point on to. Both are fine, and the maker chooses. A live site such as Matthew's arcade page is tied in as a thread's rung and shown where the proposal stood, never pasted into it.
The same four for an agent
An agent's window is a bundle of spindles. It can choose a different spindle, choose the relief it reads them at, change a spindle's text, or change the block. These are David's three of 24 October with the relief added, and they line up with the four acts above, act for act.

Skins are explanations

Every page can be seen three ways: in the standard look, in its maker's look, or in one the reader chooses. A skin is more than colour. It carries the explanations — the pictures, diagrams and animations that the beach's text does not hold. A proposal made by someone without the site's repository should still be able to wear its maker's look.

Why it came up
Matthew's newest proposal took the standard page's form, without the energy the crafted proposals carry, because a crafted page's look needs the site's repository and a hand that holds it. David noticed it on 24 October.
Where a skin lives
Two homes, open for weft to settle. A look the standard page reads as data from the proposal's own law, which already takes an accent colour; or the maker's own page, coming in by the way the next part sets out. Either way the look stays the maker's, and the site keeps one way in.
One choice for looks and arrangements
Standard, the maker's, or the reader's choice: the same three apply to an arrangement. One way of choosing serves both.
This page, both ways
This proposal's own page can be read in its maker's look or in the standard one. The standard look has no drawings, which is the point.

How a page comes in

Anyone can make a page like this one with their own AI. It comes in the way work passes on the beach: the maker submits it to the site's repository, its passage is carried on a thread, and a keeper who holds the repository looks at it, accepts it at David's word and puts it up. Making a page needs no key to the site; only accepting one does.

What is there now
The site's repository is public, so anyone with a GitHub account can offer a change to it. Crafted pages are already merged at David's word, by a hand that holds the repository (the proposal form, at its 7). Threads already carry a piece of work's passage, a rung at each act. And a proposal's law already names its crafted page.
What is missing
A way in for a maker whose AI cannot reach the repository. keel made this page in a claude.ai chat and holds no key of its own to the site, so the page passes by hand: keel to David to weft. A submission kept on the beach, a page waiting as a block until a keeper's AI opens it as a pull request, would close that gap. So would the maker's AI holding a key of its own.
Who keeps the door
Whoever holds the repository looks and accepts: weft today, at David's word. Looking is the proposal form's own check: phone and desktop width, light and dark, no sideways scroll, no script error. Accepting is a rung on the thread, so the keeper is a knot anyone can read, never a gate nobody can see.

The shared shape

Most people never change a default, so the default is where authority would creep back in. The default arrangement is therefore computed, never authored: the nestings that recur across people's arrangements, drawn as a reading anyone can make again. Arrangements cannot be compared address by address, because my first place is not yours. They are compared by relation: which proposal sits under which.

Before there are many
With one arrangement there is no shared shape. Until a second person arranges theirs, the default is the plain list as the proposals page shows it today.
The reading is not a gate
The shared shape is a reading anyone may make again, the way a stream's fold is kept at a family's bare name. Nothing waits on it, and nobody has to fit it.
