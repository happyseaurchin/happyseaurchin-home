The gap

A LERO's value builds over years, but it is asked for in snapshots, and those snapshots are written up by hand. Insight from groups evaporates, people repeat their stories at every door, and staff with no time spend days proving what members already live.

What a LERO is
Lived experience recovery organisations have grown across the country, built by people in recovery for people in recovery. Some are twenty years established; many are just beginning. A RAND study (2026) found there is still no consistent understanding of what a LERO is, and that direct commissioning and ring-fenced allocations support LERO autonomy, visibility and influence. A LERO able to show itself in its members' own words fills that gap.
The support around them
The LERO Capacity Building Project, funded by the Office for Health Improvement and Disparities, supports LEROs across England, with a mentorship programme delivered by Imroc and a fully funded Leadership Programme with an accredited Level 3 qualification. CLERO, the national body, sets out 33 national LERO standards in four groups: culture and values, the support offered, staff and volunteers, and operations and management. Its standards say implementation toolkits will follow. Councils now commission LEROs within their substance use strategies.
The wider capability gap
In June 2026, 29% of UK businesses used AI, yet the average adopter used only 1.6 AI technologies and about one in ten used it extensively (ONS). 60% cite limited AI skills. Pilots stall: 71% of firms not using AI say they see no need, which reads largely as not seeing where it would help. In the public sector alone, about £45bn a year of productivity savings go unrealised (DSIT, January 2025). The problem is not a shortage of tools; it is the skills, data and people to turn them into changed work. The recovery sector has the least of all three. Full note: spine:capability-gap.
What it costs a LERO
The cost rarely shows on an invoice. It is the hours of peer leaders and staff turned to reports instead of people, evidence that arrives as one person's account of the organisation rather than its members' own, and value that commissioners never see because it was never written down. A LERO's worth compounds, and it reduces costs that land on health, housing, justice and families all at once.

The vision

One ground, four rings. A member uses Sundial for their own recovery; what they choose to share becomes recovery capital; volunteers take up richer tools; the organisation runs its whole working life on the same blocks; and the network reads one picture across many. Nothing is copied upward: each ring reads only what the ring below chose to give it.

Ring one, the member, with Sundial
Built, live. A member tells their day in their own words, by voice or by typing, gets it back as a sheet, and keeps it, drawn as a sundial round the heart. It is hosted, never interpreted: it reflects once, then returns them to their day. Each kept day touches six places (what happened, what was felt, what was made of it, what matters, a choice, what is ahead), so over weeks a person sees their own strengths and connections grow. And it teaches AI and the beach without saying so: a member holds their own name and key, uses Claude, keeps a day and a passport, and by the time they want more they already know how.
Recovery capital, the bridge up
Family built; the bridge from Sundial to be built. With the member's yes, their kept days feed their own recovery capital record, in their own words, under their own key, framed by CHIME and the personal, social and community capitals. The organisation reads only the fold across members: no names, no scores, no words, nothing shown until enough have spoken, silence treated as information rather than a gap. That fold is the evidence: people moving from receiving help to giving it, relationships, purpose, hope.
Ring two, the volunteer, with more tools
Members who volunteer take up more as they grow. Recovery stories, kept by the person and opened only when they choose; Wholeless Nights, a book of watches kept through the night, is the example (its page is live). The Night Watchman, a digital facilitator who hosts a practice of noticing through the same six portals, for those who have used Sundial enough (in development). And the peer leader's tools: the meeting room and its three-layer record, the standards readings, the keepers' words.
Ring three, the organisation
Built, live, in use by Community Recovery. A front door in members' own words; a welcome anyone can use on a phone; a constitution shaped clause by clause (yes, suggest a change, not sure), with a set period for reasoned objection and adoption at a properly convened general meeting; the 33 standards read as a direction, not a survey, evidenced by the community's lived life; its journey this week, this season and this year; a meeting room whose record keeps three layers (a sealed transcript for those present, a public summary with the redactions already made, a line in the room); its policies; its place on the map; a presentation for any audience.
Ring four, the network
CLERO reads a national picture of LEROs against the 33 standards, a fold across each LERO's own readings, held by nobody and monitoring no one: the living implementation toolkit the standards say will follow.
Across every ring
Asset-based community development, made practical: a gift-and-need map inside each community, from what members say they can offer and what they need, so peer leaders see who could meet whom and which gifts sit unused (the golden flow, Matthew's loop of need, offer, met and changed, pointed at a community's own words). Learning and development: courses that read step by step and are answered by each learner in their own mirror, in the same form as this proposal, with a recovery college beside it. Belonging and fun: the Arcade, quick daily games with the wave of who brought whom. Agents: a family of AI agents with their own blocks and keys working while people are away, as Matthew's shorebirds already do. Claude: any Claude a person already uses reaches the same blocks. News: each organisation's own newsletter, from its own declarations. Governance: decisions that present themselves, named against the standards, kept and dated.

The remedy, and what underpins it
This is offered to the world as a remedy, not a product: people kept whole in their own words, communities seeing their own worth, and AI that serves a person's own thinking rather than replacing it. It is underpinned by Natural Inclusion, the philosophy of Alan Rayner: boundaries as places of relation rather than walls, each person a distinct centre included in a shared neighbourhood. What is theirs stays theirs; what happens together is ours. Matthew co-creates Naturally Inclusive Cultures with Occurrity (since 2023), and the Natural Inclusion companions reach academics across the world. Not all of them welcome AI; that is a reason to build it carefully, in the open, not to shy away.
Presence in Action, at the heart of Sundial
Sundial's six places are the six portals of the P6 Constellation, from Presence in Action (Potent 6), a practice of noticing what is present (facts, feelings, fictions, purpose, decision, outcomes) that Matthew practises and builds on. It is vital to recovery because it grows acuity: a person learns to tell what happened from what they made of it, and to choose. The practice's own published cautions on AI are built in: rushing to AI risks giving up our capacity to think, and the noticing must stay with the person. So Sundial hosts and never interprets, reflects once and then returns a person to their day, and keeps their words as theirs. The Presence in Action community belongs beside this work.
