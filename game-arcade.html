In short · one tag, and nothing else as a kit

What a maker gets today

The voice. One script tag, served by the beach since this morning, that says, keeps, asks and drops a person's line into their own mirror under their own key; a mirror founded at its family's floor, so a daily game's lines land at the day's digits; a newcomer named and keyed in one tap, the same for every game and premiere; their settings, and their button where they have it on.

Behind it, one cached read of a family's mirrors and its fold, built for a crowd; one read of Ghost Wire's room alone, hard-wired to that one game; and a room for one founding, open live in the mirror. The arcade's door lists Ghost Wire by that one read and the premieres from each maker's list.

What a maker does not get: a plug (game.js answers 404), a board element, a recipe on the beach to follow with the voice, a place on the door, or the room on the door. So a second game made today is a separate system: a copy of Ghost Wire's page, by hand.

What the voice does, call by call
  • beachVoice.say({ field, at, text, name }) — a line at one address in your mirror of a family; saying again there replaces it.
  • beachVoice.keep — your whole mirror at once; ask — a question in your button's own window; drop — your mirror taken down.
  • beachVoice.propose(), enter(), made(), known() — a newcomer named (a starling and four digits) and keyed on the spot, their mirror founded in one tap; a name already on the beach is asked its key by the browser.
  • /api/mirrors?family=… — every mirror of a family and its fold, one copy a crowd shares for fifteen seconds. /api/board?game=ghost-wire — the only game it knows.

In short · found it, two tags, three calls, list it, push it; the door does the rest

The path, as it will be

For a game made with an AI and kept on GitHub Pages, in six acts. A maker and their AI do five; the beach does one. Nothing of the maker's passes through anyone's hands: no pull request, no deploy on the beach, nobody's permission.

  1. Found it. With your own Claude holding the beach: read the recipe, then write three blocks under your own key. The spine (its days or its stops; a daily game stands on the clock), the law (its mode, the briefing a player sees, one line saying how its board ranks, its page's address, its room), and the room. Later the game form does this in one tap.
  2. Two tags in your HTML: the voice, and the plug with data-game set to your game.
  3. Three calls. today() for the day's address. result() when a run ends, into the player's own mirror at that address; a newcomer is named and keyed by the voice in one tap, a typed name stays a claim. top() for the board, one cached read. Or the ready-made elements, a board and a room, and no code at all.
  4. List it. One line, the game's name, what it is and its page, on the shelf the door reads. Whose list that is, is the decision below.
  5. Push it. git push; GitHub Pages serves it. The beach learns your page from your law and your line, never from your repository.
  6. The door does the rest. It reads the shelf, draws your board from your players' mirrors, shows your room's last lines with a box to leave one, and links your page. The same path on any beach: the plug takes a beach's address.

      Money: credits, prizes and cups

      Credits work the way the beach already keeps them. They come into being only when money enters, as a ticket, and they never turn back into money. Inside, they move only by being given: entries, prizes, and sharing forward.

      How credits move
      • A pound buys a thousand credits, the rate David settled on 21 October.
      • Entering a cup costs credits. A day's top ten and a cup's winners receive credits.
      • Credits are used by sharing them forward: entering another cup, funding a day's prize, thanking a game's maker, buying a ticket for a friend.
      • Every move is a gift on the giver's own ledger and a receipt on the receiver's. Balances are computed on read, so anyone can check a cup against its table without a central book.
      • Holding credits earns nothing. A player's standing grows from what others record having received from them.
      The rate

      Settled by David on 21 October: a thousand credits to the pound. The figures move in steps of three zeros, so 100,000 credits is £100 and 23 million is £23,000, and a share can be as small as a tenth of a penny.

      It's the rate Onen's credits screen already shows. Fulcrum Volume 3's hundred to the pound is the one to bring into line.

      The top ten's prize

      A day's prize for a game's top ten comes from a pot someone funds: a sponsor, the arcade, or a share of the game's cups. It's split down the ten, and what they receive they share forward.

      The law around it

      A summary, not legal advice.

      In the UK, a paid entry with a prize is a lottery where chance plays a part, unless the skill in it stops a significant share of people entering or winning, or there's a genuine free way in. A game that mixes skill and chance counts as a game of chance whatever the mix. Prizes that grow with the number of paying entrants have drawn pool-betting warnings, as fantasy sports found. Credits bought with money may count as money's worth even though they never cash out.

      Ghost Wire has the strongest case for skill, because the same moves always give the same run. Even so, the first cups run free to enter, with fixed prizes from funded pots. A pot built from entry fees, as the TRONIC Cup's was, waits for legal advice.

      David's TRONIC Cup, designed for Armagetron Advanced, given a beach shape. A knockout is a tree: the rounds are its depth, the champion is at the root, and every player's path is the line from their seat to the root. Meeting a finalist early pays.

      How the pot is shared

      The payout law comes from David's 2021 TRONIC Winnings sheet. Makers keep an eighth of the pot. Of the players' share, each paid round gets an equal total, so a share halves with each round further from the final, and the champion takes twice a round's. Everyone beaten by the champion, the finalist, a semi-finalist or a quarter-finalist is paid on top, halving at each step.

      The game within the game

      Players choose where to sit in the draw. Sit near the strong and you may go out early, but you're paid for meeting them. Sit among the weaker to train and climb, then meet the strong later with more at stake.

      An async cup for Ghost Wire example

      A match is both players running the same wire, seeded by the match's own address, inside the round's window. The faster proved run goes through. Nobody referees: the draw is computed from each player's own result lines, and the keeper's only act is paying out.

      A cup can also name a different game for each round, so the arcade's shelf becomes the course.

      Where it comes from

      TRONIC, the Tron International Competition, ran on Armagetron Advanced from 2006: a weekly Spoon, a monthly Ladle, a seasonal Bowl and an annual Cup. The Ladle has run every month since July 2006; its 179th was on 2 August 2026. The Cup was designed for a million players in one day at a pound each, with £16,000 a player to the winning team. It was never held.

      Growth: who pays at each scale

      Each step up is paid for by whoever needs it, so nothing waits on one person's bill.

      1. Makers host their own pages for nothing, on GitHub Pages. Payments stay off it, at the beach's ticket machine.
      2. Up to a few hundred players, the shared beach and its cached reads carry it.
      3. Past that, a keeper folds each board on a schedule, and pages read only the fold.
      4. Past that, the arcade runs a beach of its own. The beach is a package that deploys anywhere.
      5. Live head-to-head play needs game servers, paid from the makers' share, as the TRONIC Cup set out to pay its server admins.
      Where AI-made games live now
      • On their makers' own pages, above all. Vibe Jam 2026 asked for free, no-login browser games that load at once, on their own domains, and drew 945 entries and over a million players.
      • Cross-listed on itch.io with the AI declared, and on the big portals: Poki has about a hundred million players a month, CrazyGames over fifty million.
      • On Reddit, which pays a developer once a game holds 5,000 daily players.
      • Games made inside chat apps sit behind sign-in walls and pay their makers nothing, so makers build with an AI and publish elsewhere.
      • Money comes from ads, tips, pay-what-you-want and sponsors. Paid-entry tournaments for browser games are close to absent; prize pools are mostly sponsor-funded with free entry.
      • Public leaderboards are easy to fake. The field's answer is the replay and the verified run, as PolyTrack and Ghost Wire both do.
