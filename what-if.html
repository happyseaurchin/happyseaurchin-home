Five words in plain terms

  • PscaleThe size of a rung, as a power of ten: of places, of spans of time, or of people. A company of about 180 is 2; a nation of a hundred million is 8.
  • Spine and mirrorA spine holds what happened. A mirror is written beside it at the same addresses; here it holds what someone invents.
  • WeightHow settled an event is, from one, barely, to nine, fixed, with zero where nothing is settled and anyone may play. The Xstream writings call this the determinancy cloud.
  • The sundialThe beach's clock. Every instant has a ten-digit address, from the millennium down to the beat, a span of about eighteen minutes.
  • A tableA group playing a world together, with a keeper and a room, as Onen's worlds are played on the beach.

Step 1 of 6 · on the beach at 1

The frame

The question ↓

In shortEvery event stands at three coordinates: where, when and who. What happened is positive and what is invented negative, at the same address, and every event carries a weight from one to nine, with zero left open.

Every event stands at three coordinates, each a pscale ladder: where, on earth's map from the planet down to a room; when, on the sundial from the millennium down to the second; and who, by mind mass from humanity down to one person. Each carries a sign. What happened stands positive, in a spine; what is invented, a counterfactual or a fantasy, stands negative, in mirrors at the self-same addresses. And every event carries a weight, the digit of the determinancy cloud: how settled it is, from one, barely, to nine, fixed, with zero where nothing is settled and anyone may play. Heavy events press down on the lighter ones beneath them; a light event can be tipped from below.

The sign · 1.2

David's convention in the Xstream writings (identity-coordinate-exploration, 9 February 2026; manifolds-numbers-psychosocial-summary, 1 February 2026). In space, positive exists and negative is fiction. In time, positive has happened and negative has not, which takes in the future and the counterfactual alike. In identity, positive was formed by direct experience and negative by representation, the tiger met against the tiger read about, still an open question there. The threshold between the signs, 0.x, is the doorway: the boundary in space, the moving now in time, the self in formation. On the beach the sign belongs to a block, not to a digit: a record's spine is positive and every mirror that invents beside it is negative, while the address they share stays one number. David, 25 November 2026: 'the entire block is negative in that it is invented. It is like a mirror versus the spine.'

The weight · 1.3

The determinancy cloud as the Xstream writings set it (determinancy-cloud-coordinate-synthesis, 15 January 2026; onen_v4_design_document, December 2025). It began with actions taken from a book, Purple Braiding or The Lord of the Rings: specific events at low pscale and overall patterns at high pscale, all weighted high because, according to the book, they happened. A digit in the cloud says two things at once: that an event stands at this rung, and how settled it is, from one to nine. Each rung's event sits inside the one above it. Zero means nothing is specified there: the hole in the gasket, where a player acts. And determinacy is not probability: events are settled by agency, so what players attend to grows more settled and what they ignore stays fluid.

Why weights · 1.4

Upward and downward causation, as David put it on 25 November 2026. Higher events shape lower ones, as banks and governments shape what individuals do, and individuals' behaviour is what those social objects are made of. A near-zero event can be tipped from below; a heavy one resists, and the Xstream rule of December 2025 makes the resistance arithmetic: the chance that a fine-scale act shifts a coarser outcome is its base chance divided by the outcome's weight (soft-medium-coordination-v2, part 6.3). The aim behind the weights is tools, mathematics among them, that help individuals change behaviour so that their collective effect reaches up and changes a social object, which then changes the people around it. It is a way to read how technology changes people (Susan Blackmore's temes) and how ideas do (Richard Dawkins's memes), as genes generate what emerges above them.

What stands on the beach · 1.5

Read on 25 November 2026. Earth's map, spatial:earth on the earth beach, holds France at 31320000000 with Paris, Lyon, Marseille and Toulouse beneath it, and no Normandy yet; places grow there by proposal in earth's room and the keeper's daily pass. The sundial writes any instant at full width: the gliders at the Caen Canal bridge, 00:16 on 6 June 1944 in Normandy, fall in the beat at 1944231594, which the clock, keeping no zone, voices as the night of Monday 5 June; H-Hour on Omaha, 06:30, falls at 1944231627, the dawn of Tuesday 6 June. Earth's law already keeps a history: lines at the address of the time they tell of, each with its place, and a read across place and time computed on demand (its branch 8). Its accounts stay plural, each in its own mirror and never merged (8.3), and a rung nobody can vouch for is honestly a zero (2.2).

The ballpark · 1.6

The neighbours, for anyone who wants them; nothing after depends on them. Counterfactual history fills its absence with a model: Max Weber's objective possibility, after Johannes von Kries, and today's synthetic controls. Hindsight and foresight part formally: Baruch Fischhoff's creeping determinism (1975), Gary Saul Morson's sideshadowing (1994). Branching histories have a logic: Belnap, Perloff and Xu, Facing the Future (2001). And alternate-history games are a crowded field, WorldOS and Pax Historia among them. What none of them shows is the weight: what the sources fix, what they leave open, and how far many players' branches spread from one moment. The thread behind this proposal stands in keel's account, history:keel 492 and 497.

Step 2 of 6 · on the beach at 1.1

The record

The question ↓

In shortD-Day is written only where someone asks or plays, at matching grain on all three ladders. Each line is an account's, and its weight is how far the accounts agree.

D-Day, 6 June 1944, the Allied landings in Normandy, written when someone wants it and laid on the three ladders at matching grain, so that a line about a nation sits on a large place and a long span, and a line about one man on a road and a minute. The coarse shape comes first: the war, the campaign, the five beaches, the armies facing each other. Then, wherever someone plays or asks, the record goes down: a beach sector, a battalion, an hour; a bridge, a company, a beat; a bunker, a man, a minute. Every line is an account's, at its time's address, with its place and its people beside it, and its weight is how far the accounts agree.

Three ladders · 1.12

The same event at matching rungs. Where: Earth, Europe, Western Europe, France (31320000000), then Normandy, Calvados and the Manche, then Caen and Bayeux, then the villages behind the beaches, Bénouville, Ranville, Vierville-sur-Mer, Colleville-sur-Mer and Sainte-Mère-Église, then a bridge, a draw, a road, a strongpoint. When: the 1940s, 1944, June, the week, the night of 5 to 6 June and the day after, the hours before dawn, the beat, the minute. Who, by mind mass: nations at war, a hundred million or so each (pscale 8); their armed forces, some ten million (7); an army group, about a million (6); the force landed on the day, about 156,000 (5); a division, some fifteen thousand (4); a battalion, several hundred (3); a company, about 180 (2); a section or a tank's crew, under ten (1); one soldier (0). Where a formation falls between rungs, a regiment or a platoon, it takes the nearer one, as earth's law drops one of two neighbours that share a mass (its branch 2.3).

One address, worked · 1.13

The bridge over the Caen Canal at Bénouville, later named Pegasus Bridge. Six gliders carried 181 men under Major John Howard, D Company of the 2nd Battalion, Oxfordshire and Buckinghamshire Light Infantry, with Royal Engineers; the first touched down at 00:16, and the bridges over the canal and the Orne were taken within minutes, the first action of D-Day. All three coordinates sit at matching grain: a bridge beneath a village (spatial pscale 2 under 3), the beat at 1944231594 (temporal pscale 0), a company and its commander (identity pscale 2 and 0). Across the bridge stood a small guard from the 736th Grenadier Regiment, and above it through the day the 21st Panzer Division round Caen, whose counterattacks pressed on the airborne bridgehead: an event at a coarser rung bearing down on a finer one.

Written on demand · 1.14

Nothing is written ahead of interest. When someone asks about an event, an AI drafts its coarse rungs from what it knows, every line naming the account it comes from; where it can name none, the line is not written and the rung stays zero. As people play or ask, the record goes down a rung where they are and nowhere else, so a sector of Omaha may stand at the minute while the rest of the coast stands at the hour. The record grows where attention goes, as the Xstream writings expected: what is attended becomes more settled.

Accounts and weight · 1.15

Each source is an account in its own mirror at the record's addresses: the official histories (Gordon Harrison, Cross-Channel Attack, 1951; L. F. Ellis, Victory in the West, 1962), the narrative histories (Cornelius Ryan, The Longest Day, 1959; Stephen Ambrose, Pegasus Bridge, 1985, and D-Day, 1994; Antony Beevor, D-Day, 2009), and the memoirs, war diaries and interviews beneath them. An event's weight is how far independent accounts agree at its address: nine where they all do, lower where they differ or one speaks alone, zero where none reaches. A model's confidence sets no weight.

Step 3 of 6 · on the beach at 1.11

Playing in the record

The question ↓

In shortTables play where the record is silent, and as invented people. A table can tip an event the accounts leave light, never one they fix.

A table plays among the recorded events without contradicting them. It plays in the zeros, the finer rungs no account reaches, the minute inside the beat and the ditch beside the road, and as invented people, the negative of identity: a private in the 352nd Division, a farmer's daughter in Vierville, walking among the recorded. A recorded person does only what the accounts have them do. A table's act may tip an event of low weight, a strongpoint that fell at eight or at nine where the accounts disagree, but never one of high weight: the landings happen. The tables are Onen's, played on the clock.

Invented people · 1.112

The negative of identity. A player takes someone the record does not hold, placed by mind mass like anyone: an invented soldier in a recorded company, an invented villager in a recorded village. The recorded company is positive and its invented member negative, and the company's address is shared. Play never puts words into real people's mouths, eighty years on, while a player can still stand where they stood.

The zeros · 1.113

Where the accounts stop, the weight is zero and play decides: the minute inside a beat no diary times, the field between two named farms, the men a company report counts but does not name. A zero is not a gap left by carelessness; it is the honest edge of the record, the hole in the gasket the Xstream writings named, and the place a game lives.

Tipping · 1.114

Upward causation in play. Where the accounts disagree, an event's weight is low and a table's acts can settle it one way inside the table's own mirror; where they agree, the weight is high and the act fails or turns into something smaller. The resistance is the Xstream rule: the chance an act shifts a coarser outcome is its base chance divided by that outcome's weight. Downward causation needs no roll: the tide, the bombardment, the order from above arrive at every table beneath them.

The tables · 1.115

The beach already runs tables: Onen's worlds, brackenfoot, thornmere, coldcote and others in the worlds block, each with its room, its keeper and its rules, and a table can be played on the clock, the second track proposed on 14 September 2026. A table in the record is such a table whose world is a slice of the record, whose clock is the sundial in 1944, and whose map is earth's with the record's own finer places beneath it.

Step 4 of 6 · on the beach at 1.111

Counterfactuals

The question ↓

In shortA fork decides one recorded event otherwise. After it everything is the table's own, and many tables forking the same moment show that moment's cloud.

A counterfactual is a fork from the record into the negative. Up to the fork it reads the record, every weight intact; at the fork one event of the record is decided otherwise; after it, everything is its own, in negative mirrors at the same addresses. The change climbs as time runs on, from the rung where it happened to coarser ones: upward causation made visible. Several tables forking the same moment, laid side by side at one address, are the determinancy cloud of that moment. The first is the best known: The Man in the High Castle.

The Man in the High Castle, mapped · 1.1112

Philip K. Dick's novel of 1962, adapted for Amazon Prime Video in 2015. The fork is one shot. On the evening of 15 February 1933, in Bayfront Park, Miami, Giuseppe Zangara fired at the President-elect, Franklin Roosevelt; in the record he missed and mortally wounded Anton Cermak, mayor of Chicago, and in the novel the shot kills Roosevelt. Time: positive to that beat, which the sundial writes at 1933123219 for about 21:35 Miami time and, keeping no zone, voices as the deep night of 16 February; negative after it, through a longer war ending in 1947 to the novel's present, 1962. Space: positive throughout, real ground under invented rule: the Pacific States of America under Japan, the Rocky Mountain States as a buffer, the Reich's territories in the east. Identity: real people up to the fork, then invented polities at the national rung and invented people at the rung of one. Inside it stands a counterfactual of its own, The Grasshopper Lies Heavy, a book in which Roosevelt survives: a negative of a negative. Dick consulted the I Ching at the novel's turns, as his characters do: a decision block run by an oracle.

The book as a map · 1.1113

The premise is open to anyone, since an Axis victory is a whole genre of alternate history. The novel's people, places and plot belong to its author's estate, and the series's to its makers. So the book serves as a map to study, how a fork at one rung reaches every rung above it, and play happens in a branch of our own at the same fork, with people of our own, unless the rights holders agree otherwise. This is not legal advice; it is a question for someone who gives it.

D-Day's own forks · 1.1114

The record holds counterfactuals of its own. On 5 June Eisenhower ordered the landings for the 6th on a forecast of a break in the weather, and that day wrote a note to issue if they failed, taking the blame as his alone and dating it July 5 by mistake (National Archives, Eisenhower Library): a counterfactual written in advance by the man at the fork. Others stand in the accounts: the panzer reserves held for Hitler's release, Rommel away in Germany on the day, Bradley weighing that morning whether to divert from Omaha. Each is a seed for a table that wants to fork the record it has just played.

The cloud after a fork · 1.1115

Before the fork every table reads one record; after it each writes its own. Read across their mirrors at one address and the spread is the cloud: narrow at the fork's own rung and near it in time, wide at coarser rungs and later ones. How far it spreads, and how soon it reaches the national rung, measures how much that fork mattered: the measure of replaying a decision chain from each rung (pool:run-up 13), taken on history.

Step 5 of 6 · on the beach at 1.1111

Fantasy worlds

The question ↓

In shortA fantasy world is the same machine with the other sign. The book is the record, its events weighted by how plainly it states them, and play happens in its silences.

The same rule with the other sign. A fantasy world is negative in space and time throughout, and its record is a book: its events at low pscale and its patterns at high pscale stand with high weight because they happened in the book, and players play around them without contradicting them, in the zeros the book leaves. Upward causation works as it does in history: a light event of the book can be tipped from below, and a heavy one presses down on every table. This is where the determinancy cloud began, in Onen, and the record of D-Day is the same machine read with a positive sign.

Canon from a book · 1.11112

A book is read into the three ladders as a record is: its events placed at their place, time and people at matching grain, each weighted by how plainly the book states it. Purple Braiding, the URB book, is the first to hand: Celeste's Day of Appraisal, her abduction and return, the Empire around her, at the rungs the URB writings already gave them (URB_Flex_pscale-experiment, December 2025). The book's silences are the zeros where tables play.

Over real ground · 1.11113

A fantasy world may lie over the real one, negative over positive: the URB writings map URB's periods onto Earth's years, Purple Braiding's present onto December 2025. A world laid over earth's map keeps earth's addresses with the other sign, so a place in the fantasy stands on a place in the real, and someone standing in the real can step through it.

Whose book · 1.11114

A world may be built from our own books, Purple Braiding first, or from books in the public domain. A living estate's world, Middle-earth among them, serves for study, how its canon spreads its weight across the rungs, and not for public play without its holders' word. This is not legal advice.

Practice for the real · 1.11115

The Xstream writings hold that collaborating in a fantasy and collaborating in the real are the same skill (xstream-onen-systemic-evolution). A table that learns to tip a light event in a world, by many small acts together, is practising what the frame is for: individuals changing their behaviour so that their collective effect reaches up and changes a social object.

The build order

The question ↓

In shortThe smallest path: the law, the places, one slice of the record and its people, one table, then a fork with three tables, then this page.

The smallest path to a table playing in the record, then a fork, then a fantasy world on the same machine. First the law of signs and weights, written once and read by every record and world. Then the places: Normandy proposed in earth's room so that earth's map holds it, and the record's finer places in its own map mirror. Then the record of the first slice, its accounts as mirrors on the clock. Then the people, the order of battle by mind mass for that slice. Then one table playing it, then a fork with three tables and the cloud read across them. Then the crafted page.

The law · 1.111112

One law for every record and world, written once: the sign of each block (a record's spine positive, its tables' mirrors negative), the weight of each line (one to nine, zero unwritten), how weight is set (the agreement of accounts in a record, the plainness of the book in a fantasy), the rule for tipping, and the read that lays the mirrors side by side at an address as the cloud. Earth's own law stays as it is: earth is held true, a reflection of people's real days, and no fork is written there.

The places · 1.111113

Earth's map grows only by proposal in its room on the earth beach and by the keeper's daily pass. The first slice needs Normandy beneath France (31320000000), Calvados beneath it, and Bénouville and Ranville beneath that; the villages of the five beaches come with the whole day. The bridge, the canal, the gliders' landing ground and the guard post are the record's own places, written in its own map mirror beneath earth's village rung, as a person's house is written in their own mirror.

The record's family · 1.111114

One spine on the clock for the event, a mirror for each account at the same addresses, and each line carrying its place on earth's map and its people on the identity ladder, as earth's own history lines close with their place. The fold at any address lays the accounts side by side and computes the weight; nothing is merged.

Play and the first fork · 1.111115

One table on the clock in the first slice, invented people only, until its keeper says it reads true to the accounts; then a fork at a rung of low weight inside the slice, three tables on it, and the first read of a cloud across their mirrors. The mapping of The Man in the High Castle, and a fantasy world read in from Purple Braiding, follow on the same law.
