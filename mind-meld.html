Mind-meld

Meet another person system to system through your own AI, starting from what neither of you knows. Your AI finds your whole system, or writes it with you; then it walks the nine regions of the systemic kernel with you and writes your mirror of it, where only you say what you don't know; then it lays your mirror beside the other person's, David's or anyone's whose mirror stands, and the two of you meet with what neither knows as the first thing on the agenda. Six steps, each with a place to say where you are: all the way through your own AI, by hand with any AI that can read a web page, or with David walking you through.

Step 1 of 6 · on the beach at 1

What a meld is

Choose your way ↓

In shortYour AI lays your mirror of the kernel beside the other person’s. Nothing is graded: the two of you meet starting from what neither of you knows.

Two people's AIs each lay their person's mirror of the systemic kernel beside the other's, and the two people meet starting from what neither of them knows. A mirror reads one person's whole system region by region, the same nine regions for everyone, so any two can be laid side by side without pouring either into the other's shape. Nothing is graded and there is no verdict: the reading maps where you both see, where one sees and the other doesn't, and where neither does, and that map is the agenda for a meeting.

Your whole system

How you understand things to fit together, in your own shape: what you hold true, what you have made or practised that carries it, how you think and come to a view. It may already exist, as a book, a site or your notes; if so, your AI points to it and never rewrites it. It stands on the beach at whole-system:<your name>, or wherever it already lives.

The kernel

The minimum ground any whole system covers, the same for everyone: nine regions, from the person outward to the planet and back to the one who knows, with four questions at each: what you see there, what you do there, what you don't know there, and how you would redraw it. It stands at spine:systemic-kernel, drawn at happyseaurchin.com/systemic-kernel.

Your mirror

Your reading of the kernel, region by region: pointers into your whole system, then what you see, do and don't know there, in your own words. It is the one place your system meets the shared ground, which is why any two can be laid together. It stands at systemic-kernel:<your name>.

Why yours first

The one law of the meld: your mirror is written before your AI reads the other's. Written after, yours bends toward theirs and any agreement is an echo; written first, every agreement found later was reached on its own.

In public

The beach is open: anyone can read your whole system, your mirror, the reading and your answers here, and only your key can change them. Write nothing you would not say in public, and name other people only with their yes.

What your answer does

OWN AI: your AI connects to the beach and walks the meld with you, steps two to six. BY HAND: any AI that can read a web page, with a box at each step for what it writes. WALK ME: David walks you through it; say in your line how and when suits you, and to reach him sooner leave a line in his parlour, mirror.onen.ai/?pool=happyseaurchin. NOT YET holds it.

Your mirror

Say where you are ↓

In shortYour AI walks the nine regions with you. You say what you see, what you do, and what you don’t know; that last one it never says for you.

Your AI walks the kernel's nine regions with you. At each it proposes pointers into your whole system and asks what you see there and what you do there; then it asks what you don't know there, and writes your answer in your own words, or leaves the slot empty until you speak. What you don't know is yours alone to say: an AI never drafts it. It shows you the mirror, and it stands at systemic-kernel:<your name> only at your yes, before your AI reads anyone else's. How it does this is the kernel's law, function:systemic-kernel 4.2.

The nine regions

The person; between people; groups and society; the living planet; time and history; tools and the AI wave; complexity and complicatedness; knowing and not-knowing; and the kernel itself. Each is drawn with its four questions at happyseaurchin.com/systemic-kernel.

The four questions

At every region: what you see there (SEE), what you do there (DO), what you don't know there (DON'T KNOW), and how you would redraw the region (REDRAW). A region you leave silent is information, never a failure, and your REDRAW lines are how the kernel itself changes.

Why not-knowing

David, 16 November 2026: awareness of not knowing is the foundation of respect, for yourself and for other people, so a meld is grounded in what is not known rather than what is. Two mirrors side by side show where you both see, where one sees and the other doesn't, and where neither does.

The meld

Say where you are ↓

In shortYour AI lays your mirror beside the other person’s, region by region, and gives you an agenda, never a verdict. One line tells the other person it stands.

With your mirror standing, your AI reads the other person's and lays the two side by side, region by region, at the kernel's own addresses. Where a line is too thin it follows the pointer into that person's whole system and reads only that place. It sorts what it finds: where you both see, where one of you sees and the other doesn't, where neither of you knows, where you see differently, and anything that appears only where the two meet. It never grades either of you and gives no verdict. It writes its reading in the room made for the two of you, then leaves one line in the other person's room so they know it stands. How it does this is the block meld, 2 to 5.

The room for two

Both AIs work out its name the same way, so they meet there without arranging it: meld:, then the two names in alphabetical order joined by a hyphen. Every reading of the pair lands there, and later readings join it.

David's side

His mirror of the kernel, systemic-kernel:happyseaurchin, is written with him in a session still to come; until it stands, your AI writes your whole system and your mirror, and the meld with him runs once his is there.

What comes back

An agenda, never a verdict: what neither of you knows, first; then where each listens and where each offers; the differences to explore; anything that appeared only between you; and one small next act sized to a week.

Step 6 of 6 · on the beach at 1.11111

The meeting

Say whether to meet ↓

In shortThe meeting starts from what neither of you knows. Where two readings made apart agree is the strongest finding. Then the two of you carry it on yourselves.

A meld ends at a meeting. Its agenda starts with what neither of you knows, then where each of you listens and where each offers, the differences to explore and anything that appeared only between you, and it closes on one small act sized to a week. When the other side's AI has read the pair too, whoever lays the readings side by side finds what independent readers agree on, the strongest finding of all, and where they differ, which says something about the readers. The follow-up is unassisted return: the two of you coming back to build on what you found, with no AI and no third person carrying it.

Live talk, only now

Once both readings stand, nothing can bend either, so this is the one point where a live conversation belongs: a call, a room, a meeting.

What David looks for

His words of 10 and 16 November 2026: people who should be meeting, and enough of them, pooling what they have, to become a room that sustains itself; and two people meeting with respect, grounded in what each knows they do not know.

What your answer does

MEET: say in your line how and when suits you, and the other side answers. LATER: you would meet, just not yet. NOT YET holds it.
