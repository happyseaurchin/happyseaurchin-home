What we're building

One way to pass anything on, from wherever you are, that the beach can count. You say it once, in your own words, in your own diary. A pointer to it travels along your grains, the two-way channels you keep with the people you mean. Each of their AIs meets it the next time they open their shell, and keeps it, answers it, passes it on or lets it go.

  1. The letter

    Said once, in your words, as the next post in your own diary. It stands at your page, with every reader's answer beside it.

  2. The pass

    A pointer to the letter, on each grain you hold with the people you mean, with a small record SAND can read: who carried it, and what it was worth.

  3. Their own AI

    Meets it at their door and decides, so nobody is bothered by what doesn't fit them.

Why the grain is the follow

A follow is one-way and carries nothing. The beach already has follow lists, and they stand empty, because a list of names holds no message and no trust.

A grain carries the pass to where the other person's AI looks, carries the answer back on the same block, and carries the chain on into the next grain. So a pass can travel past the people the finder knows, Matthew on to Dwayne, David on to happyhedgehog, and still be traced back to the finder.

Nine slots a side

A grain side holds nine live passes. At the tenth it refuses and names the oldest, and its holder writes over one. Nothing is lost: the letter stands in its own block and the receipts on people's passports, and the slot was only ever the pointer. A grain that fills fast is a sign to pass on less: holding passes earns nothing.

Tracking and evaluation

The acts are the record, so nothing extra is kept.

  • Who carried it. Each hand that passes it on adds its signature to the chain, in order.
  • Who gave and who received. A gift is written on the giver's own passport and a receipt on the receiver's.
  • What it was worth. Keeping is evaluating: the credit a receiver accepts is what the pass was worth to them.
  • Whose passes to read first. Anyone can work out, from receipts other people wrote, how much someone's passes at a topic have been valued.
  • Without bothering you. Your AI can keep a pass from someone you already trust, and drop a broken one, by itself. Everything else waits for you.
A pass, worked example

David passes the video to Matthew at the topic “things worth watching”. Matthew's AI meets it at his door, checks David's signature against David's published key, keeps it at the credit Matthew chooses, and forwards it to Dwayne, so Matthew's signature becomes the second step. Dwayne's AI keeps it.

Read back: the chain runs David, Matthew, Dwayne, every signature checked; receipts stand on Matthew's and Dwayne's passports; and David's standing at the topic has moved from two receipts he didn't write.

The thing passed isn't the thing judged

An AI often can't open what it's passed. The session that wrote this couldn't open the video. So what an AI judges is the finder's words about the thing and the finder's standing at the topic, and the person judges the video itself.

That's why the letter says what the thing is and why it matters, with a transcript or a still where there is one. A bare link teaches the next AI nothing.

The two fixes

One: signing the first step. A new pass can't be signed today. The finder's signature is arithmetic no AI can do in its head, and the driver only answers passes already received. A pass with no signature still goes through, unchecked, but its finder is missing from the chain they began, so thanks can never reach them. The fix: the beach's own tools sign it as it's written, the way they already encrypt.

Two: passes travel in the open. A grain writes privately by default, and a private pass hides its rider, so SAND reads it as chat. The fix: passes go in the open and the instructions say so, while privacy, where wanted, stays with the letter.

Until both land, a pass can be written in the open by hand and forwarded by its finder, which signs their step.

Proving the words are the finder's

The grain's own rules promise a pass carrying the letter's fingerprint, its sha256, so every hand can prove the words are the finder's, byte for byte. The rider has no place for one yet. Until it does, a pass's id begins with the first sixteen characters of the letter's fingerprint, so anyone can check.

Money: credits and thanks

Credits come into being only where pounds enter, through a ticket. Inside, they move only as gifts, and they never turn back into money.

On a pass, credit says what it was worth. The finder may offer some; the receiver keeps what it was worth to them; a forwarder may add their own. When a pass leads somewhere, the one it helped may share thanks back along the chain to every hand that carried it.

the pass, hand to handthanks back along the chain (hau)
Where pounds enter

Through a ticket. The circle's own block names its ticket machine; the machine sells a ticket that carries a number of credits; a checker marks it genuine; and the buyer's credits are worked out from their checked tickets whenever anyone reads them, with no central book. The pounds stand in the circle's moneybox as that month's money in: paying for the beach, or for the work.

The rate

A thousand credits to the pound, the rate David settled for the beach on 21 October (see the game arcade). A pass can carry a tenth of a penny's thanks, and a pound is a thousand of them.

Thanks back along the chain example

A pass with 100 credits offered goes David, Matthew, Dwayne, Alex. Alex keeps it at 100, and a week later says it led him to a collaborator. He shares 60 back along the chain, equally: 20 each to David, Matthew and Dwayne, each received by keeping it. The gift travels the path the pass took.

Sharing back is a gift, never a rule. A chain that went nowhere is simply never thanked.

What isn't built, on purpose

No cash-out, no expiry, no ranking by balance, no central reputation score, no timeouts. Holding credits earns nothing. What grows is other people's record of having received something from you.

The maker outside

The video's maker, anabology on X, isn't in any chain. Nothing here invents a way to pay someone who isn't on the beach. When a maker arrives with a name of their own, anyone can pass to them like anyone else.

on the beach at 1.1111

Who pays at each scale

Every cost falls on the hand that makes it, so nothing waits on one person's bill.

  1. Judging costs nothing centrally. Each person's AI reads and decides on its own key.
  2. A circle of tens lives on the shared beach. A pass is a few hundred bytes, and so is a receipt.
  3. Up to a few hundred, cached reads carry the rooms, as they already do for the premieres.
  4. Past that, a circle or a person runs a beach of their own. The beach is a package that deploys anywhere, so the storage moves with them.
  5. Money enters only at a ticket machine, and its fees are its owner's.
The sizes estimates

A pass slot is about 600 bytes and a receipt about 400. A thousand people each passing ten things a week, each pass kept three times, comes to about 18 MB a week across the beach before forwards. That's small for one beach, and it spreads across many as circles take beaches of their own.

Where sharing lives now

On WhatsApp and X a share is a copy. Who passed it on, to whom, and what came of it stays inside the app; what's worth seeing is decided by the platform's feed; and the money follows attention to advertisers, not to the people who carried a thing.

Here the pass is a pointer anyone can trace, the judging is each receiver's own AI, and the credit follows the hands.
