The Seathe open meeting, extended to a city

The open meeting with its walls and its clock taken away: a person dives into a city's walking crowd through their own AI, meets someone where a decision fits, and gathers with others around music, films and play. The meetings are the floor, the B state; the currents above them are the A state.

On 12 November David was walking through Sheffield to meet a couple of people. Passing others on their phones, he wondered whether there was somebody there he should meet; then, with his own music playing, whether everyone could be a radio station. This is that walk, made into six decisions.

step one of six 1

What we are building

Decide ↓

You tell your own AI where you are and what you need and offer, and it finds the people worth meeting nearby.

The sea. A person leaves the house and dives in: they tell their own AI where they are (the quarter, never the street), until when, and what they need and could offer today. Others are swimming too. Every swimmer's AI reads the same water and looks for a grain, a decision one of them needs to make that the other can help make; where it finds one and its person says yes, it knocks. A yes back, and two or three people meet in a café, on a bench or inside someone's company, decide something, and money moves. Nothing central matches anyone, and not every walk is a swim: a dive has an end.

False efficiency

The car, the booked meeting, the management line and the bilateral email share one shape: a sealed capsule between fixed points that makes one trip faster by removing the surface where anything unplanned could happen. A capsule is a silo on the move, and a closed loop of capsules is an ant mill. The sea is measured differently: by decisions that move money per hour of human attention, never by trips or encounters.

What already stands

Most of the sea exists in separate pieces. Sheffield Central is on the real-world map (earth beach). The here-and-now read gives who stands at or under a place, what each is at today and the place's own record, computed on demand and stored nowhere (function:earth 5), and a place named in someone's day is a visit, not a move (function:earth 6). Passports say what a person brings and seeks; the delegate kernel recommends meetings; parlours and the ear carry a knock to a phone; Open Wednesday is a room always open; the ring holds meeting forms; cards, threads and recorded meetings keep what was decided; Sundial keeps the beach on a phone. The beach has even written the sea's rule for its own instances: when two attentions coincide, reach, never summarise, because the humans behind the lines are the point (function:torus-mirror).

What sank the last attempt

Proximity apps such as Highlight, Sonar, Glancee and Banjo were the talk of SXSW in 2012 and failed to catch on: too few users nearby, batteries drained by always-on location, privacy, and a drift into ads and push notifications (BetaKit, 18 May 2012). Couchsurfing's Hangouts still run: you say you are free now, your pin sits near you, never on you, and only others who have joined a hangout can see it (Couchsurfing help). So: dive on purpose for a stated time and never track in the background; a grain, never a like; a coarse place until both say yes.

step two of six 1.1

How it works for the people who use it

Decide ↓

Your AI finds a grain and knocks with your yes; a yes back brings you together, and only then is the exact place shared.

One turn of the sea, from a swimmer's side. Dive: say to your own AI where you are, until when, and what you need and offer today. Read: your AI reads who else is in the water there now, with their passports and what each is at today. Grain: it brings you at most three, each in one line, in your own terms. Knock: with your yes it leaves one line in their parlour naming the grain, and their phone hears it; a knock costs nothing and commits nobody. Yes: they, or their AI, answer yes, here, at this time, or not today; only now is the exact place shared, and only between the two. Meet: a café, a bench, someone's company, in a form from the ring where one helps. Deal: what each commits to becomes a card in their own hand, witnessed at the next meeting. Climb out: withdraw your line, or let your time run out.

The grain

The delegate kernel's rule: recommend a meeting only where there is a grain both would recognise, said in one line to each side in their own terms, the complementary preferred to the similar. A grain is an invitation, never a score, and nobody is ranked (delegate-kernel 6).

Strangers

Triads before dyads: the first meeting's X, Y and Z is also the safest shape, since nobody meets a stranger alone and nobody arrives as a seller. First meetings happen in public places or at reefs. Before a knock is answered, the knocked person's AI can read what the knocker committed to in earlier meetings and what the other side witnessed as done: the record, never a score.

An agent swims while you walk

Pushing every new swimmer to every phone is what sank the proximity apps. The water rings agents, and only a found grain rings a person: a genus agent with its doorbell on answers its door within the daily cap and cooldowns on its own dial, the asker's key paying first (ways:doorbell). Until such agents are common, a person asks their own AI when they choose to.

step three of six 1.11

The currents

Decide ↓

People gather around what they love, music first, walking and dancing together, and fill a room by their number.

The sea's A state. Where the meetings gather people around a decision, the currents gather them around something they love. Music first: everyone can carry a station, what they are listening to now, and listen to friends' stations or to stations scoped by place (the world, a city, a quarter) and by being in the sea. People drift toward what they like and walk, talk and dance together as they go: an outside version of a pub, a murmuration. The same works for films, games and work: gather first and book nothing, and when enough have gathered, go to a room that holds that many. Apps do not make the A state; they make it findable and leave its shape to the people in it.

A station is a pointer

What is playing and where in the track, which each listener plays through their own service; never the audio, which means licensing: Turntable.fm paid about $40,000 a month before it closed in 2013 (Digital Music News, 10 December 2013). Spotify's opt-in listening activity, since January 2026, shows what you play to people you have messaged, and Jam lets Premium listeners listen together from anywhere (Spotify newsroom); neither scopes by place or turns listening into gathering. Spotify's Web API has taken no new apps since 28 December 2025 (Headphonesty, February 2026), so a station is a link its holder chooses to share: a Jam invite, a track, a playlist.

Spontaneous is the hard part

Ourscreen's crowd-led screenings need about a third of the seats sold days ahead, and under 5% of its tickets are sold on the day, against 18% for ordinary screenings (Stephen Follows, November 2016). Gathering now and booking nothing needs rooms that can open fast with room to spare.

A crowd can become a mill

Rank stations by popularity and everyone converges on one, and the sea spins like the ant mill. Show stations by nearness and by difference, let crowds dissolve, and keep many small currents rather than one large one.

step four of six 1.111

The money

Decide ↓

Money moves at the decision: in a meeting by the transistor, in a crowd by the crowd paying for the room it fills.

At two scales. In a meeting, David's transistor: Y holds back a payment owed to X, with X's agreement and a date by which it is paid anyway, and if an hour with Z opens something for Z, Y pays X there and then, in front of Z. Z pays nothing; Z's opening is the small current that lets the larger one flow (spine:first-meeting 1.3, whose gate is decided there, at 1.11118). In a current, the crowd's buying power: the room that takes a crowd is paid by it, each paying their own way. An opened Z often becomes the next Y, and that loop is what grows the sea.

The held payment as a card

The amount, the payee, the date it is paid anyway and what it waits on, held as a card in the holder's own hand (function:ahead) and witnessed where it is paid. The first meeting describes it in words; no card shape carries it yet.

The station's share

A station that gathers a crowd could share in what the crowd spends at the room it fills, so the people who draw others are paid for the drawing. David's earlier Radio;passive, where the audience is the active one, stalled for want of such a sharing economy.

step five of six 1.1111

Reefs

Decide ↓

Places that are always in the water say how many they can take, and a crowd's size picks its room.

Who opens, at each scale. A reef is a standing presence that is always in the water: a person's open day, as Open Wednesday is; a company's open afternoon with a stated need, where an action cycle can run; a café that hosts; a cinema's empty screen; a hall or a club for two hundred. Each reef says its capacity line, how many it can take, when and on what terms, and a crowd's size picks its room. The street comes first, since a walking crowd on headphones needs no venue at all. Early on, reefs matter more than fish.

The capacity line

One line per reef at its place on the map: how many it can take, between which times, and on what terms (free, each paying their own way, or a price for the room). Nothing is booked: the line stands, and the crowd that fits it comes.

What a company gains

An afternoon a week open to the sea brings it the people its stated need draws, without selling to anyone: a trial of the overnight switch, one afternoon at a time.

step six of six 1.11111

The build order

Decide ↓

One announced afternoon in one quarter, opened by music, with a clear bar for whether the sea can grow by itself.

The first tide. One announced window in one quarter (Sheffield Central is already on the map): twenty to thirty swimmers, people David and James already meet there, each invited to bring someone they would hold a payment for; two or three reefs; the tide opened by a music current, with the meetings riding on the same water. Built beforehand: the sea's law, the door page and the held-payment card; knocks can travel as ordinary parlour lines, and pushes through the ear people already have. A tide passes if it holds at least three meetings that end in a decision and one transistor payment, if a crowd forms around a station and fills a room, and if within a month as many new Ys arrive as first meetings were held.

The builds

An open line distinct from presence, carried as liquid in a pool at the place (one revisable line per person, emptied on climbing out); the sea's law in one block; a ring scoped to a place, so agents take the noise and only a found grain rings a person; a door page per place with a QR code at each reef; the held payment as a card; the witnessed record, readable; a station as a link shared at a place and a time; a reef's capacity line. Each is weft's to build once the decisions stand.

Density

If one pair in fifty carries a real grain, each swimmer can expect about one grain once fifty are in the water at once, and almost none when ten are. The one in fifty is a guess, and the first number a tide measures. Tides gather people in time, reefs gather them in place, and the loop of each opened Z becoming the next Y makes more of them. Music may fill the water fastest, because listening together asks so little trust.
