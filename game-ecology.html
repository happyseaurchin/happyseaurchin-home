Game Ecology

A game stands where the dice stand. The character sets how hard it plays, the player’s hand plays it, and its line goes back to the table.

Small games played inside RPG play: a character's gear opens them, the story opens the gear, and each game stands where the dice would, its result going back to the table as a line of play. The idea is Mark Beall's, his backpack and lock for Orik in October 2026; the reach is David's, anyone's game made in the arcade and played at any table, with authors, designers and observers around the characters.

The margin
The character’s force for this act, plus what the place lends, less how hard the act is. It is NOMAD’s sum before the dice.

  1. Step one spine:game-ecology 1

    The idea, in Mark's own pages

    In shortGear opens small games, the story opens the gear, and each game leaves a line for the table.

    A character's gear opens small games, the story opens the gear, and each game's outcome comes back to the table as a line of play. Orik's backpack holds what he wears and carries: the sword opens a duel, the herbs a kettle, the flint a fire in the rain, and the old iron key whatever the story says it opens. A quest stays locked until the story reaches it. What carries between quests is what Orik has, and what each leaves is a line the table can take up as his next beat.

    The kettle1.2
    The Hedge-Witch's Kettle, a craft: three herbs into the pot, then three stirs, each timed to a needle swinging through a green band. The recipe sets where the band sits, a healing draught simmering in the middle and a sleeping draught at low heat; the stirs that land set the quality, weak to masterwork; a wrong recipe gives sludge. Mark's pack opens with Orik limping into camp, and at the table his last beat at the Diggings ends with a soldier's blade across his calf (pool:300:16 at brackenfoot-open), so a healing draught is the quest the story already asks for.
    The duel1.3
    The Duel at the Fen Crossing, a fight against one of the world's figures: strike when the bandit is open, parry as his wind-up enters a pale band for a perfect parry that leaves him reeling, each strike spending stamina, a healing draught drunk mid-fight, flight costing coin. In Mark's words, timing decides the fight instead of a dice roll.
    The lock1.4
    Orik's Locks: a glowing pin rises and falls and the player taps when its gap sits on the gold line, setting every pin; a wrong tap snaps a pick, three snapped and Orik walks away; a patrol bar runs out along the bottom. Orik's passport makes him a locksmith and trap-maker, about 9 in locks (passport:Orik 1 at brackenfoot-open), and his drive is a small iron key (passport:Orik 2).
    The line for the table1.5
    Orik's Journal: every outcome written as a line the story can pick up, with a copy button, a won duel reading: Orik fought off a bandit at the Fen Crossing, bloodied but standing, and took the coin from the man's pack. The line is the joint, how a game played anywhere becomes a beat at the table.
    The narrator's hand1.6
    A demo panel in the backpack to lock and unlock each quest, standing in for the author or narrator who opens quests when the story reaches the right moment. At the table that hand is the story's own record (the pack, three steps down).

  2. Step two spine:game-ecology 1.1

    At the door

    In shortA game is offered only where the dice would roll. It is played once, never forced, and it sends its own line.

    A game stands exactly where the dice stand. NOMAD rolls only for the genuinely uncertain, and a simple act never rolls, so a simple act never plays a game and play keeps its pace. Where the dice would roll, the act's player may play instead: once, the result standing as that actor's luck, fixed before the resolver reads it and never chosen, read against the same seven bands. A player who would rather not play lets the dice roll at the same odds. The game stages its own line in the room under the player's key, so nothing is retyped, and a result not in by the window's fold leaves the act to the dice.

    A locked door, worked through1.12
    Say the party reaches a locked door with soldiers on its heels; Ugarth holds the soldiers off while Orik's player picks the lock on their phone. Picking under that pressure is a risky act, so the dice would roll, so the lock is offered. Say the lock is an ordinary one (difficulty 5, NOMAD's baseline) and the soldiers press them (situation minus 2): Orik's margin is +2, and the dice would open it for him about 63% of the time, clear or better about 30%. If Ugarth tries it, his force for patient work is about 4, his weak spot, a margin of minus 3: about 24%, clear or better about 8%, catastrophic about 8%. Without the soldiers, Orik's 9 against an ordinary lock just opens it, no roll and no game. The numbers are keel's illustration; the resolver makes the call.
    What NOMAD says1.13
    Outcome is character force plus situation force plus the dice, less difficulty (5 by default). The dice are one positive and one negative d10, a 10 exploding, luck the difference between them. A simple act with force clearly above difficulty needs no dice. Seven bands: above 10 overwhelming, 5 to 10 clear, 0 to 5 marginal, 0 stalemate, minus 5 to 0 minor failure, minus 10 to minus 5 significant failure, below minus 10 catastrophic. Roll once; a settled band is never re-rolled (rules:nomad 1 to 5). The dice are seeded per actor from the window, each actor's luck fixed before the resolver reads it, independent of the others and never chosen (grit 2.3). Brackenfoot's own rules put honest narrative judgement first, the numbers sharpening contested moments and never overriding what would plainly happen (rules:brackenfoot).
    The scene's clock1.14
    A window folds when its span has passed (grit 2.5), so a game played while the others fight has to land inside the window they are acting in. Mark's patrol bar is already the right instinct: the game's time runs on the scene's clock, and a result that misses the fold leaves the act to the dice.
    One play per act1.15
    Fight again belongs to practice in the arcade, where practice is how a hand gets good; at the table the act is played once and its band stands, as the dice's does. A contest between two players' characters, NOMAD's combat against a character, would be both players playing at once: a later game.
    The margin travels with the result1.16
    The margin is read when the game is offered, from the same passport, place and rules the resolver reads, and it travels with the result, so the resolver reads the band as it was played and never judges the margin twice. Who reads it at the offer, the resolver or the player's own AI, is for the rule to say.

  3. Step three spine:game-ecology 1.11

    Skill sets the hardness, the hand plays it

    In shortThe character sets how hard the game plays, and the player’s hand plays it.

    The answer to David's question, the player or the character: both. The act's margin, the character's force plus what the place lends less the difficulty, sets how hard the game plays, and the player's hand plays inside it, so Orik's lock plays gently (slow pins, a wide gold line, a long patrol) and Ugarth's fast and narrow. The game is tuned to the dice: at each margin an ordinary hand lands in each band about as often as the dice would put it there, and a designer sets how far a good or a poor hand can move it. A practised player gains a little, a clumsy one loses a little, and the character's skill is felt in the hands.

    The odds by margin1.112
    Under NOMAD's dice each point of margin moves about eight points of chance from failing to done: at minus 3 an act is done about 24% of the time, at 0 about 45%, at +2 about 63%, at +5 about 81%. The swing is wide; even at +5 about one act in seven fails. These shares, band by band, are the targets a game is tuned to. Computed by keel from rules:nomad 2 and 4, counting marginal as 1 to 4, clear as 5 to 10 and catastrophic as below minus 10.
    Orik's lock and Ugarth's1.113
    What a margin can change in Mark's lock: how fast the pin rises and falls, how wide the gold line is, how many pins there are, how many picks before Orik walks away, how long the patrol takes. Each is the maker's to tune; the target at each margin is the dice's.
    The other way, luck1.114
    The game as the luck alone: everyone plays the same game, its result becomes a luck number, and the formula runs as written, the character's skill showing only in the sum. Simpler to tune and kinder to slow thumbs, but the player never feels who their character is.
    The gamer's hand1.115
    Under the hardness rule a practised player could carry a clumsy character, and a strong character could be wasted on slow thumbs. The tuning to the dice bounds both, the designer's weight on the hand says how far a hand may move a band, and anyone who would rather not play keeps the dice.

    Decide1.118 · answered in game-ecology:<handle>

    How the character counts. HARDNESS: the margin sets how hard the game plays, tuned so an ordinary hand lands where the dice would, with a designer's weight on the hand. LUCK: the same game for everyone, its result a luck number in the formula.

    keel recommends HARDNESS: it is what makes Orik's lock feel like Orik's.

  4. Step four spine:game-ecology 1.111

    The pack is a reading of what stands

    In shortThe pack reads Orik’s passport, so nothing new is stored. Words go to the table; numbers stay inside a game.

    Nothing new needs storing. A character's passport already holds capability at 1 and holdings at 4 (character-sheets 1.113), so the pack reads the passport: what Orik wears and carries at 4, what he can do at 1, and the games that stand open because of them. A quest opens because the record says the story reached it: a craft taught is a line in his capability, a thing found a line in his holdings, a lock a line in the place's own words, each written by that block's own holder, never by a switch. Words go to the table; numbers stay inside a game.

    Orik's sheet as it stands at brackenfoot-open1.1112
    Passport:Orik's opening line, a short, broad locksmith, trap-maker and finder of lost things; at 1 his capability, about 8 overall, about 9 in locks, mechanisms, traps and noticing what has been touched, about 4 in open water; at 2 his drive, a small iron key whose door he will not name; at 3 his look; at 4 his holdings, not yet written.
    What the pack reads1.1113
    What he wears and carries (in Mark's pack the notched sword, the leather jerkin, the river-stone charm, herbs and draughts, flint and tinder, the old iron key), each a line in words at the passport's 4; the crafts at 1 that open a game, brewing once the hedge-witch has taught it; and from the world, the places whose words hold a lock or a market. The pack shows which games stand open now and which wait on the story.
    Numbers and words1.1114
    Mark's pack counts health out of 100, coin and stamina; the RPG counts no hit points, and an outcome short of clean success bites in words, an option closed, a threat opened, a standing spent, a wound taken (grit 2.4). A game may keep any numbers inside itself; what it sends the table is a band and a line, and what it leaves the pack is a holding in words, a good healing draught or four coin. Mark's lines nearly do this already: bloodied but standing.

  5. Step five spine:game-ecology 1.1111

    The studio around the table

    In shortCharacters play, authors place the locks, designers make the games, and observers draw and cut the shows.

    Four faces, the beach's own: character, author, designer and observer. Player-characters play the games in the moment, at the table and on their phones. Player-authors place the locks and the markets in the world and say what a key opens. Player-designers make the games, each kept on its maker's own page and plugged into the beach as a family, and tune the rules line that says which acts a game may decide and how a margin sets its hardness. Player-observers draw what happened, under the picture law at visual-consistency, and cut it into shows of about twenty minutes shared beyond the beach.

    Each face at the door1.11112
    The character picks the lock on a phone while Ugarth holds the soldiers; the author has said what the door is, how good its lock is and what lies behind it; the designer made the lock game and tuned it to the dice; the observer draws Orik at the door and the soldiers in the bracken, from the record and Mark's references.
    How a designer's game reaches a table1.11113
    The maker keeps hosting the page and adds the beach's voice and the arcade's plug, and the game becomes a family with its own room and each player's results in their own mirror (game-arcade 1, decided GO). At a table it is offered for the acts a world's rules hand to it, played at the act's margin, its band and line sent to the room. Practice runs stay on the arcade's shelf.
    The show1.11114
    Observers take in what play produces and make something of it: pictures through the studio (happyseaurchin.com/studio), where a crew weaves one instruction and fires it to an image-maker on their own key; the words from the table's record; a cut of about twenty minutes from a session, shared on social media. A game's moment, the lock giving or the duel turning, is where a show finds its beats.
    Who pays1.11115
    Every picture is made on its taker's own key and kept on their own storage, the beach holding only words and links (character-sheets 1.5, visual-consistency 1.6), and each game is hosted by its maker. In David's model for the RPG most of the money comes from observers taking in what play produces, and it flows to the experiences that engage them, the games among them. The arcade's credits come into being only when money enters (game-arcade 1.11).

  6. Step six spine:game-ecology 1.11111

    The build, smallest first

    In shortThe lock first; then the rule, the holdings, the pack, the shelf, the other games, and the show.

    The lock goes first, because it needs the least: one game, one character, one door at one table. Then the rule, at David's word: one line in Brackenfoot's rules or the arcade's recipe naming which acts a game may decide and how a margin sets its hardness. Then Orik's holdings in words at his passport's 4, the pack as a reading of the passport, the games on the arcade's shelf, the kettle and the duel the same way and then other makers' games, and last the show.

    Who does what1.111112
    Mark makes and tunes his games, writes Orik's holdings on his own key, and keeps the pack. weft drafts the rules line for David's word and lands the plug so a game can stage its line in a table's room, each code step a pull request for David to merge. David gives his word on the law. keel supplies the dice's targets for each margin, band by band, so every maker tunes against the same numbers. Every line is written under its own holder's key, never another's.
    What the first step needs in code1.111113
    The game opens with the character, the act and the margin it is played at; it sets its hardness from that margin; and it stages its result in the room as the player's own line, the band named, under the player's key. Whether the arcade's plug (game-arcade 1.12) can stage into a table's liquid window today is the first thing to check.

    Decide1.111118 · answered in game-ecology:<handle>

    The order: the lock first, then the rule, the holdings, the pack, the shelf, the other games, and the show last.
