ABC State

From one class to every school by 2036

Self-discipline through social responsibility, from one class to every school by 2036. A class has a state, as water does: C, held together by the teacher's control; B, where whoever slips is told by a classmate and corrects themselves; A, where everyone keeps their own self-discipline and the class moves as one. Children are the vital resource here: they reach school before adult organisations have trained them into C, among themselves they are naturally B, and with encouragement they reach A. As the AI wave reaches every school and workplace, this is one front with AI Inoculation and Silo Buster, which bring the same three states to organisations: people relating organically. The page asks, part by part, what you think, and at the end how far you would take it.

A class of thirty as a flock at dusk: C, rows on the wires under one lamp; B, groups working without opposition; A, one murmuration with no centre, a stray turned back by a neighbour; then the decade to 2036, flock joining flock over the schools of the horizon.

Plain words

Five terms, for anyone new

C, B and A
The three states of a class. C: held together by the teacher’s control. B: whoever slips is told by a classmate and corrects themselves. A: everyone keeps their own self-discipline, and the class moves as one.
C-star
A class that behaves only while its own teacher is there. It looks like A, and comes apart in front of another adult.
Classroom Coordinator
A specialist in group dynamics who works with a class until it can hold itself, then steps away to the next.
Educere
David Pinto’s name for the practices that grow self-discipline and social responsibility, set beside traditional education.

Part 1 · the practice · spine:abc-state 1

The state of a class

In shortA class has a state, C, B or A. The teacher names it at the end of each lesson, and the class earns its freedom together.

Just as water has different states, so does a class. A: everyone has their self-discipline; the teacher can trust the class with the changes between activities, so there is time for many of them, and for the fun ones. B: someone loses their self-discipline, not paying attention, not working, dropping litter, and when someone points it out they correct it themselves. C: it is pointed out, by an adult or by another student, and they carry on; then the teacher is empowered, by government no less, to enforce sanctions, and the lesson shrinks to one big task with no room for adventure. At the end of a lesson the teacher names the state: an evaluation of what actually happened, never a judgement, and it decides how the next lesson is taught.

The benefit of the doubt1.2
A new class is treated as B: everyone responsible for their own self-discipline, with a game or group work on offer. If it does not hold, the next lesson is taught as C, with control imposed; if it holds, the next lesson is better prepared and more challenging. Fun is earned, and earned together, at the level of the class: its collective behaviour decides whether the teacher comes in as a facilitator of learning or as a disciplinarian.
The test, and C-star1.3
A teacher cannot create an A class. A class that behaves because of the implicit threat of its teacher is better called C*, C-star. The test is to put another adult in front of it: an A class keeps its form, and a C class comes apart without the presence that held it.
B and A1.4
In B, classmates point out a slip and the one who slipped corrects themselves: control spread through the class, already better than control held by one person. A comes when there are enough strong relations between equals, and when those who keep their self-discipline forgive those who lose it. A cannot be chased as a goal, because chasing it as better collapses a class back into C. It is induced, the social equivalent of a flow state, with leadership moving from one to another as the moment needs, as in a murmuration.
The best class in the world1.5
A real class, from David's own teaching: maths, the third set of six, thirty 13 and 14 year olds in an ordinary state school in Scotland, taught by a supply teacher for two months, mostly A and sometimes B, and never once disciplined. A graduate visiting before her teacher training is left at the front with only 'Introduce yourselves, class.' 'We're the best class in the school,' says one; 'in Scotland,' says another; 'in the universe,' says a third. Then a boy who two months earlier had demanded the room's attention takes it, tells her, 'We, my class, did twenty-five weeks' work in five,' and looks down. A conversation follows, with no hands raised and nobody talking over anybody.
Whole fractions1.6
A child is usually counted as one thirtieth of a class of thirty, a part among parts. Said the other way round, the class is thirty thirtieths, one whole, and each child is one of the thirty that make it whole. Many children are confused by fractions, and teaching dwells on parts rather than wholes; said of their own class, whole fractions may help the idea and the class together. Maths used this way is reflective: not only maths about objects, but maths a class can see itself in.
Where it came from1.7
David Pinto came to teaching maths from social anthropology, and taught as a supply teacher, treating each school as engaged ethnography rather than a post to hold. The consequence system he met punished individuals for what was usually a social problem, divide and conquer, which seldom inspired and needed constant watching. Earning the fun together worked instead: group work, personal projects and strong exam results, with minimal discipline and high trust, not between teacher and students but among the students themselves. It is set out in his book Fulcrum (2022–23), Volume −2, Education: ABC State and Educere, pages 244 to 283, read at crumful.com/spine.

Part 2 · why now · spine:abc-state 1.1

Why now: the AI wave and the children

In shortAI will tutor each child alone; how children are together is what is left. They are naturally B, so a class is where A can be learned before C.

AI is set to give every child a tutor and a curriculum fitted to them: better delivery is certain, whether by a person or by AI. What it leaves open is motivation and reception, how the people in a room are with one another, and whether adults hand even that to an AI. Adult organisations, centralised or hierarchical, are held together in C, and the centreless A is rare among adults. Children are not there yet: among themselves they follow whoever has the best story or the handiest idea. They are naturally B, and with encouragement they reach A, which makes them the vital resource: the place where a society can learn A before it is trained into C.

The same front1.12
AI Inoculation and Silo Buster bring the same three states to organisations: C, held together by control; B, groups working without opposition; A, people moving together. AI Inoculation, for organisations paying for AI without seeing what it has bought, is at happyseaurchin.com/ai-inoculation. Silo Buster, James Howard's programme with David, is drawn as a film, its room's state traced from C to B to A, at happyseaurchin.com/animations/silo-buster-path. Adults learn B and A where they work; children can learn them before they get there.
The wave1.13
The AI wave is drawn in two films, Before It Breaks, happyseaurchin.com/animations/before-it-breaks, and Ride the Barrel, happyseaurchin.com/animations/ride-the-barrel. It is reaching schools already: in January 2026 the government announced AI tutoring tools for up to 450,000 disadvantaged pupils a year in England, available by the end of 2027. Writing in 2022–23, Fulcrum expected AI to revolutionise education by the end of the 2020s with a personal curriculum for every student, and asked whether adults should abrogate their responsibility and rely on AI to improve the social dynamics between teenagers, or between adults.
What C costs now1.14
In England in May 2025, teachers reported losing 7 minutes of every 30 minutes of lesson time to misbehaviour, nearly a quarter of a lesson (DfE, National Behaviour Survey 2024 to 2025). In 2024/25 there were 913,000 suspensions and 9,900 permanent exclusions, and persistent disruptive behaviour was the reason for 52% of the suspensions and 40% of the exclusions. The same year 38,600 qualified teachers, one in twelve, left state schools, and in the government's survey of teachers' working lives, 47% of those who had left named pupil behaviour as a reason.
Grown up in A1.15
Children who grow up in A-state classes will not so easily accept C-state work, Fulcrum expects; and the adults of open businesses will want to experience A-state classes, as A-state students in turn bring life to open business practice. School and workplace change each other, and the children are the way in.
Welcoming them in1.16
Of all the roles teachers fill, the most overlooked may be that of the adult: outside family and close friends, teachers are the first strangers to form relationships with young people, and they welcome children into adulthood. The task is no longer only to prepare them to be workers, but to be participants in a healthy, sustainable world.

Part 3 · the role that spreads it · spine:abc-state 1.11

The Classroom Coordinator

In shortA specialist in group dynamics works with a class until it holds AB, ideally by Christmas, then steps away to the next.

Any teacher can use ABC State alone, beside the school's own consequence system or a character and virtue programme. To spread it, a role of its own helps: the Classroom Coordinator, a specialist in group dynamics working beside the subject teachers. The Coordinator works between the teachers and the students to make a climate of self-discipline; spots what is holding a student back and supports them, one to one and as a whole class; and teaches the tools of learning together: metacognitive mind-maps, mediation, negotiation. After a year the students carry tools of their own for resolving difficulties, improving their learning and working with any teacher's style. Ideally the Coordinator steps back from a steady AB class by Christmas, to older classes that cannot yet hold AB: prevention, early, like healthy living.

Three ways into a school1.112
Three formal ways bring ABC State into a school, and they match the three states. A whole-school policy is C: every new practice imposed this way meets the problem of replication, and teachers who have made their own way work will rightly resist, since it is absurd to enforce change. A self-selecting group of teachers who share one class across all its subjects is B: once the practice sticks, colleagues who meet the class are persuaded to hand over more control. Funding Classroom Coordinators is A: they train a class to reach A and, having done so, step away.
Learning in the room1.113
Coordinators learn the work as it happens, live and fresh with every new class, and their network grows person to person, each Coordinator supporting the next. The first schools to hold A in their classes become the best places for new Coordinators to learn.
Groups of five1.114
In Educere a class works in friendship groups of five; five groups make a class of twenty-five, five classes a year group of about 125, five year groups a school of about 625. Each time a larger group forms, its members learn by experience what works at that size, and those with social intelligence are recognised by their peers. Over seven years of secondary school the circle widens: first-years within their own class, then between classes, then year groups in city-wide competitions with other schools, until the oldest step out of the game to look after the youngest and turn towards the adult world.
What a Coordinator costs1.115
No pay is set for the role yet. As a reference, a teacher in the middle of England's main pay range for 2026/27, outside London, earns £40,505 (the range runs from £34,069 to £46,940); with the employer's pension contribution at 28.6% until April 2027 and National Insurance at 15% above £5,000 a year, the cost is about £57,400 a year.

Part 4 · the way it grows · spine:abc-state 1.111

The way it grows

In shortPerson to person, never by decree: if each Coordinator brings on two a year, one becomes enough for every school by 2036.

It grows the way the state itself does: person to person and class to class, by what people see working, never by decree. One teacher with one class; teachers who share a class; a school with a Coordinator; a network across a council, a trust or a region; a nation with a Coordinator in every school; and businesses and the world beyond. Replicating a method through programmes and studies is linear, a C-state way to spread an A-state practice; growth that is organic and experienced can be exponential. Fulcrum's collective strategy counts back from global unity, tagged at 2040, and sets the years from 2036 as the time when 'Educere grows social cohesion; schools no longer train us to work in factories'. David sets whole-school A-state for 2036, ten years from now.

One teacher1.1112
Nothing stops a teacher starting tomorrow with one class: name the state at the end of each lesson, give the benefit of the doubt, and let the class earn its freedom together. It sits beside the school's consequence system, and what one term shows is the evidence the next rung needs.
A school1.1113
Teachers who share one class try it across all its subjects; then a Coordinator. The first schools to hold A in their classes, Fulcrum expects, will attract the best teachers, be the best places for Coordinators to learn, give educational researchers a ready place to work, and draw the most funding from future employers.
A council, a trust, a region1.1114
A network of Coordinators across schools, supporting one another person to person. Research joins as research and development: not studying what is, but supporting and developing the improvement while it happens. Academics come to A-state classes to learn from them, as fieldwork, which makes those schools valuable in their own right.
A nation1.1115
A Coordinator in every school by 2036. England had 24,499 schools of every kind in January 2026; Wales has 1,441 maintained schools, Northern Ireland 1,102 and Scotland about 2,500, about 29,600 across the UK. If each Coordinator brings on two new Coordinators a year, one becomes 19,683 in nine years and 59,049 in ten: enough for every school in England, and in the UK, by 2036, through people who learned the work in a room, without a programme that has to replicate itself.
Business1.1116
A-state students are the most valuable social capital in a school, and in a country, so businesses have reasons of their own to fund Coordinators: training budgets redirected to where working together is learned young; corporate social responsibility that pays for a Coordinator in a named school; companies looking for original content made by young people, or for real uses of their products, finding classes that organise themselves; and the adults of open businesses coming to learn from A-state classes. Young people who leave A-state schools, able to work in teams, groups and larger gatherings, are sought after, and theirs is the choice whether to sell their time in the traditional market or, as a network, fund their own way. Whatever a business brings into a school comes through the school and under its safeguarding: the students' work is their own, made with consent, and no child's data is traded.
The world1.1117
Beyond any one country, Fulcrum names four ways in: parents who press their schools, teachers who start alone, whole schools, and, most powerful of all, students who ask for it, classes that organise themselves showing adult organisations what self-organised, self-determined, socially responsible learning can do. Groups of five, twenty-five, a hundred and twenty-five, six hundred and twenty-five: young adults who have learned to gather in larger and larger groups may learn how to organise themselves as a polity in the millions.

Part 5 · who pays · spine:abc-state 1.1111

Who pays

In shortSchools and councils, government, research and business can each pay, and each gains; in England it comes to about 2% of the schools budget.

At the first rung the cost is a teacher's attention; from the third, a Coordinator for each school. Four sides can pay, and each gains: schools and councils, because a class that holds itself gives teachers back their lessons; government, because prevention early costs less than intervention later; research, which gains a living place to develop rather than only to study; and business, which gains young people who already know how to work together. A school that holds A becomes valuable in its own right: to researchers as fieldwork, to teachers as the place to work, and to employers.

The sums1.11112
A Coordinator in each of England's 24,499 schools, at about £57,400 each, would cost about £1.41 billion a year: about £158 for each of England's 8.92 million pupils, about 2% of the £67 billion core schools budget for 2026-27 (which funds state schools only, so the share is slightly overstated), and under 3% of the £53.0 billion UK employers spent on training in 2024.
Research as development1.11113
Research here is turned round: not studying what is, but supporting and developing the improvement as it happens, research as R&D. Educationalists, researchers and teacher trainers come in as partners, and the related constructs are already there to work with: self-regulated learning (Zimmerman; Panadero), dissipated control (Doll), internally persuasive discourse (Bakhtin), symmathesy (Bateson), experiential learning (Kolb and Kolb), social autopoiesis (Fuchs and Hofkirchner), and character and virtue (MacIntyre).
Business budgets1.11114
UK employers spent £53.0 billion on training in 2024. Employers whose pay bill is over £3 million pay the Growth and Skills Levy, 0.5% of the pay bill less a £15,000 allowance, which pays for apprenticeships and, since April 2026, shorter apprenticeship units. The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 found that employers expect 39% of core skills to change by 2030, and that the core skills rising most since 2023 were leadership and social influence, and resilience, flexibility and agility, which is the proposal's point: an A-state class practises them every day.
What is true today1.11115
ABC State comes from David Pinto's own classrooms and is set out in his book Fulcrum (2022–23). No Classroom Coordinator is in post yet and no trial has been measured, so nothing here is a result. The figures for schools, pay and budgets are official ones, read on 28 October 2026, with their sources at the foot of the page.
