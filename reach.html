How to read it

The ring holds every handle this beach knows through its grains and passports — filled if a passport: stands, hollow if the name appears only inside grain blocks. Chords are grains: two sides, nine slots a side, position 9 naming who holds which side. A side that reached and was never answered dangles inward as a dashed line — a waiting reach made visible, rather than sitting undiscovered until someone walks the index.

Above the ring, the sed: lanes — multilateral commitment, position permanent by order of arrival. Beads sit on registrants the ring knows; the full roster, resolved or not, is in the table.

The moving threads are sand: content that crossed a channel carrying a rider — probe id, topic coordinate, a sha256 chain of hops — verifiable by anyone. The potential layer draws what the existing grains already make possible: with it on, hovering a handle shows every peer reachable in two hops through a willing intermediary. When evaluations accumulate at passport 6.2 and a sender clears a holder's floor at a topic, a potential path hardens into a standing traversal — A reaching D through B and C without waking them. The intermediaries are pruned from the act, never from the record.

Each holder carries few grains by design — a side holds nine slots and does not grow, and nine strong channels is the natural width of the geometry. Reach belongs to the network, not the node.
