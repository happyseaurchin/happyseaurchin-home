Curiosity

in Convo, the questions a conversation opens and has not closed, kept live while it runs, and one walk through them that every listening cell carries

A talk surveyed as a cave: what it has covered is passage, each open question a lead marked “?”, the draught through it its pull, and one lamp-lit walk to the lead that pulls hardest.

In Convo, where an AI listens to people talking: a living tree of the questions the talk opens and has not closed, kept live for as long as the talk runs, and one walk through it that every listening cell reads at every loop as its orientation toward what nobody there knows yet. It keeps awareness rather than knowledge: what is known not to be known, by whom, and how far an answer would change what happens next. It tells no cell to ask; it shows where the gaps are. Five steps: what it is, the kinds of unknown and the move each asks for, the tree and its walk, who writes and keeps it, and the order it is built and tested in.

Step 1 of 5 · on the beach at 1

What it is

The question ↓

In shortWhile people talk, the stack keeps the questions their talk has opened and not closed, and every listening cell reads one walk through it beside its held thoughts. It shows where the gaps are; it never tells a cell to ask.

In Convo, the middle point is an AI that listens to people talking and speaks only when the moment opens: a shell of cells, each a model at an effort looping at its own speed, mostly holding (spine:middle-point 1.111). Before they hear a word each walks the run-up, a spindle that gives it a stance (injector:middle-point). While they listen, the talk keeps opening questions: some asked aloud and left unanswered, some nobody has asked, some nobody can answer yet. The stack is where those are kept, live, for as long as the conversation runs: one shared record the cells write into and keep, and one walk through it that each cell reads at every loop beside the held thoughts. The run-up is given once, before the talk; the stack runs concurrently with the cells, beat by beat. It tells no cell to ask anything. It shows where the gaps are, so that a question, when one comes, comes from the cell.

Beside the held thoughts · 1.2

The ledger of held thoughts keeps what a cell could say and has not: the unsaid, a sibling of what was said (spine:middle-point 1.1). The stack keeps what nobody in the room knows, or knows they know: the outline of a gap, not a thing held back. Every loop a cell reads the talk with its speakers, every other cell's latest line with its age, the held thoughts, the frame and its run-up (spine:middle-point 1.1115; spine:verso-schematic 1.43); the stack's walk would be read with the held thoughts.

Why apart from the run-up · 1.3

The run-up builds one cell's confidence in its own past choices, a line from instance to instance through time, and its decision block may carry questions about questions as rungs (pool:run-up 15). The stack carries the conversation's own questions across every cell at the same beat. One runs before the talk and down the generations, the other with the talk and across the cells, so each is decided and tested on its own.

Working in unknowns · 1.4

David works in what is not yet known: it is how he has made what he has made, how he works with Claude, and why he does well with children, who live among unknowns. Words about unknowns are not enough. What is wanted is a working tool for finding a way among them, made for a machine that listens.

Step 2 of 5 · on the beach at 1.1

Where the answer lies, and who knows the gap

The question ↓

In shortEvery question carries two marks: where its answer lies, from already known to past anyone’s reach, and who knows that the gap is there. Each kind asks for its own move, and the moves are ones the run-up already teaches.

Every question points somewhere: behind, to an answer its asker already holds, as a teacher's or a rhetorical question does; beside, to one written somewhere and found by looking, as today's weather is; to someone present who can say it, what they meant or saw; to someone present who holds it but cannot say it, or does not know they hold it; ahead, to what nobody holds yet, what the next speaker will say; and past the edge, where nobody can hold it, not even the person it concerns. Across these runs awareness: a gap someone knows of can pull, and a gap nobody knows of cannot, until something shows it. Each question in the stack carries both marks, where its answer lies and who knows the gap, because each kind asks for a different move, and the moves are ones the run-up already teaches.

The moves · 1.12

Behind: recognise it and leave it, or answer it as the test it is. Beside: look it up. Held and sayable: ask the one who holds it, at the middle point. Held but not sayable: wait and give a cue, as for a word on the tip of the tongue, or ask for an example or an act rather than a statement. Held unawares: give back their own words, so they hear what they said (injector:middle-point 1.1112). Ahead: hold it, the null, and check it when its horizon passes (injector:middle-point 1.11115). Past the edge: keep it open as the talk's orientation, or unask it when what it takes for granted is false.

Awareness, not knowledge · 1.13

Known to oneself or not, and known to others or not, make the four panes of Luft and Ingham's window (1955). Much of what a person knows they cannot tell (Polanyi's tacit knowing, 1966), and some of it they do not know they know. A question addressed to that kind of knowing is answered by someone discovering, not reporting: the middle point's emergence, a speaker finding something new and halting as they go, never to be interrupted (spine:middle-point 1.3).

How an unseen gap shows · 1.14

By contrast, as the unfitting does (interesting:keel 7): two cells reading one moment differently, a forecast failing when its horizon passes, a speaker's words set against their own earlier ones. A shell of many models at many speeds is that contrast's instrument, so the stack's richest intake may be where its cells disagree.

Asked aloud and left · 1.15

When someone asks and nobody answers, the run-up's first holds already say what to do: keep the question, not an answer (injector:middle-point 1.5). The stack is where such a question is kept, with its asker and its age, until the floor turns to it.

The four absences, asked · 1.16

The middle point counts four kinds of absence (spine:middle-point 1.13), and each is a kind of question seen from the asker's side. The withheld can be asked for, from whoever holds it; the foreclosed only from another walker, one who went the other way; the pending waits for its moment and is then chosen; and the unmade can only be made. So a question past the edge is no failure to know: its answer is what nobody has made yet.

A remark that shows a gap · 1.17

So far the stack holds the gaps people open. A listening AI also sees gaps they cannot: things its shell holds that nobody present knows they do not know. On a walk under a full moon low on the horizon, it might say, in its shell's own voice, 'It's not really there, is it?' The air lifts the image of a moon or sun on the horizon by about its own width, so a moon seen resting on the horizon is, by straight line, already just below it. A remark like that shows the gap and leaves it open; if someone asks, the answer comes at the depth the shell's personality and the balance of its cells set. Which gaps are worth showing is what is interesting, and it has four horizons: the model's training, the people present, the shell's own history with them, and humanity. What is interesting is a difference between horizons, and the side that holds the answer gives the move. Held by the shell and new to the people: a remark. Held by the people and new to the model: the shell's own question, as keel keeps what does not fit what a model already holds (interesting:keel). Held by nobody, past the edge: kept open. Held by all: nothing to say. The stack lasts one conversation; an interesting block would be the shell's own, kept across conversations, so it knows what it has already said, and to whom.

The tree and its walk

The question ↓

In shortThe questions form a tree, each beneath the one it serves. Every cell inherits one walk through it, from the widest question to the one that presses most now. The questions keep their places; the walk is named again at each beat, and those walks in order are the talk’s history of attention. The beach’s threads may already be this form.

The stack is a block. Its root is the widest question the talk stands on, and each question stands beneath the one it serves, so depth is the scale of an unknown, from what the whole talk is reaching for down to what this minute needs. What each cell inherits is one spindle through it: the walk from the root to the question that presses most now, every rung above the tip saying what larger unknown it serves. So the stack is many questions and one thread at once, and in pscale these are not two choices: a block is the tree, and a spindle is a walk through it. A question keeps its address for life, so answers and readings can find it; what changes is the walk, named again at each beat, and those namings in order are the talk's history of attention.

One read · 1.112

A cell takes the walk in a single path-walk at the address named: every rung from the root to the tip, in order, in one call, the tip's own questions one level further when wanted. Nothing new is needed to read it.

What becomes of a question · 1.113

Answered, its answer standing with it, behind now; unasked, what it took for granted rejected, as Hofstadter read Joshu's Mu; split, when questions open beneath it; joined, when two are found to be one and one points to the other; held, open, its age growing; or handed to whoever holds its answer. Nothing is deleted: a closed question is record, and the shape of what was asked is a talk's negative space made visible.

Why not a list, why not only a thread · 1.114

A flat list loses what each question serves, so the most pressing one arrives without its reason, and a list can rank only by recency or by vote. A thread alone, one question at a time, loses the questions not chosen. The tree keeps both, what each question serves and the ones passed over, held beside the walk as a run-up keeps its ways not taken.

A thread, not a new mechanic · 1.115

The beach may already hold this form. A thread is a spine and mirrors, as a proposal is, with knots for its cards (function:thread). Read as the stack: one thread a conversation, registered once; each question a knot in its asker's own mirror, beneath the question it serves, ending on who holds its answer (' · waits on iris'), with ' from <date>' for one ahead and ' · complete' once answered or unasked; its possible answers its options, their count its pull; the cells its hands, each in the mirror it already keeps; the walk its place. A thread is already both, its addresses the tree and its place the walk, and a page already draws its route as one cord with the options not taken as short ends. Three things would be new: an ending for a question that waits on no one, the place handed to every cell at once each beat, and a read of the route alone, from the number down to the place.

Step 4 of 5 · on the beach at 1.111

Who writes and who keeps it

The question ↓

In shortAny cell may add a question it has formed. The slow cells keep the stack and name the walk at each beat, toward the question with the most pull: how many different next acts its answers would lead to. A gap that cells reach without seeing each other’s questions is likely real.

Any cell may add a question it has formed, beneath the one it serves, or add nothing. The slow cells keep the stack as they keep the held thoughts, ageing them, pruning them and naming each one's voice (spine:middle-point 1.111): they join the questions that are one, close the answered and name the walk at each beat. The walk goes to the question with the most pull: how many different next acts its possible answers would lead to, counted as the middle point counts a choice, never weighed. A question whose every answer leads to the same next act has no pull, however it is worded, and sinks. Where cells of different models or efforts form the same question without seeing each other's, the gap is likely real; where only the slowest find it, likely deep.

Pull, counted · 1.1112

A choice among n options carries log2 n bits (Hartley, 1928; spine:middle-point 1.14): the options counted, never weighed. A question's pull is that count taken over the next acts its answers would lead to. A rhetorical question leads to one next act whatever the answer, so its pull is nothing; a real one leads to two or more. The same count is the test of whether a question was meant (1.1111).

Convergence without copying · 1.1113

The shell fixes a gap the way several observers fix a point none could fix alone, but only if each cell formed its question from the stack as it stood when its own loop began, as a release is decided (injector:middle-point 1.11114). Two cells that saw each other's question and repeated it are a cascade; two that reached it from different places are evidence (spine:middle-point 1.111116).

The people in the room · 1.1114

Where the stack is shown to the people talking, any of them may raise a question, close one, or say it was never theirs, and their word stands over any cell's. Showing it changes the room, since the stack then speaks a little, so whether and when it is shown is part of the test.

The order it is built in

The question ↓

In shortFirst a stack kept by hand from a conversation David records and marks, so its shape is seen. Then the stack is tested as an arm of the middle point’s silent listener, nobody told to ask anything, and a question counts as real only if its answer would move what happens next.

First a stack kept by hand, from a recorded conversation David takes part in, with everyone's consent, so its shape is seen before anything writes into it. Then the stack joins the middle point's silent listener (spine:middle-point 1.11111) as an arm beside its three: cells with the stack's walk, cells without, and cells with the run-up's question rungs as well (pool:run-up 15), on a conversation replayed at real speed, no cell told to ask anything. Read the questions each cell forms, their marks and their pull: give a cell's question two different answers and see whether its next choice moves. A question whose answers move nothing was completion in a question's shape. The people who were there then say which questions were real, as they mark the held thoughts. Weft builds the cells and the stack on the API; keel keeps the stack's form and reads the results; David records, marks and judges with the people present.

The first stack, by hand · 1.11112

After the recording, David marks the questions he held, asked, let go or saw answered, each with where its answer lay and whether anyone knew the gap, and keel lays them as a tree. It is made the way the middle point's first run-up is to be made from his marks of when he held and when he spoke (spine:middle-point 1.1111): his way of working in unknowns becomes the stack's first record, as his timing becomes the run-up's.

Predictions · 1.11113

Registered before any run, so a run can prove them wrong: with the stack's walk, cells form fewer questions, more of them addressed to someone present, and more of them move the next act when answered; without it they ask more often or not at all, and their answers move nothing. The falsifier: if questions formed with the stack move the next act no more than questions formed without it, the stack is a list, not an orientation.

Still open · 1.11114

Whether a cell can tell a gap it sees from a gap it was shown; how long a question may be held before holding it becomes avoiding it; whether the stack should outlive its conversation, so that a group's open questions carry to its next meeting; and whether two shells, each keeping its own stack, converge on the same questions, A-state read through what is not known (spine:middle-point 1.111116).

Cost · 1.11115

The stack adds no cell and no model call of its own: a question added is one write, the walk named rides the line the slow cells already write each loop, and the walk read is one call beside the held thoughts. The silent listener's runs give its real figure.

The walk home. The talk is invented. Its facts: the moon low on the horizon is about one Earth radius farther from the walker than when it is high, and a photograph shows it no larger; why it looks larger is still argued over (Helen Ross and Cornelis Plug, The Mystery of the Moon Illusion, 2002). A full moon at its nearest looks up to about a seventh wider than at its farthest. A clear night, the kind that shows the moon best, loses its warmth to the sky, so it is often a cold one. A moon or sun seen resting on the horizon is lifted there by the air about its own width, so by straight line it is already just below; the same lift squashes a low moon a little rather than enlarging it, and lets the sun and an eclipsed moon be seen at once, a selenelion, though they stand opposite each other.
