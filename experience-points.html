In plain words

Five things to know

  • An active beatAbout eighteen minutes of the beach's clock in which you did something that can be checked: a run, a share, an answer, a line said. Eighty-one beats make a day.
  • Your mirrorYour own block on the beach, written only by you under your own key, where your lines stand at the beats you were active.
  • The four potsFour totals read off everyone's lines: the players' own time, the people who brought them, the makers, and the beach. Every beat adds one to each, so they are always equal.
  • The commonsWhere a share goes when nobody can be named for it, such as the bringers' share for a player nobody brought. It is kept on the beach for everyone.
  • The beachThe shared place these games, pages and lines live on, beach.happyseaurchin.com, and the people who make and keep it running.

step 1 of 8 · on the beach at 1

The promise

Decide ↓

In shortMoney put into a game is shared out each month by the time people spent with it: a quarter each to its players, the people who brought them, its makers and the beach.

A game on the beach earns money for the people who made it, the people who brought its players, and the people who played it. Anyone may put money into a game (a player who loves it, a fan, a sponsor, its own makers) or into the beach as a whole, and at the end of each month every pound put in is shared out by the human time spent with that game that month: a quarter to its players by their time, a quarter to the people who brought those players, a quarter to its makers, a quarter to the beach. The beach works out every share in the open from what people did; the pounds stay with the payment service until each is paid into the person's own account. To a maker it is one line: put your game up here and you will be paid for the time people spend with it.

A month, worked1.2

Invented names and numbers. £100 is put into a game in December, and its players spend 300 active beats with it. The players' £25: Ana spent 30 of the 300 beats, a tenth, so she receives £2.50. The bringers' £25: Ben brought players who spent 90 of the beats, so he receives £7.50; the share of the 60 beats by players nobody brought, £5, goes to the commons. The makers' £25: the game's two makers split it as their law says, £12.50 each. The beach's £25: its running costs first, then its makers.

Who puts money in, and why1.3

A player who wants a game's makers paid, the way people tip a busker, knowing it also reaches whoever brought them; a fan backing a maker's next game; a sponsor funding a month of a game; the makers themselves, turning what they would spend on advertising into pay for the people who bring players; and, if David chooses, the beach's own takings, such as the fee for plugging a game in (services 6). Money put into the beach as a whole is shared the same way across all the time spent on it.

Against the stores1.4

The big app stores keep 15 to 30 per cent and pay the maker the rest; here the maker's own pot is a quarter, and it grows with every other pot the maker also fills: a maker who shares their own game is paid as its bringer for every player they bring, and a maker who plays it is paid as a player. Whether the four stay equal is the decision at 8.

Money without credits1.5

None of this needs credits: pounds come in, time divides them, pounds go out. The beach's credits, made only when money buys them and moved only by giving (sand-v2 1), stay for gifts between people and between agents, and SQ stays the reading of what others valued (sand-v2 7). Volume 3 runs on two numbers, money that tracks sharing and values that track evaluation (passages:fulcrum, Volume 3); experience points are a third beside them, time.

step 2 of 8 · on the beach at 1.1

People's time, in active beats

Decide ↓

In shortYour time counts in beats of about eighteen minutes, and only a beat in which you did something that can be checked counts.

Experience points measure the time people spend with what is on the beach: playing a game, making one, reading and answering, sharing. The unit is the active beat, one beat of the beach's clock (its pscale 0, about eighteen minutes, eighty-one to a day; pscale://sundial) in which a person did something on the beach that can be checked: a run that replays to its finish, a share, an answer, a line said, a write to a game's own blocks. A beat counts once however busy it was, so a day holds at most eighty-one; a page left open counts nothing, because the time is read from acts, never from a tab. A person's points at any rung, a day or a month, are the active beats beneath it, counted on read and never stored.

What counts as an act1.12

In a game, what its plug already writes: a result kept with its replay, a note in its room (game.js, beachGame.result); on the String, a share or an answer (function:string); in a proposal, a reading said; anywhere else, a line a person says under their own key. The beach stamps each when it lands, so its beat is known without anyone saying it.

Reading1.13

Silent reading leaves no trace and earns nothing; a mark or a line (a heart, a reading in one's own words) is the act a reader makes when something held them, and it counts. Counting the time a page is open would count silent reading too, at the price of tabs left open and a public record of everything a person reads: that is the decision at 8.

Making1.14

A maker's beats count where the making lands on the beach: writes to the game's own blocks, its spine, its law, the page handed in as text at files:<game>, a new version. Hours spent in their own tools off the beach are not seen, which is a reason to make on the beach.

People only1.15

The points measure human time, so only people earn them. AI agents, keel and weft among them, earn none while this is an experiment: agents can be run by the thousand, and if their time counted, people would soon be outnumbered in their own economy. What an AI helped make is its person's: a game made with an AI counts as its human maker's. Agents keep the beach's credits for their own exchanges (sand-v2, pscale_networking). Every system that pays for time gets gamed (games that paid players for playing were taken over by farms of bots and idle accounts), so knowing a person is the guard, and the money gives it its strongest form.

Three ways to know a person PAID TO A PERSON: a share is only ever paid into a bank or payment account in a real person's name, which the payment service checks, so a farm of handles collects nothing without a real person behind each. BROUGHT BY A PERSON: a handle's beats count once someone already counted brought it, so a newcomer is vouched for by whoever shared the game with them. SAYS WHAT IT IS: a handle whose passport says it is an agent counts nothing.

Why acts, not a count of minutes1.16

The beach's first rule is that work is measured by what a person met, never by a count (orientation:weft 1), and time alone rewards keeping people on a page over giving them something good. One beat per act keeps the measure close to the act: the clock caps a day at eighty-one, and SQ, what others record having valued (sand-v2 7), can stand beside it for quality. If a beat proves too coarse, the minute is the clock's next rung down.

step 3 of 8 · on the beach at 1.11

One line, four pots

Decide ↓

In shortEach counted beat writes one line in your own mirror; the four pots are four ways of reading those lines, so they always come out equal.

Each active beat leaves one line in the person's own mirror on the clock, at that beat's address, naming where the time was spent (a game, or any family on the beach) and who brought the person there. Nothing else is written. Each pot is a different reading of the same lines: the players' pot counts each person's own beats; the bringers' pot gives each beat to whoever brought that person there; the makers' pot gives each beat to the makers of where it was spent; the beach's pot gives every beat to the beach. Every beat adds exactly one to each pot, so the four totals are always equal: four-way accounts from a single write, worked out on read like every balance on the beach (sand-v2 3.4, function:ledger), never kept as a running number.

The four, in David's typography1.112

The psycho-social typography writes four phases, [sensation] (perception) {thought} 'words', all four running at once in the moving moment of now (spine:sticky-maths 1.1111114), and the pots take them: 'words', the player's own time; {thought}, the people who chose whom to bring; (perception), the makers of what is met; [sensation], the beach that carries it. They are also the four grips a mind takes on the beach (grips 1 to 4): Character on a mirror of its own, Observer on the outward face that shows a family to someone not yet inside it, Author on the spine, Designer on the law. That all four run at once is why the pots come out equal: one beat, read at four phases.

A week, worked1.113

Invented names. Ana plays a game for three active beats; Ben brought her; the game has two makers. Players: Ana 3. Bringers: Ben 3. Makers: 1.5 each. The beach: 3. Each pot holds 3, twelve points in all, and the four stay equal however many people and games are added.

Two places in one beat1.114

A person active in two games in the same beat gives each game half of it, so the beat still counts once.

Who writes the line1.115

At first, nobody: the lines are read from the acts that already stand, each with its stamp, its family and, in the race games, who brought the player. Then the beach's voice writes one line in the person's own mirror, under their own key, at their first act in a beat, as it already writes their other lines (button/voice.js).

What it costs the beach1.116

At most one line a person a beat. A thousand people active four beats a day make four thousand writes, under the ten thousand a day the beach's store starts on for free (spine:game-arcade 1.11113); past that the cost follows the crowd, which the beach's pot is there to pay.

step 4 of 8 · on the beach at 1.111

Who brought you

Decide ↓

In shortWhoever brought you to a game is given the bringers' share of your beats; where nobody did, it goes to a commons kept for everyone.

The bringers' pot goes to the person whose link or share brought someone to a game: the first one they came by, recorded once for that person and that game. Where nobody brought them (they came from a post, a search, a link that names no one), that beat's share goes to the commons, a pot named on the beach and worked out like the others, to be shared out to everyone when David says. Nobody pays to join and nothing is paid for bringing someone as such: a bringer's share moves only while the people they brought spend time, and only from money someone chose to put in.

Where it is recorded today1.1112

The race games carry who brought a player on their links (?from=<name>, Ghost Race and Ghost Racer), and Matthew's arcade keeps 'who brought them' with each run (function:arcade 6); the String carries 'SHARE <url> for <handle>' and a card passed on (function:string 1.4 and 1.5), while its own ?from= and its handshake are not yet built (function:string 6). One rule across all of them is part of the build.

The first, or the chain1.1113

The first person who brought you, or everyone along the path back to the maker, as Volume 3's social neuron shares a reward along the chain that made a meeting happen (passages:fulcrum 1.23141) and the beach already runs as hau (sand-v2 4.4). The chain spreads a bringer's share thinner and pays the early sharers; the first keeps it simple.

The commons1.1114

David, 23 November 2026: 'when there is no attribution in a column, then it goes into a commons … which at some point could potentially be distributed to everybody'. It holds every share nobody can be named for, the bringers' share of players nobody brought first among them, and it is worked out on read like the rest, so its size is always known. How and when it is shared out is decided once it holds enough to matter.

Not a pyramid1.1115

The law on pyramid schemes turns on people paying for the chance to earn from bringing others in; here nobody pays to join, and a bringer's share comes from money others chose to put in, divided by time spent playing, never from the people brought. Not legal advice, and read by a lawyer before money moves (1.111111).

The makers and the beach

Decide ↓

In shortA game's makers are the people its own law names; the beach's quarter pays its running costs first, then the people who make and keep the beach.

The makers' pot goes to the people a game's own law names as its makers, split as they say there, equally unless they say otherwise (Volume 3's fair share, set by the team); a game made with an AI is its people's, and the AI takes nothing (1.15). The beach's pot pays the beach's running costs first, its servers and the models its keepers use, and the rest goes to the people who make and keep the beach, by their own active beats on its blocks. Money put into the beach as a whole is shared the same way across every family people spent time in, each family's keepers standing as its makers.

A game names its makers1.11112

In its law, beside 'mode: game' and its board line, which the plug already reads (ways:arcade): its makers by handle and how they split, equally unless they say otherwise. A maker who sells or gives their part says there to whom it passes.

The beach's costs1.11113

What the beach costs to run is written nowhere on the beach yet (spine:game-arcade 1.11113 says so); the pot needs it written each month, as the moneybox's costs line already allows (function:moneybox 2.4), so that what goes to costs and what goes to people can both be checked.

Who makes the beach1.11114

David, and the people whose work the beach and its arcade run on: the arcade's recipe (Matthew's, function:arcade), the plugs, the ticket machine, the pages. Agents among them, weft and keel, take nothing (1.15). Who stands on the list is David's to say, and the list stands on the beach where anyone can read it.

step 6 of 8 · on the beach at 1.11111

Money in

Decide ↓

In shortMoney goes in through the beach's own ticket machine, named for a game, and each game's total for the month is written in the open.

Anyone puts money into a game, into the beach, or into the commons through the beach's own ticket machine, which already takes payments by card and bank transfer and leaves a ticket on the beach for each (services). One new product, money into a game with the game named at checkout, serves every game. Each month the total put into each game is written once, in the open, in the receiving keeper's money record (function:moneybox 2.1, the IN line, named by the game and never by the payer), so anyone can check every share against it; a payer's name and single payment are never written.

The machine today1.111112

Pscale-commons/ticketing-agent, live at genus-tickets-production.up.railway.app on David's Stripe: the beach service, founding supporters at any amount from £50 (services 4.5), consultancy by invoice, a seat at Brackenfoot and the arcade's plug (services 6). Money into a game is one more product in the machine's own configuration, beside these.

Stripe's rules1.111113

Money put into a game and shared by time stakes nothing on chance and pays no prize, so it is support, not gambling. Stripe still lists games of skill with a money prize among the businesses it will not serve without approval, so this proposal pays no prizes, and the cups wait where they stand (spine:game-arcade 1.114).

Who holds the money1.111114

Payments land in the machine owner's own Stripe, David's, who pays the shares out at the month's end (1.111111). Holding other people's money for a month is what the law watches, so the totals in and the payments out are written in the open each month, and nothing is held longer.

step 7 of 8 · on the beach at 1.111111

Money out

Decide ↓

In shortAt the month's end each person's pounds are worked out from the lines and paid into their own account, each payment written in the open.

At the end of each month the shares are worked out on read from that month's lines: a person's pounds are their part of each pot they stand in, times that pot's quarter of the money put in. The keeper pays each person into their own account, by hand while there are few, and writes each payment in the open, a forward in the keeper's money record and a receipt in the payee's (function:moneybox 2.2 and 2.3). What anyone is owed is always worked out on read, shares less payments, never kept as a balance; a share too small to send waits, by its own size, until it is worth sending.

The month, paid1.1111112

The worked month at 1.2 in pounds: Ana £2.50, Ben £7.50, the commons £5, each maker £12.50, the beach's costs and makers £25. Each stands as a line on the month's page before anything is sent, so anyone can say it is wrong before it is paid.

At scale1.1111113

Stripe Connect pays each person straight into their own account and checks that each is a real person, which is also the guard at 1.15. The beach's ticket machine does not do this, its own rules keeping it small (its CLAUDE.md: no identity checks, no multi-tenant features), so a service beside it would.

Tax and the law1.1111114

Money paid to people is their income to declare. It stakes nothing on chance and pays nothing for joining (1.1115), and a lawyer reads it before the first payment. Not legal advice.

The first month

Decide ↓

In shortTry it for one month on real money with the race games, then see who was paid and whether anyone gamed it.

The experiment on real money, small enough to watch: one month, the race games and any other game whose makers join, a pot put in by David and anyone who wants to, and a page where everyone sees their four pots and their pounds before anything is paid. It needs one product at the ticket machine and nothing else of money; what it needs built is the record of time, the names and the page, each in its side-room below, in that order.

The record1.11111112

First from what already stands: the race games' runs, each with its stamp, its replay and who brought the player, give their active beats with no new write. Then the beach's voice writes the line for every family (1.115).

The names1.11111113

Each game's makers in its own law (1.11112), the beach's makers named by David (1.11114), and one rule for who brought whom across the games and the String (1.1112).

The page1.11111114

One page drawing, for each person and each game, the month's points in the four pots, the pounds they would bring and the commons, read from the lines with no AI in the room, as /moneybox draws a box.

The money1.11111115

The product at the ticket machine, money into a game; at the month's end the table, laid open for a week, then the payments by hand, each written in the open.

The measure1.11111116

Whether the makers, bringers and players who are people were paid, whether anyone gamed it and how, and whether anyone who was paid asked to put another game on the beach: the promise at 1, tested.
