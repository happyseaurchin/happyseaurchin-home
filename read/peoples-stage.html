A proposal for FLIRT 2026 · Brighton, 2 and 3 November

The People's Stage

Delegates arrive already knowing who they should meet. They spend the two days making decisions together. The stages carry the voices the delegates themselves ask to hear.

From David Pinto, with keel (Claude). Built on the beach, an open commons any AI app can use.

Why change how a conference works

Most of a conference goes on finding out who is there. People hear information they could have read beforehand, and the people they most needed to meet are often the ones they pass in a corridor.

Stage time usually goes to sponsors and to people who are good at promoting themselves. It works like a game where players pay to win: whoever buys the kit beats whoever built their skill from scratch. A festival about responsible technology can choose its voices another way.

How the two days work

  1. Before

    Each delegate's own AI finds their meetings

    With the AI they already use, Claude, ChatGPT or another, each delegate builds a full picture: who they are, the change their organisation is in the middle of, what they hope for and what they need. The AIs read these pictures against each other and propose meetings where two people could make a decision together. Delegates arrive with a short list and a reason for each meeting that both sides recognise.

  2. During

    Meetings are for deciding

    Ten minutes at a table, set up from a phone in a minute. Each meeting ends with one next step for each person, or none. Time that used to go on discovering who is there goes on doing something together.

  3. Then

    The delegates choose who is heard

    After meeting someone, a delegate can say that person should be heard by a table, a room, a hall or everyone, and why, in their own name. The number of different delegates who say so decides. By the close of each day the stages carry the people twenty or thirty others asked to hear. Nobody pays for a slot, and nobody counts for themselves.

Already running

The first piece is live now and free to try on any phone, with no app and no sign-up. People answer under a name they make up, their answers are public, and they can take them down at any time.

  • The Brighton room

    happyseaurchin.com/premiere/brighton-room

    Two minutes from a QR code. Starlings over the West Pier make a word, each person says what they came for and what they could give, then sees the whole room beside their own answers. A few times a day the room names the people whose lines meet.

  • With your own AI

    The room's last screen hands each person one line to paste into the AI they already use. With nothing to set up, it reads the room with them and plans who to find. With a five-minute connector it can put up their profile, find a time from their calendar and set up the meeting.

  • Ten minutes

    happyseaurchin.com/ring/ten-minutes

    A meeting design for two people: one minute to say hello, seven to talk, two to name a next step.

  • The people's stage

    Recommendations, each signed and timed, counted at set points through both days. Anyone can read the count and see why someone has been asked onto a stage. On the first evening we will host a small stage of our own for the voices the delegates choose.

What it holds to

  • Every answer stays in its writer's own name and can be withdrawn.
  • Each person's own AI does the work, so no platform sits in the middle reading everyone.
  • Nothing goes up without the person's yes, and nothing about anyone is guessed.
  • No money buys a meeting or a slot on a stage.
  • Success is counted in meetings held and decisions made, never in time spent on a screen.
Our question to the organisers

Would you test it with us?

What you already have

You may already have a way for delegates to find each other. We would like to hear about it, and we will fit around it rather than compete with it.

A collaborative trial

Would you host an open session, or a corner of each day, where delegates try three to five engagement systems side by side, ours among them, and compare them together at the end of the day and the next morning?

What we would measure

The meetings that actually happened, the next steps agreed in them, the voices the delegates asked to hear, and the delegates' own verdict on which system helped them most.

What it costs you

A slot in the programme and a mention to delegates. There is no charge, and the room runs alongside the festival either way.
