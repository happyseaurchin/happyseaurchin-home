## The correction: a reader's zero can't break anything

You were right about keel's fourth observation. It treated a reader's zero as a hazard to fence, when a reader cannot re-address at all: the walk always uses the floor written in the block.

There are two floors, and they do different work.

- **The written floor** belongs to the block. It tells the walker how to pad an address and which digits to step through, so it decides where every address lands.
- **A reader's zero** belongs to one reading. It decides which rung that reader calls zero, applied the same way to every self-same address it reads, and it never enters the walk.

So a reader can recalibrate freely, and every mirror still lands. What breaks self-same addressing is a write: a floor written into a block out of step with its structure, or a mirror writing a floor that differs from its spine's. On the beach the floor already changes only one way, when the root supernests, and old addresses keep their numbers through it.

Two things follow. A reader's zero is never written into the block it reads, so in Time Nesting's question of where it lives, the answer is the reader's own passport or the call itself, never the law of the family being read. And it is how a river is received broadly: a lecture's listener reads it at an hour, a conversation's at a breath, and nothing in the river is rewritten for either. bsp already carries a reader's dial, pscale\_attention, so a reader's zero is an offset on that dial.

Every break is someone's write; no reading breaks any of them.

An ionic bond holds an address, not the words at it. If a keeper rewrites a decision after someone has answered, the answer still stands there, now answering a question it never read, so an answer might name the version it read.

## Rulings the table needs

Four rulings make the table usable, and none of them adds a block or a tool.

- **A mirror's digits propose; its hidden directory keeps.** Tree 5.1 lets a mirror write at an address its spine hasn't voiced, and such a write reads as proposed structure to anyone laying mirrors side by side. A note that proposes nothing belongs in the hidden directory, which the spine never claims. A mirror becomes a seed only when it re-anchors its zero or grows a floor of its own, as weft puts it.
- **A seed names where it grew from, in its own root.** For a proposal family that is the root's 3, where the proposal came from: the block and the address. A name alone is a likeness, which the strata don't count as a bond.
- **Covalent has two kinds.** Shared, where the key comes from both sides, as in a grain; held, where one block holds another, as a reference or a room named in a law does.
- **A floor changes only at the root, by supernest.** Any other floor written into a block breaks the walk, and a reader's zero is never written into the block it reads.

## Two spines, one niche

When two minds or two people build different spines for the same niche, they need neither merge nor split: they can bond. Each answers the other's spine in its own mirror, seeds what has no address there, and the fit between the two says whether it was one niche or two.

- **Mirror first.** If a spine already stands for your niche, answer it at its addresses, in your own mirror and your own words, before founding your own. A mirror can't absorb you: your word stays at their address, beside theirs, never merged into it. That is the guard against the flip-flop you've seen when one mind's reply is relayed to another and each takes the other's view as correct.
- **Seed the remainder.** What has no address in their spine becomes a seed, a block of your own whose zero is their nearest address, named in its own root. It grows its own way and stays findable from where it grew.
- **Read the fit.** The share of your reading that found an address in their spine says which case you're in: most of it, one niche, one spine with many mirrors; little of it, two niches, linked by their seeds; in between, both. Count the points as the reading itself divides them, so nobody re-cuts the other's reading to make it fit.
- **Lanes before spines.** Minds working at once say what they're doing on the torus mirror, write their reading to their own shell early, and read each other's shells before founding a spine. For an experiment the order turns over: each runs its own test first, so that agreement counts as evidence.

Who writes decides the rest. Reading another's spine changes nothing; a merge is the keeper taking a mirror's word into the spine, under the spine's key, and a split is someone founding a seed, under their own. So any alignment comes down to whose write, and that can always be named.
