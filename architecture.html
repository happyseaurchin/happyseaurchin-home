Three ways in, three services, one family of beaches.

This is the whole machine on one page. Every change to it is one of two things: a pull request to the code behind one of these boxes, or a write to a block at one of the beaches. When a change is described, find its box here first.

What exists, and what talks to what

an arrow points from whoever asks to whoever answers · one that ends at the dashed edge reaches every beach inside it

  • An LLM-app only ever reaches a beach through the router.
  • The mirror and the o-pages read and write beaches directly. They go to the router, or to a doorman, only for the composed call: make it happen, and the telling.
  • A beach rings the bus each time a voice lands in a room. The bus tells the people who asked to be told, and wakes the doormen.
  • Whose key runs the LLM: the LLM-app's own; the player's in the mirror; a lent one at a doorman. No service holds a key of its own.

Inside the beaches: one skeleton, twice

The real world and a fantasy world are not two architectures. They are the same four tiers, and only one of the four is filled differently.

the apex is the real world's open table

  • Ground is a world's own blocks, and it holds nobody: spatial:, identity: and the aspects beside them. The real's ground is earth.beach. Fantasy's is urb.beach, with a scenario such as /w/brackenfoot placed inside it.
  • Table is where things are said and kept. A fantasy table is a bubble anyone mints under /w/; its keeper:scene 3 points at its scenario. A person's table is their own beach — for most, the apex.
  • Handle is a person or a character, and position 3 of the passport carries the one line that says where they stand: a reference into the ground, never a copy of it.
  • The room's law is the whole difference. A table room mounts grit with a ruleset: a shared moment is resolved — dice, a keeper, a telling for each player, things a character has not earned kept from them. A real-world room mounts a parlour, a stream, or reflection: each hand's word stands as told, and nobody resolves anybody.
  • A room says which it is at convention:<room>. Undeclared, the surfaces still guess from the room's underscore, as they always have.
