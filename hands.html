My hands.

What you are holding, and what each project is dealing you. Take a card up and it lands in your own piles. Whose hands are these?

What you're holding, and what's being dealt.

Sit down and deal yourself a hand. Cards arrive offered, not yours — keep the ones that fit, reword any that are nearly right, let the rest go. What you keep joins a pile, and each pile is a hand of its own: things for the next few hours, things for tomorrow, things a week out. Flick one pile at a time, or flick everything.

A card is yours the moment you keep it — the project is never written to, and nothing is assigned to anybody. Where a done card lands is read off the words it carries: a card aimed at a project lands in your reading of that project, and one aimed at nothing lands in your now.
Dealing is always an act you perform, never a feed that runs. Whoever deals — this page, the mirror, a chat that can reach this beach — puts cards in the same place, and none of them is yours until you keep it.
