The weightings, run

A game sells a thousand £5 tickets. Who could ask for what?

Each ticket is shared the moment it lands, by the game's own law: 80% to its creator, 10% to the people who brought its players, 5% held for its players' tournament, and 5% to the beach's server. The month below is invented, 360 players and five distributors, and runs through the beach's own reader, so what it shows is what the reader would say.

Every name and number is invented, and the working is the beach's. A share is potential until it is asked for: Shore's keeper checks an ask, then pays it by hand.

The game's law · a support line

A ticket, shared as it lands

A game's law carries one support line, and the reader shares every ticket by it at the moment the ticket lands. These are the lines it would read for an invented race game, Reef Run, at David's weights: 80% on the creator, 10% to distributors, 5% to the players for a tournament to come, and 5% to the beach's server.


      
The weights are in the reader's order, the players, the bringers, the makers and the beach (spine:experience-points 1.11). The line 'the players:' is new with this lab: it sends the players' part to the game's cup instead of to each player by their beats.

        What the numbers are
        
      
        Each is potential: the most that person could ask Shore's keeper for, their exact share of each ticket as the game stood when it landed, added up and made whole pence once. Nothing moves until they ask, and the keeper checks the ask against their record before paying (spine:shore 1.11, 1.111). What they were paid before comes off.
        
      
        The creator's, the cup's and the server's parts are fixed by the law: 80, 5 and 5 pence in each pound, whoever plays. Only the distributors' 10% depends on people: whose shares brought the players, and how much those players raced.

            The same code
            
      
            What runs here runs on the beach
            
      
            The month is invented in your browser by weightings-world.js as the blocks the beach would hold: the game's law, its room of finished races, the String of who brought whom, and the ledger's lines. Then it is shared by weigh.js, the same file the beach's XP reader runs on the real blocks. So this lab and the reader agree to the penny.
            
      
            On the beach, the reader answers two questions. /api/xp?family=<name> gives a family's four columns at any moment, and how a sum would be shared. /api/xp?ledger=shore runs the weightings over Shore's ledger: every sum that names a family is shared as that family stood when the sum landed, and what each person was paid comes off. Its checks are node scripts/check-xp.mjs, among them this month run both ways, sum by sum and by the running index, agreeing to the penny.
            
    
            
    
            
      
            Shore's ledger, read now
            
      
            reading ledger:shore through the reader…
            
      
            The ledger is the keeper's: one line for each sum in, 'IN — £<amount> · from <a name> · for <a family's name, or anything>', and one for each reward paid (function:shore 8). A sum for a game shares by that game's law; a sum for anything waits for the keeper.
            
    
            
  
            

            


            
  
            Where this came from
            
  
            David's weights, and his question
            
  
            David to weft, 25 November 2026, on Shore's weightings: 'Especially for use case of a game, and users get £5 tickets which weigh the game linear system with 80% on Creator, 5% players (future tournament), 10% distributors, and 5% beach server. And eventually after 1000 tickets, the potential reward for the different parties.' His answers at spine:experience-points of 23 November set the rest: the columns WEIGHTED, 'determined by collective decision' (1.8, where he added, 'I suspect money distribution to players is through tournaments'); the bringers' share along the CHAIN (1.11118); makers split equally (1.111118).
            
  
            Whether Shore reads shares this way, or writes them as credits when the money lands, is his decision at spine:shore 1.1118 (WEIGHTINGS, CREDITS or NOT YET). Experiment 5 shows what that choice changes.
