Where it stands
In plain words

Five things to know

  • VersoThe middle point's app: a page on David's phone and two programs on his Mac that listen to people talking at three speeds and speak only at the middle point. Live at /verso since 5 November 2026.
  • The middle pointThe moment verso may speak: after someone's thought has landed, while nobody else is about to say it, and while the topic is still open.
  • A held thoughtSomething verso has noticed and not said, kept with its urge to be said. The urge grows when cells at different speeds each release it, and fades as the talk moves on.
  • The run-upSix choices each cell walks before it hears a word, so it starts the talk with a stance; the choices it passes become its first held thoughts.
  • A colourA key a group shares: lines sealed in it read as words to its holders and as one grey line to everyone else.
How it speaks

Two keys, turned together

  1. A held thought's urge reaches the threshold, 3 at the start: a release from all three speeds.
  2. The floor is more open than the window, 0.6 at the start: a pause of almost a second.
  3. In the spoken-to mode, someone has also said its name in the last forty seconds.

Then it speaks a phrase at a time and stops at once when someone starts to talk. A risk to anyone's safety always speaks.

The judge

Decide ↓

In shortBefore it speaks in front of anyone, verso replays one real recorded conversation silently, and the people who were in it judge where it would have spoken.

Before verso speaks in front of people it is judged on real talk, by the people who were in it. The run-up names a judge, not compute, as what holds the work back (spine:run-up 1.11112), and the middle point's order begins with the silent listener: a real conversation recorded with everyone's agreement, its moments marked by David, replayed at real speed in three arms with verso marking where it would have spoken, and judged by the people who were there, a missed moment counted as a miss (spine:middle-point 1.11111, which David answered GO). Everything for it is built (thread 19); what has never happened is the recording (spine:verso-schematic 1.749).

The recording1.112

Ten minutes first, then the hour, under a cap David names; kept off the public beach, on the Mac or in a grain among the people recorded, with David's marks pinned to their times in the recording (middle-point:weft 1.11118 and 1.111118). Matthew asked for no recordings of recovery groups yet (middle-point:Phenomemental 1.118).

What the judges mark1.113

On the replay a judge taps the score: it spoke well, it should have spoken, the silence felt right, and whether each of verso's marks was a real moment (spine:verso-schematic 1.71). Matthew asked that each mark also be rated as opening or closing, and that holding back too much and stepping in too much be counted apart (middle-point:Phenomemental 1.111118); James asked for one agreed measure, whether the people found each interruption helpful and how often it held back (middle-point:emojisword 1.8).

A judge by measure1.114

Determinacy asks whether a run-up works when its intention's odds against the strongest rival climb at a steady slope and cross over within a few hand-offs, read by T, rather than when judges grade it well on average (spine:determinacy 4.8, an open question asked of David among others). The people's marks are what any such measure is checked against (spine:run-up 1.11112).
