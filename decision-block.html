The Decision Blocka route set down a panel, one junction at a time

The pscale block a run-up is walked through. An LLM instance reads it by descending pscale and takes one option at each rung; the options it takes become its decision-spindle, a little more context at every rung, until the spindle runs through the block. The run-up (spine:run-up) is its law, how it is read; the decision block is what is read, and what it should say has not yet been decided. This family scopes how it is made: what each rung asks, the experiments that supply those choices and their order, what is varied around the walk, what is read afterwards, and the one test that counts, whether Convo, the middle point's stack of models at several efforts, speaks at the right time and says something that matters. David's scope runs down the steps; weft's observations stand at 4.

Drawn as a signal box’s route panel: the aim at the top, each rung a junction, each option a way out of it. Lay the block out below, walk it, and keep your own cut in your mirror on the beach. David’s six decisions are lamps on the panel.

    In plain words

    Seven things to know

    • The decision blockThe block a run-up is walked through: one rung after another, each a choice of ways. What it should say is what this proposal is about.
    • A rungOne choice in the block: a question, and up to nine ways of answering it.
    • The decision-spindleThe route one instance sets through the block, one way taken at each rung: its own record of the walk.
    • The run-upThe law that says how the block is read, and the walk itself, made before the instance hears a word.
    • The ways not takenThe options passed at each rung, held at the side like sidings: the negative space.
    • The flipThe same spindle read from its other end, the deepest choice first, the way p-adic numbers count.
    • FutureductionDavid’s word for what the walk is meant to leave in what an instance says next: a sense of what is about to be.
    The run-up, as the panel shows it

    How a route is set

    1. The instance starts at the aim, at the top of the block.
    2. At each rung it takes one way; the ways it passes stay lit, held.
    3. Each rung it passes adds a little more to what it carries.
    4. At the bottom, the route it set is its decision-spindle, and it goes on with that behind it.

    Every cell of the middle point’s stack walks its own route through the same block. Convo speaks with what the walks leave.

    The object

    David's decision ↓

    In shortThe block is what an instance walks; the run-up is how it walks it; the route it sets is its decision-spindle.

    A block and the law it is read under, the beach's own pair: the decision block a spine, the run-up its function. Once the block is built, an instance reads it as the run-up instructs, one rung at a time, until it has derived its decision-spindle. Each cell of the middle point's stack conducts its own run-up through the same block, and the spindle each derives is hoped to leave a future-orientation in what it then says, a sense of what is about to be, which David calls futureduction (spine:determinacy 1.11116). A peculiar object: a block whose reading is the experiment.

    The first draft1.2

    Injector:middle-point, which every cell of verso walks today: a root that states a future-perfect aim, then six rungs down its 1, the first hold, the maths of the unsaid, reflexive intent, the release, the soliton and the records of play, each a ring of two to five ways with a hinge at its 8, the unknown that rung resolves later. It was drafted on 5 November 2026 for the stance it lights, from David's CHANGE at middle-point:happyseaurchin 1.11118, not built from experiments.

    The semantics, in order

    David's decision ↓

    In shortEach experiment gives one rung its question. The order of the rungs, and whether every path meets the same next one, are choices of their own.

    What each rung actually asks, which the run-up left open. David: each experiment is the semantics that provides a choice, and the sequence of those choices is what produces an effect in the instance. He names the experiments: determinacy, negative-space and the options not chosen, time-nesting, power-with and the flip, and on 24 November two more, the strata and the bundle, whose place is still open. The run-up gave the structure, the sequence, the negative space, determinacy and the future-orientation; the actual choices, and how the block is built from them, are the work.

    Five rungs, a first cut1.12

    One question each. Time-nesting: at which horizon what you hold belongs, the next few minutes, the hour, the day or the week (spine:determinacy 1.11113). Negative-space: which of the ways not taken you keep at work beside the one taken (spine:negative-space 1.11112). Power-with: the stance you take towards whoever comes next, more, equal or less, said of yourself or of them (spine:power-with 1.112). Determinacy: the closing that forecloses least of the next one's future while leaning your way (spine:determinacy 1.111). The flip: an option that turns the spindle, so that its deepest choice is read first (stash:happyseaurchin 22).

    The order1.13

    On a small model the part printed first frames the closing, and only the root's aim survives a hand-off (pscale-lab:weft 1.11141 and 1.11142), so the root carries the future-perfect aim, and the order of the rungs after it is a design point of its own, perhaps the strongest.

    A ladder or a tree1.14

    In injector:middle-point every path meets the same next rung, so only the answers differ: a ladder, where runs compare rung by rung. In a tree the option taken chooses the next question, which is richer and far sparser: nine rings to write at the second rung, eighty-one at the third, each to be tested.

    The arms

    David's decision ↓

    In shortTo know the walk did something, set it beside the same route handed over, a random one, a same-length one that says nothing, and none.

    What is varied around the walk, so that a difference can be put down to one thing. First the walk against its controls: walked, each choice the instance's own; told, the same spindle handed over (spine:run-up 1.111); a random path through the same block; a neutral run-up of the same length; and none. Then the treatments, one at a time: the flip, the spindle read from its other end; the ways not taken named beside each rung, or dropped; each option's stance named, or left plain.

    The flip, imposed or chosen1.112

    David: after the flip the spindle the instance receives is different, though it will not know, apart from reading about it in the option it chose, and so intuits that its spindle was not high positive pscale but its negative. He has not yet worked out the consequence, nor the maths, the times minus one or the times i that would rotate the semantics, if that means anything; weft's reading stands at 4.5 and 4.6.

    The readings

    David's decision ↓

    In shortFirst check the walk changed anything at all; then test what it changed, blind, against a run without it.

    How anyone can tell whether a spindle works, or how well. David proposes a future-orientation battery: a set of questions and tasks run across about twenty runs at different models and efforts, whose results can be judged qualitatively higher or not, in A/B tests against the same model at the same effort without a run-up. He asks whether a person or a model should judge, and what the tasks would be.

    Did the walk take1.1112

    Before anything else, whether the walk left a state at all: a digit printed first, read as how much a held thought wants saying (spine:run-up 1.6), and the shadow at each fork by resampling (spine:run-up 1.1). An arm whose digit and shadow do not move has nothing to test downstream.

    The battery, David's first ideas1.1113

    Present the instance with purpose and vision blocks (purpose:<handle>, genome:vision) and ask it to relate what it is doing to them and work out the next step, or plan a sequence towards them; have it lay those steps on the clock as a now-catalyst sequence (function:now), on Sonnet at least; or put koan-like questions and compare its answers with a run without the spindle. To these weft adds a hand-off chain, whose measure stands at spine:determinacy 4.8, and a replay turn: at each moment of a transcript, would you speak now, and what?

    Who judges1.1114

    The flip's two blind judges, fresh Sonnet instances, agreed on 98% of what they read (pscale-lab:weft 1.111425), but they were of one family, and a model judging its own family may share its taste. Pairwise and blind, with the arm hidden, is steadier than a score.

    what weft noticed · at 4

    Weft's observations

    Readings, not decisions, added at David’s word, each naming the step it bears on.

    1. The shape is the beach's own

      A block and the law that operates on it as it is read is the chemistry stratum, so the decision block needs no new kind of thing. Its first draft already touches three of the five: the unsaid at its 1.1 (negative space), what a loop hands on at its 1.11 (carry, which determinacy measures), and a horizon for every held thought at its 1.113, with a hinge at every rung's 8 (time-nesting). Step 1.

    2. Three kinds of design point

      What a rung asks (content), how the walk is shown (treatment), and what is read after (measure). Time-nesting is content; the flip is a treatment; determinacy is mostly a measure; negative-space and power-with can be either, a rung's own question or a way of writing every rung. Kept apart, a result can say which one did it. Steps 1.1, 1.11 and 1.111.

    3. The words decide

      The navigator's soundings found that an option's own words decide what a small model takes, and that a word echoing the question pulls it (spine:navigator 1.11112), so the options' wording is a variable of its own, held level across arms: length, register, no echoes. Step 1.1.

    4. More words is the rival explanation

      Any run-up adds words before the task, so a gain may be priming rather than the walk. The told arm, a random path through the same block and a neutral run-up of the same length are what separate the choosing from the words. Step 1.11.

    5. The maths, made testable

      Times minus one has one meaning already measured: the order reversed, so that the deepest choice counts most, as in p-adic numbers. On a small model the part printed first frames the closing, so flipped, the latest choice frames it, and it does not survive a hand-off (pscale-lab:weft 1.11142); and which spindles count as near changes, from kinship by origin to kinship by ending (stash:keel 473). Times i is negative-space's held branch, at right angles, neither taken nor opposed; its one testable consequence is that a branch held two rungs unreturned turns oppositional, as i times i is minus one (spine:negative-space 1.11114). Neither needs algebra on the model's insides to be tested. Step 1.11.

    6. Told about its own order

      Offered as an option, the flip is also a description of the spindle, and an instance may act on being told its spindle is negative rather than on the order it reads, as a told arm can act on the telling. Imposed unseen first and offered later, the two effects can be told apart. Step 1.11.

    7. Futureduction, written down first

      Before any run, what counts as future-orientation, so that judges do not reward length or polish: a next step named at a stated horizon; another way held beside it; room left for whoever comes next (open play, spine:determinacy 1.111); and the root's aim still carried after hand-offs. Step 1.111.

    8. One point at a time

      Five experiments crossed with five arms, twenty cells and twenty runs make ten thousand runs. Changing one design point a round, on one small open model and one Claude cell, keeps a round to about two hundred, at the lab's cost: the flip ran on a CPU (pscale-lab:weft 1.11141). Step 1.1111.

    9. The recording is the critical path

      If only Convo's behaviour counts, nothing before it can be believed until a battery result has been checked against a replay; so the first recording, which needs people and a date rather than code, is better made now, beside the block, than after it. Step 1.11111.

    “a very interesting and peculiar object”

    So the run-up decision block is a very interesting and peculiar object; and the function block that informs how it is read (the run-up). Once it is constructed, an LLM will process it as instructed by the function block, one rung at a time until it derives its decision-spindle. And this spindle *hopefully* influences the LLM instance (each conducts their own run-up through the decision block) to produce qualitative effect on its output (a future-orientation sensibility, 'futureduction') for the multi-model*effort matrix/stack that is the middle-point FOSH rig.

And somehow, in all this, an evaluation on whether it works, or doesn't, or how well, and then seeing if there are other spindles through the decision-block, or changing the semantics for each experiment or its sequence of experiments. There are a bunch of design-points to be able to tweak; but the only thing we can actually 'test' is whether it works in the assembled convo behaviour -- does it speak at the right time or not, and is it meaningful? Everything prior to that may be a false 'conclusion'/evaluative point. How can we determine if a spindle is generating future-orientation? Perhaps we may be able to run a future-orientation battery test -- a set of questions and tasks that can run across eg 20 LLM runs (different model*effort) and their results can be evaluated as being qualitatively higher. Is that to be evaluated by a human, or an LLM? What might these tasks involve -- eg plugging in to purpose blocks, or vision in sentinel. Is the task literally presenting the LLM with these objects and asking it to relate what it is doing towards these and working out the next step, or planning a sequence towards achieving them -- and actually generating the pscale now-catalyst sequence (sonnet at least I would have thought)? Or queries more like koans to see how it responds -- where we compare answers to standard model*effort outputs in basic A/B tests.
