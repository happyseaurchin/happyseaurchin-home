The Hidden Basin

History drawn as a landscape. The flock circles a held centre. Past the bar lies a second basin, where it would circle a centre no one holds, and no path that starts near the centre can reach it. The seven forks in the log are moments when a channel was possible. One fork alone falls back. Two neighbouring forks open a channel.

Reading the chart

The shape

This is the textbook picture of a hidden attractor: a stable centre, an unstable cycle around it, which is the bar, and a stable cycle outside it, which is the ring. Every path that starts near the centre stays in the old basin, so a study of the paths that actually happened can never find the ring. Hidden attractors are found by continuation. You start from a simpler system in which the ring can be reached and change it step by step toward the real one. Classrooms, couples and dance floors are such simpler systems.

The track

The pencil line is what happened. It begins on the ring, among seasonal forms in which people could move away, disobey and reorganise, Graeber and Wengrow's three freedoms. It crosses the bar with the first temple ledgers, at Uruk and soon after at Susa, when the count moved from tokens in the hand to marks on clay. Then it spirals inward through coin, print, the Bank of England and Arkwright's mill to the seven forks. The dotted courses are the forks not taken.

The centre

Western study ends at the observer, and contemplative method begins there. On this chart that turn happens at the centre. Watched without grasping, the held centre loses its hold, and the flock circles a centre that turns out to be empty, derived from the movement around it. Press and hold the chart and your hold becomes the centre. Let go, and the old centre holds again until no one is holding it.

Soundings

The small italic numbers are soundings, as on a sea chart: the depth of the landscape at that point. Watch the soundings on the bar as forks open. A single fork deepens its sector a little. Where two neighbours are open, the water over the bar deepens enough for a channel, which is marked with a red and a green buoy.

The three states

C is held by a centre. B is split, in clusters, or circling inside the old basin. A is one flock on the ring, its centre implied by the movement. In XQ notation the held centre is the oppositional axis [−1, +1], where 1 + 1 = 2 keeps the parts apart. The ring is 1ⁿ on [0, 1], n participants sharing one circle.

Fulcrum

Each fork is paired with one of the solutions David Pinto has worked on since 1988: Educere and ABC State (1999), XQ Mathematics (2007), StrategOS and Intrabeing (2009), Living Money and Ecosquared (2012), Seed Crystals (2018), and Open Business and the Action Cycle (2021). They are gathered in the Fulcrum book (2023), with pscale and the beach underneath (2025–26).

What the chart claims

This is a model to think with, not a measurement. The forks are real history. The claim is in the geometry: that neighbouring forks reinforce each other, that one alone falls back, and that a held centre stops everything. Test that claim against what you know of history.
