Sweden left the land some 70 years after Britain, then followed the same arc

Farming fell below a fifth of output by 1851 in Britain but only in 1922 in Sweden. Industry's share then peaked in both around mid-century, at 49% of British output and 46% of Swedish, before services took over: 81% and 75% of output in 2025.

How the series are built

What the bands measure

Each band is a sector's share of value added at current prices, so the three always add to 100%. These are shares of output, not of jobs; Our World in Data notes that in these records agriculture's share of workers is always higher than its share of output.

Industry means mining, manufacturing, construction and utilities; agriculture includes forestry and fishing; services are everything else.

Joins and gaps

The long-run series runs to 2001 (Finland), 2008 (Belgium), 2010 (Sweden), 2011 (most) or 2016 (US). After that the panels use the World Bank's current figures, rescaled so the three sectors add to 100%. Where both sources cover the same years they differ by up to six points, with industry lower in the World Bank figures for most countries and higher for Belgium, Finland and the US, so a small step at the join is a change of source, not an event.

Britain before 1851 and the US before 1909 have benchmark years ten apart. Faded spans have no data at all: Britain 1851–1920 and 1938–1960, the Netherlands 1913–1970, France 1938–1970, Japan and Korea 1940–1953, Spain 1958–1970. Latest data: 2025, or 2024 for Japan and 2021 for the US.
