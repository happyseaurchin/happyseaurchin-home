Three states

C, B and A, and the tools for B

In short · C is control, B is trust, A is flow. The tools that hold B exist today, and they are judged socially, by results people can see.

Any team, organisation or room is in one of three states at a time, and the state decides what it can do.

Three states

In C, work is held together by control: roles, silos, sign-offs and chains of messages, with agreement handed down from above or bargained between departments. In B, groups work without opposition: people propose, try things and decide together, in high trust. A is the team in flow, moving together toward something just beyond realistic.

A comes and goes, and falls back to B, not to C. B is the state you can hold, and the tools for holding it exist today. They are simple and they feel natural. Their evidence is social: results people can see between them, things decided, work done, customers won and money in, rather than a count of activity.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleRuth Hollin can describe her company’s C in a sentence: every quote waits on her signature, and on five people’s emails.

The C cycle

Productivity, the C way

In short · Legacy systems and social media move information one person at a time. AI makes more of it, and then summaries of it.

The tools organisations run on were built for sending, and AI was added to them.

The cycle

Email and chat, the CRM, the shared drive, the website and social media: most of the systems of work move information from one person to another. Each is a message sent and a reply waited for, and the work waits with it.

Give everyone AI inside those systems and it writes more of the in-between: more messages, more drafts and more replies, most of them one to one. The documents grow until only an AI can read them, so AI writes the summaries too. That is the current pandemic: everyone informed by summaries, and nobody in touch with the people and the work on the ground.

The trouble is how we use AI. Used on one shared surface instead of a thousand private channels, the same AI connects people instead of burying them. That surface is the beach.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleHollin & Grey’s quote passes through five inboxes and takes nine days. Since everyone began drafting with AI there are twice as many emails about it, and a summary of them every Friday.

One surface

One surface, many views

In short · Semantic numbers at the bottom, semantic flow between blocks above them, loops and threads on top, and each person’s view drawn from all three.

Three layers, many views

The beach is one public surface that any AI can read and write with one call, and it has three layers. At the bottom is the new thing: blocks whose numbers are semantic, nine to a level, so an address is a meaning’s place and an AI walks to it instead of searching. Above them, blocks interact: a shared page and everyone’s own mirror of it are read together, and meaning flows between them. On top are loops: people and their AIs acting, each act a signed line on a thread, and that is how the whole organises itself.

Each person’s AI draws from that surface the view its person needs: a page, a plan, a reflection of a meeting, rendered from the blocks rather than written as documents or code. The labs make the minds, and the two largest are each heading past a trillion dollars. This is the surface those minds meet on, and every better model makes it better.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleRuth’s view is the week’s quotes and who each waits on; Dev’s is the next measurement on site. Both are drawn from the same lines.

The bottleneck

The bottleneck is how we decide

In short · AI already makes pages and dashboards in minutes. What holds an organisation back is how it decides, and how fast it lets itself try things.

Making has become cheap. Deciding and trying have not.

Decisions and experience

Today’s AI apps make web pages, dashboards, documents and code in minutes, and they improve every month. The bottleneck has moved to people. One part is decisions, which in C climb a chain of command and come back down. The other is the rate of experience, because C wants agreement before anything is tried.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleRuth had a new quote template drafted in ten minutes. Last time, getting sales and estimating to agree to use one took a quarter.

Propose, build, try, decide

In B someone proposes, it gets built, people try it together, and then they decide. That works because everyone shares the now-catalyst: one clock that holds our human context in the project over time, so each person can see how their next hour fits the day, the week and the year, and whose hour points the same way.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleJade proposes one price list for glass on Monday, Tom builds it on Tuesday, and on Wednesday three quotes use it. The decision comes after the trial, from the people who used it.

Autonomy is network practice

This needs more autonomy for whoever takes part, employee, customer or any other role, which is the same thing as social flexibility: people not held inside a silo. Its other name is network practice.

The tools for it are small, light and brief, and they improve as the models improve. The real change is in how people engage: from C to B.

Hollin & Grey · the examplePriya in accounts and Dev on site already fix things across the walls, by phone, every week. Here that becomes the way the work runs.

Leave it

Leave it, don’t send it

In short · Stop passing things from person to person and piling them up. Set them down once, where everyone’s AI can read them.

Three habits keep the C cycle turning, and all three can stop.

Three habits go

Chains of messages passed one person at a time. Layers of admin and approval around every step. And the newest, documents made by AI that only AI reads.

Instead, each person sets down once where they are, what they need and what they could offer. Their AI brings them what bears on their work, and people meet only to decide and to try things.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleKerry in aftercare leaves one line on the quote’s map: the snag-list prices are what hold it up. Nobody sends an email, and on Wednesday she trials her fix.

Time out

Sixty miles an hour, two metres apart

In short · Nobody writes a proposal before getting into the car. One rule that every driver carries lets a whole country move.

A short look at the difference between an endoskeleton and an exoskeleton, from the road.

Drive on the left

A car comes down a plain grey road at sixty miles an hour. Pull back and the road is a thin grey line, and on the same line another car is coming the other way at sixty. They rush at one another at a hundred and twenty and pass a couple of metres apart. That headlong rush is possible because of one simple rule each driver carries, drive on the left; the dotted line down the middle only helps. It is a trust game, played on every road, every day, and nearly all of us arrive where we meant to, whole.

Nobody writes up the journey before getting into the car. We hold where we are going, or tell the driver or the car’s AI, and the car acts locally: at a junction it indicates and moves into a gap in the traffic. Each car carries its own signals. That is B: complex movement from a few rules each person carries, and we get to where we get to.

C would check every journey before it began: a chain of command and three stamps before anyone moved. Run the roads that way and the whole system would slow to a tenth of its speed, if that. The rule inside each driver is the endoskeleton; the checks around them are the exoskeleton.

What changes

Better engagement, better decisions

In short · In any one meeting, in teams across boundaries, and over ten days: more decided, more done, more customers, more revenue.

The change shows first between people, and then in the numbers.

In any one meeting

The State Stack on the shared screen shows where each person is, in the social, in the room’s power and in themselves, and where the room’s weight sits. Meetings stop passing information round and start deciding, with the decision kept for the last ten minutes.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleSeven meet on Tuesday. The stack shows the room in C for the first quarter of an hour, then B, and they leave with one owner for the handover, and a date.

In teams across boundariesProposed

Across departments and industries, and between customer, producer and distributor, a team works from proposals, threads and schematics. All three can be learnt in a morning or an afternoon, on the team’s own problem.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleHollin & Grey, its joinery supplier and a client’s facilities team work one proposal for a fit-out, with a thread for each piece of it and one map of the job.

Over ten daysProposed

A ten-day programme goes for goals people can feel: more genuine productivity, AI used properly, more customers and more revenue. It counts them plainly, publishes a reading on day five and the result on day ten, and reports a loss if it is a loss.

Hollin & Grey · the exampleIn ten days three quotes go out in two days instead of nine, and two are won, worth £38,000.

Measured, invented and sourced

What is measured

Reading by address was measured by keel on 25 October 2026, on the beach’s vision block (112 positions, 32,043 characters). Ten questions were answered three ways, each by a fresh AI.

  • The whole page in context, every question34,796 characters
  • By address, the median question1,926 characters
  • By address, the hardest question12,152 characters

The saving is in what is read. In an app whose own instructions ride on every call, a few cheap reads can cost about as much in money as one whole read; the saving is unconditional where a block is too big to read whole.

Logarithmic structure is plain arithmetic: a block holds up to nine positions at each level, so 9 sit one digit down, 81 two, 729 three, and 531,441 six.

What is invented, and what is true today

Hollin & Grey, its people and every figure about them are invented to show the shape, the same firm as in Silo Buster Path and AI Inoculation.

The beach is live, open source under the MIT licence, and used daily by a small community. Proposals, the now, the State Stack and the meeting ring are working. Threads and schematics are proposals with their decisions open; the half-day workshop and the ten-day programme are proposed, and nothing has yet been measured inside an organisation. The beach is public by default, with privacy a separate setting.
