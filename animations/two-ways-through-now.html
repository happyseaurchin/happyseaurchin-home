What is a choice, and what is not

Why opposite ends can meet

Joining +∞ to −∞ is a choice, and it costs something: the closed circle forgets which way you went out. It pays for itself by making 0 and ∞ partners. The reciprocal x ↦ 1/x sends 0 to ∞, and because 0 is reached from both sides, its partner has to be reached from both sides too. Keeping the two infinities apart is the other honest choice, and then opposite directions never meet.

Unfolded, the only way from + to − passes through now. Folded, there is a second way, round through the meeting point. Moving along that second way is what rotation is.

Which way is anticlockwise

Nothing in arithmetic tells i from −i. Swapping them everywhere, which is complex conjugation, swaps clockwise with anticlockwise and changes nothing else, so calling one of them positive is a convention.

The controls separate the two kinds of change. Turning the axis is a rotation and never changes the senses. Flipping the fold, or swapping which process is +, is a reflection and always does. Through every one of them the two directions stay opposite.

The fork in the units

With i2 = −1, eiθ reaches −1 after half a turn, so opposite directions are joined by rotation. With j2 = +1, ejθ = cosh θ + j sinh θ never reaches −1, and the two directions stay on separate branches of the hyperbola. Folding the line is the step that lets a half-turn carry + into −.

The four forms and the threshold

[tiger] is the tiger as it strikes the senses, (tiger) the tiger recognised, {tiger} its web of associations, and ‘tiger’ the word. Sensing reads them in that order; projecting reads them in reverse. Fulcrum Volume −1 already gives the negative to the projective sense.

The threshold sits half-way along each arm, between (tiger) and {tiger}: sensing crosses it into awareness, projecting crosses it back into the preconscious. After the pinch each loop holds all four forms once. Turn the axis half a turn and the sensory loop lands in the quadrant layout, with now at +1, sense in the first quadrant, and the threshold at −1.

At now the loops meet in kind. The word said passes into the word heard, and the fear projected into the body is sensed again.

Past and future

Both processes run forward in time; they differ in what they face. Sensing works on what has already arrived: light from the stripes, the movement. Projecting draws on memory, the last tiger or a film, to reach towards what has not happened: the claws, the run, the gate. The page gives + to the past-oriented direction, as you suggested. Swap it with “+ is” and only the names of the turns change.

Left to right, or forward along the path

Read the line left to right and time runs one way across the whole page, from −∞ through now to +∞. The price is that one process is read backwards: on the − arm the point meets its forms in reverse order. Folded, the left-to-right point goes once round the circle, the same way all round, so both halves turn alike. At the pinch it can no longer pass straight through now. It has to turn back there.

Run each process forward from now instead, and before the pinch the point has to jump back to now between them. The pinch closes the gap. Then one smooth path runs round both loops with each process forward the whole way, turning one way and then the other. Forward in time is forward along the path, not rightwards on the page, and what tells the two processes apart is the sense of their turn.
