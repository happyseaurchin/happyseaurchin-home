An animated river of ideas · 500 BCE – 2026

The Observer's River

Ideas about society and mind, seen from above as currents. People are fish, placed where their work entered the water. A current's width shows how much it carried; colours run side by side where currents meet. Higher on the map, the observer looks in; lower, the observer looks at.

Width: how much a current carried Stripes: currents a person carries Thread: a transfer between currents Taper: a current cut off

Fish arrive as the water reaches their year. Slow the speed to ×1/4 or ×1/8 to read each one, or step with the arrows.

Tap a name to light that current and dim the rest. Use the box to hide or show it.

    How to read it

    • Currents are traditions of thought. A current can rise as a trickle long before the person usually credited with it: the organic current flows from Heraclitus to Whitehead, and on past him.
    • Fish are people, placed at the year of the work that matters here. Later fish swim ahead, downstream. A larger fish named or widened its current.
    • Stripes on a fish are the other currents that person carried. Carriers such as Bateson, Varela and Devereux are where transfers happen.
    • Width is an editorial judgement of how much each current carried in Western thought about society and mind. It is not measured.
    • Colour bands inside a current show what flowed into it and kept its colour. The mainstream at the bottom ends multicoloured, but its slate core stays widest.
    • Shades of one colour are reinventions: introspection returns as micro-phenomenology; process thought returns as new materialism; Buddhist practice returns as mindfulness.
    • Height roughly marks where the observer stands: higher is looking in (first person), the middle is being among others, lower is looking at (third person). Anthropology climbs; psychology dives in 1913.
    • Time is stretched toward the present. The first two thousand years are compressed into the left edge.

    Using it

    • Press play to run from the first springs to 2026, or drag the timeline. The speed menu goes down to ×1/16. The camera follows the water front; drag or pinch to look around, and the target button hands the camera back.
    • Tap a fish for its card, or a current to light it. The arrows (or the arrow keys) step from fish to fish.

    Caveats

    • Each person appears once, at one work, though most worked across decades. Their cards name other dates.
    • Ancient dates are traditional or approximate. The Buddha's dates are disputed; the map uses the 5th century BCE.
    • The selection follows one argument: from anthropology to the postmodern turn, the Buddhist method as a control, therapy as a model at social scale, and the tools that feed psocial. Large traditions outside that path are left out.
