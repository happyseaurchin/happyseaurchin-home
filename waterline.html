Six words, plainly

The rest of the page leans on these.

  • The labs

    The companies that build the large AI models, Anthropic, OpenAI and others, which now ship whole products inside their own assistants.

  • The beach

    An open place on the web where records are kept as numbered blocks that any AI can read and write. Anyone can run one, a company included.

  • A shell

    A person’s own record on a beach, kept under their own key: their AI remembers from one conversation to the next, and the record stays theirs.

  • A colour

    A seal on a line, so that only the colour’s holders can read it. Anyone else sees one grey line naming the colour.

  • A facilitator

    Someone who helps a group organise itself with no authority over it. The role moves between people, and leaves traces.

  • A connector

    One of the few people everyone goes to. Across many organisations, 3–5% of people supply 20–35% of the collaboration that adds value.

Step 1 of 81

The wave

Say what you make of it ↓

In shortProducts are turning into features of the labs’ own assistants, faster each season. Organisations pay for AI and see little back, while their people already bring their own.

Every few months the AI labs now ship, as features of their own assistants, things that used to be whole products: dashboards, briefing notes, review decks, an assistant that sits in every team channel. Most organisations already pay for AI, yet most see no measurable return from it, while their people use their own AI at work whatever the official line. The wave is not coming; it is already in the building.

What shipped, and when

1.2

Claude Cowork, an assistant that carries out whole tasks on a person's own files, on 12 January 2026, and its role plugins on 30 January; Claude Tag, a shared, proactive Claude inside Slack channels, on 23 June; OpenAI's Data agent in ChatGPT Work, which turns questions about company data into shareable dashboards, on 10 September; Claude Docs and Slides on 16 September; and Claude Dashboards, live views over a company's data warehouse showing the query behind every number, on 8 October 2026.

The market noticed

1.3

After the Cowork plugins, software sold by the seat sold off hard in early February 2026: a Goldman Sachs basket of US software stocks fell 6% in a day, and a Jefferies trader called it the SaaSpocalypse. Investors were pricing in agents doing the work that per-user software charged people for.

Money without results

1.4

MIT's NANDA researchers reported in August 2025 that 95% of businesses had seen no measurable return from generative AI, and traced it to a learning gap: systems that do not keep feedback or adapt over time. In the same study, workers at more than 90% of companies used their own AI tools at work, while only 40% of those companies had bought an official subscription.

The test for anything new

1.5

The head start a new product gets before a lab ships the same thing is shrinking: David's working guess was three to six months, and for the newest goal-tracking startups it has been one or two (step 1.1). The test that matters is whether each model upgrade improves a product or absorbs it. Silo Buster is built to be improved: it runs on whatever AI each person already uses.

Step 2 of 81.1

What it floods

Decide ↓

In shortThe dashboards, briefings and agents that goal software sold as its AI future now come free from the labs. The record and the routine are what survive.

WorkBoard shows what the tide takes. It is a Silicon Valley company, founded in 2013, that helps managers set goals and follow them through. Most of what it sold as its AI future, scorecards, briefings and a 'chief of staff' agent, the labs now give away inside their own assistants. What they cannot ship is the official record of who owns which goal, and the routine an organisation runs around it. Record and routine on one side, view and nudge on the other: that split runs through everything below.

Who WorkBoard is

1.12

Based in Redwood City, California, with offices in the US, Europe and India. About $140M raised over six rounds, the last a $75M Series D on 4 May 2021 led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2, alongside Andreessen Horowitz, GGV, Workday Ventures, Microsoft's M12, Capital One Ventures and SVB Capital. It bought its main rival Quantive (formerly Gtmhub, which had raised over $161M) on 28 May 2025. Headcount was estimated at about 136 in March 2026, down from 513 in 2023: a late-stage company buying up its rivals, not a startup.

The head start, measured

1.13

From a feature's launch to the first lab release that did much the same: WorkBoard's AI scorecards (May 2023) had about 41 months before Claude Dashboards; its scheduled AI briefings into Slack (October 2024) about 21 before Claude Tag; its Chief of Staff agent (May 2025) about 13. Two 2026 newcomers had far less: Brev, a $3.3M pre-seed on 23 April for goal tracking in Slack, about 2 months; STRGY AI in Helsinki, €1M on 12 August for an always-on strategy view, about 1.

Going headless by choice

1.14

MCP is the standard that lets an AI assistant plug into other software. WorkBoard's own MCP server opens its goals, KPIs, scorecards, meetings and business reviews to Claude, ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot and Cursor through nearly 60 tools, and Perdoo, Tability and Profit.co do the same. The vendors are handing the interface to the labs and defending only the record and the routine.

Microsoft left the field

1.15

Microsoft stopped developing its goals product, Viva Goals, in December 2024 and retired it on 31 December 2025, naming no successor. WorkBoard says many of its 29 new enterprise customers in the first half of 2025, a half in which its sales grew 30% and half came through Workday and Microsoft, had replaced Viva Goals.

What survives the tide

1.16

Ask of any product whether it is a record or a routine, which a lab could copy only by running the customer's organisation, or a view or a nudge, which a lab ships as a feature. WorkBoard's best odds lie in becoming the goals record every AI reads, priced by use rather than by seat. Silo Buster stands on the same side of the line: the beach is a record, and the ten days are a routine (steps 1.1111 and 1.111111).

Step 3 of 81.11

The gap where managers were

Decide ↓

In shortAs managers go, something fills the gap: better tools for those left, one shared vendor agent, or each person’s own AI with facilitators. Silo Buster is the third.

Organisations are taking out layers of middle management and expecting AI to fill the gap. It can be filled three ways. Give the managers who remain better tools, which is WorkBoard's answer. Put one shared vendor assistant in every team channel, so that coordination lives in one vendor's memory. Or let each person's own AI work from a record they own, with facilitators rather than managers helping the whole organise itself. Silo Buster is the third way.

The flattening

1.112

Gartner predicted that through 2026, one organisation in five would use AI to flatten its structure, removing more than half of its middle-management positions. Most of the flattening so far is cost-cutting, not self-organisation.

The agent boss

1.113

Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index keeps the boss and adds agents beneath, proposing that everyone take on a new role as an agent boss. The hierarchy stays; the people in it supervise software.

One shared agent per channel

1.114

Claude Tag gives each Slack channel one shared Claude, with its own memory and the tools an admin has approved; Anthropic's product lead calls it the company's first natively multiplayer, proactive product. As managers go, this becomes the default way to fill the gap: coordination held in one vendor's memory rather than in the people doing the work.

The real contest

1.115

WorkBoard needs a hierarchy to cascade goals through, so it loses its users as the managers go; the shared agent keeps every user. Silo Buster's real rival for the gap is the shared vendor agent, not WorkBoard. And because those agents use whatever tools an admin approves, the beach can work inside them rather than against them.

Facilitators, not managers

1.116

The research has names for people who coordinate without authority: Jody Hoffer Gittell's boundary spanners, Rob Cross's connectors, Buurtzorg's coaches. Holacracy's lead link and Microsoft's agent boss both kept a boss, which a facilitator must never drift into. Three rules keep the role honest: no authority, visible traces, and a role that moves between people.

Step 4 of 81.111

What the evidence says

Say what you make of it ↓

In shortWhere facilitators hold no authority, traces show, teams are small and the result is counted, organising without managers works. Take those away and it fails.

Self-organising is not new, and the record is mixed. Where relationships across roles are strong, quality, efficiency and well-being rise, and a few connectors carry a large share of the useful work between people. Where managers were simply removed and nothing took over what they did, it went badly. The difference is in the doing: facilitators without authority, traces anyone can see, small teams with clear outcomes, and a result people can count.

Relational coordination

1.1112

Jody Hoffer Gittell's measure of shared goals, shared knowledge and mutual respect across roles has been tested in 73 industry contexts and 36 countries, and linked to better quality, efficiency and worker well-being, including where separate agencies must work together. It calls for redesigning formal structures rather than removing them, and its validated survey can be run before and after.

The connectors

1.1113

Across more than 300 organisations, Rob Cross and colleagues found that 3–5% of employees supply 20–35% of value-added collaboration, and that leaders' guesses about who they are are only about half right. They are also the people burning out, so a method must take load off them, with their own AI doing the routing, rather than send them more.

Buurtzorg

1.1114

The Dutch home-care organisation runs about 14,000 nurses in 950 self-managing teams, supported by only 18 to 20 regional coaches who hold no authority or targets and advise only when a team asks. Teams see each other's results and go to each other for help. Harvard researchers credit highly qualified staff, coaches who only help, and strong shared IT for communication; the beach is a candidate for that third ingredient.

Zappos, GitHub, Holacracy

1.1115

About 18% of Zappos staff left after its switch to Holacracy; a year on, turnover had reached 29%, staff described confusion, and favouritism had found new routes to power. GitHub dropped its flat model as it grew and needed far more coordination. Holacracy itself kept lead links to hold leadership and direction.

The conditions

1.1116

Silo Buster is viable when facilitators hold no authority and the role rotates; when every act of facilitation leaves a trace, as threads record who did what, so no hidden hierarchy forms; when teams are small with clear outcomes; and when what is offered is the ten-day result, revenue, clients or performance, rather than the absence of managers. It is not viable as a top-down 'no managers' overhaul of a large firm.

Step 5 of 81.1111

The ten days

Decide ↓

In shortAn hour, one message, the next morning, a group, ten days on a result, four counts, then go on, reshape or stop.

Silo Buster starts small and asks nothing new of anyone. In a facilitated hour each person tells the AI they already use where they are, what they need and what they can offer, and one read across those answers gives the room its picture. One message to everyone, answered through their own AI, finds by the next morning the connectors, the quiet achievers with AI, and the people the system holds back. A group forms around a result it can count and works on it for ten days. On day ten it decides together: go on, reshape, or stop.

The path, scene by scene

1.11112

The Silo Buster path runs from the mess, through who comes, their form, a complex field, two signals, money on the table and two pulls, defence and drive, to the group; then beyond realistic, the hour, day, week and ten days, and a resilient ecology. Nothing new to adopt: each person reflects through the AI they already use, and most of the work is unlearning. The animated path is at happyseaurchin.com/animations/silo-buster-path.

The four counts

1.11113

Over the ten days the experiment counts four things: messages asking where work is; messages and hours spent finding a time to meet; how many of five plain questions a fresh assistant can answer from the shared surface; and the share of people writing their own weekly lines on days 6 to 10 without being asked. The last is the one that matters, and it passes at 40%.

Money at every step

1.11114

Money moves at each step of the path rather than into one central pot, because something real happened. The first-meeting proposal sets out how, with David's own answers, at happyseaurchin.com/first-meeting.

The notebook

1.11115

The method is the product; the beach is the notebook. Each person's lines, the group's threads and the four counts are kept there, readable by any AI a person already uses, so nobody has to adopt a new tool and the facilitation leaves its traces.

Step 6 of 81.11111

After the ten days

Decide ↓

In shortThe group stays small, reaches past its walls and leaves its links behind; over time the organisation changes shape.

If the counts pass, the group does not become a new department. It reaches past what seemed realistic, drawing in people from other organisations who can help, and the links it made stay for the next result. Over time the organisation changes shape: connectors carry less because their AI does the routing, facilitation passes from hand to hand and leaves traces, the network people already work through becomes visible, and work crosses the walls between organisations as easily as the walls between departments.

Beyond realistic

1.111112

In the path, the group reaches past its own walls: people from other companies help, each answering through their own AI on a record no single company owns, without waiting for a manager's say.

A resilient ecology

1.111113

The path ends not in a bigger organisation but in a meshwork across companies: many small groups, each with its own results, joined by the people they share. It holds through wave after wave of change because its links run through people rather than org charts.

Strongest between organisations

1.111114

Silo Buster's strongest ground is the work between organisations, where nobody can be the manager anyway: multi-agency partnerships, supply chains, consortia and charities. WorkBoard works inside one company, and Slack Connect needs an admin on each side to approve the other.

What changes inside

1.111115

Connectors stop being the bottleneck; facilitation becomes a role people take in turn and leave traces of; goals are found and joined rather than cascaded; and what a person gave stays traceable after the group moves on, through the threads that recorded it.

What could go wrong

1.111116

A hidden hierarchy forms where facilitation leaves no trace; connectors burn out if the method sends them more rather than less; and a company that wants walls will balk at an open beach (step 1.111111). Each has an answer in the conditions at step 1.111, and none is solved by software alone.

Step 7 of 81.111111

The storage layer

Decide ↓

In shortThe beach keeps the record: the company’s own beach, each person’s shell, open beaches, and colours that seal lines.

Underneath sits the beach: records kept as numbered blocks that any AI can read and write. A company can run a beach of its own; each person keeps a shell, their own record, on a beach of their choosing; open beaches hold what is shared; and a colour seals lines so that only its holders can read them, even in an open block. Work content stays with the company; the person keeps their own record and the addresses that point into the company's.

Precedents for each piece

1.1111112

Organisations running their own linked servers is how Matrix works: France's Tchap has over 300,000 users and the German armed forces' BwMessenger over 100,000. Personal data stores that organisations must ask to read: Flanders' Solid pods, for its 6.5 million citizens. Shared spaces across companies: Slack says 80% of the Fortune 100 use Slack Connect. People bringing their own AI: workers at over 90% of companies, against 40% of companies buying it.

Colours

1.1111113

A line sealed in a colour can sit in any shared block, and only the colour's holders can read it; everyone else sees one grey line naming the colour and its turn. Outsiders can leave sealed letters for the colour. Changing who holds it starts a new turn with a fresh key, so someone who leaves keeps the old lines and sees none of the new.

Who owns what

1.1111114

In the UK, as in most places, what employees make in the course of their work belongs to the employer. So work content lives in the company's beach and its colours, and the personal shell holds the person's own record plus addresses pointing into company blocks. Because the beach references rather than copies, a leaver's links go grey and nothing walks out with them.

Keys, leaks and walls

1.1111115

The company should keep its own colours, so it can admit people, start new turns and recover access. A colour hides content, not activity: on an open beach, block names, positions, timing and colour names stay visible, so sensitive work belongs on the company's own beach. A firewalled company beach needs its own copy of bsp-mcp inside the firewall, since the public connector cannot reach it; self-hosting is how Matrix won over governments.

Why the labs lift it

1.1111116

The beach never tried to own the interface. Whatever AI a person uses reaches it through bsp-mcp, on the official MCP Registry since 3 July 2026, so each model upgrade improves the beach rather than replacing it. WorkBoard's record and the beach's can even meet inside one person's own AI, both open through MCP.

Step 8 of 81.1111111

Where it stands

Decide ↓

In shortWorkBoard is a rival premise, not a rival product; the labs’ shared agents are the real contest; and three next steps, if it goes on.

Beside WorkBoard, Silo Buster does not compete feature for feature; it competes with the premise that coordination runs down through managers, and the two can run side by side where a hierarchy remains. Beside the labs' shared agents, it offers each person's own AI and a record they own. Beside Holacracy and Buurtzorg, it borrows what worked and leaves what failed. If it goes ahead: measure relationships before and after the ten days, set out how a company runs its own beach, and pilot between organisations first.

WorkBoard

1.11111112

Built around the manager: goals cascade down, and AI agents help managers. The opposite premise; the two can coexist where a hierarchy remains, and they meet through MCP inside a person's own AI.

The labs' shared agents

1.11111113

Claude Tag, or Microsoft's agent boss: one shared vendor AI tracks progress and prompts everyone. The real rival for the gap managers leave.

Holacracy

1.11111114

Roles and circles, coordinated through governance meetings, with lead links kept. A cautionary tale, after Zappos.

The Buurtzorg model

1.11111115

Small self-managing teams, coaches with no authority, and shared IT. The closest precedent: borrow its rules.

Relational coordination

1.11111116

Jody Hoffer Gittell's relationships across roles, measured by a survey and built through structures that cut across departments. An ally and the evidence base: its survey, run before and after the ten days, gives buyers evidence they already recognise.

Network analysis

1.11111117

Rob Cross's method finds the connectors from collaboration data. It overlaps the one message of the ten days, which asks people directly instead.
