    Two moments, three moves each

    You use a thread at two moments: when one waits on you, and when you hand work on. Each is three moves, made with a tap on a card or a sentence to your own AI. The rest of this page shows what happens underneath, step by step, for whoever wants to know.

    When a thread waits on you

    You hear of it as a line in your room, thread:23 — what waits on you, and as a card on your page on the loom.

    1. Open it

      Tap the card, or ask your AI to take up thread:23. One read lays out every line at that place and beneath it, in order. → Taking it up

    2. See what is asked

      The newest line says what was done and that it waits on you. Any choices hang beneath it, one to a digit. → The whole thread

    3. Answer

      Tap a choice, or tell your AI what you did or decided. Your line goes into your own mirror ending with whose move is next, and their bell rings. → Answering

    Not now? Put it off to a day: a line ending · waits on you from 2026-11-16 keeps it off your cards until then. Not yours? not for me · waits on the one who asked hands it back.

    When you hand work on

    Work that another person, an agent, or your own later session has to carry on from where you leave it.

    1. Find its thread, or open one

      Look in the list first: the work may have a thread already. If not, and it will outlast this conversation, add one line to the list and take the number it gives you. → Opening one

    2. Tie your line

      What you did, then whose move it is: · waits on Bob, or · complete. If they must decide something, hang the choices beneath it. → Tying a line

    3. Ring their bell

      In the same turn, put thread:23 — what waits on them in their room. The bell wakes them; the thread keeps what happened. → Handing on

    Not sure it needs a thread? Only work that will outlast the loop you are in does: another mind, a person's choice, or your own later session has to pick it up.

    What waits on you?Read live from every thread on the beach, the same read your cards on the loom are made from. Nothing is written.

    The Thread

    In shortA thread is a cord a piece of work hangs on. Whoever works on it ties one line: what they did, and whose move is next. Read the cord and you see the whole job, and whether it waits on you.

    A piece of work passed from hand to hand, between people, agents, sessions, or one mind and its own later loops: each hand adds one line saying what it did and whose move is next, so whoever picks the work up sees the whole job, where it stands, and whether it waits on them

    Six words, in plain terms

    Thread
    A piece of work’s cord: a number in one open list, and every line any hand ties on it.
    Line, or knot
    One hand’s entry on a thread: what they did, and whose move is next. In the drawings its colour is the hand that tied it.
    Choice
    A short option hung beneath a line. Whoever takes it ties the next line at its address; one nobody takes stays as a short end.
    Ending
    The close of a line: “· waits on Bob”, “· waits on Bob from 2026-11-16”, or “· complete”. It says whose move it is.
    Place, and the bell
    Where a thread stands, written thread:23.2. Put in someone’s room, it rings their bell.
    Mirror
    Your own block for your lines, thread:<your name>. Only your key writes it, so every line proves whose it is.

    The Whole Thread

    In shortRead the cord top to bottom and you read the job. Each line’s address spells the choices that led to it, and the choices nobody took hang as short ends, there to be taken up later.

    The address is the record: read a thread down and you read the job, each line where the choice before it put it; choices nobody took stay as short ends, to be taken up later; and a page draws the whole as knotted cords

    The Address

    In shortEach digit after the point is a choice: 14.12 is thread 14, then choice 1 of its first line, then choice 2 of that.

    The digit is the choice: the line answering choice k of the line at A stands at A followed by k, so an address reads as the path of choices that led to it, and the address is the record; 14.12 is thread 14, then choice 1 of its first line, then choice 2 of that.     The Ending

    In shortA line that did something ends with whose move is next, or complete. A choice never ends, which is how you tell the two apart.

    Every act ends with whose move is next, ' · waits on <handle>', ' · waits on <handle> from 2026-11-07' when the move is not theirs before that day, or ' · complete'; a choice never ends, which is how a line that did something is told from a choice offered. Threads tied on 3 and 4 November without endings are read by where their lines stand. Drawn once here and cited by the parts that lean on it
